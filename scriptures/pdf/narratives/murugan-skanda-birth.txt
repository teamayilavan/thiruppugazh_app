The birth of Skanda, the son of Shiva who leads the army of the gods, is told in the great Sanskrit epics as well as in the Puranas, and the tellings differ in their details. What they share is a simple outline: the power of Shiva was too great for any one bearer, it passed from Agni, the god of fire, to the river Ganga, and the child was born in a thicket of reeds and nursed by the six Krittikas, the stars of the Pleiades.

In the Ramayana the story is told to Rama by the sage Vishvamitra. The power of Shiva is borne first by Agni and then by the Ganga, who cannot hold it and leaves it at the foot of the Himalaya; the child is born there, the Krittikas nurse him, and he is called Karttikeya and Kumara (Ramayana 1.36.5–18; 1.37.1–28). The Mahabharata tells that the fiery energy of Rudra went to Agni and to the Ganga, and that the Krittikas found the child in the reed thicket; there he is named Skanda and Guha (Mahabharata 9.43.7–14; also 13.84–86). The Vana Parva preserves a different birth again, in which the child is born of Agni through Svaha (Mahabharata 3.213–221). The Shiva Purana tells the story with the anger of Parvati at the gods for disturbing the Lord (Rudra-samhita, Kumara-khanda, chapter 2), and the Skanda Purana has its own account (Kedara-khanda, chapter 27).

The Tamil Kanda Puranam of Kachchiyappa Sivachariyar tells it in its own way. The gods ask Shiva for a son to destroy the asuras (Utpatti Kandam 906). Sparks of fire come forth from the forehead eyes of Shiva's six faces (914–915), and Shiva bids Vayu and Agni carry them to the Ganga (917). The Ganga places them in the Saravana pond (937), where the child appears on a lotus (950). The six Krittikas come to him; the child becomes six, and each of them feeds one (966–967). Later Uma takes up the six forms in her hands, and they become one, Kandan, with six faces (1034–1035).

The Thiruppugazh follows the Tamil telling. In song 476, sung at Chidambaram, Murugan is the child born from the eye of the Lord of Thillai. In song 1005, Uma and the six maidens call him their child. Song 929, at Karuvur, praises the one born in the Saravana from the Supreme Lord. The epic story and the Tamil one meet in the reeds of the Saravana, which give Murugan one of his oldest names.
