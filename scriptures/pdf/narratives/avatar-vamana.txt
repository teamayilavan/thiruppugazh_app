The story of Vamana is told at length in the eighth book of the Bhagavata Purana. The Lord appeared to Aditi and Kashyapa and was seen by the sages as a dwarf student, a brahmachari (Bhagavata Purana 8.18.12–13). Hearing that King Bali, the demon king who had conquered the three worlds, was performing a great sacrifice on the northern bank of the Narmada, he went there; his brilliance made the priests wonder whether the sun had come down among them (8.18.20–22). Bali welcomed him, washed his feet and invited him to ask for anything (8.18.26–32).

The dwarf asked for only three paces of land, measured by his own steps (8.19.16). Bali urged him to ask for more, but the dwarf replied that one not content with three paces would not be content with a continent (8.19.18–24). Bali's teacher Shukracharya warned him that this was Vishnu himself, who would take the three worlds (8.19.29–35). Bali, after a silence, answered that as the grandson of Prahlada he could not go back on his word (8.20.1–4), and he held to it even when his teacher cursed him (8.20.14–15). With his wife Vindhyavali he washed the Lord's feet and poured the water of the gift (8.20.17–18).

Then the dwarf grew until Bali saw all the worlds within the Lord's body (8.20.21–23). With his second step he covered the heavens, and there was no room left for a third (8.20.34). Brahma washed the foot with water from his pot, and that water became the Ganga (8.21.4). Garuda bound Bali with Varuna's ropes (8.21.26–27), and the Lord asked where he should place his third step (8.21.29). Bali asked him to place it on his head, fearing dishonour more than any loss (8.22.2–3). The Lord sent him to Sutala and promised to protect him there (8.22.32–35).

The Valmiki Ramayana tells it in brief, naming the dwarf as Trivikrama, born of Aditi (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kandam 29.9, 19, 20), and Kamban has Viswamitra tell it to Rama: the gift of three paces, the dwarf growing to fill earth and sky, and his return to the milk ocean (Kamba Ramayanam, Velvi padalam 15, 24–27). Andal sings of "the one who grew tall and measured the worlds" (Thiruppavai 3, 24), and Thirumazhisai Alvar tells how Brahma's water on the measuring foot fell on Shiva's head (Nanmugan Thiruvandhadhi 9).

Arunagiri recalls the story in eighteen songs, almost always as "the one who measured the worlds". Song 134 (Palani) praises Murugan as the nephew of the Lord who was glad to measure the world; song 970 (Sri Purushamangai) the Mayan who measured the great earth; and song 367 (Thiruvannamalai) the small one who measured all the worlds and whom none can measure.
