The Bhagavata Purana tells the birth of Brahma as part of the sage Maitreya's account of creation to Vidura. When the three worlds lay submerged in the waters of dissolution, Vishnu lay alone on the serpent Ananta (Bhagavata Purana 3.8.10). When the time came for the worlds to begin again, the subtle seed of creation, stirred into motion, pierced through his navel (3.8.13) and took the shape of a lotus bud, which lit up everything like the sun and dried the waters of the flood (3.8.14). The Lord himself entered that lotus, and from it was born the self-born one, Brahma (3.8.15).

Brahma, seated in the whorl of the flower, could see no world at all. He looked about in every direction, and so came to have four heads, one facing each quarter (3.8.16). He understood neither the lotus nor himself. He asked who he was, sitting on the lotus, and where it had sprung from, and he went down through the stem toward the navel, but could not find its root (3.8.17–19). Giving up the search, he returned to the flower and fixed his mind in meditation, and at its end he saw within his own heart what he had not found by searching (3.8.21–22): the Lord lying alone on the white serpent bed upon the water, the jewels of the serpent's hoods lighting up the darkness (3.8.23). Then he saw the lotus in the Lord's navel, the waters, the air and the sky, and, moved to create, he began with prayers to the Lord (3.8.32–33).

The Alvars hold this picture close. Thirumazhisai Alvar opens his Nanmugan Thiruvandhadhi with the words that Narayana made the four-faced Brahma (Nanmugan Thiruvandhadhi 1), and Thirumangai Alvar sings of the Lord who swallowed the worlds and made four-headed Brahma on the lotus of his navel, naming Srirangam as his home (Periya Thirumozhi 5.4.1).

Arunagiri does not tell the story; he uses it as a title. In four songs Vishnu is the father of Brahma, and Murugan is that Lord's nephew. Song 150 (Palani) speaks of the dark cloud who sleeps upon the sea and gave the world Brahma; song 175 (Palani) of Sridhara, the good father of the four-faced one; song 612 (Oothimalai) of the father of the four-faced one; and song 743 (Thirunavalur) of Narayana, from whom the four-faced one, learned in the four Vedas, was born. Elsewhere in the songs Brahma is often simply the one who sits on the lotus.
