The peacock on which Murugan rides and the cock on his banner are, in the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, the last gift of the war with Surapadman. The Puranam tells their origin at the end of the Yuddha Kandam, after the asura king has been split in two by the spear.

Surapadman had a boon that he could not be destroyed. When the spear, having bathed in the Ganga, returned to Murugan's hand (Yuddha Kandam 2888), the two halves of the asura's body rose again, one as a cock and one as a peacock, and came to fight the gods' commander once more (2889). One shone like a gem-bright mountain and one like a heap of emeralds; each had grown feet and wings, and they came roaring (2890). The text says that it was the asura's fate, to work out the fruit of his old deeds and to come into service, that made him take this form and come before Murugan (2891).

But when the peacock came before Murugan, the hatred went out of him. Under the glance of the Lord, Surapadman put away his enmity and became calm in mind, as black iron turns to gold (2892). The poet adds that even those who have done evil become pure when they stand before Kumara, and that Surapadman, the son of Maya, received boundless grace and was saved (2893).

Then Murugan, in his grace, turns to the cock and tells it to become the banner on his chariot (2894). The cock agrees, rises into the sky with a roar that makes the thunder and the fire tremble, and becomes the red-crested cock on the flag (2895). To the peacock Murugan says, "Carry us," and, leaving the mount he had before, he rides it (2896). The peacock spreads its tail and flies; as it goes the sea breaks, the great serpent that bears the world trembles, and the six-faced lord is carried across the sky and to every quarter (2897 to 2900).

The Puranam tells of another peacock in the same war: before this, Indra himself had taken the form of a peacock and carried Murugan into the fight (2775 to 2780). But it is the peacock that was Surapadman, and the cock on the banner, that stay with Murugan, and they are the peacock and the cock that the songs of the Thiruppugazh know.
