In some forty-eight songs the Thiruppugazh says that Murugan's spear dried up the sea, or made it boil, to drive out the demon Surapadman. It is a good example of how the poet condenses a long story into a few words.

The Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, in the Yuddha Kandam, does not tell it in these words. In the Puranam it is Surapadman himself who goes into the sea. On the last day of the war, when the spear is already coming at him, he leaves the sky and goes into the middle of the sea (Yuddha Kandam 2864), and it is there that he stands as the great mango tree. The spear does not drive him out; it follows him.

What the text does say about the sea and the spear is this. As the spear flies at the tree, it breathes out a mass of flame that spreads over all the seven oceans and the sky and the regions around, so that the whole world seems to be on fire (2882 to 2883). Earlier, while Murugan and Surapadman exchanged arrows, the poet says that fire rose from the arrows and caught the earth, and that the sea dried up and the Ganga went dry (2592). And when Murugan rode the peacock that had been Indra, the beating of its wings made even the wide sea dry up (2776).

So the picture in the songs, a spear that dries the ocean, is Arunagiri's compression of these images: the flame of the spear over the seas, and the dried waters of the war. It is not the same as another tale of the Tamil country, in which the Pandya prince Ugrakumaran, with a spear given him by Shiva, makes the sea dry up (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 13). The Kanda Puranam supports the songs only in the images above, not in a single act of the spear drying the sea.
