Among all the stories of Murugan, none is repeated in the Thiruppugazh more often than this: that the child Murugan taught his father Shiva the meaning of the sacred syllable Om, the Pranava. The story begins with Brahma. In the Kanda Puranam of Kachchiyappa Sivachariyar, Brahma, slighting the young Murugan, is questioned about the Vedas and creation (Utpatti Kandam 1210). When Brahma begins with the first word of the Vedas, Murugan asks him the meaning of that one syllable, Om (1213). Brahma stares, bewildered, and cannot answer (1214). Murugan strikes him on his four heads with his knuckles (1218), shuts him in prison, and takes over the work of creation himself (1219).

Shiva then comes to Murugan with Vishnu and the gods and asks him to set Brahma free (1246). Murugan leads Brahma by the hand to his father (1256), and Shiva tells Brahma to resume his work as before (1258). Then the Lord smiles at Murugan, takes his hand and seats him on his lap (1259). Embracing him, he asks: Brahma could not explain the meaning of the word Om; do you know it? If so, tell it now (1260). Murugan replies that so ancient and secret a truth should not be told openly for all to hear (1261). Shiva laughs and offers his own ear, and the six-faced one tells him the truth of the one word; and the Lord of wisdom, hearing it, is pleased (1262). His doubt removed and rejoicing, Shiva gives his young son a place of honour and returns to Kailasa (1263), while Murugan remains on Mount Skanda, seated on a golden throne (1264). The Puranam does not say what was whispered.

The Kanda Puranam sets the scene on Mount Skanda. Later Tamil tradition places it at Swamimalai, near Kumbakonam, where Murugan is worshipped as Swaminatha, the teacher of the Lord, and where he is shown as the guru with Shiva as his pupil.

Arunagiri returns to the story in nearly seventy songs. The famous song 6 at Thiruvarunai, "Muththaith tharu", says that Murugan taught the three-eyed Lord what comes before all scripture. Song 136, at Palani, speaks of the teacher who recited the Pranava into his ear; song 176 of Parasivan hearing the secret of the Pranava; song 240, at Thiruthanigai, of the one word spoken so that both of the Lord's ears rejoiced. Songs 213 and 227 at Swamimalai call Murugan the guru of the Lord with the crescent in his hair. For the poet, the title Gurunatha, teacher of the Lord, is one of the dearest names of Murugan.
