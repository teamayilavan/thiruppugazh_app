The Bhagavata Purana tells that the cowherds of Vraja used to hold a yearly sacrifice to Indra, the god of rain, and that the child Krishna persuaded his father Nanda to turn the offering instead to Govardhana hill, to the cows and to the brahmins, since it was the hill and the herds that sustained them (Bhagavata Purana 10.24). Indra, learning that his sacrifice had been set aside, grew angry with Nanda and the cowherds and sent against them the clouds of the world's destruction (10.25.1–3).

The clouds poured down rain with wind, lightning, thunder and hail until high and low ground could not be told apart (10.25.8–10). Shivering with cold, the cowherds and their cattle came to Krishna for shelter, the cows covering their heads and their calves with their own bodies (10.25.11–13). Krishna saw that this was Indra's doing and resolved to protect those who had taken refuge in him (10.25.14–18). With one hand he lifted Govardhana, as easily as a child picks up a mushroom (10.25.19), and called the people of Vraja to come under it with their cows and carts, assuring them that the hill would not fall (10.25.20–22). He held it up for seven days without moving, unmindful of hunger and thirst (10.25.23). Indra, astonished and humbled, called back his clouds; when the sun came out Krishna told everyone to come out, and set the hill back in its place (10.25.24–28). Indra later came to ask his pardon (10.27). The image of the hill balanced on Krishna's little finger is the form in which tradition and temple art most often show it.

The Alvars return to the scene often. Periyazhwar describes Indra's thundering rain at Thiruvaypadi and the Lord holding up the hill as an umbrella (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 4.7.6), and the one who lifted the hill to protect the cows (4.10.9); Thirumangai Alvar sings of him making the hailstorm useless with the hill (Periya Thirumozhi 8.6.9); and Andal praises the hill raised as an umbrella among the Lord's great deeds (Tiruppavai 24).

Arunagiri recalls the lifting in about five songs. In song 116 (Palani) the Lord holds the hill as an umbrella so that the storm loses its force; in song 674 (Thiruvalangadu) he shelters the calves under the hill before they come to harm; in song 770 (Sirkazhi) he relieves the herds' suffering in the driving rain; and in song 1183 he is the one who lifted the hill in the rain and saved the cattle. Murugan is praised as his nephew.
