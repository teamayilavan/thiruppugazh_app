Cheraman Perumal, who appears among the sixty-three Nayanmars under the name Kazharitrarivar, was a king of the Chera country on the western coast and a devotee of Shiva. The Periya Puranam devotes a long section to him, and much of it is the story of his friendship with the saint Sundarar: how the two met, how they honoured each other, and how the king missed his friend when they were apart (Periya Puranam, Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam 112–118, 173).

The same section announces the end of that friendship on earth. Sekkizhar says that Sundarar left the land of the Kaveri, came to the Chera capital Mahodai, and from there went up to holy Kailasa on a great elephant; he adds that he will tell later of the king's act of spurring his horse ahead (Kazharitrarivar Nayanar Puranam 174). The fuller account of the ascent was not among the sections read for this book, so the details of the ride are not given here from the Puranam. Tradition adds that the king whispered the five syllables of Shiva's name into his horse's ear, and that the horse rose into the sky and went before his friend's elephant.

The king is also remembered as a poet. The Tirukkailaya Gnana Ula, the "Ula of Kailasa", is ascribed to him and is preserved in the eleventh book of the Shaiva canon (Tirumurai 11). It is a poem of the Lord's procession at Kailasa. The poem itself does not name Sundarar; the link between the poem and the journey to Kailasa with his friend comes from the tradition that the king sang it on arrival there.

Arunagiri gathers the story into a single passage of song 170, sung at Palani. As the song's commentary reads it, Cheraman Perumal sought the friendship of Sundarar and rode a dancing horse to Kailasa in his company, and there sang the "Adi Ula", the first of the Ulas. Arunagiri calls Murugan the Lord of the land where the king ruled, and of Vayalur. For him the story is one of friendship that would not be left behind: a king who did not wait to be called, and who arrived with a poem in his hands.
