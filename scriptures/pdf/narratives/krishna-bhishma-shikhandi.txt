Bhishma, son of the river Ganga and grandsire of both the Kauravas and the Pandavas, fought on the Kaurava side in the war at Kurukshetra and for ten days could not be overcome. The Mahabharata's Bhishma Parva tells how the Pandavas, despairing, went to him and asked how he might be defeated, and how he told them: he would not fight Shikhandi, who had been born a woman, and if Arjuna attacked him from behind Shikhandi he would not resist. Shikhandi's story, as told in the Udyoga Parva, is that of Amba, the princess whom Bhishma had carried off and later sent away, reborn to bring about his end.

On the tenth day it was done as he had said. The verse of the critical edition is brief: placing Shikhandi in front, Arjuna pierced Bhishma in the battle (Mahabharata, Bhishma Parva 6.114.53). In the widely told account that follows in the same parva, Bhishma knows that the arrows striking him are Arjuna's, falls from his chariot as the sun goes down, and lies on a bed of arrows that keep his body from the ground. The fighting stops, and warriors of both armies come to honour him. He asks for a pillow, and Arjuna gives him one of arrows. Having the boon of choosing the time of his own death, he waits for the sun to turn north before he dies, and in the meantime instructs Yudhishthira at length, in the Shanti Parva.

The episode is remembered for its sorrow as much as for its strategy. Bhishma keeps his word to the end, even when it costs him the battle, and Arjuna fights the elder he loves best only because Krishna, his charioteer, holds him to his duty.

Arunagiri touches the story once, and briefly. In song 155 (Palani) he recalls the Lord who drove the chariot, the warrior who was "a eunuch" (that is, Shikhandi), and the Kuru land given to the five Pandavas, praising Murugan as the nephew of that Vishnu who was the Pandavas' friend. The reading of these lines is not certain, and the song names neither Bhishma nor his fall directly.
