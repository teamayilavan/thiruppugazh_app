When the Pandavas had completed their twelve years in the forest and their year in hiding, and Duryodhana still refused to restore their kingdom, Krishna went himself to the Kuru court at Hastinapura as the Pandavas' envoy, hoping to prevent the war. The Mahabharata's Udyoga Parva tells how he declined to lodge with Duryodhana and went instead to the house of Vidura, the wise half-brother of King Dhritarashtra, and stayed there (Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva 5.89.34–35).

In the assembly Krishna pleaded for peace before the blind king and the elders, Bhishma, Drona and Vidura among them, and they urged Duryodhana to accept. He would not. Earlier in the same parva Yudhishthira had sent word that he would be content with as little as five villages, and in the telling best loved in the Tamil country it is Krishna who carries that request to the court; Duryodhana answers that he will not give up even as much land as the point of a needle.

Duryodhana then planned to seize Krishna by force. Krishna, the epic says, laughed aloud and told him: "Suyodhana, in your delusion you think that I am alone" (5.129.2–4). He then showed a blazing form in which Brahma stood on his forehead and Rudra on his chest, with the gods all about him, and at the sight of that terrible self the kings shut their eyes (5.129.5–12). Only a few, among them Bhishma, Drona and Vidura, were able to look. Then Krishna withdrew the vision and left the hall, and the way to war lay open.

The Alvars often call the Lord the Pandavas' messenger: Periyazhwar twice speaks of him as the envoy of the five (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 1.8.3 and 3.9.5), and Thirumangai Alvar sings of the one who went as messenger for the five (Periya Thirumozhi 5.5.6 and 8.6.9), at Thiruvallikkeni as the envoy to Duryodhana (2.3.5).

Arunagiri uses the same image in about eight songs. In song 289 (Thiruthanigai) the Lord is "a messenger to the Kuru king"; in song 574 (Viralimalai) he is Dharma's envoy, named together with Vidura, Drona and the hundred brothers; in song 747 (Thiruvetkalam) he is the Madhava who set Vidura and the serpent-bannered Duryodhana at odds; and in song 1055 he wanders alone as an envoy, so that the hundred brothers and their kings would fall before Arjuna. Murugan is praised as the nephew of this envoy of peace.
