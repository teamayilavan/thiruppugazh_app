The Periya Puranam tells that when Sambandar was in his third year (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 53–54), his father Sivapadahridayar went one morning to bathe in the great temple tank at Sirkazhi. The child followed him, crying, and when the father tried to send him back he stamped his feet and would not go, so the father took him along (vv. 56–57). He seated the boy on the bank, bowed toward the shrine of the Lord of the Boat, and went down into the water (v. 59). There he went under for a long recitation, and the child, not seeing him, began to weep (vv. 60–61).

Sekkizhar describes the crying with tenderness: the tears, the small fists rubbing the eyes, the trembling lips (v. 62). The child looked up at the top of the Thoni shrine and called out, "Amme! Appa!" (Mother! Father!) (v. 63). Shiva, seated in that shrine, came down with the Goddess on the bull to the side of the tank (v. 64). He asked Uma to feed the child the milk from her breast in a golden bowl (v. 66); she filled the bowl, mixed into it the nectar of the knowledge of Shiva, which no one can measure, wiped the child's tears and put the golden cup into his hands (vv. 67–68).

The Puranam adds that because the Father and Mother of all had done this, the child became the one joined to Shiva-wisdom, Thirugnana Sambandar, possessing knowledge that even gods and sages find hard to reach (vv. 69–70). The father's return and the child's first hymn, "Todudaiya Seviyan", follow immediately (vv. 72–76; Tevaram 1.1.1), and are told in their own story. The details of the light and the staging in the film are added for the screen; the text gives the tank, the cry, the divine pair, the bowl and the milk.

Arunagiri returns to this moment in at least ten songs. He speaks of the child who drank the milk from Uma's breast and then sang, and he links the gift directly to the Tevaram: the hymns, in his telling, are what that milk became. Song 426 (Thiruvannamalai) recalls the milk the Goddess gave; song 822 (Thiruvarur) asks whether Murugan's speech is milk, honey or the divine milk fed by Uma; song 837 (Thirukkudavayil) and song 1287 tie the milk to the gift of wisdom and to the hymns. For Arunagiri this is the moment when Murugan, born as Sambandar, received the Mother's wisdom in order to sing Shiva's praise in Tamil.
