Manikkavasagar, the author of the Tiruvasagam, was born at Thiruvathavur in the Pandya country and is known also as Vathavurar, after his town. The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam of Paranjothi tells that he rose to be the chief minister of the Pandya king Arimarthana. When the army needed horses, the king gave him the key of the treasury and sent him to Perunthurai, a port where horses from overseas were landed (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 58).

At Perunthurai the Lord sat as a teacher in the shade of a kurundam tree, with disciples around him. Vathavurar bowed before him and received his teaching, and the darkness of his mind was removed. When he went back to the tree, the teacher could no longer be seen, and he wandered about it like one who had lost his heart. He spent the king's gold on the temple at Perunthurai and kept no account of it. When the time allowed by the king ran out, he prayed, and a voice told him that the horses would come and that he should go back to Madurai without fear (padalam 58). The Thiruppugazh commentary on song 512 adds that the Lord placed his feet on the minister's head and showed him his form.

The king waited. When the promised day passed and no horses came, he had the minister punished, and Vathavurar held only to the Lord's feet (padalam 59). Then the Lord turned the foxes of the forest into horses and his attendants into grooms, and came at their head as a horseman. He handed over the horses, exchanging a rope as a sign of the sale; the king rose to greet him without knowing why, and honoured him with fine cloth, and the minister was set free (padalam 59). That night the horses turned back into foxes and ran into the forest. The king, believing himself cheated, had the minister made to stand on the hot sands of the Vaigai, and the Lord in answer sent the river into flood (padalam 60), which leads to the story of Vandi and her pittu (padalam 61).

The minister's own work is the Tiruvasagam (Tirumurai 8), which opens with the Sivapuranam and its greeting to the five syllables. Arunagiri recalls the story in song 512, sung at Chidambaram, where the minister later went: the Lord appeared at Perunthurai as the master of Manikkavasagar, who had come to buy horses for the king, set his feet on his head and taught him the meaning of true knowledge. The horses that were foxes are, in the song's telling, part of the same grace.
