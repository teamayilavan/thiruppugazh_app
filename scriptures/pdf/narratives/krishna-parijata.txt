The bringing of the parijata tree to earth is the sequel to Krishna's victory over Naraka. Naraka had stolen the earrings of Aditi, the mother of the gods, and after his death the earth goddess returned them to Krishna (Bhagavata Purana 10.59.23). The Bhagavata then tells, in a few verses, how Krishna went to the abode of Indra and gave Aditi her earrings, and how Indra and his queen worshipped Krishna and his beloved consort Satyabhama. At Satyabhama's request Krishna uprooted the heavenly parijata tree and put it on the back of Garuda, and after defeating Indra and the other gods he brought it to his capital (10.59.38–39). Planted there, it beautified the garden of Satyabhama's palace, and bees followed it all the way from heaven, greedy for its fragrance and sweet sap (10.59.40).

The Bhagavata adds a sharp comment of its own. Indra had bowed to Krishna, touching his feet with his crown, and begged him to recover what had been stolen; yet once his purpose was achieved he chose to fight the Lord. "What ignorance there is among the gods!" the narrator exclaims, blaming the pride that wealth brings (10.59.41).

The story is told at much greater length in the Harivamsa and the Puranic tradition, where Satyabhama's longing for the tree, the refusal of Indra's queen and the contest between Krishna and Indra become a full episode, and it has long been a favourite subject of dance and drama in South India under the name Parijatapaharanam. The account here follows the Bhagavata alone. In the Tamil tradition Andal recalls it in a single question: did not Kannan bring the fragrant parijata tree from heaven for his beloved? (Nachiyar Thirumozhi 4.8).

Arunagiri refers to the story once. In song 885, sung at Thanjavur, he praises Murugan as the nephew of the conch-bearing Lord, the first Keshava, who brought Indra's tree down to the earth, the jewel-chested hero of great valour. As so often in the Thiruppugazh, a whole episode is gathered into a single epithet: the uncle who could bring a tree of heaven to earth is honoured, and through him, his nephew.
