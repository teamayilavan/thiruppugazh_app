When Rama reached the southern shore with the monkey army, the sea lay between him and Lanka. After Rama's anger at the ocean, the Lord of the Sea himself appeared and told him that among the monkeys was Nala, son of the divine architect Vishvakarma, who could build a causeway that the sea would bear. Nala undertook the work, and the monkeys broke trees and rocks and carried them to the shore (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 22.50–78).

Valmiki counts the progress day by day: on each of five days a fresh stretch of the bridge was laid across the water (Yuddha 22.66–70), until it reached Lanka. He then describes the finished causeway and the gods and heavenly beings who came to look upon it (22.72–78). Over it the whole army crossed to the island.

Kamban fills the scene with the energy of the work. When Nala called for stone, he took in one hand a mountain that three crores of monkeys had lifted together and laid it in place, showing his strength and making the sea tremble (Kamba Ramayanam, Setu Bandhana Padalam 8). As the army's leaders shifted the hills, the clouds fled crying and the yakshas and their women ran away (10). Nala broke stones and packed them close, laid mountains side by side and smoothed them with sand (46). In Kamban the bridge reaches Lanka within three days, and the cheering of the army splits the sky (65); lying on the sea for the Lord who would end Sita's sorrow, it shone like Adishesha saying, "Run upon my back" (66). The much-loved tale of the squirrel that rolled in sand to help, and was stroked by Rama, is a later devotional tradition and not part of these passages.

The Azhwars sing the bridge often. Periyazhwar speaks of the monkeys dropping mountains into the dark sea to make the causeway (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.9.10) and of the dam built of mountains across the ocean (2.6.8), and Kulasekhara Azhwar recalls Rama crossing on it to Lanka (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.7).

The bridge appears in fourteen of Arunagiri's songs, more often than almost any other moment of the war. Usually it comes in a phrase of the closing line: the nephew of the Lord who blocked the sea and went to Lanka. Song 166 (Palani) speaks of the one who dammed the wave-tossed ocean and cut down Ravana's crowns; song 840 (Vedaranyam) of the one who, at the word of the Lord of the Sea, blocked the ocean and gave Lanka to Vibhishana; and song 1125 joins the bridge to the arrow loosed at the sea.
