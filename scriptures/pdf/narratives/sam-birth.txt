Sirkazhi, on the Chola coast, was known by twelve names, and the Periya Puranam lists them all: Brahmapuram, Venupuram, Pugali, Venguru, Thonipuram, Poontharai, Sirapuram, Puravam, Shanbai, Kazhi, Kochchaivayam and Kazhumalam (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 14). Sekkizhar opens his account of the town with its paddy fields and flower gardens, its tall white houses and its streets where Brahmin children played at the sacrificial rites (vv. 10–13).

In that town lived Sivapadahridayar, of the Kavuniya gotra, a man of disciplined life rooted in the Vedas (v. 15). His wife Bhagavathiyar came of the same tradition, and both families had long been devoted to Shiva and to the holy ash (vv. 16–17). The Puranam says that Sivapadahridayar was grieved to see the Jains and Buddhists gaining ground and the worship of Shiva declining, and that he prayed for a son who would turn the people back (vv. 18–19). He and his wife worshipped at the shrine of the Lord of the Boat, Thoniappar, at Sirkazhi, and in due course she conceived; the family performed the rites of each month of pregnancy with great joy (vv. 20–21).

The child was born on the day of the star Thiruvathirai (Ardra), with the planets, the poet says, standing at their highest (v. 22). The verses that follow describe the birth as a turning point for Shaivism, for the Vedic way and for the Tamil language itself (vv. 23–26). When it happened, the people of the town felt a thrill of joy before they knew its cause, and only afterwards learnt that a son had been born in the Kavuniya house (vv. 27–28). The gardens opened, a cool breeze blew from the Malaya hills, and the gods rained down flowers while the priests of Sirkazhi performed their fire rituals (vv. 29–30). The details of the household itself, beyond the names, the place and these signs, are not given, and the film adds them for the screen.

The Thiruppugazh does not tell the birth as an episode. Arunagiri uses it as a title for Murugan: the one who came to Pugali (song 134, Palani), the poet born among the Kavuniyas of Pugali (song 261, Thiruthani), the child who grew up in famous Kochchai (song 758, Thiruvarathurai). Song 764 and song 774, both sung at Sirkazhi itself, praise the town and say that Murugan was born there as Sambandar, and song 837 says he hid his twelve shoulders, his peacock and his spear so that no one would know him when he took birth as a Brahmin boy. That Murugan himself was the child is the belief of the songs, not a statement of the Periya Puranam.
