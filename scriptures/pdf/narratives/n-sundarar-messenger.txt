Sundarar's household life is told at length in the Periya Puranam, and one of its best-loved episodes is the night the Lord became his messenger. Sundarar was married to Paravai of Tiruvarur. At Tiruvorriyur he married Sangili, and when the news reached Paravai, she was overcome by an anger she could not master (Periya Puranam, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar Puranam 314). When Sundarar came back to Tiruvarur and sent people wise in the world's ways to speak for him, she would not be moved (318). In despair he turned to the Lord in the middle of the night, asking him to come and remove her anger (323), and calling him mother and friend (328).

The Lord agreed, and the Puranam describes his walk through Tiruvarur with great tenderness: the one whom Vishnu and Brahma could not find went to Paravai's house as a messenger, with gods, sages and his hosts following, the streets filling with flowers (331–336). At her door he left them outside and went in alone, in the form of the brahmin priest who worshipped him in the temple (337). Paravai, who was awake, opened the door, wondering why the priest had come at midnight (338–340). He asked her to let Sundarar come back. She replied that it was not fitting for such a man to carry such a message, and that she would not agree; the Lord, smiling within, did not reveal himself and turned back (341–346).

Sundarar, waiting, was in anguish, and when he heard he fell down and said his life would leave him if she still refused (347–355). The Lord promised to go again (356). Meanwhile Paravai understood that the priest had been the Lord himself, and was full of dread at having refused him (359–360). He came a second time in his own form, with all his hosts, so that her house looked like Kailasa; she fell at his feet, and he asked her again to receive Sundarar (361–364).

Arunagiri returns to this night in four songs. In song 134 (Palani) Shiva once went to Paravai's house as a messenger. In song 1298 he was willing to go to her many times, and Arunagiri asks Murugan to be as great as his father. Song 940 (Pattaliyur) says the world was amazed at his compassion for Paravai in her separation, and song 1317 (Pazhamudircholai) that he went as messenger to please Sundarar, the poet. For Arunagiri, the Lord who knocks at a door at midnight for a friend is the father of Murugan.
