Thirugnanasambandar, the child saint of Sirkazhi, is one of the three poets of the Tevaram, and his life fills the longest of the lives in the Periya Puranam. He was born at Sirkazhi, also called Kazhumalam and Pugali, in the Chola country (Periya Puranam, Thirugnanasambandar Puranam 2). When he was a small child, crying by the temple tank while his father bathed, the Lord on the hill of Tirutoni saw him and bade Uma feed him milk in a golden bowl (66); she gave him the milk of the wisdom of Shiva (68). When his father asked who had fed him, the child sang the hymn "Todudaiya seviyan" and pointed out the Lord who had done it (76; Tevaram 1.1.1).

The centre of his story is Madurai. The Pandya king, known in tradition as Koon Pandyan, followed the Jains, but his queen Mangayarkkarasi and his minister Kulachchirai were devoted to Shiva, and they invited Sambandar. Sambandar reassured the frightened queen in a hymn ("I am no child to the Jains", Tevaram 3.39.1). When the king was struck with a burning fever on his right side, Sambandar smeared him with holy ash, singing the hymn on the holy ash, and the fever left him; the king grew cool like one bathing in a pond (Periya Puranam 764–765; Tevaram 2.66.1). Then came the contests. A fire was made for the test of the palm leaves (Periya Puranam 779). In the river test, Sambandar's leaf was cast into the Vaigai and went upstream against the flood while the Jains' leaf was swept away (846); at that moment the king's hunched back was made straight (847), and the leaf halted at Edagam, where Kulachchirai took it up (850; Tevaram 3.32.1). The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam tells the same events (padalams 62 and 63).

The Periya Puranam goes on to say that the defeated Jains, eight thousand of them, were made to mount the stakes by the minister (855–856). This hard passage is recorded here because the sources tell it; it is not shown in the film.

Arunagiri refers to Sambandar in about twenty songs, and for him the Madurai story is part of Murugan's own glory, since he regards Sambandar as Murugan himself come in Tamil (songs 668, 1282). Song 104, at Palani, recalls the Pandya's fever healed with ash; song 261, at Thiruthanigai, Sambandar of Pugali who spread the holy ash; song 542, at Thirukkazhukundram, the palm leaf that went against the river; song 1287, the Tamil child who drank Uma's milk. Many of the songs, such as 17, 149 and 150, also name the impaling of the Jains, as the tradition of his time told it.
