When Rama and the monkey army reached the southern shore, the ocean lay between them and Lanka. Rama resolved to ask the lord of the sea for a way across. He lay down on a bed of kusha grass on the shore, facing the ocean, and waited in prayer for three nights, but the sea-god did not appear. Valmiki then tells how Rama's patience gave way to anger: he declared that he would dry up the ocean so that the army could cross on foot, took up his bow and loosed his arrows into the waters (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha 21.17–26). The ocean was thrown into turmoil and its creatures were terrified. In the following chapter the sea-god rises from the waters, explains that it is his nature to be deep and impassable, and advises that Nala, a skilled builder among the monkeys, construct a bridge, which he promises to support.

Kamban gives the same scene with great force. Rama's anger rises from the thought that the proud sea has not given even a reply of yes or no, and it flares in him like fire springing up in the midst of water (Kamba Ramayanam, Kadal Seeriya Padalam 4). His face shines like the sun at the end of an age, and as he asks for his bow, Lakshmana too burns with anger (12). He takes the bow, binds on the quiver and strikes the string, and its sound echoes through the worlds (13). Then the fire of his arrows turns the ocean into a sacrificial pit, with fish, serpents and mountains for fuel and the foaming water for the ghee of the offering (16).

The Alvars connect the episode with the bridge that follows. Kulasekhara Alvar sings of Rama's arrow stirring the ocean, which then asks that a bridge be built (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.7), and the commentary on Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 2.6.8 tells of the bow and the arrow at the ocean and the damming of the sea.

Arunagiri refers to the episode in six songs. In song 1125 (a general song) Murugan is the nephew of Hari, who loosed an arrow to scorch the wave-filled sea. In song 738 (Thiruvathigai) the Lord who sleeps on the serpent in the ocean of milk sends a fiery arrow into the ocean. In song 820 (Thiruvarur) Narayana chooses a single arrow so that the lord of rivers, the ocean, cries out. Song 771 (Sirkazhi) speaks of the hero who set one arrow to his hand so that the long sea faded, and song 1091 (a general song) of the arrow that dried up the sea.
