The tenth book of the Bhagavata Purana tells that in the first month of the cold season the unmarried girls of Gokula kept a vow to the goddess Katyayani, eating only plain food. At sunrise each day they bathed in the Yamuna, made an image of the goddess from earth, and worshipped her with fragrances, lamps and flowers, each praying that Nanda's son might become her husband (Bhagavata Purana 10.22.1–5).

One morning, when they had left their clothes on the bank and gone into the river, Krishna took the garments, climbed a kadamba tree and teased them, while his friends laughed. He told them to come out and take their clothes, adding that he had never told a lie. The girls, laughing in their embarrassment and shivering in the water, begged him to give back their clothes, calling themselves his servants (10.22.9–15). Krishna told them that bathing unclothed in the river was an offence against the gods, and that they should bow with joined hands; they did so, and he gave the clothes back. They felt no resentment, only joy in his nearness, and he told them that their vow had been accepted and their wish would be fulfilled (10.22.19–27). In the Bhagavata the story is a lesson in surrender, and it is usually read in that light.

Andal tells the same episode in her own voice in the Nachiyar Thirumozhi, though her tree is the kurundham. The girls beg the Lord who danced on Kaliya to return the clothes he has hung in the tree (Nachiyar Thirumozhi 3.2), complain that making them stand in the water while he sits in the branches is mere mischief (3.3), and ask him at least to stop pretending not to see their distress (3.4).

Arunagiri follows the Tamil form of the story. In song 614 (Thenserigiri) the Lord plays his bamboo flute and then climbs the tree with the girls' garments; in song 1244 he is "the Mayan who climbed the kurundham" with the women's fine cloth; and in song 1072 he is the cloud-coloured one who climbs the kurundham tree. In each, Murugan is praised as his nephew. Arunagiri gives only the image, never the scene in detail.
