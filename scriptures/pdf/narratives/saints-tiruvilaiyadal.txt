The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam of Paranjothi Munivar tells the sixty-four "sacred sports" of Shiva at Madurai, where he is worshipped as Sokkanathar, the beautiful Lord, with the Goddess Meenakshi. In each, the Lord steps into the life of the city to rescue a devotee, humble a proud man, or settle a question of learning, and he does it with a lightness that the word "sport" is meant to convey. Several of these stories are among the best loved in the Tamil country.

The most famous belong to Manikkavasagar. Sent by the Pandya king to buy horses, he met the Lord as a guru under the kurundam tree at Thiruperunthurai and spent the money on the temple (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 58). To save him, the Lord turned the jackals of the forest into horses, made his attendants their grooms, and led them into Madurai himself (padalam 59); at night the horses became jackals again and ran wild through the stable and the town (padalam 60). When the Vaigai flooded and each household had to build up its share of the bank, the Lord came as a labourer to carry earth for Vandi, an old woman who sold pittu, a steamed rice cake; when the work was left undone, the king struck him with his cane (padalam 61). Manikkavasagar himself sings of these deeds: the jackals made into horses and sold to the Pandya (Tiruvasagam, Kirththit Tiruvagaval, lines about 35–36; Ananda Malai 7), and the Lord of Madurai who carried earth for wages and was struck by the king's stick (Tiruvammanai 8). That the blow fell on every living being is the tradition of the telling.

Another group of padalams concerns the Tamil Sangam of Madurai. The poor Brahmin Dharumi is given a verse by the Lord on the fragrance of a woman's hair, and the poet Nakkeerar disputes it (padalam 52). Nakkeerar is burnt for his fearless argument, and then forgiven and brought back to the Sangam (padalam 53), and the Lord teaches him the grammar of meaning (padalam 54). The details of the dispute are told in different ways and are kept brief here.

Arunagiri names these sports in passing. Songs 409, at Thiruvarunai, and 1249 speak of the jackals turned into horses for the Pandya; songs 960, at Madurai, and 1313, at Pazhamudhircholai, name Manikkavasagar of Vadavur and his horses; song 756 recalls the Lord who carried the sand of the Vaigai at Madurai; song 957 praises Sokkar of Madurai, who delights Uma; and song 1192 remembers Nakkeerar of Madurai and his Tamil.
