The story of the fever is the centre of the Madurai episode in the Periya Puranam. The Pandya king, whom Sekkizhar calls Maran and later tradition remembers as Koon Pandyan or Nindrasir Nedumaran, had come under the influence of the Jain teachers, while his queen Mangaiyarkkarasi and his minister Kulachchirai remained devoted to Shiva. They sent for Sambandar (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 603–613), who came to Madurai and was lodged in a monastery near the temple of Alavai (vv. 645–668).

When the king heard of his arrival he asked the Jains what was to be done. They proposed to drive the boy out of the city by setting his lodging on fire through sorcery, and the king consented (vv. 686–689). The spells failed, and so, at night, they set real fire to the hall (vv. 697–700). Sambandar, grieved that such a thing should be done where Shiva's devotees slept, reasoned that the fault belonged to the king who ruled the land (vv. 702–704). In the hymn beginning "Seyyane Thiruvalavai" he asked that the fire set by the Jains go to the Pandya, but slowly ("paiyave"): for the sake of the queen's marriage thread, the minister's devotion, and because the king was destined to return to Shiva's way and be touched by the holy ash (vv. 704–705). The fire became a burning fever in the king's body (v. 706).

The Jains could not cure it. When Sambandar was brought to the palace they proposed to divide the king's body: they would cure the left side by their methods, and he the right (vv. 762–763). They stroked the left side with their peacock feathers and it only burned more; Sambandar sang the hymn of the holy ash, "Mantiramavathu Neeru" (Tevaram 2.66.1), and rubbed the ash on the right side, which became as cool as a lotus pond (vv. 764–765). The king, hot on one side and cool on the other, told the Jains they had lost and asked the boy to cure him completely; a second touch of the ash took the whole fever away (vv. 768–770). The queen and minister rejoiced, and the king said he had been saved (vv. 771–772). Sambandar also sang the hymn "Maninernvizhi" to reassure the queen (Tevaram 3.39.1). The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam retells the episode (padalam 62).

Arunagiri refers to the cure in a dozen songs. Song 171 (Palani) recalls the flames that afflicted the Pandya's body; song 573 (Viralimalai) says Murugan, as Sambandar, cured the fever of the Pandya. Many songs, such as 527, 789 and 962 (Madurai), join the cure of the fever to the straightening of the king's hunched back. In the Puranam the straightening comes later, during the river test (v. 847).
