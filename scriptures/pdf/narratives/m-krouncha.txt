The Krouncha mountain that Murugan's spear pierces is, in the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, an asura turned into a mountain by a sage's curse. The Puranam tells how it came to be a mountain in the Asura Kandam, and how it was pierced in the first kandam, in the tale of Tarakan.

Krouncha was an asura, and a master of illusions. When the sage Agastya was making his way south, at Shiva's command, Krouncha took the shape of a mountain across his road, so that the road itself led into the mountain (Asura Kandam 1214 to 1215). Agastya went in; the way stretched on without end, and then it was lost, and fire, whirlwind and darkness gathered around him (1216 to 1217). The sage saw through the trick with the clear mind of the learned, laughed, struck the mountain with his staff so that it was full of holes, and cursed it: "Remain a mountain, a home for the asuras, and a trouble to ascetics and to gods; the holes my staff has made will be places for your illusions; and in time you will die by the spear that Murugan holds" (1218 to 1221). He sprinkled water over it with a mantra, put an end to the illusion, and went on south (1222).

From that day Krouncha stood as a mountain, a fortress of the asuras (1223). Beside it, in a city of illusion, lived Tarakan, the younger brother of Surapadman (Utpatti Kandam 1331 to 1332), and when Murugan's host came, Krouncha worked its magic against Veerabahu and his warriors, so that they trembled and lost their wits (1433).

The end came with the spear. Murugan sent it at Tarakan and the mountain, and all the illusions of the worlds fell away; it pierced the chest of Tarakan and the body of the mountain together, with a noise as if the earth had burst and Meru split (1509 to 1516). So the curse of Agastya was fulfilled, as he had said.

The songs of the Thiruppugazh speak of Murugan piercing the Krouncha, and sometimes use the words for the whole war, but the story they have in mind is this one: the sage's curse, the mountain of illusions, and the spear that ended it.
