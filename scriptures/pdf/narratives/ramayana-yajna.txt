After the death of Tataka, Vishwamitra brought Rama and Lakshmana to his hermitage, Siddhashrama, where he intended to complete a sacrifice that demons had repeatedly defiled. A sage engaged in such a rite could not fight, and it was for this that he had asked Dasaratha for his son. Vishwamitra entered upon the observance in silence, and the two princes undertook to guard the sacrifice.

Valmiki tells that the brothers kept watch for six days and nights without rest. On the sixth day the altar fire blazed up, and the demons Maricha and Subahu, the sons of Tataka, came through the sky with their followers to pour impure things upon the altar. Rama loosed at Maricha a weapon that did not kill him but hurled him a hundred yojanas away into the sea, senseless but alive; with a weapon of fire he killed Subahu, and with a weapon of wind he scattered the rest. The sacrifice was completed, and the sages praised the prince (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 30). Maricha's survival matters to the story, for he returns years later as the golden deer.

Kamban gives the moment of fear a striking image. When the deceitful demons appeared, the great ascetics trembled like the gods who, when the poison rose to destroy them, ran to the god with the crescent moon in his red matted hair; and they cried, "O one of the colour of collyrium, we take refuge in you" (Kamba Ramayanam, Velvi Padalam 39). Then the divine arrow of the consort of Sri threw one of the two warriors born of the fearsome Tataka into the sea and sent the other to the city of Death (41).

Kulasekhara Azhwar remembers the episode in the same verse in which he sings of Tataka: when the sacrifice was protected, Subahu was killed and Maricha fled (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.2).

Arunagiri brings the sacrifice into one song, joined to the death of Tataka. In song 366, at Thiruvanaikka, he speaks of the sages in their forest hermitage who perform the Gayatri japa and worship, and of the Lord who, to protect their sacrifice hall, killed Tataka when she came against it; Murugan is the nephew of that ocean of grace. In the Thiruppugazh the guarding of the sacrifice stands for the Lord's protection of those who worship.
