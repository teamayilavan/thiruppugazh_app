When Ravana had fallen, Vibhishana hesitated to perform his brother's funeral rites, since Ravana had lived in cruelty and unrighteousness. Valmiki tells that Rama urged him to do so, saying that enmity ends with death and that Ravana was now as much his own as Vibhishana's. Vibhishana then performed the rites for Ravana with full royal honour.

Afterwards Rama asked Lakshmana to consecrate Vibhishana as king of Lanka. Rama himself did not enter the city, for the years of his exile were not yet complete. Water was brought from the sea in golden vessels, and Lakshmana consecrated Vibhishana in the midst of the demons of Lanka as their king (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 112.16). Rama had already promised him the kingdom when he first took refuge, and the coronation fulfilled that word.

Kamban tells of two crownings. At the moment of the refuge on the shore, Rama, looking with grace on Vibhishana, who had bowed before him saying that he was saved, orders that the faithful companion be given the kingdom (Kamba Ramayanam, Vibhishanan Adaikkala Padalam 141–142). After the war comes the formal rite. When the gods had performed the sacred bathing according to the true rule of the Vedas, at the command of the Lord, the young lion Lakshmana with his own hand placed the crown on Vibhishana's head (Vibhishanan Mudi Soottu Padalam 6). The king of victorious Lanka then shone on his throne like a dark hill crowned with the sun, set upon a seat of glowing gems, while his people rose up cheering (7).

The Azhwars remember the crowning among Rama's last acts in Lanka. Periyazhwar recalls that after killing Ravana Rama crowned Vibhishana and then returned to Ayodhya with Sita to be crowned himself (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 4.1.1), and includes the crowning in his retelling of the whole story (4.9.1).

Arunagiri returns to it in five songs. In song 161, at Palani, Vibhishana stands to wear the golden crown while Ravana with his army and kin goes into the fire, and Murugan is called the nephew of Raghupati Ramachandra. In song 840, at Vedaranyam, the Lord who blocked the sea and crushed the demons gives Lanka to Vibhishana. Song 423, at Thiruvannamalai, speaks of the kingdom given to Vibhishana together with release, and song 841 of the Lord who made one king of Lanka. Song 128, at Palani, of the long crown given to the younger brother, may refer to Vibhishana or to Sugriva. For Arunagiri the crowning is the Lord's answer to surrender: the brother who took refuge is made king.
