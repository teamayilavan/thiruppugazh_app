Kanchipuram's great Shiva temple, the Ekambam, takes its name from a single mango tree, and its story is the story of the Goddess's penance there. The Kanchi Puranam of Sivagnana Munivar tells it in brief in its opening verses of invocation. The Goddess, the daughter of the mountain, was worshipping Shiva at Kanchi; the Lord, unable to bear waiting until her worship was complete, sent the river Kampa to her like a messenger, so that she clasped him tightly; and so the Lord of the one mango tree, who had taken her in a love that could not be restrained, bore the marks of her embrace (Kanchi Puranam, invocation v. 4). A later verse of the same invocation praises the Goddess of the Kamakkottam, Kamakshi's shrine, as the one who gave him the marks of her breasts (v. 6). The Kanda Puranam also knows the Goddess's shrine at Kanchi (Yuddha Kandam 2386).

The fuller story told at the temple adds details that are not found in the parts of the Kanchi Puranam that could be read. Parvati, it says, came to Kanchi to do penance, made a lingam of sand under the mango tree on the bank of the river Vegavati, and worshipped it. When the river flooded, she embraced the lingam to save it from being washed away, and her bangles left their marks on it. Shiva then appeared and wedded her. These details, the Vegavati and the marks of the bangles, are temple tradition, and the film says so.

The Thiruppugazh knows the story chiefly through its most intimate detail. Song 338, sung at Kanchipuram, describes Shiva as bearing on his chest a scar made by the Goddess's breast, and says that Uma, seeking him through a penance at Kanchi, at last became one with him. Song 352, also sung there, says that she separated herself from her oneness with Shiva, rode the great bull to Kachchi (Kanchi), and did penance to rid herself of delusion, and that Shiva looked on her penance with melting compassion. Song 347 says that Mother Kamakshi performed many wonderful deeds at Kanchi, and song 438 names Kamakshi among those who rejoice in Murugan's presence.

For Arunagiri this is a story of devotion answered. The Goddess's embrace is an act of worship, holding fast to the Lord when everything threatens to carry him away, and the mark it leaves is a sign of their union. The songs to cite are 338, 347 and 352 (Kanchipuram) and 438 (Thiruvannamalai).
