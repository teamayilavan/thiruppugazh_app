The story of the spider and the elephant is the story of the sacred grove of Thiruvanaikka, near Tiruchirappalli on the Kaveri, and of the Chola king Kochchengat, one of the sixty-three Nayanmars, as the Periya Puranam tells it.

In the Chola country, near the Chandra tirtha on the Kaveri, there was a cool forest (Kochchengat Chola Nayanar Puranam 1). There, under a white jambu tree, the Lord appeared, and a white elephant of great austerity worshipped him every day, bathing him with water carried in its trunk and adorning him with fragrant flowers (2). From this the place took its name, Thiruvanaikka, the sacred grove of the elephant. A spider endowed with wisdom also lived there, and so that dry leaves should not fall on the Lord's head, it spun from the thread of its own mouth a canopy over him (3).

The elephant, going to worship, judged the web improper and destroyed it. The spider spun it again, and the next day the elephant destroyed it again (4). Grieving that the thread it had spun to keep the leaves from the Lord was being torn away, the spider rose in anger, entered the elephant's trunk and bit it; the elephant struck its trunk on the ground and fell (5). The spider too lost its life. The Lord granted a boon to the elephant, and in his grace ordained that the spider should be born in the Chola line and protect the world (6).

The Chola king Subadeva and his queen Kamalavati worshipped at Chidambaram, and, being childless, the queen prayed for a son (7, 8). The spider was conceived in her womb (8). When the time of birth came, astrologers said that if the child were born one nazhigai later he would rule the three worlds, and the queen asked to be held up by her feet so that the birth would be delayed. When the hour came she bore the child, called him "my red-eyed one", Kochchengannan, and died (9 to 11). Remembering his former birth by the Lord's grace, Kochchengat built many temples of Shiva, and first of all, at Thiruvanaikka, a temple together with the white jambu tree (12 to 14). Temple tradition adds that the elephant and the spider had been attendants of Shiva, born on earth under a curse; that is not in the Periya Puranam.

The Thiruppugazh recalls the story in song 358, sung at Thiruvanaikka. The poet praises the place where a spider, in deep contemplation of Shiva, living in the forest on the bank of the Kaveri, spun a web that served as a canopy over the Lord, and he sings of Murugan dwelling there.
