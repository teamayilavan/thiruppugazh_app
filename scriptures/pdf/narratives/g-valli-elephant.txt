The elephant in the millet field is a small episode in the long story of Murugan's wooing of Valli, but it is the moment that turns the story, and it is the moment in which Ganesha takes part. The Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar tells it in the Daksha Kandam, in the padalam of Valli's marriage.

By this point Murugan has come to Valli's mountain in several forms. Last of all he has come as an aged ascetic of the Saiva path (Daksha Kandam 1894), has greeted her father Nambi and given him holy ash (1895), and has asked Valli for food and then for water, which she gave him (1900 to 1904). But when the old man declared his love, she was outraged and told him to be gone (1905 to 1909).

Then Murugan thought of his elder brother, the elephant-faced Vinayaka, who at once came roaring as a wild elephant (1910 to 1911). Frightened, Valli ran back to the old man and clung to him, begging him to save her from the elephant (1912). Murugan thanked his brother, and Vinayaka withdrew (1914 to 1915). Then Murugan showed Valli his true form, with six faces and twelve arms, with the spear and the peacock (1916). The text says only that Vinayaka came as an elephant; it does not say that the elephant had one tusk.

The songs of the Thiruppugazh remember the scene in four places, each in its own way. Song 24, sung at Thiruchendur, says that Valli was frightened when the tusked elephant, Lord Vinayaka, appeared before her. Song 146, sung at Palani, says that Ganapati, the supreme god, took the form of a raging elephant and came near Valli, who in her fear rushed to embrace Murugan for refuge. Song 446, sung at Thirukkalathi, gives Murugan words of comfort: do not be afraid of this elephant, do not cry, come to me. Song 715, sung at Uthiramerur, adds the single tusk that the Puranam does not mention: at the right moment Vinayaka came to his brother's aid with love, running in the form of an elephant with one tusk, and Valli, seeing it, ran to Murugan.

For Arunagiri the episode shows the affection between the two brothers as much as the wooing itself. Vinayaka comes the moment he is thought of, does what is needed and goes, and the poet praises his help as an act of love (song 715). Through it, the story of Valli's fear becomes a story of her coming to Murugan.
