As the war at Lanka neared its end, Ravana sent out the last great force of his rakshasas against Rama. Valmiki describes the host coming out in chariots, on elephants and horses and on foot, and Rama meeting it alone (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha 93.7–34). The rakshasas could not see Rama himself; they saw only his golden bow, drawn so fast that it seemed to turn like a circle of fire, and the arrows that came from it. Confused, some of them saw Rama everywhere at once, while others saw him as one, and in a short part of the day the whole army was destroyed. The gods and sages, Valmiki says, praised the deed.

Kamban tells the destruction of Ravana's forces in the Padai Thalaivar Vadhai Padalam, the section on the killing of the army's commanders. The monkeys and the rakshasas meet in open battle, both armies fighting while the gods look on in fear (Kamba Ramayanam, Padai Thalaivar Vadhai Padalam 36). Then, in a single verse, he gives the scale of it: of the rakshasa army, counted in ten "floods", Rama himself cut down six, and Lakshmana the remaining four (Padai Thalaivar Vadhai Padalam 52). The Tamil "flood", vellam, is a vast number, used for armies past counting.

The Alvars, too, remember Rama as the one who destroyed Ravana together with his demon army. Kulasekhara Alvar includes it in his short life of Rama (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.7).

For a film, the battle is best shown as light and smoke rather than as bodies. The point of the episode in the tradition is not slaughter but the strength of one righteous warrior against a host gathered for a wrong cause, and the loneliness of Ravana once that host is gone.

This is one of the episodes Arunagiri returns to most often: some nineteen songs recall it, usually in a single line describing Vishnu, whose nephew Murugan is. In song 200 (Palani) he speaks of the Lord who cut off Ravana's heads in Lanka and won so that even his army perished. In song 750 (Vriddhachalam) Rama, the cloud-coloured Raghava, destroys Ravana together with his whole clan with a single arrow, and in song 524 (Thiruvenkatam) he bends his bow so that the ten crowns of the king of Lanka are scattered. Other songs, such as 9 (Thiruparankundram) and 27 (Thiruchendur), praise Hari as the one who defeated the hosts of the asuras.
