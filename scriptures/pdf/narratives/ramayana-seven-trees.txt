In the hills of Kishkindha, Rama made an alliance with Sugriva, the monkey prince who had been driven from his kingdom by his elder brother Vali. Rama promised to kill Vali, but Sugriva, who had seen his brother's tremendous strength, could not yet believe that anyone could overcome him. He told Rama of Vali's might, and Rama resolved to give him proof.

Valmiki tells that Rama took his bow and loosed a single arrow at a row of seven great sala trees. The arrow pierced all seven, passed through the hill beyond, and entered the earth beneath, and then in a moment returned to Rama's own quiver (Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 12.3–5). Sugriva, seeing it, was overjoyed, bowed before Rama and was at last assured that Vali could be defeated.

Kamban, in the chapter named after the marāmaram, the great trees, slows the moment down. Before loosing the arrow Rama sets the string of his bow ringing, and the sound alone brings a fear that has no equal (Kamba Ramayanam, Maramarap Padalam 12). Then the master of the bow releases the fierce arrow (15), and Kamban adds that after passing through the seven it goes on still and does not stop (16). The image of the single arrow that pierces seven trees and the depths beneath became one of the best-remembered signs of Rama's power.

The Tamil devotional poets used the story as a sign that the Lord removes his devotee's doubts. In Vaishnava commentary on Kulasekhara Azhwar's verse the arrow through seven huge trees is said to have been loosed to give Sugriva confidence against Vali (Perumal Thirumozhi 2.2), and the commentary on a verse of Periyazhwar likewise explains it as removing Sugriva's doubt (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 4.1.3). In these places it is the commentary, not the verse, that names the episode.

Arunagiri brings the seven trees into one song, and joins them in a single breath with the deaths of Vali and Ravana. In song 731, at Thiruvamathur, he calls Murugan the nephew of the dark-hued one who loosed one arrow so that, together with the angry Vali, the seven trees fell, and the night-walker's ten crowned heads and twenty arms perished. For Arunagiri the single arrow is the mark of the Lord whose power needs only one shot.
