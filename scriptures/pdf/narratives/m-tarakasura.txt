Tarakan, whom the Sanskrit tradition calls Taraka, is the younger brother of the asura king Surapadman. In the first kandam of the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, his death is the first great battle of Murugan's war against the asuras, and is told in the padalam named for him, the Tarakan Varaip Padalam (verses 1329 to 1531).

The Puranam first shows him in his fortress. Murugan's host marches on and comes to the Krouncha mountain, which afflicts all who take refuge in its neighbourhood (1329). The mountain was once an asura, turned by a sage's curse (1331); and at one side of it, in a city of illusion, lives Tarakan, the brother of Sura. He is a fierce warrior who wears the discus of Vishnu as an ornament, and he is the master of the place (1332). He waits there in the fort with the host of asuras (1336).

When Murugan's army approaches, Tarakan hides in a cave of the mountain (1416). Veerabahu and the warriors who go ahead are bewildered by the illusions that the mountain and its master throw around them; Tarakan, seeing them stunned, takes them for dead and comes out with joy, climbing the mountain (1425, 1433).

Then Murugan sends his spear. Its light destroys all the illusions of the worlds, and it flies at Tarakan and the mountain together (1509). The spear pierces Tarakan's chest and the mountain with a roar as though the earth were splitting and Meru breaking; Tarakan leaps up, roaring, and falls to the ground (1515 to 1516). The poet compares his fall to that of Meru when the mountains lost their wings (1519).

The songs of the Thiruppugazh name Tarakan in several lines as one of the asuras Murugan destroyed, often together with the Krouncha mountain, and the story that stands behind them is this victory.
