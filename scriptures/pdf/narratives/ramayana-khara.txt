In the Dandaka forest, Ravana's sister Surpanakha tried to win Rama and then to harm Sita, and was driven off and disfigured by Lakshmana. She went to her brother Khara, who held the region of Janasthana with a great force of rakshasas. Khara first sent a small band against the princes, and when it was destroyed he marched out himself with fourteen thousand rakshasas and his generals Dushana and Trishira. Rama sent Lakshmana with Sita to the safety of a cave in the hills and met the whole host alone, while the gods and sages watched.

Valmiki ends the battle with Rama's arrow striking Khara in the chest and bringing him down, so that the fourteen thousand rakshasas of Janasthana were destroyed by Rama alone (Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya 30.26–31). Sita then embraced him in joy.

Kamban tells the battle at length in the Karan Vadhai Padalam, and gives each general his fall in turn. Trishira drives his chariot high through the sky with a roar (Kamba Ramayanam, Karan Vadhai Padalam 127), and Rama pours arrows down on him as a cloud pours down rain (131). Trishira loses two of his heads and fights on before he falls (136). Dushana loses his head to Rama's arrow (158, 161). Khara, last of all, loses first his strong right arm (182) and then his head (185). The Kamba version therefore differs in its details from Valmiki's, where Khara is struck in the chest, but both make the same point: one warrior, calm and alone, overcame a great host.

The Alvars include the battle in their summaries of Rama's forest life. Kulasekhara Alvar sings of the killing of Viradha, the cutting of Surpanakha's nose and the destruction of Khara and Dushana, followed by the shooting of Maricha (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.5), and the commentary on Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 4.1.3 speaks of Rama destroying the demon army at Janasthana.

Arunagiri recalls the episode in two songs. In song 961 (Madurai) he praises Mukunda, who with his bow destroyed the fierce Khara together with Trishira, the steadfast Kumbhakarna and the ten-headed one who had conquered the gods, and calls Murugan the nephew who delights that Lord's heart. In song 1295 (a general song) Murugan is the nephew of the Lord with the conch in his hand, who sent forth his arrows so that the whole clan of mighty and victorious rakshasas perished.
