Hanuman's journey to Lanka is the heart of the Sundara Kandam. Leaping across the ocean, he searched the city by night until he found Sita in the Ashoka grove, gave her Rama's ring as a token, and received from her a jewel to carry back to Rama. Before leaving he chose to test the strength of Lanka: he laid waste the grove and fought the rakshasas who came against him, until Ravana's son Indrajit bound him with a divine weapon and he allowed himself to be brought before Ravana.

Ravana's first thought was to kill the messenger, but he was reminded that an envoy may not be put to death, and he ordered instead that the monkey's tail be set on fire (Valmiki Ramayana, Sundara 53.3–5). The rakshasas wrapped the tail in cloth soaked in oil, lit it and paraded Hanuman through the streets. Sita, hearing of it, prayed to the god of fire to be cool to him, and the fire did not harm him. Hanuman then freed himself, grew to his full size and leapt from rooftop to rooftop, setting the city ablaze (Sundara 54.6–47). When the city was burning he quenched his tail in the sea, and, fearing for a moment that Sita too might have been harmed, he was relieved to learn that she was safe.

Kamban describes the fire with his usual sweep. It seizes the flags, burns the canopies, embraces the tall pillars and runs round the long walls until every great house is aflame (Kamba Ramayanam, Ilangai Eriyoottu Padalam 1). The fires lodged in the houses, he says, seemed to take back their true form, like those who cast off illusion and return to their own nature (6). The blaze spreads like the fire at the end of an age that drinks up the waters and burns the mountains, until it reaches Ravana's own palace (32). Ravana and the women of his household escape in the flying chariot Pushpaka (37). Kulasekhara Alvar includes the burning of Lanka in his short life of Rama (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.6).

Arunagiri recalls the burning of Lanka in seven songs, and almost always he credits it to Rama acting through Hanuman. In song 1265 (a general song) Murugan is the nephew of the protector who, through the monkey, set the city of Lanka on fire. In song 835 (Enkan) Lanka is burnt and ruined through "that monkey", and in song 700 (Perungudi) the Lord comes with the monkey who set the fire, so that the great sea trembled. Song 373 (Thiruvannamalai) recalls the great city sinking into flames with armoured Hanuman in the battle that followed.
