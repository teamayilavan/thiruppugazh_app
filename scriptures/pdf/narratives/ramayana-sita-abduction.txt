During the last years of their exile, Rama, Sita and Lakshmana lived in a leaf hut at Panchavati beside the Godavari. There Surpanakha, sister of Ravana, saw Rama and asked him to take her as his wife. Rejected, she rushed at Sita, and Lakshmana, at Rama's word, cut off her ears and nose (Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda 18.21). Kamban has Rama learn of the deed and ask the wounded woman who she is (Kamba Ramayanam, Surpanakhai Padalam 116). Surpanakha went first to her brother Khara, whose army Rama destroyed, and then to Ravana in Lanka.

Ravana resolved to carry Sita off. He compelled the demon Maricha to take the form of a golden deer; Rama went after it, and when Maricha, struck by Rama's arrow, cried out in Rama's voice, Sita sent Lakshmana after him. Ravana then came to the hut in the guise of a mendicant. Sita received him as a guest and told him who she was, and when he revealed himself and asked her to become his queen she refused him with contempt. Valmiki tells that Ravana took hold of her and carried her off through the sky (Aranya 49.23). The aged vulture Jatayu, a friend of Dasaratha, tried to stop him and was struck down.

Kamban changes the seizure in a way that has shaped Tamil devotion. In his account Ravana does not touch Sita at all: he digs up the ground around her hut to the depth of a yojana and rises into the sky carrying it, with Sita upon it (Ravana Soozhchi Padalam 72–73). The change preserves Sita's honour even in the moment of her greatest helplessness.

The Azhwars rarely describe the abduction itself, preferring to sing of Rama's grief and of the war that followed; in the Ramayana as they knew it, this is the event that sets the whole of Rama's southern campaign in motion.

Arunagiri refers to it in song 272, at Thiruthani, in a passage that compresses the whole story. He speaks of the brute who seized her and carried her on a chariot through the clouds, and kept her in his house in a city of tall painted towers and great armies; and of the day when the Lord, chief among bowmen, ended that demon's course and tore his line up by the roots. Murugan is the nephew of that Lord. For Arunagiri the abduction exists to be answered, and the line moves at once from Sita's captivity to Ravana's fall.
