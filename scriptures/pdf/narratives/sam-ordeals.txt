After the king's fever was cured, the contest between Sambandar and the Jain teachers at Madurai was carried on in two public tests, both told in the Periya Puranam and retold in the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam (padalam 63).

The first was a test by fire. Before the king and his court a fire was lit, and each side was to commit a palm leaf of its scripture to it (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 773–783). Sambandar opened the bundle of his hymns, took out a leaf, and with a hymn proclaiming that Shiva alone is the true reality, put it into the flames (vv. 784–785). Because the hymn written on it took as its subject the Lord who shares his body with the daughter of the mountain, Sekkizhar says, the leaf stayed green in the fire (v. 786). The Jains, anxious, put in their own leaf, and it burned like cotton (vv. 787–788). When the time was over Sambandar's leaf was taken out, fresher and greener than before, and all who saw it wondered (v. 789).

The Jains then proposed a test by water: each side's leaf would be put into the flooded Vaigai (vv. 799–807). Sambandar wrote the hymn beginning "Vazhga Andhanar" (Tevaram 3.54.1), and the Puranam spends several verses on its meaning before he put it into the river with his own hand (vv. 840–845). The Jains' leaf was carried away, while his went upstream, cutting through the water (v. 846). The hymn on the leaf included a blessing on the king, and as it was sung, the Puranam says, the Pandya's hunched back became straight (v. 847). The gods rained flowers, the king stood astonished, and the Jains were afraid (v. 848). The minister Kulachchirai rode after the leaf on a swift horse, and Sambandar sang the hymn of Thiruvedagam (Tevaram 3.32.1); the leaf stopped beside the temple there, and the minister waded in and took it up (vv. 849–850). What followed the tests is told in the next story.

Arunagiri refers to these tests in about ten songs. Song 920 (Poovalur) speaks of the leaf that went against the flood in the contest; song 542 (Thirukkazhukundram) of the leaf that proclaimed Shiva as the pure one; song 104 (Palani) of the Tevaram written by Murugan as Sambandar, which he places above even the Thirukkural; and song 666 (Velur) of the straightening of the Pandya's back. In many songs the leaf in the fire, the leaf in the river and the king's cure are gathered into a single line of praise.
