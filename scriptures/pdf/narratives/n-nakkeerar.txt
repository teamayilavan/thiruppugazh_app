Nakkeerar was one of the great poets of the old Madurai assembly, and the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam remembers him for his courage. When the Lord gave the poor brahmin Dharumi a poem to win the Pandya king's prize, Nakkeerar found fault with it and would not yield even when the Lord appeared before him; for his pride he fell into the golden lotus tank, was lifted out by the Lord, and was then taught grammar and the true meaning of poetry (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalams 52, 53 and 54).

The story that the Thiruppugazh tells of him is a different one, and it does not appear in those padalams. In the tradition, Nakkeerar was at his prayers by a pool at Thiruparankundram when his attention was drawn away, and a spirit seized him and shut him in a cave with many others who had also broken their worship. There he sang the Tirumurugatruppadai, and Murugan came and set them all free. The familiar detail of the leaf that fell half into the water and half on the bank, and became half a fish and half a bird, is part of this popular telling and is not given here from any text.

The poem itself is real and ancient. The Tirumurugatruppadai stands first among the Ten Idylls of Sangam poetry and is also included in the eleventh book of the Shaiva canon (Tirumurai 11.17). It is a "guide" poem: it tells a seeker where Murugan may be found, naming Thiruparankundram, Tiruchendur, the hills where he dances and Pazhamudircholai among his places. That it was composed in the cave is the tradition, not something the poem says of itself.

Arunagiri returns to this story often, in at least nine songs. In song 104 (Palani) many poets were caught by a treacherous spirit with the face of a horse, and Murugan came to the aid of Nakkeerar, one of them. In song 1192 the poet sits in a round cave at Thiruparankundram, and Murugan waits in hiding to hear his rich Tamil. In song 92 (Tiruchendur) Murugan opens the door of the cave with his own hands, and in song 726 (Siruvai) the poet keeps his daily rites even in the cave. Songs 431, 681, 1008 and 1279 praise the hymn and the grace it won. For Arunagiri, Nakkeerar is the poet who sang his way out of darkness, and he asks, as in song 681, for the same grace.
