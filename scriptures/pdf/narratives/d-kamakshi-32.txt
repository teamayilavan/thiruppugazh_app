Among the many names that Arunagiri gives the Goddess, one belongs to Kanchipuram. In five songs of the Thiruppugazh she is the one who, as Kamakshi of Kanchi, carried out the thirty-two dharmas, the works of right action and charity. The phrase is used as a title, and the songs assume that the listener knows what it means.

What the songs themselves say is this. Song 291, sung at Thiruthani, calls her the supreme consort of Shiva who performed the thirty-two religious duties at Kanchi, and in the same lines says that she gave the Tamil language its three branches of letters, music and drama. Song 322, sung at Kanchipuram, calls her the creeper-like daughter of Himavan, in whose womb the universe was conceived, and the young woman who carried out the thirty-two duties without any flaw. Song 438, sung at Thiruvannamalai, names Kamakshi beside Vishnu and Brahma as one who is like the wish-yielding karpaga tree and who did many charitable acts at Kanchi. Song 540, sung at Vallimalai, calls her "aram valar sundari", the beautiful one in whom dharma grows. Song 894 again calls her the Goddess who performed the thirty-two dharmas.

The list of the thirty-two is not given in any of these songs, and no written source for it has been found. The Kanchi Puranam of Sivagnana Munivar honours the Goddess of the Kamakottam, Kamakshi's shrine at Kanchi, in its invocation (Kanchi Puranam, invocation v. 5), but the word for thirty-two does not appear in the parts of that Puranam that could be read, and the thirty-two charities are not described there. The tradition of the thirty-two dharmas therefore rests on the songs and on the living tradition of Kanchi, through which it reached Arunagiri's commentators.

For that reason the film made from these songs does not name the thirty-two. It shows the Goddess giving: food, water, shelter, light and care, and the people of Kanchi giving in her name. This is faithful to the spirit of the songs, in which Kamakshi's charity is not a catalogue but a quality of the Mother of the world, whose compassion has no limit.

Arunagiri always mentions this charity on the way to Murugan, who is her son. The songs to cite are 291 (Thiruthani), 322 (Kanchipuram), 438 (Thiruvannamalai), 540 (Vallimalai) and 894 (Kuratti).
