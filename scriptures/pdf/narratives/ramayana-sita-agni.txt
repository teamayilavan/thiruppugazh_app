After Sita was brought to Rama at the end of the war, Valmiki's account takes a turn that readers have found painful for as long as the poem has been read. Before the assembled army Rama declared that he had fought to remove the insult to himself and his line, and spoke harshly to Sita of her long stay in Ravana's house. Sita replied with dignity that what had been beyond her power was her body, seized against her will, while her heart, which was in her power, had always been his. She then asked Lakshmana to build a fire and entered it. Agni, the god of fire, rose from the flames carrying her, returned her to Rama unharmed and declared her pure (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 118.5). Valmiki has Rama then say that he had never doubted her, but that her purity had to be shown before the world.

Kamban tells the ordeal with intense sympathy for Sita and for Lakshmana. Lakshmana, with tears pouring from his eyes and sobbing as if his own life had been drawn out of him, prepared the fire according to rule, and the lotus-born lady walked up to it (Kamba Ramayanam, Pirattai Thiruvadi Thozhuta Padalam 66). Sita said, "If I have been stained in mind or in word, burn me in your anger, O lord of fire," and bowed to her husband (71). Then Agni rose, his own body burning, carrying in his hands the lady who had sunk into the flames, his hands joined in worship, crying out to the one who is the crown of the wisdom of the Vedas (73).

This is an episode the tradition has never found easy, and this book does not try to explain it away or to defend it. It is told here as the texts tell it, and what endures in both tellings is Sita's steadiness: she does not plead, and the fire itself bears witness for her.

The link to the Thiruppugazh is slight. Song 161, at Palani, has been associated with this episode because it speaks of entering the fire. Read closely, however, the line says that while Vibhishana stood to wear the golden crown, Ravana with his soldiers and his kin went into the fire, and the gods returned to their homes; Murugan is the nephew of Raghupati Ramachandra who brought this about. The fire there is the destruction of Ravana's house, not Sita's ordeal. Arunagiri, who names Sita's release in several songs, does not otherwise speak of this scene.
