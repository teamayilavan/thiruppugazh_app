The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam of Paranjothi, the book of Shiva's sacred sports at Madurai, gives two episodes about the Tamil Sangam, the ancient assembly of poets. The Thiruppugazh adds to the second of them a claim of its own: that the child who judged the poets was Murugan.

In the first episode the Sangam is founded. Shiva himself, as Somasundarar, the Lord of the Madurai temple, comes as a poet and gives them a plank to sit on. It is a cubit square and seats one person, but it extends to make room for poets, and it gives a place only to those who are worthy (padalam 51). In this telling there are forty-eight poets, and Somasundarar is counted as the forty-ninth.

In the second episode the poets quarrel over whose poems are best, and, having no one fit to judge them, appeal to the Lord. Shiva tells them that in the city there lives a merchant named Dhanapati, whose wife Gunasalini bore him a son after long penance. The boy is called Rudrasarman; he is not yet five years old, and he has been mute from birth. They are to take him to the Sangam, and he will judge their verses. The poets wonder, but obey. At empty verses the boy shows no sign. Where there is beauty of words, it shows in his eyes; where there is depth of meaning, the hair on his body stands up; and where beauty, meaning and music come together, his face blossoms and his eyes fill with tears of joy. In this way Kapilar, Paranar and Nakkeerar are shown to be the greatest, and the other poets are honoured each according to his worth (padalam 55).

The Puranam names the boy as Rudrasarman, the merchant's son. It is the songs that say he was Murugan. Song 126, sung at Palani, says that at Shiva's bidding Murugan was born in the merchant community and grew up under the name of Rudrasarman, and taught the forty-nine poets seated on the Sangam plank. Song 568, sung at Viralimalai, ties the story to the tradition that the forty-nine poets of the Madurai Sangam disputed the meaning of a treatise on love composed by Shiva, the Iraiyanar Akapporul, and that Murugan came as the mute boy to settle its true meaning. Song 987 says that he came as Rudrasarman to make known the real meaning of a poem. Song 992 praises him as the one who sat on the famous plank of the Sangam, and song 451, sung at Chidambaram, as the Sangam poet who made Tamil clear. For Arunagiri the story makes Murugan the true judge of Tamil poetry.
