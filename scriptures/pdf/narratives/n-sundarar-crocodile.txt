Avinashi, in the Kongu country, is the Thirupukkoliyur of the Tevaram, and its best-known story belongs to the saint Sundarar. Tradition tells that years before his visit, a little boy of the town went to bathe in the tank and was taken by a crocodile. When Sundarar came to Avinashi he found the boy's parents grieving, and on the bank of the tank he sang a hymn to the Lord of the temple, asking that the child be given back. The crocodile came to the bank and gave the boy back alive.

The story is not told in the Periya Puranam, at least in the sections read for this book, where the word for crocodile occurs only in unrelated verses. Its earliest witness is Sundarar's own hymn at Thirupukkoliyur Avinashi (Tevaram 7.92). In its fourth verse the saint asks the Lord to command Kala, Death himself, to make the crocodile on the bank give back the child (Tevaram 7.92.4). The hymn does not tell the story; it speaks as if everyone knew it, and asks for the miracle in the course of the praise. The fuller details, the two boys at the water, the parents' grief and the time that passed, belong to the later tradition and are given here as tradition.

What stands out in the hymn is how the saint prays. He does not command the crocodile. He asks the Lord to command Death, since it is Death who holds the child. The saint speaks to the Lord as a friend who may ask anything of him, and asks for a life to be given back.

Arunagiri tells the story in song 1320, sung at Pazhamudircholai. The song speaks of the Lord as the old dispenser of justice, who listened with delight to the great Tamil hymn of Sundarar, sung among devotees in ecstasy, when the saint pleaded with the crocodile to come out quickly and give back alive, before his parents, the boy it had swallowed. The commentary on the song says the child had been taken a year before. For Arunagiri the point is that a Tamil hymn, sung in faith, was heard by the Lord; and Murugan is praised as the son of that Lord.
