The tortoise is the quiet part of the churning of the Ocean of Milk. The Bhagavata Purana first names it in a single line, among the deeds of the Lord in that age: by churning the milk ocean Ajita produced the nectar for the gods, and in the form of a tortoise he carried the great mountain Mandara on his back (Bhagavata Purana 8.5.10).

The full account comes in the telling of the churning itself. The gods and demons, having made a truce, wound the serpent Vasuki around Mandara and began to churn. But the mountain had no support in the sea, and in spite of the strong arms that held it, it sank into the water (8.7.6). The gods and demons were disheartened, and their faces seemed to shrivel (8.7.7). Seeing this, the Lord, whose resolve never fails, took the wonderful form of a tortoise, entered the water and lifted the mountain (8.7.8). Encouraged, they began again; the mountain rested on the back of the tortoise, which spread out like a great island (8.7.9), and the Bhagavata adds that the Lord felt the rolling of the mountain as a pleasant scratching of his back (8.7.10). He was at the same time above the mountain, holding it with one of a thousand hands, and within the gods, the demons, the serpent and the mountain, giving them strength (8.7.11–13).

The Valmiki Ramayana gives the same act briefly: Vishnu, as a tortoise, held Mandara at the bottom of the ocean (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kandam 45.29). The Vishnu Purana tells the churning, with the tortoise, in its ninth chapter of the first book (Vishnu Purana 1.9). Among the Alvars, Periyazhwar has Yashoda address the child Krishna as the Lord who took the forms of the boar, the tortoise and the fish (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.3.7).

Arunagiri mentions the tortoise only in passing. Song 1272, one of the general songs, lists the forms the Lord took, the tortoise and the fish, the dwarf and the lion, and praises Murugan as the nephew of that Lord who rests on the serpent bed. Song 723 (Thiruvakkarai) does not name the tortoise but describes the work it made possible: the Lord who set the great mountain in the middle of the sea, wound the serpent around it, churned, and raised the nectar and gave it away.
