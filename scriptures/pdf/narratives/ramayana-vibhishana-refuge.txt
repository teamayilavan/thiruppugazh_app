As Rama's army gathered on the shore across from Lanka, Ravana's younger brother Vibhishana counselled him in assembly to return Sita and make peace. Ravana answered with scorn and called him a traitor. Vibhishana left Lanka, and with four companions he came through the sky to the place where Rama's army was encamped (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 17.17–26). Standing in the air, he announced himself as Ravana's brother and asked for refuge (Yuddha 19.4).

Sugriva was deeply suspicious and urged Rama to have him seized or killed as a spy. Rama asked the opinion of his counsellors; most were wary, but Hanuman spoke for Vibhishana, judging from his bearing and his words that he had come in good faith. Rama then gave the decision for which the episode is best remembered: he would not turn away anyone who came to him as a friend, and to anyone who sought refuge with him, saying "I am yours," he would grant safety from all beings; this, he said, was his vow. He added that he would grant it even if Ravana himself came. Vibhishana was brought down and received.

Kamban makes the farewell in Lanka especially moving. Vibhishana says to Ravana, "In every way I could, I have told you what is right and fitting, and you will not understand; father, forgive my fault," and the noble one leaves the city (Kamba Ramayanam, Vibhishanan Adaikkala Padalam 12). His four counsellors, Anala, Anila, Hara and Sampati, come with him (13). In Kamban's account Rama's assurance of protection is carried to Vibhishana through Sugriva, who embraces him (121), and when they meet Rama tells him: "We were five with Guha before; then with the son of the one who circles the mountain, we became six; and now with you, who came to us in love, we are seven" (143).

Vaishnava tradition treats this as the great example of the Lord's promise to those who surrender. Periyazhwar sings of Vibhishana's surrender and the kingdom given to him (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.9.10).

Arunagiri names the refuge in one song. In song 623, at Kunrakkudi, he speaks of the noble younger brother of Kumbhakarna who came face to face with Rama and bowed, and of how, gladly, he told the inner secrets of the demons of Lanka; Murugan is the nephew of the cloud-hued Lord who won. Here the refuge is the turning point of the war: the one who surrenders becomes the ally through whom victory comes.
