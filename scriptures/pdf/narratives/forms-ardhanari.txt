Ardhanarishvara, the Lord who is half woman, is one of the best known forms of Shiva. The right half of the figure is Shiva, with matted hair, ash and serpent; the left half is the Goddess, with her jewels and silk. The form says, in a single image, that Shiva and his power are not two.

The Shiva Purana counts this form among the incarnations of Shiva. When the creatures made by Brahma did not increase, Brahma turned to Shiva, and Shiva appeared to him in the half-female form, so that the work of creation could go on (Shiva Purana, Satarudra-samhita, chapter 3).

The Tamil hymns take the form as a matter of everyday praise. Appar, at Ekambam in Kanchi, sings of the Lord whose body is half male and half female (Tevaram 4.7.9); at Pazhanam, of the Lord who took the form of half a woman (4.36.7); at Avaduthurai, of the Lord with a woman as one half, who is the Light (5.29.3). He sings at Aiyaru, "you are male and female, and without birth" (6.38.5), and at Tiruppuvanam of the Lord who appears in a form both male and female (6.18.2). In another verse he notices the two ear ornaments of the Lord, one a man's and one a woman's (4.18.2).

At Kanchipuram the form is tied to a temple story. The tradition of the Ekambareshwara temple tells that the Goddess did penance there beneath a mango tree, worshipping a linga of sand, and that she won the left half of the Lord's body. This belongs to temple tradition; the hymns cited above praise the form without telling the story.

In the Thiruppugazh the form appears in about forty songs, often in a single word: Murugan is the son of the Lord who has Uma at his side or at his left. Song 179, at Palani, names the trident-bearing Lord of Kailasa with Uma on his left; song 391, at Thiruvarunai, the destroyer of the cities who shares his body with the Goddess; song 463, at Chidambaram, the Lord with the Goddess in his half. Song 719, at Madhuranthakam, comes closest to the Kanchi story: it praises the Lord of Kanchi who melted for the daughter of the mountain and gave her half of himself. For Arunagiri, Murugan is the child in whom that one father-and-mother is made visible.
