Jayantan, whom Sanskrit calls Jayanta, is the young son of Indra and his queen Sachi. In the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar he is shut up with the other devas in the prison of the asura king Surapadman at Mahendrapuri (Mahendra Kandam 337). The poet gives him a share of the story that few other characters receive: three of the padalams of the Mahendra Kandam are named for him, his lament in the prison, his dream, and the words with which Veerabahu, Murugan's chief captain, comforts him (padalams 9 to 11; the visit to the prison is at verse 450).

The captivity ends only when Surapadman is dead. After the long war, Murugan, seated on his peacock, tells Veerabahu to go to the city of the fallen king and bring Jayantan and all the devas out of prison. Veerabahu goes into the city and to the place of the prison, and Jayantan and the devas come away from it and make their way across the battlefield to Murugan's feet (Yuddha Kandam 2935 to 2943).

With the demon dead and the devas free, the order of heaven is restored. In the Deva Kandam Indra again rules the golden city, and a messenger carries the news that Surapadman has fallen and the devas are out of prison to Indra's queen on Mount Meru (Deva Kandam 36 and 41 to 43). It is this rescue that the Thiruppugazh recalls when it says that Murugan protected Indra's son and gave heaven back to Indra.
