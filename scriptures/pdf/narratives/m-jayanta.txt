Jayantan, whom Sanskrit calls Jayanta, is the young son of Indra and his queen Sachi. In the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar he is shut up with the other devas in the prison of the asura king Surapadman at Mahendrapuri. When Indra has fled, the poet turns to those left behind in the prison: the little prince Jayantan, born of Sachi, sat in that hard prison together with the gods (Mahendra Kandam 336 to 337). Among them he is like a lone dark cloud resting on the sea on the day the nectar rose from it (338).

The poet gives him a share of the story that few other characters receive: three of the padalams of the Mahendra Kandam are named for him, his lament in the prison, his dream, and the words with which Veerabahu, Murugan's chief captain, comforts him (padalams 9 to 11). Veerabahu's visit is told with care. Holding in his heart Murugan, who is the root meaning of Om and dwells always in every life, he recites the mantra and enters the closely guarded prison of the asuras (449). As he comes in to where the mighty Jayantan and the gods are held, the guards, whose bodies are like smoke, lose their strength, caught in the net of the great mantra, and fall into a stupor (450); they stand like figures in a painting, and Jayantan and the gods look up and see the captain of the army of the spear (451).

The captivity ends only when Surapadman is dead. After the long war, Murugan, seated on his peacock, tells Veerabahu to go to the city of the fallen king and bring Jayantan and all the devas out of prison. Veerabahu goes into the city and to the place of the prison, and Jayantan and the devas come away from it and make their way across the battlefield to Murugan's feet (Yuddha Kandam 2935 to 2943).

With the demon dead and the devas free, the order of heaven is restored. In the Deva Kandam Indra again rules the golden city, and a messenger carries the news that Surapadman has fallen and the devas are out of prison to Indra's queen on Mount Meru (Deva Kandam 36 and 41 to 43).

It is this rescue that the Thiruppugazh recalls. Song 582, sung at Viralimalai, says that Murugan protected Indra's son, freed him from the prison and gave heaven back to Indra to rule. In the Puranam the prison is opened by Veerabahu at Murugan's command; the song, as songs do, gives the deed to the Lord himself.
