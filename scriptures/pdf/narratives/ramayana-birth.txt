King Dasaratha of Ayodhya had long been without a son. Valmiki tells how, after the great sacrifice, his three queens gave birth to four sons: Kausalya to Rama, Kaikeyi to Bharata, and Sumitra to the twins Lakshmana and Shatrughna. Rama, the eldest, is described as a portion of Vishnu himself, born for the good of the world (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala 18.7–14).

Kamban gives the birth a cosmic frame. Before it, Vishnu tells the gods to go down to earth and be born as monkeys in the forests and mountains, with their hosts, to help him (Kamba Ramayanam, Thiru Avatara Padalam 18). Then Kausalya gives birth to the one who once held all the worlds within himself, whom even the Vedas cannot fully know, shining with the beauty of a dark rain cloud (Thiru Avatara Padalam 101). The child is named Rama, and Kamban links the name to the Lord who came to the help of the elephant that cried out to the one who sleeps on the serpent (Thiru Avatara Padalam 115).

The Tamil tradition loves the picture of Rama as a small child in his mother's arms. Kulasekhara Alvar wrote a whole decad of lullabies in Kausalya's voice, singing to the infant Rama at Thirukkannapuram as the one who would one day scatter the ten heads of the lord of Lanka (Perumal Thirumozhi 8.1). In another decad he begins his life of Rama with the birth in Ayodhya, in the line of the sun, to end the sorrow of the gods (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.1).

The exact words of Kausalya's call to her child are not found in a particular verse of Valmiki or Kamban; they are Arunagiri's own, drawn from the common tradition of the Lord as a mother's baby.

Arunagiri uses the image once, in song 68 (Thiruchendur). There Kausalya calls the child lovingly: "Come here, my king, come; come and take your milk; come, let me deck you with flowers." The child who comes when she calls is the Mayan, Vishnu, and the song calls Murugan the nephew in whom that Lord delights. In a single affectionate line Arunagiri brings the Lord of all the worlds down to a small child running to his mother, and then turns from him to praise his nephew.
