The story of Brahma's imprisonment is told in the first kandam of the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar. It shows Murugan as a child who is wiser than the creator himself, and it is the background to the many songs that praise him as the one who put Brahma in prison.

Brahma, seated on his lotus, had recited the Pranava, the letter-form of the sacred syllable Om, and begun to open out the Vedas (Utpatti Kandam 1213). Murugan laughs and asks him the meaning of the first letter. Brahma opens his eyes wide, is ashamed, stammers and stands in confusion; he does not know it (1214). The poet remarks that if even the god on the lotus is confounded, then our own small knowledge is a matter for laughter (1217).

Murugan then makes Brahma fall, strikes him with his foot, and has him chained in a prison on the Skanda mountain, with his attendants (1219). Then, with the creator in prison, Murugan sets about the creation of living beings himself (1220, 1223). Many ages pass in this way, and at last Vishnu, thinking how Brahma may be freed, turns to the gods and the sages (1224).

They go to Shiva and say: "Lord, your son, when Brahma could not give the meaning of the first word of the Veda, put him in prison, and himself carries on the creation" (1229). Shiva comes to Murugan. Murugan seats him on a great jewelled seat and asks, "You who are the soul of souls, what has brought you here?" (1245). At the end Murugan takes Brahma by the hand and sets him free before his father; and Brahma, with true love, bows low to Shiva (1256).

The songs refer to the story as Murugan having "put Brahma in prison", and the Puranam shows why: the creator did not know the meaning of Om.
