The burning of Kama, the god of love, is told in the Ramayana as part of the history of a place. When Vishvamitra leads the young Rama past a hermitage, he explains that Kama once attacked Shiva there while Shiva was practising austerities, and was burned by the anger in Shiva's eye. His body fell away, and from then on he was called Ananga, "the bodiless"; the country where his limbs fell was named Anga (Ramayana 1.23.10–14). The Bhagavata Purana mentions in passing that Kama had earlier been burned to ashes by Rudra's anger, when it tells of his rebirth as Pradyumna, the son of Krishna (Bhagavata Purana 10.55.1).

The fuller story belongs to the Shiva Purana, the Linga Purana and the Skanda Purana. The gods needed a son of Shiva to defeat the demon Taraka, but Shiva sat in deep meditation while Parvati served him. Kama was sent to stir Shiva's heart toward her, and was burned to ash by the fire of Shiva's third eye (Shiva Purana, Rudra-samhita, Parvati-khanda, chapter 19; Linga Purana 1.101; Skanda Purana, Maheshvara-khanda, Kaumarika-khanda, chapter 24). The Shiva Purana later tells of his revival (Parvati-khanda, chapter 51). Parvati then won Shiva by her own penance, and from their union came the son who would lead the gods.

The Tamil saints sing of the burning many times. Sambandar says Kama came to unite the mountain king's daughter with Shiva, and the fire-spitting eye burned his body (Tevaram 2.31.6), and asks how the Lord could burn with a glance the one who had shot five arrows from his bow (Tevaram 2.3.2). Appar pictures the spring breeze and the cuckoo accompanying Kama, who in his telling is sent by Vishnu (Tevaram 4.14.9). Sundarar calls Kama the archer with the sugar-cane bow, burned by a sidelong glance (Tevaram 7.9.4). Tirumular counts the deed among the eight heroic acts of the Virattam temples, placing it at Kurukkai, and reads it as the inner mastery of desire (Tirumandiram, second tantra, section 2, verse 8).

For Arunagiri this story matters especially, because it leads to Murugan's birth. He mentions it in more than twenty-five songs, often naming Shiva simply as the one who burned Kama. He calls Shiva the father who burned Kama with his eye (song 649, Kathirkamam), and lists "Kamari", the enemy of Kama, among his titles (song 577, Viralimalai). Other examples are songs 70 and 103 (Thiruchendur), 124 (Palani) and 242 (Thiruthani).
