The story of the net is the fifty-seventh of Shiva's sacred games at Madurai in the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam. Shiva, the Puranam says, was once alone with the Goddess and began to explain to her the meaning of the Vedas. Her mind was elsewhere, for it was an hour for loving words and not for weighty teaching. For this neglect of the Vedas Shiva said that she would be born as a fisherman's daughter. When she grieved, he promised that he would come himself to marry her (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 57).

The punishment seemed too heavy to their sons. Murugan and Ganesha threw the books of the Vedas into the sea. Shiva then turned on Nandi, who kept the door, and asked who had let the boys in; Nandi answered that they were Shiva's own sons and he could not stop them. Nandi was cursed to pass his days as a shark in the sea, and Murugan to be born mute in a merchant's house in Madurai, while Ganesha was forgiven (padalam 57).

On the eastern shore of the Pandya country the chief of the fishermen had no child and had been praying to Shiva at Madurai. In the shade of a punnai tree he found the Goddess lying as a small child, and took her home to his wife. She grew up among the fisherfolk. Nandi, as a shark, eluded the fishermen's nets and did them harm, and none of them could master it. The chief declared that whoever caught it would have his daughter. Shiva then came as a young fisherman, saying he had come to live by casting his net. He caught the shark and drew it to the shore. When the girl was brought before him, he said that she had eyes like fish, and her father said that her name was Meenakshi. The Puranam puts it neatly: the shark was caught in his net, and she in the net of his eyes. He married her and released her from the curse, and before returning to Madurai he stayed at Thiruuttarakosamangai and explained the Vedas to her again, while she listened with reverence (padalam 57).

Arunagiri knows the story well. Song 291 names the Goddess as the one who came to the earth as the fishermen's daughter, and song 1165 tells of the baby girl on the seashore, raised by the fisherfolk, and of Shiva who came to woo her. In that song Murugan's part is told with love: he came into the world to show his grace and to end the curse on Nandi, who had been born as a shark.
