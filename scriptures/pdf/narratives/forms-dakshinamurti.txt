Dakshinamurti, the Lord who faces the south, is Shiva as the teacher. He is pictured as a young man seated beneath a banyan tree, with a group of aged sages at his feet, one hand in the gesture of knowledge. Tradition names the sages as Sanaka and his brothers, and says that he taught them in silence: the deepest truth was passed to them without a spoken word.

The Tevaram returns often to the scene under the banyan. Appar sings at Pazhanam that beneath the banyan the Lord taught all the dharmas to the four, the subtle meaning of every text (Tevaram 4.36.6). At Tirunageswaram he says that, seated under the banyan, the Lord taught the four the four aims of life, dharma, wealth, pleasure and liberation, together with the Vedas and their six limbs (6.66.2). He sings of the same teaching at Naraiyur, Tiruvarur, Nagai-Karonam and Maraikkadu (6.74.9; 6.30.3; 6.22.9; 6.23.9), and at Anniyur (5.8.8). Sambandar praises the Lord who dwells in the shade of the stone banyan (2.24.3), and Sundarar asks him, with a devotee's familiarity, why he sat under the banyan teaching dharma to the great ascetics (7.99.2). In the Tiruvasagam, Manikkavasagar says that under the banyan he taught the four the secret of the four Vedas (Tiruchchazhal 16).

These hymns speak of a teaching given to "the four", and of what was taught: dharma and the meaning of the Vedas. They do not themselves dwell on the silence. That the teaching was given without words is the well-known understanding of the form in the later tradition, and Arunagiri reflects it.

In the Thiruppugazh the form is rare but striking. In song 110, at Palani, Shiva is praised as the Lord of the silent teaching, crowned with the moon and arugu grass. In song 823, at Periyamadam, the poet turns the title to Murugan himself: he addresses him as Dakshinamurti, whom Brahma could not know. This fits the poet's larger picture, in which Murugan is the teacher even of his own father, who whispered the meaning of Om into Shiva's ear. The silent teacher under the banyan and the child teacher at Swamimalai are, for Arunagiri, two faces of the same grace.
