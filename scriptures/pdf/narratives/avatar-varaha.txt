The Bhagavata Purana tells of the boar, Varaha, as part of the account of creation in its third book. When the earth had sunk into the waters, Manu asked Brahma to lift her up, since she was to be the home of all living beings (Bhagavata Purana 3.13.14–15). Brahma thought long about how it could be done and decided to leave it to the Lord (3.13.16–17). As he was thinking, a tiny boar, no bigger than the tip of a thumb, came out of his nostril, and while Brahma watched it grew as large as an elephant, standing in the sky (3.13.18–19). Brahma and the sages wondered what it could be, and when it roared like a mountain they knew it for the Lord and chanted the Vedic hymns to him (3.13.20–25).

The boar leapt into the sky, scattering the clouds with his hooves, and plunged into the ocean, searching for the earth by her scent (3.13.26–28). The ocean split before him (3.13.29). He found the earth lying in the depths, lifted her easily on his tusks and brought her out of the water, and he killed the demon Hiranyaksha, who tried to fight him (3.13.30–31). The sages praised him: the earth on his tusks was like a lotus held by an elephant rising from the water, and they felt themselves purified by the drops shaken from his body (3.13.34–44). Then he set the earth upon the water (3.13.46). The fight is told at greater length in later chapters. Hiranyaksha met the boar carrying the earth and mocked him; the Lord bore the insults because the earth on his tusks was afraid, and first set her safely on the water (3.18.2–8). Then they fought, and the Lord at last struck the demon down with a single blow (3.19.25–26).

The Vishnu Purana tells of Narayana as the boar who raises the earth (Vishnu Purana 1.4). Among the Alvars, Thirumangai Alvar praises the Lord who as the boar lifted the earth from the flood (Periya Thirumozhi 8.8.3), Bhutattalvar names the boar who raised the earth drowned in the deluge (Irandam Thiruvandhadhi 30), and Periyazhwar's Yashoda calls the child Krishna the one who became the boar, the tortoise and the fish (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.3.7).

Arunagiri recalls the boar in two songs. Song 545 (Perainagar) praises Murari, Vishnu, who rose in a beautiful form and dug up the earth, and who also came as the angry man-lion from the pillar; song 233 (Swamimalai) also names the boar. In both, Murugan is the nephew of this Lord.
