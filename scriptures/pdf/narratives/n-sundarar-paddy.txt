Kundaiyur Kizhar is one of the sixty-three Nayanmars, and his whole story is one of quiet service. The Periya Puranam introduces him as a farmer of Kundaiyur, a man of the Vellala community skilled in tilling the land, devoted to Sundarar (Periya Puranam, Eyarkon Kalikkama Nayanar Puranam 10). For many days, without fail, he sent paddy, lentils, sweet sugarcane and many other good things to the house of Paravai, Sundarar's wife at Tiruvarur, so that the saint's household was always provided for (11).

Then the rains failed and the land's wealth shrank, and he could not gather enough paddy to send. Ashamed, he went without food and lay down to sleep in sorrow (12–13). The Lord came to him and said that he had given paddy to Sundarar through him, and at the Lord's command Kubera, lord of wealth, filled the whole of Kundaiyur with paddy, piled like mountains that hid the sky (14). When the farmer woke and saw it he was amazed, and he went to tell Sundarar that no human hand could carry what the Lord had given (15–18).

Sundarar came to Kundaiyur, looked up at the mountain of paddy that seemed to measure the sky, and saw that unless the Lord himself sent people to carry it, it could never reach Paravai's house (19). He went to the nearby temple of Thirukkolili and sang a hymn asking for bearers (20). A voice from the sky answered, by the Lord's grace, that his own attendants would carry the paddy, filling not only Paravai's house but all of Tiruvarur (21). Sundarar went back to Tiruvarur, and that night the Lord's dwarf attendants took up the mountain of Kundaiyur, filled Paravai's house, and heaped paddy over the whole town (24–25). At dawn the people of Tiruvarur wondered in what world such paddy had grown, and said it was Sundarar's gift for the town (26–27).

Arunagiri recalls the story in song 172, sung at Palani. The commentary reads it as the Lord's delight in his devotee Sundarar, skilled in Tamil poetry, and his care for Paravai, "like a golden creeper": to please her, he commanded the delivery of paddy heaped up like a mountain. Murugan, the song says, is the child of that Lord. The farmer's love and the Lord's generosity meet in the same night's work.
