The binding of Krishna to the mortar is told in the ninth chapter of the tenth book of the Bhagavata Purana, and it gives him one of his best-loved names, Damodara, "the one with a rope (dama) round his belly (udara)". One morning Yashoda was churning curd herself, singing of her son's childhood deeds. Krishna came wanting to be nursed and caught hold of the churning rod; she took him on her lap, but when the milk on the stove began to boil over she put him down and hurried to it. Angry, the child broke the curd pot with a stone, went into another room and began eating the fresh butter (Bhagavata Purana 10.9.1–6).

When Yashoda came back she saw the broken pot and guessed who had done it. She found him sitting on an upturned wooden mortar, giving curd and butter to the monkeys and glancing about nervously; when he saw her coming with a stick, he jumped down and ran (10.9.7–9). The one whom yogis fail to reach in meditation was caught by his mother; and seeing him cry and smear the kohl over his face, she threw the stick away and decided to tie him up instead (10.9.9–12).

Then came the wonder. The rope she brought was short by the width of two fingers. She tied on another, and it too was two fingers short; she joined every rope in the house, and the gap could not be closed (10.9.15–17). Only when the child saw his mother tired and perspiring, the flowers falling from her hair, did he take pity on her and let himself be bound (10.9.18). The Bhagavata draws the lesson plainly: the Lord who controls the universe allows himself to be controlled by those who love him, and neither Brahma nor Shiva nor the goddess Lakshmi received such grace as Yashoda (10.9.19–20). The chapter ends with Krishna, still bound, turning his eyes to two arjuna trees in the yard (10.9.22).

The Alvars love the scene. Periyazhwar sings of the child tied to the mortar by Yashoda, crawling off with it and snapping the twin trees (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.9.9), and Thirumangai Alvar speaks of Krishna who ate butter and was bound for it (Periya Thirumozhi 8.2.5).

In the Thiruppugazh this is one of the most often recalled scenes of Krishna's childhood. Song 629, sung at Kundrakudi, names him directly as Damodaran and Murari,. Song 1098 tells how Yashoda tied his two hands tightly to the mortar for drinking the curd, ghee and milk of every cowherd's house, and how little Gopalakrishna cried. Song 908 follows him crawling with the mortar as the maruda trees fall.
