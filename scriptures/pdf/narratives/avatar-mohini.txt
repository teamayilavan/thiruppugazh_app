The Mohini episode follows directly on the churning of the Ocean of Milk. When Dhanvantari came out of the sea carrying the jug of nectar, the demons snatched it from him by force, and the dismayed gods took refuge with Hari (Bhagavata Purana 8.8.35–36). He told them not to grieve: he would bewilder the demons by setting them to quarrel, and so fulfil the gods' wish (8.8.37). The demons did begin to quarrel over who should drink first (8.8.38), and the Lord then took the form of a woman of extraordinary beauty, Mohini (8.8.41–46).

As the demons fought, throwing and snatching the jug, they saw her coming towards them. They marvelled at her beauty and questioned her: who was she, where had she come from (8.9.1–3)? Calling themselves brothers, all sons of Kashyapa, they begged her to settle their quarrel and divide the nectar fairly (8.9.6–7). Mohini smiled and answered them as if in jest, asking how they could place such trust in a woman they did not know (8.9.8–9). Her joking made them more confident, and they handed her the jug (8.9.11). She agreed to divide it if they would accept whatever she did, honest or not, and they assented (8.9.12–13).

Gods and demons fasted, bathed and made offerings, and sat facing east in an arena adorned with lamps and garlands; Mohini entered and seated them in separate rows (8.9.14–20). She went first to the demons and pleased them with sweet words, then served the nectar to the gods (8.9.21). The demons kept silent, bound by their promise and unwilling to quarrel with her (8.9.22–23). One demon, Rahu, disguised himself as a god and drank; the Lord cut off his head with the discus, and the head, touched by the nectar, lived on as a planet (8.9.24–26). When the gods had almost finished, the Lord revealed his own form before the demons (8.9.27). The Bhagavata draws the lesson: the place, the work and the aim were the same for both, but those who sheltered at the Lord's feet received the result (8.9.28).

Arunagiri never names Mohini. His songs praise instead the Lord who gave the nectar to the gods. Song 46 (Thiruchendur) speaks of the primal Lord who gave the nectar to the frightened gods so that they might live; song 169 (Palani) of the great Mayan who shared out the nectar so that all the gods would live; song 367 (Thiruvannamalai) of the cloud of grace who churned the nectar and ended the hunger of the gods; and song 952 (Keeranur) of the gracious Achyuta who had the milk ocean churned and gave out the nectar.
