This is not a story but a place. Vishnu is worshipped in many great temples of the Tamil country, and above all at Srirangam, on an island in the Kaveri, where the Lord, called Ranganatha or Periya Perumal, the Great Lord, lies on the serpent Adisesha. It is the same picture as the Lord resting in the Ocean of Milk, given a home on earth, and the Alvars returned to it again and again.

Kulasekhara Alvar opens his Perumal Thirumozhi with a longing to see Periya Perumal reclining on Adisesha at Srirangam (Perumal Thirumozhi 1.1). Thondaradippodi Alvar's Thiruppalliyezhuchchi is a song to wake Ranganatha at dawn, and in its last verse the poet asks only to be accepted as a servant of the Lord's devotees (Thiruppalliyezhuchchi 10). Periyazhwar asks the Lord of Srirangam, who bears the conch and the discus, to keep him in mind at the hour of death (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 4.10.2).

The same Lord stands on the hill of Venkatam, at Tirupati. Kulasekhara would rather be born a fish in the pool at Thiruvengadam than rule heaven or earth (Perumal Thirumozhi 4.2). Nammazhwar surrenders at the feet of the Lord of Venkatam, on whose chest Lakshmi says she will not leave him even for a moment (Thiruvaimozhi 6.10.10), and Thirumazhisai Alvar says that the Lord stands at Venkatam so that all may take refuge at his feet (Thiruchchandha Viruththam 81). Thirumangai Alvar names Thiru Ooragam at Kanchi among the Lord's homes, together with Kudanthai and Thiruppernagar (Periya Thirumozhi 1.5.4).

Arunagiri, singing to Murugan, names these temples in his one-line praises of Murugan's uncle. Song 652 (Kasi) speaks of Narayana asleep on the serpent bed at Srirangam; song 134 (Palani) of the great Lord of Srirangam who rests on the serpent's hood; song 549 (Thiruchirappalli) of the Lord who belongs to Srirangam; and song 971 (Ilanji) of the Lord of Thiruvarangam. Song 362, sung at Thiruvanaikka, across the river from Srirangam, names the king of Srirangam, and song 727 (Siruvai) names the Lord of Thiru Ooragam at Kanchi. In every case Murugan is praised as the nephew of the Lord who lies in the temple.
