When Ravana had fallen, Rama sent Hanuman into Lanka to tell Sita of the victory (Valmiki Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 113). Hanuman found her in the Ashoka grove, where she had spent her captivity under guard, and gave her the news; Sita was at first so overcome with joy that she could not speak. Valmiki tells that Hanuman offered to punish the demon women who had tormented her, and that Sita refused, saying that they had acted on their master's orders and that the noble should show compassion even to those who do wrong. She sent back word that she wished to see her husband.

Rama then asked Vibhishana to bring Sita to him bathed and adorned (Yuddha 114). Vibhishana brought her in a palanquin through the press of monkeys and demons who had gathered to see her; Rama directed that she should come before them all, and she walked the last distance and looked upon his face.

Kamban tells the same journey in images of reverence. When Rama spoke, Vibhishana, whose crown shone with both darkness and light, said, "I will bring her as you wish," set upon his head her golden feet, tender as young leaves, and went (Kamba Ramayanam, Pirattai Thiruvadi Thozhuta Padalam 25). They seated her in a vimana like a deer within the disc of the moon, with heavenly women following and monkeys and demons running on every side (34). At last she, who had thought that her life had already left her, saw before her the one of leaf-green hue and coral lips, standing with the bow in his hand (44). What followed, Sita's ordeal by fire, belongs to the next episode.

Kulasekhara Azhwar ends his account of the war with Rama returning home with Sita (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.7).

In the Thiruppugazh the freeing of Sita is the goal of the whole war, and Arunagiri names it in six songs. In song 166, at Palani, the Lord who dammed the ocean and cut down the jewelled crowns of the terrible Ravana called Janaki, his very life, and took her back with valour. In song 252, at Thiruthani, he cut off the ten heads like mountains and released Sita, whom Arunagiri calls the Lakshmi of the Vedas, from prison. Songs 435 (Thiruvannamalai) and 833 (Ettikudi) likewise speak of Sita brought out of captivity. For Arunagiri the Lord whose nephew Murugan is, is above all the one who frees the captive.
