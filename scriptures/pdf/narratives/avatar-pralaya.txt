The picture of Vishnu as an infant lying on a banyan leaf, holding the worlds within himself while the waters of dissolution cover everything, is told in the Bhagavata Purana as a vision granted to the sage Markandeya. Pleased with the sage's lifelong devotion, the Lord offered him a boon. Markandeya asked only to see the Lord's power of illusion, by which the world appears as it does, and the Lord, smiling, agreed (Bhagavata Purana 12.9.1–7).

Some time later, while the sage was at his evening worship on the bank of the Pushpabhadra, a great wind arose, clouds poured down rain, and the oceans rose until they covered islands, mountains and the sky. Of all beings only Markandeya remained, wandering alone through the dark waters for ages, not knowing sky from earth (12.9.10–19). At last he came upon a small island with a young banyan tree in flower and fruit, and on one of its branches, lying within a leaf, an infant whose radiance swallowed the darkness (12.9.20–21). The child was dark like an emerald, smiling, and held one foot in his hands with a toe in his mouth (12.9.22–25).

As the sage drew near, the child breathed in and drew him inside like a mosquito, and there Markandeya saw the whole universe arranged as it had been before the flood (12.9.27), down to the Himalayas, the river and his own hermitage; then the child breathed out and cast him back into the waters (12.9.30). The child glanced at him with a loving smile, and when the sage ran to embrace him, the child vanished, and with him the tree, the flood and the dissolution itself; Markandeya found himself in his hermitage, just as before (12.9.31–34).

The Alvars keep the same picture. Poigai Alvar names, in a single verse, the Lord who at the deluge swallowed the worlds and lay upon a banyan leaf (Mudhal Thiruvandhadhi 19).

Arunagiri recalls the image in five songs, always in one line and without the sage; he keeps only the child on the leaf and the worlds held within. Song 970 (Sri Purushamangai) speaks of the Mayan who measured the earth and once slept on a banyan leaf; song 276 (Thiruthani) of the tall one who holds the round earth in his belly; song 402 (Thiruvannamalai) of the one who once swallowed all the worlds; and song 1103 of the Lord who measured the earth and swallowed it. In each, Murugan is this Lord's nephew.
