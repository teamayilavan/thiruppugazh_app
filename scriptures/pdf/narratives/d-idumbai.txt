The forest of Idumbai appears only once in the Thiruppugazh, and only in passing. In song 574, sung at Viralimalai, Arunagiri praises Krishna before turning to Murugan. Krishna, he says, cleverly brought about the victory of the Pandavas and the fall of the hundred Kauravas led by Duryodhana; he went as the messenger of Dharma, the eldest Pandava, who had lived in the forest of Idumbai where the Goddess Durga was present; he drove the great chariot of Arjuna, who had done penance with the holy ash on his forehead; and Murugan is his nephew.

Idumbai is the Tamil form of the name Hidimba. In the Mahabharata the Pandavas, escaping with their mother Kunti from the house of lac that had been set on fire to kill them, enter a terrible forest where the rakshasa Hidimba lives. His sister Hidimbi comes to them, Bhima kills Hidimba, and Bhima and Hidimbi have a son, Ghatotkacha (Mahabharata, Adi Parva 1.139–143, in the critical edition's numbering). This is most likely the forest that the song has in mind, though the song does not name the episode.

The Mahabharata's account of this episode does not say that the Goddess Durga dwelt in Hidimba's forest. That detail belongs to the song, or to the tradition on which Arunagiri drew, and it is not developed further anywhere in the Thiruppugazh. For that reason the film made from this passage tells no story. It shows the Pandavas only at a distance, as wanderers in a deep forest, and it shows the Goddess not at all, except as a light that watches over them. It reflects a feeling common in the Tamil country, where small shrines of the Goddess stand at the edges of forests and under great trees, that the wild places are hers and that those who must live in them are under her protection.

For Arunagiri the forest is one brief image in a song about Krishna, who is Murugan's uncle. The song moves from Krishna's deeds in the Mahabharata to Murugan, and in doing so it gathers the Goddess of the forest into the same family of grace. The only song to cite is 574 (Viralimalai).
