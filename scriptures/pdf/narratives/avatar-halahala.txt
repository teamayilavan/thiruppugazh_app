The poison is the first and most terrible thing the churning of the Ocean of Milk brought up. In the Bhagavata Purana, as gods and demons churned the sea, it became turbulent, its great creatures came to the surface, and it first produced a fierce poison called halahala (Bhagavata Purana 8.7.18). As this uncontrollable poison, the kalakuta, spread up and down in every direction, all the gods, with the Lord himself, went to Shiva, feeling unsheltered and afraid, and sought refuge with him (8.7.19). They found him on Kailasa with Bhavani and praised him at length, asking him to save them from the poison spreading through the three worlds (8.7.20–35).

Shiva, moved by compassion, told Bhavani that it was his duty to protect suffering beings, and that by doing good to others he pleased Hari and all creatures; therefore he would drink the poison (8.7.36–40). Bhavani, who knew his power, consented, and he took the whole poison in the hollow of his palm and drank it (8.7.41–42). It left a blue mark on his throat, which became his ornament (8.7.43). Everyone, Bhavani, Brahma and Vishnu among them, praised the deed, and the scorpions, cobras and other poisonous creatures took the few drops that fell from his hand (8.7.45–46). Strengthened, gods and demons went back to the churning (8.8.1).

The Valmiki Ramayana tells it a little differently. There the halahala rose from the serpent Vasuki and from the rocks, and Shiva drank it at Vishnu's word (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kandam 45.19, 20, 25). The same event is told from Shiva's side in the story of the blue-throated Lord.

The Alvars, singing of Vishnu, remember the churning rather than the poison; Andal's last verse begins with "the Madhava who churned the ocean" (Thiruppavai 30).

Arunagiri brings both gods into a single line. Song 27 (Thiruchendur) calls Murugan the son blessed by the one who drank the poison, and the nephew of the tall cloud who churned the sea. Song 46 (Thiruchendur) tells that the halahala went to Shiva and that the primal Lord, who sleeps on the roaring sea, gave the nectar to the frightened gods so that they might live. Song 169 (Palani) says the Lord helped on the day of churning so that no one came to harm, saw that Shiva received the poison, and shared out the nectar so that all the gods might live. Song 70 (Thiruchendur) names Shiva as the dark-throated one.
