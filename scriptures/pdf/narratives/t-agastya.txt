In several songs of the Thiruppugazh, Arunagiri praises Murugan as the teacher of the sage Agastya, who in the Tamil tradition is the first master of the Tamil language. The songs speak of it as something every listener would know.

Song 180, sung at Palani, says that Agastya, the great master of Mount Pothigai, worshipped Murugan alone, in chaste Tamil, and that when he fell at Murugan's feet the Lord gave him his blessing in full. Song 915, sung at Vayalur, says that Murugan lovingly taught him the texts of pure knowledge and increased the wisdom of the sage who was learned in ancient Tamil. Song 954 says that Murugan taught the essence of true knowledge both to Shiva and to Agastya, who knew the three branches of Tamil. Song 1081 says that Murugan once spoke the essence of Tamil knowledge into Agastya's ears so that he might learn it, and song 1299 calls Murugan the lord of the three branches of Tamil, worshipped by the small sage. In song 619 the hill of Pugazhimalai is praised as a place that Agastya himself extolled.

The Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar knows Agastya well, but not as Murugan's student. It tells how, when the whole world gathered at Kailasa and the earth tilted, Shiva sent the small sage south to the Podiya hill, which is Pothigai, so that the world would come level again (Utpatti Kandam 805 to 807). The sage, troubled at being sent far from Shiva's feet, asked how he was to go (808). In the Asura Kandam he is again called the Tamil sage on his journey south (1214, 1222, 1224). No passage of the Puranam tells of Murugan teaching him Tamil; the teaching belongs to the songs and to the tradition of the Tamil country.

That tradition is old and widespread. It makes Agastya the author of the first Tamil grammar, a work now lost, and the teacher of the poets who came after him. The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, telling of the Sangam at Madurai, speaks of the grammars from Agastya's down to Tolkappiyar's as the works the Sangam poets studied (padalam 51).

For Arunagiri the story places Murugan at the very source of Tamil. If the father of Tamil learned the language from Murugan, then the poet who sings to Murugan in Tamil is singing to the teacher of Tamil itself. The songs return to this again and again (songs 180, 915, 954, 1081, 1299), and the small sage of Pothigai stands in them as the first devotee who praised Murugan in the Tamil tongue.
