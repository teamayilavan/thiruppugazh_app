The story of Vandi belongs to the Madurai cycle of the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam of Paranjothi, and it closes the episodes of Manikkavasagar's life in that city. The Pandya king, angry that his minister had spent the treasury on the temple at Perunthurai and that the horses he brought had turned back into foxes, had him made to stand on the burning sand of the Vaigai; the Lord answered by sending the river into flood (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 60).

To contain the flood, a share of the riverbank was measured out to every household. Vandi, an old woman devoted to Somasundarar, the Lord of Madurai, lived alone and earned her living by selling pittu, a steamed sweet of rice flour; she had neither husband nor son to send, and she prayed to the Lord for help (padalam 61). A handsome young labourer came by asking for work. She told him she had no money, only pittu, and he asked for the crumbled pieces as his wage. He ate first, praising the pittu, and then went to the river, where he sang and played and did little work, so that when every other share of the bank had been closed, Vandi's alone still let the water through (padalam 61).

The king came along the bank with a golden cane in his hand, found the gap, and struck the labourer once on the back. At that moment the labourer emptied his basket of earth into the gap, the bank was complete, and he disappeared. The blow fell on every living being in the world: on the king's own back, on his minister, and, the telling says, even on the paintings on the walls. The king knew then that the labourer had been the Lord. A voice from heaven pointed out his wrong, a heavenly chariot came for Vandi, and the king went to Manikkavasagar to ask his pardon (padalam 61). The account here follows a modern prose rendering of the padalam, not a verse-by-verse reading.

Arunagiri returns to this story in three songs. In song 960, sung at Madurai, the Lord who turned foxes into horses danced on the banks of the Vaigai, gladly carried loads of earth, and took pittu as wages from the old woman Vandi. Song 1317 (Pazhamudircholai) recalls that when the Vaigai flooded he came as a labourer content with crumbled pittu, and was caned by the king. Song 756 (Thirukkoodalaiyatrur) speaks of the Lord who came to Madurai in another form, by the Vaigai. For Arunagiri the Lord who works for an old woman's pittu, and takes a blow on her behalf, is the father of Murugan.
