The war between Murugan and the asura king Surapadman, told in the Yuddha Kandam of the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, lasts many days, and Surapadman is the last of his house to fall. His brothers and sons have died in battle, and on the final day he still has the boon by which he cannot be destroyed (Yuddha Kandam 2885).

When the gods see Surapadman rising into the sky to come at them, they flee in terror and cry to Murugan to save them (2856 to 2860). Murugan looks at the spear in his hand and tells it to go and split the asura's chest in a moment (2861). The spear, blazing with the light of countless suns, goes forth, and all the dark illusion that Surapadman had put around himself drops away (2862). Surapadman sees his magic fail and the spear coming at him; but trusting in his endless boon he smiles, and is filled with fury (2863).

Then the asura does what the songs remember. He goes into the middle of the sea (2864) and there takes the shape of a mango tree. The text calls it a huge tree, 1,100 yojanas tall, with blossoms like gold and fruit green as emerald and ripe as red gems, its branches spreading their shade over the seas, the sky and the whole earth (2865 to 2867). The moon and the sun and the elephants of the quarters are afraid at the sight (2868). The tree shakes, and the worlds shake with it; the tortoise and the serpents that bear the earth reel, and the gods run for shelter to Meru and Kailasa (2872 to 2876).

Murugan's spear now flies at the tree. It is a flame that spreads over the seas and the heavens, and even Brahma and Vishnu, who had once fought over which of them was supreme, draw back in fear as it passes (2877 to 2883). It strikes the great mango at its root, and the tree falls with a groan; yet because of his boon, Surapadman does not die (2884 to 2885). He takes his old shape again, draws his sword and rushes at Murugan; but the spear tears open his chest, splits his body in two and fells him in the sea (2886 to 2887).

The songs say, in a line, that Murugan's spear split the mango tree. The Puranam has the tree fall first and the asura's body be split afterwards; and the two halves do not die. What became of them is the story of the peacock and the cock.
