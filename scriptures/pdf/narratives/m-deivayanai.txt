Deivayanai, whom the Sanskrit tradition calls Devasena, is the daughter of Indra, and she is the first of Murugan's two wives; the second is Valli, the hunter's daughter. The Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar foretells the two marriages in the first kandam, where Deivayanai and Valli are named as the future wives of the child of the Saravana (Utpatti Kandam 1275 to 1277). It tells of her marriage in the Deva Kandam, in the padalam named for it (Deva Kandam, padalam 2, verses 36 to 303).

As a girl she was brought up by Airavata, the great white elephant of Indra, who tended her as the Milk Ocean once tended the goddess Lakshmi (Utpatti 1276). The Puranam returns to her only after the war. When Surapadman is dead and the gods are free, Indra resolves to give his daughter to the victorious Murugan, and tells Brahma and the gods of his wish; they are glad and agree (Deva Kandam 36 to 37). He remembers that she had done hard penance beside a pool for this lord, and that the fruit of it had now come (38).

Indra sends a messenger to Mount Meru, to his queen, to bring their daughter (40). The messenger tells her that Surapadman is dead, that the gods are out of prison, and that Murugan has come to Thiruparankundram; Indra has asked that she come there with the girl at once (41 to 43). She is overjoyed, mounts Airavata with her daughter, and goes through the sky to Thiruparankundram (44 to 46). The goddesses who have just been freed from the prison of Mahendrapuri gather round to welcome the queen (47 to 48).

Indra, with the host of the gods, then praises Murugan. "You have broken the strength of the asuras and freed the gods," he says; "what can a small one like me do in return? My daughter is here on this hill; marry her and take her to your strong shoulder" (51 to 53). Murugan answers that she too has done great penance, and that the wedding day shall be fixed as Indra wishes (54). Indra sends messengers to invite the gods and sages and the kings of the earth, and tells the divine builder to prepare the wedding hall (55 to 59).

Indra's messengers also go to the earthly king Mucukundan; overjoyed, he sets out in his golden chariot with his army, and arrives at Parankundram, where the other kings and their hosts gather round the hill (84 to 91, 130 to 134). The rest of the padalam, to verse 303, tells of the wedding itself.

The Thiruppugazh names Deivayanai in many songs, often only to say that she is Murugan's wife beside Valli; but some, the songs that call her the daughter of Indra, given to him, follow this tale. It is the reason Thiruparankundram is said to be the place of their wedding.
