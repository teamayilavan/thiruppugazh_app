The story of Poompavai belongs to Mylapore, on the coast near present-day Chennai, and the Periya Puranam tells it near the end of Sambandar's life. Sivanesar, a wealthy merchant of Mylapore and a devotee of Shiva, heard of the child saint's fame and vowed that his daughter Poompavai and all his wealth would be given to Sambandar (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 1033–1053). While she was gathering flowers in a garden with her companions, the girl was bitten by a snake, and in spite of every remedy she died (vv. 1055–1058). Her father had the body cremated, and kept her bones and ashes in a pot in a room of his house, waiting for the saint to whom she had been promised.

When Sambandar came to Mylapore, Sivanesar told him what had happened, and the saint asked for the pot to be brought before the gate of the Kapaliccharam temple (vv. 1084–1085). A great crowd gathered, including, the Puranam notes, followers of other faiths who had come to watch, and the gods crowded the sky (v. 1084). Looking at the pot, Sambandar called her by name and declared: if it is true that the fruit of being born in this world is to feed the devotees of Shiva and to see his festivals with one's own eyes, then come, before all the world (vv. 1086–1087). He sang the hymn beginning "Mattitta Punnai" (Tevaram 2.47), whose refrain asks Poompavai whether she will go without seeing the festivals of Mylapore (v. 1088). With the verses she took form again inside the pot, growing, the poet says, to the age of twelve; and when he sang the closing verses, the pot broke and she rose from it like a lotus opening (vv. 1089–1091). Sekkizhar describes her beauty at length (vv. 1100–1109).

Sivanesar fell at the saint's feet (v. 1110). Sambandar told him to take his daughter home (v. 1112). When the merchant pleaded that the saint should marry her, as he had vowed, Sambandar answered that since he had brought her back to life by the grace of Shiva, it was not fitting, and he explained the teaching of the scriptures to comfort the family (vv. 1113–1115). Then he went into the temple (v. 1116). The staging of the garden and the pot in the film is added for the screen.

In the Thiruppugazh, three songs recall the miracle. Song 693, sung at Thirumayilai (Mylapore) itself, praises the town of the Vedas and sacrifices where it happened; song 150 (Palani) and song 944 (Avinasi) also recall it. The songs say that Murugan, as Sambandar, brought back as a girl one who had become only bones.
