The Bhagavata Purana tells that when Kamsa, king of Mathura, was driving the chariot of his sister Devaki on her wedding day with Vasudeva, a voice from the sky warned him that her eighth child would kill him. Kamsa seized her to kill her, and was stopped only by Vasudeva's promise to hand over every child she bore. Kamsa kept the couple in prison, and when the children were born he killed them one after another (Bhagavata Purana 10.1). The eighth child, Krishna, was carried away on the night of his birth to the cowherd village of Gokula, and Vasudeva brought back the newborn daughter of Yashoda in his place (10.3).

The fourth chapter takes up the story from there. The guards, waking at the child's cry, ran to tell Kamsa, who rushed to the delivery room fearing that his death had been born (10.4.1–3). Devaki pleaded with him: this was a girl, his own niece, and killing a woman was beneath him; he had already killed so many of her children; let him give her this last one (10.4.4–6). Kamsa tore the child from her and seized her to dash her against a stone, but she slipped from his hands and rose into the sky as the eight-armed goddess, Vishnu's younger sister, and told him that the one who would end him had been born elsewhere (10.4.7–12). Shaken, Kamsa released Devaki and Vasudeva and asked their pardon (10.4.14); but the next day, on his ministers' advice, he resolved to have every infant born in the past ten days killed (10.4.29–31), the beginning of the long series of attacks on the child Krishna in Vraja.

The tradition gives Kamsa weight as a tragic figure ruled by fear. He is Krishna's maternal uncle, and the cruelty is all the darker for being turned against his own sister's children.

That is what Arunagiri remembers. In song 1274 he speaks of the wicked uncle who, not treating his nephew as a nephew, plotted to kill him; the irony is pointed in a song addressed to Murugan, himself so often praised as the beloved nephew, the marukan, of Vishnu. In song 181 (Palani) he recalls the Lord who saw through all the deceits that Kamsa sent against him and overcame them. In both, Murugan is praised as the nephew of that Krishna.
