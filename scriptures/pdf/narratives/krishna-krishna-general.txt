Many of the Thiruppugazh songs that name Vishnu do so through a particular story: the elephant saved from the crocodile, the cart kicked over, the hill lifted. Some, however, name Krishna only by an epithet, without pointing to any one episode. This portrait gathers the scenes told in the other Krishna stories of this book into a single picture of the Lord whom Arunagiri calls Murugan's uncle.

The Bhagavata Purana gives Krishna's childhood in the cowherd village of Gokula as a series of wonders set inside ordinary family life. As an infant he drank the life of the ogress Putana with her poisoned milk (Bhagavata Purana 10.6.10–13) and kicked over a heavy cart with his soft feet (10.7.7). As a toddler he was accused of eating earth and showed his mother the whole universe in his mouth (10.8.32–39), and when he stole butter and fed it to the monkeys, every rope she brought to bind him was two fingers short until he chose to let her (10.9.8–18). Dragging that mortar, he pulled down the twin arjuna trees and freed the two sons of Kubera (10.10.26–28). Grown into a youth, he entered Mathura, broke the tusk of Kamsa's elephant and ended the tyrant's reign (10.43.14; 10.44.34–37).

As a king in Dwaraka he remained, above all, a friend. When the poor brahmin who had studied with him came to his door, Krishna ran to embrace him and washed his feet with his own hands (10.80.18–20). In the Mahabharata he is the friend and counsellor of the Pandavas, Arjuna's charioteer in the war at Kurukshetra, and the one present when Yudhishthira was anointed king (Mahabharata 12.40.2, 12.40.12). The Bhagavata sums up his purpose on earth as removing the burden of the earth (Bhagavata Purana 11.1.1).

The song for which this portrait was made is 586, sung at Thiruchengode, in which Murugan is called "the nephew of Mukunda of the calves", that is, Krishna the cowherd, friend of the cows. The phrase does not refer to any one deed; it simply calls up the cowherd Lord. Elsewhere Arunagiri uses the same habit of naming. Song 629 calls him Damodaran and Murari, song 1098 the crying child Gopalakrishna, and song 1196 the Mayan, the wonder-worker, who drove Arjuna's chariot. Behind each name stands a story, but in a line like that of song 586 Arunagiri asks his listeners to remember them all at once.
