The Bhagavata Purana gives a whole chapter to Krishna's flute. In autumn, when the waters were clear and the forest of Vrindavan was full of flowers, bees and birds, Krishna entered it with Balarama and the cowherd boys, grazing the cows and playing on his flute (Bhagavata Purana 10.21.1–2). Hearing it, the young women of Vraja began to describe him to one another, but as they remembered him their words failed them (10.21.3–4). The chapter pictures him as he walks: a peacock feather in his hair, blue flowers at his ears, cloth yellow as gold and a garland of forest flowers, entering the forest like a great dancer while his friends sing his praises (10.21.5).

The rest of the chapter is spoken by the gopis, and it describes what the music does to the world. The peacocks dance (10.21.10); the deer gaze at him with love (10.21.11); the cows drink the sound with raised ears, and the calves stand still with milk in their mouths and tears in their eyes (10.21.13); the birds sit on the branches with closed eyes, like sages (10.21.14); the rivers check their currents and embrace his feet with their waves, offering lotuses (10.21.15); and a cloud shades him from the summer sun (10.21.16).

Periyazhwar sings of the same music in Tamil. He describes the boy who wears his locks down his back and plays his sweet flute (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.4.9), whose little fingers stroke the holes of the flute (3.6.8), and at whose playing the trees pour honey and the flowers drip (3.6.10); elsewhere he calls in the cows by blowing on his bamboo flute (3.9.9).

Arunagiri turns to the flute in about eight songs. In song 43 (Thiruchendur) the fingers move in order over the holes of the bamboo, and the birds of the wide sky come down to earth to listen; in song 158 (Palani) the flute's music guards the herds as they graze; in song 1009 the Lord gathers the cattle with the music of his bamboo; in song 1170 the Mayan who swallowed the earth plays on the bamboo. Each time, Murugan is praised as the nephew of the cowherd with the flute.
