The Linga Purana tells that Brahma and Vishnu once disputed which of them was the source and lord of all, and that while they argued a column of fire, flaming without beginning or end, appeared between them (Linga Purana 1.17). To settle the matter Vishnu took the form of a boar and dug downwards, and Brahma took the form of a swan and flew upwards. Neither found an end, and both came back to praise the pillar, out of which Shiva revealed himself (Linga Purana 1.17–20).

The Shiva Purana tells the story at greater length (Vidyesvara-samhita, chapters 5–9). There the quarrel grows into a battle, and the column of fire appears to stop it (chapters 6–7). Vishnu returns and admits that he has not found the foot; Brahma claims to have seen the top. His false claim, and Shiva's anger at it, lead into the story of Brahma's fifth head (chapter 8). The Skanda Purana places the manifestation of the fiery Linga at Arunachala and describes Vishnu's search of its lower part (Skanda Purana, Maheshvara-khanda, Arunacala-khanda, chapters 10–11). The hill of Tiruvannamalai is worshipped as that fire, and temple tradition recalls it each year with the great lamp lit on the summit.

The Tamil hymns return to the story again and again. Tirumular gives it a section of its own, "Seeking the Feet and the Crown": Brahma and Vishnu each claim to be the Lord, Shiva stands as a blazing fire, and Vishnu confesses that he has not found the feet (Tirumandiram, second tantra, section 8, verses 1–2). Sambandar recalls the two gods searching while the Lord stood as a vast flame, often in the ninth verse of a hymn (Tevaram 2.25.9, 2.108.9, 3.15.9, 2.117.10). Appar (Tevaram 4.50.1, 5.34.9, 6.40.7) and Sundarar (Tevaram 7.22.8) say the same: neither the feet nor the crown could be seen. The point in every case is not the defeat of two gods but the boundlessness of the third.

Arunagiri uses the story as a single line of praise for Murugan's father. He calls Shiva the Supreme whose beginning and end are hard to see (song 733, Thirukkovalur), and he recalls the fire that Brahma and Vishnu could not measure in songs 205 (Swamimalai), 360 (Thiruvanaikka), 479 (Chidambaram) and 957 (Madurai). The child of that endless light, the songs imply, is the one who comes close to his devotees.
