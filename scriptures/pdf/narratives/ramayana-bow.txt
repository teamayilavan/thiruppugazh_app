King Janaka of Mithila kept in his house a great bow that had once belonged to Shiva and had been handed down in his family. His daughter Sita had been found in a furrow of the earth, and Janaka had resolved to give her in marriage only to a man strong enough to string that bow. Many kings had tried and failed. When the sage Vishwamitra brought the young princes Rama and Lakshmana to Mithila, the bow was brought out on its heavy cart, and Rama, at the sage's word, lifted it with ease and drew it, and it broke at the middle with a sound like thunder. Valmiki has the break described to Janaka, the bow snapping at its handgrip (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala 67.17).

Kamban tells the scene with great richness. Rama lifts the bow, which is like a golden mountain, as though it were the garland to be placed on Sita, the rare jewel of the world (Kamba Ramayanam, Karmukap Padalam 33). When it breaks, the gods are terrified, crying out that the whole sphere of Brahma's creation has split, and Kamban asks what could even be said of the shaking of the earth (Karmukap Padalam 35). In Kamban's telling, Rama and Sita have already seen one another from a distance before the contest, so the moment is also the fulfilment of a mutual love.

King Dasaratha was summoned from Ayodhya, and the weddings followed: Rama married Sita, and his three brothers married princesses of the same house. On the journey home, the warrior-sage Parashurama confronted Rama with the bow of Vishnu and challenged him to string it. Rama did so at once, and Parashurama, recognising him, was humbled and withdrew (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala 75.12–16). The Alvars tell the two events together: Kulasekhara Alvar sings of Rama breaking Shiva's bow to win Sita and humbling Parashurama on the way back (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.3), and Periyazhwar speaks of Rama going with Vishwamitra to Janaka's sacrifice and breaking the bow (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.10.9).

Arunagiri refers to the episode in three songs. In song 649 (Kathirgamam) he praises the Mayan who broke the great long bow, after killing Tataka with one arrow and guarding the sage's sacrifice. In song 799 (Thiruvidaikkazhi) the bow snaps with a crack at Mithila, and Hari is joined with the Lakshmi whom Janaka gave him. In song 968 (Sri Purushamangai) Rama breaks the strong bow, marries Janaki, and then gives up his crown to honour his mother's word.
