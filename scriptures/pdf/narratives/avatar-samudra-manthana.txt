The churning of the Ocean of Milk is told most fully in the eighth book of the Bhagavata Purana. When the gods had lost their strength, Vishnu advised them to make a truce with the demons and to churn the milk ocean together for the nectar of immortality, using Mount Mandara as the churning rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope (Bhagavata Purana 8.6.22–23). The Valmiki Ramayana tells the same plan in a few verses: the gods and demons resolved to churn the Milky Ocean with Mandara and Vasuki (Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kandam 45.17–18). Thirumazhisai Alvar adds the cause the Tamil tradition remembers: after the sage Durvasa's curse, the gods' wealth went into the milk ocean, and the Lord churned it out again (Thiruchchandha Viruththam 81).

Gods and demons uprooted Mandara, but it was too heavy to carry and fell on them, until Vishnu came on Garuda, revived the fallen, and carried the mountain to the sea (8.6.33–39). Vasuki was called and promised a share of the nectar. Vishnu and the gods took the head of the serpent, but the demons refused the tail as beneath them; the Lord smiled and took the tail himself, with the gods following (8.7.1–4). When the unsupported mountain sank, the Lord took the form of a tortoise and held it up (8.7.6–8).

The churning first produced the deadly poison, which Shiva drank (8.7.18–19, 42). Then came the cow of plenty, the white horse Uccaihshravas, the four-tusked elephant Airavata and the jewel Kaustubha, which Vishnu took for his chest (8.8.1–6). Lakshmi appeared, shining like lightning; she looked through the whole assembly for one without fault, and placed her lotus garland on Vishnu alone (8.8.8, 8.8.17–24). Last came Dhanvantari, the master of medicine, carrying the jug of nectar, and the demons snatched it from him at once (8.8.31–35). The gods turned to the Lord, who told them not to grieve (8.8.36–37); the Mohini story follows.

The Alvars name the churning often: Andal's Thiruppavai ends with "the Madhava who churned the ocean" (Thiruppavai 30), and Bhutattalvar speaks of the churning for Lakshmi and the nectar (Irandam Thiruvandhadhi 30).

Arunagiri recalls it in nine songs, praising Murugan as the nephew of the Lord who churned the sea. Song 27 (Thiruchendur) speaks of the tall cloud who churned the ocean; song 723 (Thiruvakkarai) of the Lord who set the great mountain in the sea, wound the serpent round it, and raised the nectar and gave it away; and song 952 (Keeranur) of the one who had the milk ocean churned and gave out the nectar.
