The birth of Murugan in the Saravana lake is told in the first kandam of the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, and it is the story to which the Thiruppugazh returns most often when it speaks of his origin.

The Puranam begins with Shiva. By his own grace, from the eyes in the foreheads of his six faces, he lets fall sparks of fire, and they spread through the whole of the heavens and the earth (Utpatti Kandam 914). He tells the gods who stand before him to carry the sparks and let them go into the swollen waters of the Ganga, and they bow and agree (917, 923). Agni, the god of fire, takes them first; but the heat makes him sweat and burn, and, unable to take another step, he flings them into the roaring Ganga (935). The Ganga, too, grows weary, and carries the fire to the Saravana, the reed-lake on the slopes of the Himalaya where the lotus blooms (940).

There, in the lotus of the lake, Murugan takes his form (950). The six women of the Krittika come to the lake; and he who has six faces becomes six separate children (966). The women, watching his play, are filled with wonder and fear, and instead of thinking of him as mere babies they worship him as a god (976).

The story ends with the Goddess. Shiva and Parvati come to the lake, and Parvati sees the six children (1032). She takes them up in her arms and embraces them, and as she does so the six forms become one, with six faces and twelve arms (1034). He receives the name Kandan, and is Parvati's son (1035).

The Thiruppugazh speaks of this birth in many songs, calling Murugan the child of the Saravana, the child of the six mothers, and the one with six faces and twelve arms. The Puranam shows how each of these names came to be.
