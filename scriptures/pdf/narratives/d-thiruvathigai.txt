Thiruvathigai, near Panruti on the river Gedilam, is the shrine of the Veerattanam, where Shiva is worshipped for a deed of valour; and the deed remembered here is the burning of the three cities of the asuras. The story is among the oldest told of Shiva and is told more fully elsewhere in this book: three cities of gold, silver and iron, held by the asuras, could be destroyed only by a single arrow, and Shiva, with Mount Meru as his bow, burned them all at once.

Sundarar's hymn to Thiruvathigai Veerattanam ties the deed to this place. In its ninth verse he praises Shiva who bent the bow so that the three cities of the unrepentant asuras were burned, and who accomplished it with a single arrow; who wears the hide of the dark, rutting elephant; who has three great eyes; who pressed down the twenty shoulders and ten heads of the demon king who did not think of him; and who dwells at the northern Veerattanam by the rushing Gedilam (Tevaram 7.38, verse 9). The hymn speaks of Shiva alone. That the Goddess is worshipped beside him at Thiruvathigai, under the names Abirami and Tripurasundari, is the tradition of the temple.

Arunagiri gives her a larger part. In song 292, sung at Thiruthani, he describes the Goddess as the one who brought forth the whole world in its order; as Uma, the peacock-like daughter of Himavan; and as the one who bent the great Mount Meru like a bow and, in an instant, burned the three cities at Thiruvathigai. She is Abirami, the most beautiful, who shares the left half of the body of Shiva, the Lord whose form is fire, who is beyond the reach of words and whom Vishnu and Brahma could not comprehend.

To say that the Goddess herself bent the bow is not to contradict the older hymn. For Arunagiri, Shiva and the Goddess are one body, and the deed of one is the deed of both; the bow that burned the cities was drawn by the Lord whose left half is the Mother. In this way the songs turn the most famous of Shiva's battles into a picture of their unity, and the shrine of the burning into a shrine of the Goddess as well.

The song to cite is 292 (Thiruthani), which names Abirami and Thiruvathigai together.
