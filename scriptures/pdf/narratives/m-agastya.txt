The songs of the Thiruppugazh say that Murugan taught the sage Agastya, who lives on Mount Pothigai in the south, the essence of Tamil, and that Agastya in turn gave the Tamil language to the world. Arunagiri speaks of it with the confidence of a tradition that everyone knew; and it is a tradition that the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar, as far as I could read it, does not tell.

What the Puranam does say of Agastya is this. At the great gathering at Kailasa, so many came that the earth tilted. Shiva looked at the small sage and told him to go south, to the Podiya hill, saying that when he was there the world would come level again (Utpatti Kandam 805 to 807). Agastya, the Tamil sage, was afraid that he had done something wrong, since Shiva was sending him far from his feet, and asked how he was to go (808). In the Asura Kandam he is called the Tamil sage again, on his way south (1214, 1224).

In his journey he meets the asura Krouncha, who takes the form of a mountain to trap him. Agastya sees through the trick, and curses the mountain to stay a mountain until it should be destroyed by the spear that Murugan holds (Asura Kandam 1219 to 1221). When Murugan's spear later pierces Krouncha, the sage's word comes true (Utpatti 1509 to 1516). This is the only link in the Puranam between Murugan and Agastya.

A keyword search of all six kandams of the Puranam found no passage in which Murugan teaches Agastya or gives him the Tamil language. The teaching belongs to the songs and to the wider tradition of the Tamil country, in which Murugan, as lord of the Tamil hills, is the teacher of the sage who is the father of Tamil. The story is retained here because the songs tell it, but it should be read as tradition rather than as a tale of the Puranam.
