Manikkavasagar, the poet of the Tiruvasagam, came from Thiruvadavur near Madurai, and is often called Vadavurar after his village. The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam of Paranjothi Munivar tells that he served the Pandya king of Madurai as a minister, and that the king sent him with a great sum of money to buy horses. On the way, at Thiruperunthurai, he met the Lord, who sat as a guru beneath a kurundam tree. Vadavurar received his teaching, spent the king's money on the temple, and afterwards could not find his teacher again (Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 58, Tiruvadavurar-ukku upadesitha padalam).

What follows is told in the next padalams: the king's anger, the Lord's promise of horses, the jackals of the forest turned into horses and back again, and the flood of the Vaigai (padalams 59 to 61). These belong to the larger story of Manikkavasagar and Madurai. This film and this account dwell on the turning point under the tree.

The poet's own words are the best witness. In the Tiruvasagam he calls on the Lord of Thiruperunthurai in the kurundam grove: "Light, flame, lamp of all-encircling brightness" (Tiruvasagam, Arutpathu, verse 1). In the Kirththit Tiruvagaval, where he lists the deeds of the Lord, he includes the grace by which the Lord once sat beneath the kurundam (lines about 59–60). Again and again in the work, the poet's longing returns to the guru he met at Perunthurai and then lost. The name Manikkavasagar, "he whose words are gems", is the name by which tradition knows him; it is said to have been given by the Lord himself.

Arunagiri sang at Thiruperunthurai itself. In song 844 he names Manikkavasagar under the kurundam tree at Perunthurai, with Shankara as his guru. In song 7, at Thiruparankundram, he praises Shiva who danced in the hall, and the guru who gave grace under the kurundam tree; and in song 1174 he calls Murugan the son of the destroyer of the cities and links him with the guru of the kurundam. For a poet who himself sought a guru and found grace, the meeting under the tree was a story close to his own.
