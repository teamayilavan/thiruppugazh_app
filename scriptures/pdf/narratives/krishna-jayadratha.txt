On the thirteenth day of the war at Kurukshetra, Arjuna's young son Abhimanyu was killed after forcing his way alone into the Kaurava battle formation, while Jayadratha, king of the Sindhus, held the other Pandavas back at its entrance. The Mahabharata's Drona Parva tells how Arjuna, learning of it, vowed that he would kill Jayadratha before sunset the next day or else enter the fire himself, and how on the fourteenth day the Kauravas set Jayadratha deep at the rear of their army, guarded by their greatest warriors, while Arjuna, with Krishna driving his chariot, fought his way toward him through the whole day.

The constituted text of the critical edition tells the end plainly. As the day closed, Krishna urged Arjuna in haste: "Dhananjaya, cut off the head of the wicked king of Sindhu" (Mahabharata, Drona Parva 7.121.15–16), and Arjuna's arrow took Jayadratha's head as one takes a bird from the top of a tree (7.121.32).

The better-known version, in which Krishna hides the sun, appears in the critical edition only as an interpolated passage, printed among the variant readings: there Krishna remembers his discus Sudarshana and has darkness cover the sun so that it seems to have set, and later withdraws the darkness (7.121.14 and 41, starred passages). It is this version that the Tamil tradition has kept. Periyazhwar sings of the kings guarding Jayadratha while they counted the hours, of the Lord hiding the sun with his divine discus, and of Arjuna's arrow sending Jayadratha's head rolling away (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 4.1.8).

Arunagiri follows the Tamil tradition. In song 568 (Viralimalai) he speaks of the cowherd king who, with his sharp discus, hid the sun so that the one who had vowed might see his purpose through; in song 574 (Viralimalai) Jayadratha is named among the enemies faced by Vijaya's charioteer; and in song 902 (Vayalur) the discus makes the sun set at midday. Murugan is praised as the nephew of that Lord, Vishnu, who brought day and night at his will to keep his devotee's word.
