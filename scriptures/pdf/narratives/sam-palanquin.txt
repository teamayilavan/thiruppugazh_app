In the early years of his pilgrimage the child Sambandar travelled from shrine to shrine with his father and a growing band of devotees. The Periya Puranam tells that on the road to Maranpadi the devotees with him suffered in the heat and the boy, chanting the five syllables, walked on; and, says the poet, the sun drew in its thousand rays and sank into the western sea, as if it were itself afraid of tiring him (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 191–192). The party spent that night at Maranpadi (v. 193).

The Lord of Thiruvarathurai, on the river, took the child's weariness to heart (v. 194). The Puranam says that Shiva resolved to give him a palanquin, an umbrella and golden trumpets, all of flawless pearl (v. 195), and appeared that night in a dream to the Brahmins in every house of Nelvayil, the town of the shrine, telling them: "Gnanasambandan is coming to us; take from us the palanquin of pearls, the jewelled umbrella and the trumpets, and give them to him" (vv. 196–197). In the morning the Brahmins found they had all been told the same thing, went to the temple in wonder, opened it at the hour of waking, and found the palanquin, the white umbrella of moon-like pearls and the trumpets there (vv. 198–201). Shiva also appeared to the child himself in a dream and told him to accept the gifts he gave (vv. 205–206).

At dawn the child rose, put on the holy ash, and set out (vv. 207–208). With conches, drums and trumpets the Brahmins of Nelvayil carried the gifts toward him (vv. 202–204), met him on the road and told him: "Receive the great grace that the Lord of Arathurai has given you" (vv. 210–211). He bowed to them as the grace of the Lord who dances in the hall (v. 212). From this time the pearl palanquin appears in the Puranam as the saint's vehicle: when he leaves Sirkazhi later he rides it under the white umbrella (v. 282), and at Vedaranyam he steps down from it to greet Appar (v. 577). The scenes of the road at dusk and the child's expressions are the film's own additions.

The Thiruppugazh names this gift in a single song. Song 758, sung at Thiruvarathurai itself, calls Murugan the one who, as Sambandar, received the palanquin from the Lord of that shrine, and joins it to his other titles: the boy who grew up in Kochchai and the one praised by the sages. For Arunagiri the gift is a sign of the honour Shiva showed to his own son born on earth.
