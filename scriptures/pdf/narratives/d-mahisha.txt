The Devi Mahatmya, the "Glory of the Goddess", is a section of the Markandeya Purana (chapters 81 to 93 in the edition used here), and its middle episode tells how the Goddess destroyed Mahishasura, the demon who took the form of a buffalo.

Mahishasura defeated the gods in a long war and made himself Indra, lord of heaven (Markandeya Purana 82.2). The beaten gods went to Brahma and then, with him, to Shiva and Vishnu and told them what had happened (82.3). Their anger gathered into a blaze of light, and from it the Goddess took form. Her face came from the brilliance of Shiva, and the other gods gave of their own radiance for the rest of her body (82.13). They gave her weapons too: Shiva drew a spear from his own and gave it to her, and Vishnu made a discus from his (82.20).

She then destroyed the armies of the demon. In the last combat she said to him, "Roar, roar for a moment, fool, while I drink; when I strike you down, the gods will roar here." She sprang up, pressed his neck with her foot and struck him with her spear. Mahishasura, crushed by her foot, came half out of his own mouth, still fighting, and the Goddess cut off his head with her great sword (83.37–40). The gods, led by Indra, then sang a hymn of praise that begins with the words "Shakradayah", "Indra and the other gods" (84.1–2).

In the Thiruppugazh the deed is mentioned only in passing, among the many exploits of the Goddess.
