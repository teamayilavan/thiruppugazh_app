The Devi Mahatmya, the "Glory of the Goddess", is a section of the Markandeya Purana (chapters 81 to 93 in the edition used here), and its middle episode tells how the Goddess destroyed Mahishasura, the demon who took the form of a buffalo.

Mahishasura defeated the gods and made himself Indra, lord of heaven (Markandeya Purana 82.2). The beaten gods went with Brahma to Shiva and Vishnu and told them what had happened (82.3). In their anger a great light poured out of Vishnu, Brahma and Shiva and out of all the other gods, and came together into one, like a mountain on fire; it became a woman whose radiance filled the three worlds (82.9–12). Her face came from the brilliance of Shiva, and the other gods gave of their own radiance for the rest of her body (82.13). They gave her weapons too: Shiva drew a spear from his own and gave it to her, and Vishnu made a discus from his (82.20). Honoured by all the gods, she laughed aloud again and again, and the worlds shook and the seas trembled (82.31–33).

She then destroyed the armies of the demon. When his host was dwindling, Mahishasura himself came in the form of a buffalo and terrified her attendants (83.21). Bound by her noose, he became a lion, then a man with a sword, then a great elephant whose trunk she cut away, and then a buffalo again (83.29–33). He hurled mountains at her with his horns, and she broke them to dust with her arrows (83.35–36). In the last combat she said to him, "Roar, roar for a moment, fool, while I drink; when I strike you down, the gods will roar here." She sprang up, pressed his neck with her foot and struck him with her spear. Mahishasura, crushed by her foot, came half out of his own mouth, still fighting, and the Goddess cut off his head with her great sword (83.37–40). Then the gods, led by Indra, sang a hymn of praise that begins with the words "Shakradayah", "Indra and the other gods" (84.1–2).

In the Thiruppugazh the deed is named only in passing, among the many exploits of the Goddess. Song 575, at Viralimalai, praises the fierce and valorous Durga as the destroyer of Mahisha, and song 1126 names her as the one who killed Mahishasura, among her other deeds.
