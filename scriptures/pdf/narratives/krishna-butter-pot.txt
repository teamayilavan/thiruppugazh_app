Among the scenes of Krishna's childhood in Vraja, none is better loved than the theft of butter. The Bhagavata Purana gives it in the voice of the cowherd women, who come to Yashoda to complain (Bhagavata Purana 10.8.29–31). Krishna, they say, lets the calves loose before milking time and laughs when the householder is angry; he steals curd, milk and butter and shares them with the monkeys, and if the monkeys will not eat, he breaks the pot. When there is nothing to steal he teases the small children until they cry (10.8.29).

When the women hang their pots high out of reach in swinging slings, he and his brother pile up planks and turn a grinding mortar upside down to climb on, and pierce the pots, knowing exactly what is inside; and in a dark room the jewels on his body light the way (10.8.30). When he is caught he answers, "I am not the thief; you are." Yet now, the women say, he sits before his mother like a good boy, and seeing his frightened eyes and lovely face they cannot bring themselves to scold him, and Yashoda only smiles (10.8.31).

The Alvars of the Tamil country made the story their own, often in the voice of a mother or a neighbour. Periyazhwar sings of the child who swallowed butter and drank curd from the pot (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.1.3), who gulped down the butter churned three times a day (3.1.5), and whose throat was slick with it (3.2.6). He also tells how the boy was caught red-handed in the cowherd women's street and tied up (2.10.5), and Thirumangai Alvar has a girl's mother speak of the Krishna who was caught and bound for stealing butter (Periya Thirumozhi 8.2.5).

Arunagiri returns to the butter thief in about ten songs, usually in one closing line. In song 121 (Palani) the cowherd women scold the Lord as a "curd thief"; in song 683 (Vada Thirumullaivayil) he climbs up in the cowherds' houses to the hanging pots; in song 987 he stretches his hand into the hanging net; in song 1098 he drinks the curd, ghee and milk of every cowherd home and is tied to the mortar by Yashoda. In each, Murugan is praised as the nephew of this playful thief, the same Vishnu who lies on the serpent in the sea.
