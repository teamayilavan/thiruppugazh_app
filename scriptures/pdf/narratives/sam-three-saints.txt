Appar, called Thirunavukkarasar, "King of Speech", was an older contemporary of Sambandar who had returned to Shaivism after years among the Jains. The Periya Puranam tells how he came to Sirkazhi to see the child saint, a figure of devotion with the holy ash on his body and a small hoe in his hand for weeding temple courtyards. Sambandar went out to meet him, each bowed to the other, and they worshipped together at the Thoni shrine; after some days Appar set out on his own pilgrimage, and they parted in close friendship (Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam 270–274). Their paths crossed again in later years, and the Puranam shows them travelling and singing together.

The best-known episode of their friendship took place at Vedaranyam (Thirumaraikkadu), on the coast. When the two arrived, the townspeople welcomed them, and at the meeting Sambandar stepped down from his pearl palanquin to greet the older saint (vv. 576–578). At the temple they found that the great doors, which the Vedas had worshipped and closed in ancient times, could not be opened, and that the people went in by a side entrance (vv. 579–580). Sambandar, addressing his companion as "Appar", asked him to sing a hymn so that the doors would open and they could enter in front of the Lord (v. 581). Appar sang a hymn of ten verses and the doors did not move; grieved, he pleaded in the closing verse, and the doors opened while the devotees and the gods cried out with joy (v. 582). The two went in and worshipped with tears (vv. 583–585).

When they came out, Appar asked Sambandar to sing so that the doors would close again, and so that from then on they could both open and close (v. 586). Sambandar sang, and the doors closed (v. 587); flowers rained down (v. 588), and from that day the doors were used in the ordinary way (v. 589). The Puranam then follows the two saints into the town of Maraikkadu (vv. 597–609). Sekkizhar presents the event as two kinds of grace working together, not as a contest between the saints.

The Thiruppugazh does not narrate the doors. Song 121 (Palani) lists Maraikkadu among the shrines Sambandar sang, with Kazhi, Maruthavanam and Thirumarugal; song 343 (Kanchipuram) lists holy places in the same way; and song 1166 names Sambandar as one of the three great saints of the Tevaram, with Appar and Sundarar, the last of whom lived later and does not appear in this story.
