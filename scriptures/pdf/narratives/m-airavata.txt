Airavata is the great white elephant of Indra, the king of the gods. In the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar he has a place in the story of Murugan's marriage, and it is this place that the songs of the Thiruppugazh recall when they say that Deivayanai was brought up by Airavata.

The Puranam, in the first kandam, where it foretells the two marriages of Murugan, to Deivayanai and to Valli (Utpatti Kandam 1275 to 1277), describes Deivayanai in the court of Indra and says that the mighty Airavata tended her, as the Milk Ocean once tended the goddess Lakshmi, its daughter (1276). The image is of a great guardian caring for a young girl, as the sea cared for the goddess who rose from it, and the Puranam uses Airavata again when it tells of her journey to the wedding.

That journey comes after the war. When Surapadman is dead and the gods are free, Indra resolves to give his daughter to the victorious Murugan, and he remembers that she had done hard penance beside a pool for this lord (Deva Kandam 36 to 38). He sends a messenger to Mount Meru, to his queen, who is told that the demon is dead, that the gods are out of prison, and that Murugan has come to Thiruparankundram; Indra asks that she come there at once with the girl (40 to 43). At the news the queen is filled with wonder, her sorrows leave her, and she rises with her daughter (44). Bowed to by those around her, she mounts the mighty Airavata with the young girl beside her and goes through the sky (45). She comes to Thiruparankundram, the hill famed on earth, and stays in a hall there, the Puranam says, "with the elephant of two tusks" (46). The goddesses who have just been freed from the prison of Mahendrapuri gather round to welcome her (47 to 48), and the wedding follows (see the story of Deivayanai).

The Puranam gives Airavata no words and no deeds of his own beyond these. He is the guardian of the girl's childhood and the bearer who brings her to her marriage, and that is enough for the songs.

Twelve songs of the Thiruppugazh mention Airavata in this connection, among them 172 (Palani), 259 (Thiruthani), 430 (Thiruvannamalai) and 449, 457 and 469 (Chidambaram). They use the elephant as a mark of Deivayanai's birth in the household of Indra, and of the care she received there: Murugan is praised as the lord of the maiden whom Airavata reared.
