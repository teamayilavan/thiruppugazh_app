Airavata is the great white elephant of Indra, the king of the gods. In the Kanda Puranam of Kachiyappa Sivachariyar he has a place in the story of Murugan's marriage, and it is this place that the songs of the Thiruppugazh recall when they say that Deivayanai was brought up by Airavata.

The Puranam, in the first kandam, where it foretells the marriages of Murugan, describes Deivayanai in the court of Indra and says that the mighty Airavata tended her, as the Milk Ocean once tended the goddess Lakshmi, its daughter (Utpatti Kandam 1276). The image is of a great guardian caring for a young girl, and the Puranam uses it again when it tells of her journey to the wedding.

In the Deva Kandam, when Surapadman is dead and Indra has decided to give his daughter to Murugan, his queen sets out from Mount Meru, mounted on the mighty Airavata with her daughter beside her, and so reaches Thiruparankundram (Deva Kandam 45 to 46).

Twelve songs of the Thiruppugazh mention Airavata in this connection. They use the elephant as a mark of Deivayanai's birth in the household of Indra, and of the care she received there.
