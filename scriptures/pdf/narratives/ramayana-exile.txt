On the eve of Rama's coronation as heir, Ayodhya was full of preparations. In the palace, however, Queen Kaikeyi, stirred by the counsel of her maid Manthara, called upon King Dasaratha to grant two boons he had once promised her. Valmiki gives the scene in full: she asks that her own son Bharata be consecrated in Rama's place, and that Rama be sent to live in the forest for fourteen years (Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya 11). Bound by his word, the old king could not refuse, though the demand broke him.

Kamban is severe toward Kaikeyi, calling her in one line the worst of all evil ones (Kamba Ramayanam, Kaikeyi Suzhchip Padalam 14). Yet the centre of his scene is Rama's calm. It is Kaikeyi who tells Rama the king's command, that he should go to the forest and return after twice seven years (Kaikeyi Suzhchip Padalam 111). Rama hears it, Kamban says, like a man freed from an affliction by a gracious one (Kaikeyi Suzhchip Padalam 113), and answers simply that he will go to the forest that very day and that he takes his leave (Kaikeyi Suzhchip Padalam 114). When the news reaches Kausalya she swells with grief, sobs and falls (Kamba Ramayanam, Nagar Neengu Padalam 8).

Sita refused to remain behind, and Lakshmana insisted on going with his brother. Valmiki describes the three leaving the city as the people of Ayodhya follow the chariot in tears (Valmiki Ramayana, Ayodhya 40). The Alvars recall the same events: Kulasekhara Alvar sings of Kaikeyi's demand, the crossing of the Ganga with Guha and the giving of the sandals to Bharata at Chitrakuta (Perumal Thirumozhi 10.4), and Periyazhwar of the boons granted and Rama's going to the forest with Lakshmana (Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.10.3).

The tradition does not ask the listener to hate Kaikeyi. In the end, it is Rama's willing acceptance that gives the episode its meaning: he goes not as a victim but as one who will not let his father's word be broken.

Arunagiri names the exile in three songs, and his favourite phrase for it is that the Lord honoured his mother's word. In song 968 (Sri Purushamangai) Rama, after breaking the bow and marrying Janaki, gives up the crown in the city, excelling in obedience to his mother's word. In song 597 (Thiruchengode) the other mother sends him into the forest, and he enters it with his brother and with the daughter of the Earth. In song 1265 (a general song) he goes into the great forest with the young prince following him.
