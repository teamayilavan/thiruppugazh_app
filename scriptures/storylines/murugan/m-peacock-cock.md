# The Peacock and the Cock (After the War)

`m-peacock-cock` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tense, then tender, then triumphant

## One-line story

The two halves of the fallen demon rise again as a peacock and a cock and come roaring at Murugan; under his glance, the hatred leaves them, and they become his mount and his banner for ever.

**Tradition note:** the Puranam tells of another peacock earlier in the war, when Indra took that form to carry Murugan (2775-2780). The film shows only the peacock that was Surapadman.
**Added for the screen:** the colour of the birds' glow, the narration.

## Characters

- **Murugan:** calm, six-faced, with the spear.
- **The cock:** large and bright, roaring.
- **The peacock:** emerald, with a magnificent tail.
- **The sea and the sky.**

## The storyline

### 1. The spear returns (15 sec)
The spear, having bathed in the Ganga, flies back into Murugan's hand, shining.
**Narration:** "The spear came back to his hand."

### 2. The rising (25 sec)
Over the sea where the demon fell, two shapes rise from the waves: one a mountain-bright cock, one a heap of emerald that unfolds into a peacock. They have feet and wings, and they roar.
**Narration:** "But the two halves of the demon rose again, as a cock and a peacock."

### 3. They come (20 sec)
They fly at Murugan, roaring, over the water. Murugan stands calm, spear in hand.
**Narration:** "They came to fight him once more."

### 4. The glance (25 sec)
The peacock comes before Murugan's face. Murugan looks at him. The roar goes out of the bird; his eyes soften; the dark in him turns, like black iron turning to gold.
**Narration:** "But when the peacock came before him, the hatred went out of him, as black iron turns to gold."

### 5. The banner (25 sec)
Murugan turns to the cock: "Become the banner on my chariot." The cock agrees, rises into the sky with a roar that makes thunder tremble, and becomes the red-crested cock on the flag.
**Narration:** "He told the cock to be his banner. And the cock rose, and became the flag."

### 6. The mount (20 sec)
To the peacock Murugan says, "Carry us." He climbs onto its back, leaving his old mount.
**Narration:** "And to the peacock he said, 'Carry us.'"

### 7. The flight (25 sec)
The peacock spreads its tail and flies. The sea parts below him; the great serpent that bears the earth trembles. The six-faced lord is carried across the sky to every quarter.
**Narration:** "And the peacock flew, and the six-faced lord was carried to every part of the world."

### 8. Together (10 sec)
Murugan on the peacock, the cock on the banner against the sky.
**Narration:** "He had lost a war, and he found a place at the Lord's feet."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs refer to this story: 323 (Kanchipuram), 732 (Thachur), 945 (Avinashi). The peacock and the cock appear in every picture of Murugan, so the story stands behind many songs that do not name it.

## Production notes

- **Redemption is the point.** Treat Surapadman's change as a grace, not a defeat: the music should lift when the hatred leaves.
- **The glance** (scene 4) is the heart of the film; hold it a beat longer than the rest.
