# The Peacock and the Cock (After the War)

`m-peacock-cock` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tense, then tender, then triumphant | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The two halves of the fallen demon rise again as a peacock and a cock and come roaring at Murugan; under his glance, the hatred leaves them, and they become his mount and his banner for ever.

**Tradition note:** the Puranam tells of another peacock earlier in the war, when Indra took that form to carry Murugan (2775-2780). The film shows only the peacock that was Surapadman.
**Added for the screen:** the colour of the birds' glow, the narration.

## Characters

- **Murugan:** calm, six-faced, with the spear.
- **The cock:** large and bright, roaring.
- **The peacock:** emerald, with a magnificent tail.
- **The sea and the sky.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; tense at first, softening at the glance, lifting into joy at the flight.

### 1. The spear returns (15 sec)
**Visual:** The war is over. Far away the spear dips into the Ganga, shining, then flies back over the land into Murugan's hand. He stands by the sea, calm.
**Narration:** "Surapadman had fallen into the sea, split in two by the spear. The spear bathed in the holy Ganga, and came back, shining, to Murugan's hand."
**Sound:** water; a bright returning chime.

### 2. The rising (25 sec)
**Visual:** Over the place where the demon fell, the water begins to boil. Two shapes rise from the waves: one a great cock, bright as a mountain of gems; the other a heap of emerald that unfolds into a peacock. They grow feet and wings, and they roar.
**Narration:** "But the demon had a boon, and he could not be destroyed. The two halves of his body rose again from the sea. One was a cock, shining like a mountain of gems. One was a peacock, green as a heap of emeralds. They had feet and wings, and they roared."
**Sound:** bubbling water; two great cries.

### 3. They come (20 sec)
**Visual:** The two birds fly at Murugan over the water, wings beating. The gods behind him shrink back. Murugan stands still, the spear at his side.
**Narration:** "They came flying at him, to fight once more. The Puranam says it was the demon's fate. His old deeds had led him to this shape, and brought him before the Lord."
**Sound:** heavy wingbeats; a building drum.

### 4. The glance (25 sec)
**Visual:** The peacock comes right before Murugan's face. Murugan looks at him: no anger, only a deep, kind gaze. Hold the moment. The roar dies; the bird's fierce eyes soften; the darkness in his feathers turns, slowly, to a warm gold-green light.
**Narration:** "But when the peacock came before Murugan, and Murugan looked at him, the hatred went out of him. His mind grew calm, as black iron turns to gold. The poet says that even those who have done evil become pure before the Lord. Surapadman received boundless grace."
**Sound:** the drums stop; a single held note; then a gentle rising melody.

### 5. The banner (25 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan turns to the cock. The cock bows its head. Then it rises into the sky with a roar that makes the thunder tremble, and settles on the flag of Murugan's chariot, a red-crested cock on the banner.
**Narration:** "Then Murugan turned to the cock."
**Dialogue:** Murugan: "Become the banner on my chariot."
**Narration (continued):** "The cock agreed. It rose into the sky with a roar that shook the thunder, and became the red-crested cock on his flag."
**Sound:** a crowing call that becomes a fanfare.

### 6. The mount (20 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan turns to the peacock, which lowers its neck. He leaves the mount he had before and climbs onto its back.
**Narration:** "Then he turned to the peacock. He left the mount he had ridden before, and spoke to the bird that had been his enemy."
**Dialogue:** Murugan: "Carry us."
**Sound:** soft wind in feathers.

### 7. The flight (25 sec)
**Visual:** The peacock spreads its tail, a wheel of eyes, and flies. The sea breaks beneath its wings; far below, the great serpent that bears the world trembles. The six-faced lord is carried across the sky toward every quarter.
**Narration:** "The peacock spread its shining tail and flew. The sea broke beneath its wings, and the great serpent that holds up the world trembled. And the six-faced lord was carried across the sky, to every part of the world."
**Sound:** a soaring, joyful theme.

### 8. Together (10 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan on the peacock, the cock on the banner, against a clear evening sky.
**Narration:** "He had come to fight the Lord. He found a place with him forever."
**Sound:** the theme settles on a warm chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs tell how the split halves of Surapadman became Murugan's peacock and cock: 323 (Kanchipuram), 732 (Thachur), 945 (Avinashi). The peacock and the cock appear in every picture of Murugan, so the story stands behind many songs that do not name it.

## Production notes

- **Redemption is the point.** Treat Surapadman's change as a grace, not a defeat: the music should lift when the hatred leaves.
- **The glance** (scene 4) is the heart of the film; hold it a beat longer than the rest.
