# The Elephant Who Raised a Princess (Airavata and Deivayanai)

`m-airavata` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tender, then festive | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Indra's great white elephant Airavata watches over Indra's daughter Deivayanai as she grows, and carries her and her mother across the sky to her wedding with Murugan.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Deivayanai is in Indra's court, and the mighty Airavata tends her, as the Milk Ocean once tended its daughter, the goddess Lakshmi | Kanda Puranam, Utpatti Kandam 1276 |
| She does hard penance beside a pool for this lord | Deva Kandam 38 |
| After Surapadman's death, a messenger tells Indra's queen on Mount Meru that the demon is dead, the gods are free, and Murugan is at Thiruparankundram | Deva Kandam 40-43 |
| The queen, overjoyed, mounts Airavata with her daughter beside her and goes through the sky to Thiruparankundram | 44-46 |
| The goddesses freed from Mahendrapuri's prison gather to welcome the queen | 47-48 |

**Added for the screen:** the look of Airavata's care (playing, shading, lifting), the scenery of the journey, the narration.

## Characters

- **Airavata:** the great white elephant, gentle with the girl. Draw him with two tusks: the Kanda Puranam calls him "the elephant with two tusks" when he carries the queen to Thiruparankundram (Deva Kandam 46), though later art often gives him four.
- **Deivayanai:** a young girl, then a young woman, calm and graceful.
- **Indra's queen:** loving and quick.
- **The messenger.**

## Places

Indra's court; a quiet pool for penance; Mount Meru; the sky; Thiruparankundram.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. A girl in Indra's court (20 sec)
**Visual:** The golden city of Indra at dawn: pillared halls, clouds drifting through the courtyards. A small girl, Deivayanai, runs along a terrace. Behind her, filling half the frame, walks a great white elephant with two long tusks.
**Narration:** "Long ago, in the golden city of Indra, the king of the gods, a little girl was growing up. Her name was Deivayanai, Indra's daughter. And wherever she went, a great white elephant went too."
**Sound:** soft bells, a light flute; the slow, heavy step of the elephant.

### 2. The guardian (25 sec)
**Visual:** Airavata shades her from the sun with his ear; lifts her gently onto his back; sprays a fine mist of water over a garden she has planted. In a soft dissolve, an image of the Milk Ocean rising in waves around the goddess Lakshmi, then back to the elephant and the girl.
**Narration:** "This was Airavata, the mount of Indra, mightiest of elephants. Yet with her he was gentle. The old poet says he tended her as the Ocean of Milk once tended Lakshmi, its own daughter. He shaded her, carried her, and watched over her as she slept."
**Sound:** the flute warms; the hush of waves in the dissolve.

### 3. The penance (20 sec)
**Visual:** Years pass in the colour of the light. Deivayanai, now a young woman, sits beside a still pool, hands folded, eyes closed. Airavata stands at a distance, keeping guard. A reflection in the water: a spear, a peacock feather, gone at once.
**Narration:** "She grew, and her heart turned to prayer. Beside a quiet pool she did long and hard penance, for a lord she had not yet seen. The elephant kept watch."
**Sound:** stillness; one sustained drone.

### 4. The news (20 sec)
**Visual:** Far away, smoke clears over a battlefield by the sea. A messenger of the gods flies up to Mount Meru, where Indra's queen sits among her attendants with her daughter. He bows and speaks quickly, with joy.
**Narration:** "Then the long war ended. The demon king Surapadman was dead, and the gods were free. A messenger came running to the queen on Mount Meru."
**Dialogue:** Messenger: "The demon has fallen. The gods are out of prison. Murugan is at Thiruparankundram, and Indra asks you to come there with your daughter, now."
**Sound:** a rising drum under the messenger's words.

### 5. Joy (15 sec)
**Visual:** The queen rises, her face full of light; she takes her daughter's hands. Attendants scatter flowers. Deivayanai lowers her eyes, smiling.
**Narration:** "The queen was overjoyed. The penance of her daughter had borne its fruit."
**Sound:** the melody of scene 1 returns, brighter; small bells.

### 6. The mount (25 sec)
**Visual:** Airavata is led in, decked with a gold cloth and a jewelled frontlet. He kneels. The queen mounts, and her daughter sits beside her. The elephant rises slowly and turns his great head toward the south.
**Narration:** "So the mighty Airavata was brought, decked in gold. He knelt for them, as he had knelt for the little girl so many years before. The queen mounted, with her daughter beside her, and the elephant rose and turned to the south."
**Sound:** a conch; the elephant's low call.

### 7. Across the sky (25 sec)
**Visual:** The elephant walks on the clouds; the earth unrolls below, green and gold. Ahead, a green hill rises from the plain: Thiruparankundram, a light on its summit. Birds fly beside them.
**Narration:** "Through the sky they went, over forests and rivers, over the kingdoms of the earth, toward a green hill in the south. On that hill the young lord who had won the war was waiting."
**Sound:** wind and a long, flowing melody.

### 8. The welcome (15 sec)
**Visual:** On the hill, a crowd of goddesses, freed from the prison of the demon city, gather round as the elephant kneels. They take the queen's hands. Deivayanai looks up the hill. Final image: Airavata, standing back, the guardian whose work is done.
**Narration:** "The goddesses who had been in prison came to welcome them. And the great white elephant, who had raised a little girl, brought her to her wedding."
**Sound:** the melody resolves; a single bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Twelve songs mention Airavata in this connection: 172 (Palani), 259 (Thiruthani), 430 (Thiruvannamalai), and 449, 457 and 469 (Chidambaram). They say Deivayanai was brought up by Airavata, the white elephant of Indra.

## Production notes

- **Soft, warm colours.** This is the quietest story of the Murugan set: the elephant should be a loving presence, never a spectacle.
- **Pair with `m-deivayanai`,** which tells the wedding.
