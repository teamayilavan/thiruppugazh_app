# The Elephant Who Raised a Princess (Airavata and Deivayanai)

`m-airavata` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tender, then festive

## One-line story

Indra's great white elephant Airavata watches over Indra's daughter Deivayanai as she grows, and carries her and her mother across the sky to her wedding with Murugan.

## Characters

- **Airavata:** the great white elephant, traditionally four-tusked, gentle with the girl.
- **Deivayanai:** a young girl, then a young woman, calm and graceful.
- **Indra's queen:** loving and quick.
- **The messenger.**

## Places

Indra's court; a quiet pool for penance; Mount Meru; the sky; Thiruparankundram.

## The storyline

### 1. A girl in Indra's court (20 sec)
In the golden court, among the gods, a small girl plays: Deivayanai, Indra's daughter. Behind her, vast and white, stands Airavata.
**Narration:** "In the court of Indra a little girl was growing up, watched over by a great white elephant."

### 2. The guardian (25 sec)
Airavata shades her from the sun with his ears, lifts her onto his back to see the garden, bows his head so she can reach a flower. As the Milk Ocean once tended its own daughter, Lakshmi, so the elephant tends her.
**Narration:** "Airavata tended her as the Milk Ocean once tended Lakshmi, its daughter."

### 3. The penance (20 sec)
Deivayanai, now older, sits beside a still pool in prayer. Airavata keeps watch at a distance.
**Narration:** "She grew, and she prayed at a pool, for a lord she had not yet met."

### 4. The news (20 sec)
On Mount Meru a messenger arrives before Indra's queen: "The demon is dead. The gods are free. Murugan is at Thiruparankundram, and Indra asks that you bring the girl at once."
**Narration:** "Then the demon was dead, and a messenger came to the queen on Mount Meru."

### 5. Joy (15 sec)
The queen's face lights; she calls her daughter. Deivayanai comes, calm and glowing.
**Narration:** "The queen was overjoyed."

### 6. The mount (25 sec)
Airavata kneels; the queen and Deivayanai climb onto his back. He rises into the air, ears wide.
**Narration:** "Airavata knelt, and carried them both."

### 7. Across the sky (25 sec)
The elephant crosses clouds, rivers, hills, over the world below. The girl looks ahead with quiet eyes.
**Narration:** "They flew through the sky, to the hill where Murugan waited."

### 8. The welcome (15 sec)
At Thiruparankundram, the goddesses freed from prison gather with garlands and lamps to welcome the queen. Airavata lowers himself.
**Narration:** "And the goddesses who had been in prison came to welcome them."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Twelve songs mention Airavata in this connection: 172 (Palani), 259 (Thiruthani), 430 (Thiruvannamalai), and 449, 457 and 469 (Chidambaram). They say Deivayanai was brought up by Airavata.

## Production notes

- **Soft, warm colours.** This is the quietest story of the Murugan set: the elephant should be a loving presence, never a spectacle.
- **Pair with `m-deivayanai`,** which tells the wedding.
