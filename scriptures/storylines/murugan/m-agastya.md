# The Teacher of the Teacher (Murugan and Agastya)

`m-agastya` | Murugan | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: gentle, reverent, a little humorous | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The smallest sage is sent south by Shiva to level the world; there, on a southern hill, the songs say Murugan taught him the essence of Tamil, and he gave it to the world.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| At the great gathering at Kailasa so many come that the earth tilts; Shiva tells the small sage Agastya to go south to the Podiya hill, saying the world will come level when he is there | Kanda Puranam, Utpatti Kandam 805-807 |
| Agastya, "the Tamil sage", is afraid that he has done wrong, since Shiva is sending him far from his feet, and asks how he is to go | 808 |
| In the Asura Kandam he is called the Tamil sage again, on his way south | 1214, 1224 |
| On the road the asura Krouncha tries to trap him; Agastya curses the mountain to stay until Murugan's spear destroys it | Asura Kandam 1219-1221 (see `m-krouncha`) |
| **Tradition (the songs):** Murugan taught Agastya, who lives on Pothigai in the south, the essence of Tamil, and Agastya gave Tamil to the world | Thiruppugazh songs only |

**Honesty note:** a keyword search of all six kandams of the Kanda Puranam found **no** passage in which Murugan teaches Agastya or gives him the Tamil language. Scenes 1 to 4 follow the Puranam; scenes 5 and 6 are the tradition of the songs and the Tamil country, and the film says so in its narration.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals of every scene; the narration.

## Characters

- **Agastya:** a short, radiant sage, grey beard, a water pot, a quiet smile. The father of Tamil in the tradition.
- **Shiva:** calm and kind.
- **Murugan** (tradition scenes): young, gentle, as teacher.
- **The people of the world** (final scene): farmers, poets, children.

## Places

Kailasa; the Podiya (Pothigai) hill in the south; green Tamil country.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; a little light humour in scenes 2 and 4, reverent at the end. Scenes 1 to 4 follow the Kanda Puranam; scenes 5 and 6 are the tradition of the songs, and the narration says so.

### 1. The tilted world (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa at the great gathering: gods, sages, kings and heavenly beings crowd every slope. The earth beneath groans and slowly tips toward the north; rivers slide, mountains lean. Shiva, calm, looks across the crowd to the very back, where the smallest sage of all stands with his water pot.
**Narration:** "Once, at a great gathering on Kailasa, so many beings came that the whole earth tipped toward the north. Shiva looked over the crowd, past the gods and the kings, to the smallest sage of all. His name was Agastya."
**Sound:** a deep groan of the earth; a single calm tone for Shiva.

### 2. The question (25 sec)
**Visual:** Agastya comes forward and bows, uneasy, his small face troubled. Shiva speaks gently and points to the south.
**Narration:** "Shiva told him to go south, to the Podiya hill. Agastya, the Tamil sage, was afraid he had done something wrong, to be sent so far from Shiva's feet."
**Dialogue:** Agastya: "Lord, why must I go so far from you? And how am I to go?"
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Go to the south. When you are there, the world will come level again."
**Sound:** a soft, questioning flute; a warm chord under Shiva's answer.

### 3. The road south (20 sec)
**Visual:** The little sage walks south with his staff and water pot. Behind him the land settles: hills sink back, rivers run straight, the horizon comes level.
**Narration:** "So Agastya set out for the south. And as he walked, step by step, the tilted world came level behind him."
**Sound:** a steady walking rhythm; the earth settling with a long sigh.

### 4. The mountain trap (25 sec)
**Visual:** A mountain rises across his road where none stood; the road runs into it. Inside, the passage twists and vanishes; fire and darkness close in. Agastya laughs, strikes the rock with his staff until it is full of holes, and speaks his curse.
**Narration:** "On the way a demon named Krouncha turned himself into a mountain to trap him. But the sage saw through the trick, laughed, and struck it with his staff. He cursed it to remain a mountain until the day Murugan's spear should pierce it."
**Sound:** rumble, crackling fire, then a bright laugh and the knock of the staff.

### 5. On Pothigai (30 sec)
**Visual:** The southern hill, green and misty, waterfalls and sandalwood trees. Agastya sits under a tree. A young figure of light comes up the path: Murugan, gentle, with his spear and three lines of ash on his brow. He sits beside the sage, and they speak softly; light gathers around their words like small birds.
**Narration:** "Here the old book falls silent. But the songs of the Thiruppugazh tell what the Puranam does not. They say that on the southern hill Murugan himself came to the sage, and taught him the essence of Tamil, the true knowledge that frees the soul. The teacher of all the Tamil land had a teacher too."
**Sound:** birdsong, water, a gentle veena.

### 6. Tamil to the world (25 sec)
**Visual:** Agastya walks down the hill. The light of the words flows out from him across the green Tamil country: into the mouths of children reciting, poets writing on palm leaves, farmers singing in the fields, singers in a temple hall.
**Narration:** "And so, the tradition says, the sage came down from the hill and gave that language to the world. In every village, on every tongue, the gift of the young teacher went on."
**Sound:** many voices joining softly, a rising melody.

### 7. The song (10 sec)
**Visual:** A child in a temple courtyard sings, in the same light, a line of the Thiruppugazh. The camera rises to the hills.
**Narration:** "Centuries later, Arunagiri sang of it as if everyone knew it, and asked for that same knowledge for himself."
**Sound:** the child's voice, then one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Seven songs refer to Murugan teaching Agastya: 598 (Thiruchengodu), 627 (Kundrakudi), 878 (Thiruchakrappalli), 915 (Vayalur), 954 (Thanichayam), 1081 and 1152 (general songs). In 598 and 1152 Arunagiri asks for the same knowledge that Murugan taught the sage. The Kanda Puranam itself does not tell this.

## Production notes

- **Keep the line between text and tradition clear** in the narration, as scene 5 does.
- **Agastya is small.** The theme is that the small sage carries the great gift.
- **Pair with `m-krouncha`,** which tells the mountain scene in full.
