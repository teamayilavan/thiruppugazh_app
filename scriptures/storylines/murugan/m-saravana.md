# Born in the Reed Lake (the Saravana birth)

`m-saravana` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: awe, then tenderness, then joy | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

From sparks of fire that fell from Shiva's eyes, carried through fire and river to a lotus lake, a child is born who becomes six children, and who becomes one again in his mother's arms.

**Added for the screen:** the colours and motion of the sparks, how each god looks, and the wording of the narration. The only spoken line (Shiva's instruction) is a plain rendering of verses 917 and 923, not a quotation. The verses give the events; the camera is ours.

## Characters

- **Shiva:** calm, white ash, matted hair with the moon; shown with his six faces turned toward the sparks.
- **Agni:** the fire-god, flame-red, who burns and sweats under the weight.
- **Ganga:** a river-goddess, silver and weary.
- **The six Krittika women:** gentle, star-like, in pale blue and white.
- **The child:** one baby on a lotus; then six babies; then one child with six faces and twelve arms.
- **Parvati:** the Goddess, loving and radiant.

## Places

Kailasa (fire and light); the roaring Ganga; the Saravana reed lake on the Himalayan slopes, with lotuses and tall reeds.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. Sparks of fire (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa at dusk. Shiva's six faces turn outward. From the eyes in his foreheads, sparks of fire pour out and scatter across the sky like falling stars, filling heaven and earth.
**Narration:** "On Mount Kailasa, Shiva let fall sparks of fire from the eyes in the foreheads of his six faces. They scattered like falling stars, and they spread across the whole of heaven and earth."
**Sound:** a low drone, then a rush of bright chimes.

### 2. The gods are asked to carry them (20 sec)
**Visual:** The gods stand before Shiva, bowing. He lifts a hand toward a swollen river far below. They bow and agree; Agni steps forward and gathers the sparks.
**Narration:** "Shiva told the gods to take the sparks and let them go into the swollen waters of the Ganga. The gods bowed, and agreed. And Agni, the god of fire, was the first to carry them."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Take these sparks, and let them go into the waters of the Ganga."
**Sound:** soft, solemn; the sparks hum.

### 3. Agni burns (20 sec)
**Visual:** Agni walks, the sparks heavy in his arms. His flames flicker; sweat runs down him; he stumbles. He cannot take another step. He throws the sparks into the roaring river.
**Narration:** "But the heat was too great, even for the god of fire. He sweated, and he burned, and he could not take another step. At last he flung the sparks into the roaring Ganga."
**Sound:** a hiss as the sparks meet the water.

### 4. The weary river (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Ganga takes the fire in her hands, grows weary, and flows with it down to a quiet place: the Saravana lake among the reeds on the slopes of the Himalaya. The sparks settle on the water.
**Narration:** "The Ganga, too, grew weary. She carried the fire as far as she could, until she set it down in the Saravana, the lake of reeds on the slopes of the Himalaya, where the lotus blooms."
**Sound:** the river slows; reeds whisper; a soft flute.

### 5. The child in the lotus (20 sec)
**Visual:** The reeds hush. On the still water a lotus opens. In its heart lies a baby, glowing like a spark of the fire that made him. He opens his eyes.
**Narration:** "The reeds were still. The water was still. Then a lotus opened, and in its heart a child took form, shining like the spark that made him. And he opened his eyes."
**Sound:** the first quiet in the film; one sustained note.

### 6. Six mothers (20 sec)
**Visual:** Six women of the Krittika come down to the lake. As they bend over the lotus, the six-faced child becomes six children, each one playing. The women gasp, afraid, then kneel and worship.
**Narration:** "Six women of the Krittika came down to the lake. As they bent over him, the six-faced child became six children, each one at play. They were filled with wonder, and with fear. And they bowed down to worship him as a god."
**Sound:** soft laughter of babies; the music warms.

### 7. The mother's arms (30 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva and Parvati arrive at the lake. Parvati sees the six children. She takes them up and holds them to her, and as she embraces them the six become one: a single child, six faces, twelve arms, radiant. The women and Shiva look on.
**Narration:** "Then Shiva and Parvati came to the lake, and Parvati saw the six children. She took them up in her arms and held them close. And as she embraced them, the six became one: one child, with six faces and twelve arms. She had found her son."
**Sound:** quietest music of the film; a long, held chord when the six become one.

### 8. The name (15 sec)
**Visual:** Close on the child on his mother's lap. The six faces smile. The reeds and the lake behind him glow with light.
**Narration:** "He was named Kandan, and he was Parvati's son. In the songs he is still called the child of the reed lake, the child of the six mothers, the one with six faces and twelve arms."
**Sound:** the opening theme, softly; a bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri sings of this birth in over twenty songs: the child of the reed lake, the child of the six mothers, the six-faced, twelve-armed one. Songs to show: 145 and 191 (Palani), 372, 394 and 415 (Thiruvannamalai), 533 (Vallimalai). Each name in the songs is a scene in this film.

## Production notes

- **Dignity.** The six faces and twelve arms are sacred iconography: draw them cleanly and symmetrically, never comically.
- **The sparks are light, not flame.** Nothing burns on screen except Agni's strain.
- **The six becoming one is the heart of the film.** Give it the most time and the quietest music.
