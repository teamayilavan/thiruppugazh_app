# Born in the Reed Lake (the Saravana birth)

`m-saravana` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: awe, then tenderness, then joy

## One-line story

From sparks of fire that fell from Shiva's eyes, carried through fire and river to a lotus lake, a child is born who becomes six children, and who becomes one again in his mother's arms.

**Added for the screen:** the colours and motion of the sparks, how each god looks, and the wording of the narration. The verses give the events; the camera is ours.

## Characters

- **Shiva:** calm, white-ash and matted hair, with the moon; shown here with his six faces turned toward the sparks.
- **Agni:** fire-god, flame-red, who burns and sweats under the weight.
- **Ganga:** a river-goddess, silver and weary.
- **The six Krittika women:** gentle, star-like, in pale blue and white.
- **The child:** one baby on a lotus; then six babies; then one child with six faces and twelve arms.
- **Parvati:** the Goddess, loving and radiant.

## Places

Kailasa (fire and light); the roaring Ganga; the Saravana reed lake on the Himalayan slopes, with lotuses and tall reeds.

## The storyline

### 1. Sparks of fire (20 sec)
Kailasa at dusk. Shiva's six faces turn outward. From the eyes in his foreheads, sparks of fire pour out and scatter across the sky like falling stars. They fill the heavens and the earth.
**Narration:** "On Mount Kailasa, Shiva let fall sparks of fire from the eyes of his six faces, and they spread across heaven and earth."

### 2. The gods are asked to carry them (20 sec)
The gods stand before Shiva, bowing. Shiva tells them to take the sparks and let them go into the swollen waters of the Ganga. They agree.
**Narration:** "He told the gods to carry the sparks to the Ganga. They bowed, and agreed."

### 3. Agni burns (20 sec)
Agni, god of fire, takes the sparks first. The heat overwhelms even him: he sweats, he burns, he cannot take another step. He flings the sparks into the roaring river.
**Narration:** "Even Agni, the god of fire, could not carry them. They were too bright, too hot. He threw them into the Ganga."

### 4. The weary river (20 sec)
The Ganga takes the fire, grows weary, and flows with it down to a quiet place: the Saravana lake among the reeds on the slopes of the Himalaya. The sparks settle on the water.
**Narration:** "The Ganga carried the fire as far as she could, and set it down in the lake of reeds."

### 5. The child in the lotus (20 sec)
On the still water a lotus opens. In its heart lies a baby, glowing. The reeds sway. The child opens his eyes.
**Narration:** "And in the heart of a lotus, a child was born."
*Mood:* the music softens, the first quiet in the film.

### 6. Six mothers (20 sec)
Six women of the Krittika come down to the lake. As they bend over the lotus, the six-faced child becomes six children, playing. The women are amazed and a little afraid; then, seeing the play of the babies, they kneel and worship.
**Narration:** "Six women of the Krittika found him. He became six children, one for each of them. They had come to care for babies, and they stayed to worship a god."

### 7. The mother's arms (30 sec)
Shiva and Parvati arrive at the lake. Parvati sees the six children. She takes them up and holds them to her, and as she embraces them the six become one: a single child, six faces, twelve arms, radiant. The six women and Shiva look on.
**Narration:** "When Parvati held them, the six became one, with six faces and twelve arms. She had found her son."

### 8. The name (15 sec)
Close on the child on his mother's lap. The six faces smile. The reeds and the lake behind him glow with light.
**Narration:** "He was called Kandan, and he was Parvati's son."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri sings of this birth in over twenty songs: the child of the reed lake, the child of the six mothers, the six-faced, twelve-armed one. Songs to show: 145 and 191 (Palani), 372, 394 and 415 (Thiruvannamalai), 533 (Vallimalai). Each name in the songs is a scene in this film.

## Production notes

- **Dignity.** The six faces and twelve arms are sacred iconography: draw them cleanly and symmetrically, never comically.
- **The sparks are light, not flame.** Nothing burns on screen except Agni's strain.
- **The six becoming one is the heart of the film.** Give it the most time and the quietest music.
