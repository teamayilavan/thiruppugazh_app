# The Prince in the Prison (Jayanta Freed)

`m-jayanta` | Murugan | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: sorrow, hope, release

## One-line story

Indra's young son is held in the demon king's prison with the other gods; when the demon falls, Murugan sends his captain to set them free and give heaven back to Indra.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Jayantan, Indra's son, is shut in Surapadman's prison at Mahendrapuri with the other devas | Kanda Puranam, Mahendra Kandam 337 |
| Three padalams are named for him: his lament in the prison, his dream, and Veerabahu's words of comfort | Mahendra Kandam, padalams 9-11 |
| Veerabahu visits the prison | 450 |
| After the war, Murugan, on his peacock, tells Veerabahu to go to the fallen king's city and bring Jayantan and all the devas out of prison | Yuddha Kandam 2935-2943 |

| Order is restored: Indra rules the golden city again; a messenger tells Indra's queen on Meru that Surapadman has fallen and the devas are free | Deva Kandam 36, 41-43 |

**Added for the screen:** the content of Jayantan's dream (the text names the padalam, not what he dreamed), the look of the prison, and the narration. Where the song's commentary says Murugan freed him, the Puranam has Veerabahu do it at Murugan's command; the film follows the Puranam.

## Characters

- **Jayantan:** a young prince of the devas, fine-featured, thin from captivity, in torn silk.
- **The devas and goddesses:** in the prison, with tired eyes.
- **Veerabahu:** Murugan's chief captain, strong and kind.
- **Murugan:** calm on his peacock.
- **Indra and his queen** (end).

## Places

The prison of Mahendrapuri, the demon's city; the battlefield; Mount Meru; the golden city of heaven.

## The storyline

### 1. The prison (25 sec)
Mahendrapuri, a dark stone hall. The devas sit in chains, their silks dull. Among them, a young prince: Jayantan. He looks up at a high window and sings a low lament.
**Narration:** "In the city of the demon king, the gods were kept in prison. Among them was a young prince, the son of Indra."

### 2. A messenger and a dream (25 sec)
Veerabahu, Murugan's captain, enters the prison and speaks to Jayantan with comfort. That night, in sleep, Jayantan dreams: a light, then a spear.
**Narration:** "Murugan's captain came to him with words of comfort. And that night, the prince dreamed of hope."

### 3. The war (20 sec)
Quick cuts of the great war, banners and light; at the end the demon king Surapadman is split and fallen.
**Narration:** "The war was long. But it ended, and the demon king fell."

### 4. The command (20 sec)
Murugan, on his peacock, turns to Veerabahu: "Go to the city of the fallen king. Bring Jayantan and all the gods out of prison."
**Narration:** "Murugan sent Veerabahu to bring the prisoners out."

### 5. The door opens (20 sec)
Veerabahu strides into the quiet city, reaches the prison, and opens the doors. Light streams in on the faces of the gods.
**Narration:** "Veerabahu went into the city, and opened the prison."

### 6. The walk (25 sec)
Jayantan and the devas come out, blinking, and cross the battlefield together, step by step, past the fallen banners, to where Murugan waits. They bow at his feet.
**Narration:** "Jayantan and the gods walked across the field of battle to Murugan's feet."

### 7. Heaven again (20 sec)
A messenger flies to Mount Meru, to Indra's queen: "The demon is dead. The gods are free." Her face brightens. In the sky, the golden city shines; Indra takes his throne.
**Narration:** "A messenger took the news to Indra's queen. Heaven was Indra's again."

### 8. The prince's smile (10 sec)
Jayantan at his father's side, bright-eyed, in fresh silks.
**Narration:** "And the prince was free."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 582 (Viralimalai) says Murugan protected Indra's son, freed him from the prison, and gave him heaven to rule. This is the only song assigned to this story.

## Production notes

- **A gentle story.** Give the prison dignity and the release warmth: no violence is shown beyond a single cut of the war.
- **Credit Veerabahu** as the one who opens the door, as the Puranam does.
- **Open question for the owner:** whether to name Jayantan's dream in a later pass, after reading padalam 10 line by line.
