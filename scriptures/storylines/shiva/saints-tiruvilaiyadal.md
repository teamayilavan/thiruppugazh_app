# The Sports of the Lord at Madurai (Tiruvilaiyadal)

`saints-tiruvilaiyadal` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: playful, wondrous, affectionate | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Shiva as Sokkanathar, consort of Meenakshi, works sixty-four wonders at Madurai: turning jackals into horses, carrying earth for the Vaigai bank and being struck by the king's cane, and arbitrating the poet Nakkeerar's dispute.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, playful and affectionate, like a grandparent telling favourite tales. About two words a second.

### 1. The city of the Lord (20 sec)
**Visual:** Madurai at dawn: tall temple towers, the river Vaigai, pilgrims crowding the streets. Inside the temple, Sokkanathar, Shiva as the handsome Lord of Madurai, sits beside Meenakshi, and smiles.
**Narration:** "Madurai is the city of Sokkanathar, the beautiful Lord, and of Meenakshi, his queen. The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam tells of the sixty-four sacred sports he played there. Sports, because the Lord did them as lightly as a game, out of love."
**Sound:** temple bells; a playful flute.

### 2. The jackals (25 sec)
**Visual:** A dark forest at night. A pack of jackals gathers. The Lord, dressed as a horse trader, waves his hand: the jackals shimmer and become fine horses. His attendants become grooms, and he leads the herd into Madurai.
**Narration:** "For his devotee Manikkavasagar, who had spent the king's gold on the temple, the Lord turned the jackals of the forest into horses. His own attendants became the grooms, and he himself rode at their head into Madurai."
**Sound:** yelps that turn into neighing; galloping.

### 3. Jackals again (20 sec)
**Visual:** Night in the royal stable. The horses shake, shrink, and turn back into jackals, who howl, slip out and run through the sleeping town, overturning pots.
**Narration:** "But that night, the horses turned back into jackals. They howled in the stable, and ran wild through the town. All Madurai woke up in wonder."
**Sound:** howling; clattering pots; a comic drum roll.

### 4. Earth for a sweet (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Vaigai in flood. Every household must carry earth to build up the bank. An old woman, Vandi, who sells steamed rice cakes, has no one to work for her. A young labourer with a basket appears and offers to help, for a few of her cakes.
**Narration:** "Once the Vaigai flooded, and every home had to carry earth to build up the river bank. Vandi, a poor old woman who sold rice cakes, had no one to work her share. So the Lord came as a labourer, and carried earth for her, for his wages in cakes."
**Sound:** rushing water; a light, cheerful work song.

### 5. The cane (20 sec)
**Visual:** The labourer eats cakes, sings, dances, and naps; Vandi's share of the bank is left unfinished. The king, inspecting, strikes him on the back with his cane. The blow ripples outward: the king himself, the courtiers, the animals all flinch at once.
**Narration:** "But the labourer ate, and played, and slept, and the work was not done. The king struck him with his cane. And tradition tells that every living being felt that blow, the king too."
**Sound:** a single sharp stroke; a ripple of sound; silence.

### 6. The poem (20 sec)
**Visual:** The hall of the Tamil Sangam, poets seated. A poor Brahmin, Dharumi, reads out a short poem the Lord gave him, asking whether a woman's hair has its own fragrance. The fierce poet Nakkeerar rises and points out a fault.
**Narration:** "The king had asked a question about the fragrance of a woman's hair. The Lord gave a poor priest, Dharumi, a poem to answer it. But the poet Nakkeerar stood up and said the poem was wrong."
**Sound:** murmurs in the hall; a lute stops.

### 7. The eye opens (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Lord himself appears in the hall, and the eye on his forehead opens with a flash of light. Nakkeerar is shaken, but does not bow. Then a flash; he is struck down. Later the Lord, smiling, lifts him up and brings him back into the Sangam.
**Narration:** "The Lord came himself, and opened his forehead eye. Even then, the story goes, Nakkeerar held to his word. He was struck down by the fire. But the Lord forgave him, brought him back to the Sangam, and taught him."
**Sound:** a crackle of fire; then a warm, forgiving chord.

### 8. The sixty-four (15 sec)
**Visual:** Sixty-four small lamps light up, one by one, around the temple tank of Madurai. Sokkanathar and Meenakshi smile.
**Narration:** "There are sixty-four such sports. And Arunagiri remembers them, singing of Murugan as the son of the Lord who played in Madurai."
**Sound:** bells; a gentle closing melody.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the sports of the Lord at Madurai. Songs to show: 409 (Thiruvarunai) and 1249 (jackals into horses), 756 (Thirukkoodalaiyatrur: earth for the Vaigai), 957 and 960 (Madurai), 1192 (Nakkeerar).
