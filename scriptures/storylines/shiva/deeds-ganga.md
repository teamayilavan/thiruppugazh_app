# Ganga in the Matted Locks (Bhagiratha)

`deeds-ganga` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: determined, grand, then gentle | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The king Bhagiratha prays for the Ganga to come down to free his ancestors; her fall would break the earth, so Shiva catches her in his matted locks and lets her flow out gently.

## What the story rests on

|---|---|

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Bhagiratha:** a young king, devoted, thin from penance.
- **The Ganga:** a river-goddess, bright, impetuous.
- **Shiva:** calm, with matted locks like a great forest.
- **The ancestors:** ashes of sixty thousand sons of Sagara (shown as a gentle mist).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; determined in the penance, grand at the fall, gentle and light at the end.

### 1. The ashes (20 sec)
**Visual:** A deep, dim place beneath the earth: low mounds of grey ash, glowing faintly, with soft, restless lights hovering above them.
**Narration:** "Long ago, the sixty thousand sons of King Sagara were burned to ash by the anger of a sage. Their spirits could find no rest. Only the holy river Ganga, flowing over their ashes, could set them free."
**Sound:** a low, hollow wind.

### 2. The penance (25 sec)
**Visual:** King Bhagiratha, young and resolute, lays down his crown and walks into the mountains. He prays among the snows through the seasons. Brahma appears on his lotus and blesses him, then lifts a warning hand.
**Narration:** "Their descendant, King Bhagiratha, left his kingdom to bring Ganga down from heaven. He prayed for many years. At last Brahma granted his wish, but he warned him: the earth cannot bear her fall. Only Shiva can."
**Sound:** a steady, patient drone; a bell for Brahma.

### 3. The danger (20 sec)
**Visual:** High in the heavens, Ganga: a vast, shining river-goddess, her waters coiling like clouds, ready to leap. Far below, the small round earth.
**Narration:** "Ganga was mighty, and she was proud. If she fell from heaven with all her force, she would break the earth apart and wash the worlds away."
**Sound:** the distant roar of water.

### 4. Shiva agrees (20 sec)
**Visual:** Bhagiratha stands on the tip of one toe, arms raised, while a year of seasons passes over him. Shiva appears, calm, and nods.
**Narration:** "So Bhagiratha prayed again, now to Shiva. For a whole year he stood on the tip of one toe, with his arms raised. Shiva was pleased with him."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "I will bear her on my head."
**Sound:** a warm chord.

### 5. The fall (25 sec)
**Visual:** Ganga leaps from the sky in a white torrent. Shiva stands on the mountain, perfectly still, his matted hair spread wide. The whole river pours straight into his locks and vanishes.
**Narration:** "Ganga leapt down from the sky with a roar. The Ramayana says she thought, in her pride, to sweep the Lord himself down into the underworld. But Shiva stood calm and still, and she fell straight into his matted hair."
**Sound:** a thundering rush; then sudden quiet.

### 6. The maze (20 sec)
**Visual:** Inside the coils of his hair, a small shining Ganga wanders like a stream through a forest of dark curls, beside the crescent moon, turning this way and that, finding no way out. A faint smile on Shiva's face.
**Narration:** "And there she was lost. In those coils of hair she wandered round and round, for years, and could not find the way out. The Tamil hymns say he hid her in his hair, beside the crescent moon."
**Sound:** a gentle, circling melody.

### 7. The release (20 sec)
**Visual:** Bhagiratha bows once more. Shiva loosens a single lock; a thin, clear stream slips out into a lake below and parts into seven gentle streams.
**Narration:** "Bhagiratha prayed once more. Then Shiva gently loosed a single lock, and let Ganga flow out softly into the Bindu lake, in seven gentle streams."
**Sound:** a soft trickle, then flowing water.

### 8. The freeing (15 sec)
**Visual:** One stream follows Bhagiratha's chariot across the land and down to the grey ashes. As the water touches them, the restless lights rise, bright and calm, into the sky.
**Narration:** "One stream followed Bhagiratha's chariot all the way to the ashes. As her water touched them, his ancestors rose to heaven, free at last."
**Sound:** the water's song; a rising, peaceful chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri often names Shiva as the Lord who holds the Ganga and the moon in his matted hair. Songs to show: 32, 52 and 77 (Thiruchendur), 199 (Palani), 519 (Kailasa), 652 (Kasi).

## Production notes

- **Play it for wonder.** The tangle in the locks is gentle humour.
- **The ashes are symbolic** and must be shown respectfully.
