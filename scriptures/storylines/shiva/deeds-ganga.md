# Ganga in the Matted Locks (Bhagiratha)

`deeds-ganga` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: determined, grand, then gentle

## One-line story

The king Bhagiratha prays for the Ganga to come down to free his ancestors; her fall would break the earth, so Shiva catches her in his matted locks and lets her flow out gently.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Bhagiratha:** a young king, devoted, thin from penance.
- **The Ganga:** a river-goddess, bright, impetuous.
- **Shiva:** calm, with matted locks like a great forest.
- **The ancestors:** ashes of sixty thousand sons of Sagara (shown as a gentle mist).

## The storyline

### 1. The ashes (20 sec)
A barren land; heaps of ash. A king stands over them in grief.
**Narration:** "Bhagiratha's ancestors lay in ashes, and could not be freed."

### 2. The penance (25 sec)
He does long penance, on one foot, through winters and summers.
**Narration:** "He prayed for the river Ganga to come down from heaven."

### 3. The danger (20 sec)
In heaven, Ganga prepares to leap: if she falls, the earth will break.
**Narration:** "But if she fell, the earth would break."

### 4. Shiva agrees (20 sec)
Bhagiratha prays to Shiva, who nods: "I will bear her."
**Narration:** "So he prayed to Shiva, who said: 'I will bear her.'"

### 5. The fall (25 sec)
Ganga plunges in a roaring cascade onto Shiva's head, lost in his matted locks.
**Narration:** "She fell, and was lost in his hair."

### 6. The maze (20 sec)
She wanders for years among the locks, unable to find the way out.
**Narration:** "She wandered in his matted locks."

### 7. The release (20 sec)
At last Shiva lets her go: a gentle stream at a lake.
**Narration:** "And he let her flow out gently."

### 8. The freeing (15 sec)
Ganga follows Bhagiratha's chariot to his ancestors' ashes, and they are freed.
**Narration:** "And the ancestors were free."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

29 songs recall Ganga in Shiva's locks, including 32, 52, 77 and 199.

## Production notes

- **Play it for wonder.** The tangle in the locks is gentle humour.
- **The ashes are symbolic** and must be shown respectfully.
