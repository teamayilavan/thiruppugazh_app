# The Boy Who Held the Lingam (Markandeya and Yama)

`deeds-yama` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tender, then terrifying, then triumphant | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The boy sage Markandeya, fated to die at sixteen, clings to the Shiva linga; Yama's noose falls around the linga as well, and Shiva comes out of it, kicks Yama down, and gives the boy lasting youth.

## What the story rests on

**Honesty note:** the Tevaram hymns give the worship and the kick, and place it at Kadavur (Thirukkadaiyur), which the last scene names as the place of Appar's hymn. The age of sixteen, the noose round the linga and the lasting youth are the common telling; the narration marks them as "the story tells" and "tradition tells".
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Markandeya:** a boy of sixteen, devout, bright-eyed.
- **His parents:** gentle and anxious.
- **Yama:** the god of death, dark and dignified, on a buffalo.
- **Shiva:** bursting from the linga.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice; tender at the start, hushed when Yama comes, bright at the end. About two words a second.

### 1. A short life (20 sec)
**Visual:** A small hermitage among green fields. A bright-eyed boy, Markandeya, runs and laughs. His parents watch from the doorway; the mother wipes her eyes, the father lays a hand on her shoulder.
**Narration:** "The story is told of a boy named Markandeya. He was a joy to his parents. But they carried a sorrow they could not speak of. Their son, they had been told, would live only until he was sixteen."
**Sound:** a gentle flute; birdsong; one low, sad note under it.

### 2. The worship (25 sec)
**Visual:** Seasons pass over the hermitage. The boy grows. Each morning he bathes a small Shiva linga with water, milk and curd, lays flowers on it, and sits with folded hands, lips moving.
**Narration:** "So the boy gave his days to Shiva. The Tamil hymns remember him well. Appar sings that he worshipped with milk, curd and ghee, with water and flowers, and that he chanted the five holy letters, Namasivaya, again and again."
**Sound:** water poured; a soft bell; a whispered chant as rhythm, not words.

### 3. The appointed day (20 sec)
**Visual:** Dusk on the sixteenth year. The sky darkens. A great cloud gathers over the fields, and out of it steps a dark buffalo, and on it Yama, the lord of death, tall and grave, holding a noose.
**Narration:** "Then the appointed day came. The light grew strange. Out of a dark cloud came Yama, the lord of death, on his buffalo. He was not cruel. He had come to do his duty, as he does for all."
**Sound:** the music falls away; slow hoofbeats; a deep drum.

### 4. The noose (25 sec)
**Visual:** The boy sees Yama. He does not run. He throws his arms around the linga and holds it tight, eyes shut. Yama raises the noose and casts it. The loop of rope falls round the boy, and round the linga as well.
**Narration:** "The boy did not run. He held the linga with both arms and called on Shiva. Yama threw his noose. And the story tells that it fell around the boy, and around the linga too."
**Dialogue:** Markandeya: "Lord, I am yours. Do not let me go."
**Sound:** the whirr of the rope; a held breath of silence.

### 5. The Lord comes (20 sec)
**Visual:** The linga splits open with white light. Shiva steps out of it, matted hair flying, the crescent moon in it, eyes blazing. The whole hermitage is lit as if by noon.
**Narration:** "The noose had touched the Lord himself. The linga broke open with light, and Shiva came out of it, blazing, for the sake of the boy who held him."
**Sound:** a crack of light; a rising chord.

### 6. The kick (20 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva lifts his left foot. A flash of white light: Yama falls back, his buffalo stumbling, the noose dropping loose. No blow is seen, only the flash and the fall.
**Narration:** "With his foot, Shiva struck Yama down. The singers of Shiva tell it again and again: the Lord who kicked Death for the sake of a child. So he is called Kalantaka, the ender of death."
**Sound:** one great drum stroke; then stillness.

### 7. The gift (20 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva lifts the trembling boy and places a hand on his head. The boy's face shines. The parents run in and fall at the Lord's feet. Behind them, Yama rises, bows, and goes.
**Narration:** "Then the Lord lifted the boy and blessed him. Tradition tells that he made him sixteen for ever, young in years, old in wisdom, never to fear death again."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "You are mine. Death will not touch you."
**Sound:** warm strings; the flute returns.

### 8. Kadavur (15 sec)
**Visual:** A temple tower at sunrise over green fields. Inside, a carved image of the Lord with his foot raised over a fallen figure, and a boy holding a linga.
**Narration:** "At Kadavur in the Tamil country, Appar sang of this deed. And Arunagiri, praising Murugan, calls him the son of the Lord who kicked Death away."
**Sound:** temple bells; a final calm chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls Shiva kicking Yama in nine songs. Songs to show: 214 (Swamimalai), 491 (Chidambaram), 617 (Theerthamalai), 1106, 1160 and 1176 (general songs), 1330 (Thiruppoovanam).

## Production notes

- **Yama is not evil.** He does his duty. The kick is a flash of light.
- **The boy's fear is real;** keep the noose symbolic.
