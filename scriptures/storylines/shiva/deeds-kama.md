# The God of Love Burned (Kama)

`deeds-kama` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tense, fiery, then compassionate | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The gods send Kama, the god of love, to break Shiva's meditation so that he will marry Parvati and father a commander for their army; Shiva's forehead eye opens and burns Kama to ash, and Rati's grief wins him a bodiless life.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Kama attacked Shiva and was burned by his eye; he became Ananga ("bodiless") | Valmiki Ramayana 1.23.10-14 |
| Kama's destruction by Shiva's third eye; later revival | Shiva Purana, Rudra-samhita 3 (Parvati-khanda) ch.19, ch.51 |
| Destruction of Kama by Shiva | Linga Purana 1.101 |
| Passing statement: Kama earlier burned by Rudra's anger | SB 10.55.1 |
| Songs 70, 77, 103, 124 and others (27 songs) recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration. The film is modest.

## Characters

- **Kama:** a graceful young god with a sugarcane bow and flower arrows.
- **Rati:** his wife, tender and devoted.
- **Shiva:** in deep meditation, still as a mountain.
- **Parvati:** nearby, serving.
- **The gods:** watching anxiously.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; tense as Kama goes, a sudden fire at the eye, then tender and compassionate at the end.

### 1. The gods' need (20 sec)
**Visual:** The gods gather in a darkened heaven, worried; the shadow of a great demon, Taraka, falls across their city.
**Narration:** "A demon named Taraka was troubling the gods, and only a son born to Shiva could defeat him. But Shiva sat alone, deep in meditation, and thought of no marriage at all."
**Sound:** a low, uneasy drone.

### 2. The meditation (25 sec)
**Visual:** A snowy Himalayan peak. Shiva sits motionless, eyes closed, snow resting on his shoulders. Each day Parvati, the mountain king's daughter, climbs up quietly with flowers and water, lays them before him, and goes.
**Narration:** "High in the Himalaya, Shiva sat as still as the mountain itself. Every day Parvati, the daughter of the mountain king, came quietly to serve him, bringing flowers and water. Day after day, he did not open his eyes."
**Sound:** wind over snow; a soft, patient flute.

### 3. Kama is sent (20 sec)
**Visual:** Indra calls Kama, the god of love: young, bright, with a bow of sugar cane strung with humming bees and five flower arrows. Beside him are his wife Rati and the spirit of Spring.
**Dialogue:** Indra: "Only you can wake him. Go, for the sake of all the worlds."
**Narration:** "So the gods called Kama, the god of love. With his bow of sugar cane and his five flower arrows, with his wife Rati and with Spring beside him, he went to the mountain."
**Sound:** a light, rising melody.

### 4. The arrow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Spring comes too early to the snowy peak: blossoms open, a cuckoo calls. Kama hides behind a flowering tree. As Parvati approaches with her flowers, he draws his bow and lets a flower arrow fly toward Shiva.
**Narration:** "Spring came early to the mountain. Flowers opened in the snow, and the cuckoo began to sing. Kama hid among the blossoms. And as Parvati drew near, he drew his bow, and let a flower arrow fly at the heart of the Lord."
**Sound:** birdsong; the soft twang of the bowstring.

### 5. The eye (25 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva's stillness is stirred. His eyes open; he turns and sees Kama among the flowers. Then the third eye in his forehead opens, and a beam of pure white flame leaps out.
**Narration:** "Shiva felt his meditation stirred. He opened his eyes, looked for the cause, and saw Kama. Then the eye in his forehead opened, and a flame leapt out of it."
**Sound:** sudden silence; then a single blazing chord.

### 6. The ash (20 sec)
**Visual:** Where Kama stood there is only a burst of light, then a small heap of white ash under the blossoms. His sugar-cane bow lies beside it. No body is shown.
**Narration:** "In a moment the god of love was gone. Only a little white ash lay on the ground. The Ramayana says that from that day Kama was called Ananga, the one without a body."
**Sound:** a soft hiss; falling petals.

### 7. Rati's grief (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rati kneels by the ash, weeping. Shiva looks down at her, his face softening. He raises his hand in blessing over her.
**Narration:** "Rati wept over the ash of her husband. Her grief moved the Lord. He promised her that Kama would live again, without a body, and still be felt in every heart."
**Sound:** a sorrowful melody that slowly warms.

### 8. The marriage (10 sec)
**Visual:** Parvati at her penance; then Shiva and Parvati together in a golden light; then the glow of a newborn child, Murugan.
**Narration:** "In time Shiva wed Parvati, and their son Murugan was born to save the gods."
**Sound:** wedding bells; a bright, rising theme.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri often names Shiva as the one who burned Kama with his forehead eye, the father of Murugan. Songs to show: 70 and 103 (Thiruchendur), 124 (Palani), 242 (Thiruthani), 577 (Viralimalai), 649 (Kathirkamam).

## Production notes

- **The burning is symbolic.** Show light, not flesh.
- **Rati's grief and the Lord's compassion** are the emotional end.
