# Daksha's Sacrifice

`deeds-daksha` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: grave, tragic, then merciful

## One-line story

Daksha insults Shiva and holds a sacrifice to which Shiva is not invited; his daughter Sati dies there, Virabhadra wrecks the sacrifice, and Daksha is later revived with a goat's head.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|

| Sati dies | SB 4.2-4.5 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration; the film is shown without any violence beyond a storm of wind and light.

## Characters

- **Daksha:** a proud king, with a crown.
- **Sati:** Daksha's daughter and Shiva's wife, gentle and dignified.
- **Shiva:** a calm figure, then angry.
- **Virabhadra:** a fierce form born of Shiva's anger.
- **Brahma and the gods.**

## The storyline

### 1. The great sacrifice (20 sec)
Daksha's sacrifice hall, packed with gods; every seat is full but one.
**Narration:** "Daksha held a great sacrifice."

### 2. The insult (20 sec)
He insults Shiva before the gods; no invitation has been sent.
**Narration:** "And he left Shiva out."

### 3. Sati comes (25 sec)
Sati arrives uninvited, to see her father; he speaks of Shiva with contempt.
**Narration:** "His daughter Sati heard him."

### 4. Sati's sorrow (20 sec)
She cannot bear it; she leaves her body.
**Narration:** "She could not bear it, and she died."

### 5. The anger (20 sec)
On Kailasa, Shiva's face darkens; from his locks springs a fierce form: Virabhadra.
**Narration:** "Shiva's anger took form as Virabhadra."

### 6. The storm (30 sec)
Virabhadra and his host sweep through the hall; the sacrifice is overturned.
**Narration:** "He wrecked the sacrifice."

### 7. The prayer (20 sec)
Brahma comes to Shiva with folded hands: "Forgive."
**Narration:** "Brahma begged Shiva to forgive."

### 8. The goat's head (10 sec)
Daksha rises, with a goat's head, and bows.
**Narration:** "And Daksha was given life, and a goat's head."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Songs 309, 458, 461 and 491.

## Production notes

- **Sati's death is shown as a light**, not as a body.
- **The goat's head is a symbol** of humility, not a joke.
