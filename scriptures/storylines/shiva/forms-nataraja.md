# The Dancer of the Golden Hall (Nataraja)

`forms-nataraja` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: rhythmic, ecstatic, cosmic | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Shiva dances: the Ananda Tandava in the golden hall at Chidambaram, the dance contest with Kali at Thiruvalangadu, the dance in the burning ground, with his foot on the dwarf Muyalakan, while Vishnu, Brahma and the serpent Adisesha watch.

**Added for the screen:** the choreography, the music and the narration.

## Characters

- **Nataraja:** a four-armed dancer with a drum, a flame, and a raised foot, ringed by fire.
- **Muyalakan:** a small dark dwarf underfoot.
- **Vishnu, Brahma, Adisesha:** onlookers.
- **Kali:** in the contest.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, riding on the rhythm of the drum; still at the start and the end, quick and joyful in the dance. About two words a second.

### 1. The golden hall (20 sec)
**Visual:** Night at Chidambaram, the old Thillai. Lamps flicker along a long stone corridor. At its end, a small hall with a roof of gold glows in the dark. Inside, all is still.
**Narration:** "In the Tamil country there is a town called Thillai, which we know as Chidambaram. In its temple stands a small hall with a roof of gold. The poets call it the hall of wisdom. There, they say, the Lord dances."
**Sound:** silence; one lamp crackling; a single drum tap far away.

### 2. The drum (20 sec)
**Visual:** A small hourglass drum in a hand begins to beat. In another hand, a flame wavers. Anklets shine. The figure is still half in shadow.
**Narration:** "First comes the sound of the drum. Then the flame. Then the ringing of the anklets. Arunagiri hears it too: the golden anklets ringing, the deer and the axe in his hands."
**Sound:** the drum beat builds; anklet bells join.

### 3. The dance of joy (30 sec)
**Visual:** Nataraja is revealed, four-armed, inside a ring of fire. One hand holds the drum, one the flame, one is raised to say "do not fear", one points down to the lifted left foot. His matted hair flies out, with the crescent moon and the Ganga in it. He whirls; the ring of fire turns with him.
**Narration:** "This is the Ananda Tandava, the dance of joy. Appar saw it and sang: the curved brow, the red lips, the gentle smile, the matted hair, the white ash on a body like coral, and the golden foot raised high. If one may see this, he said, even a human birth is worth having."
**Sound:** full rhythm; drums, cymbals, a soaring nagaswaram.

### 4. The one beneath the foot (20 sec)
**Visual:** The camera drops to the right foot, planted on a small dark dwarf who lies curled and restless. As the dance goes on, the dwarf grows quiet.
**Narration:** "Under his right foot lies a dwarf, Muyalakan. The hymns say the Lord keeps him there, quelling his rage. Tirumular says the dwarf is our own pride, the self that forgets. The dance holds it still."
**Sound:** the drum softens to a heartbeat.

### 5. The watchers (20 sec)
**Visual:** Around the hall, in soft light, stand Vishnu and Brahma, hands folded, gazing. Above them the great serpent Adisesha lowers his many hoods in wonder.
**Narration:** "Vishnu and Brahma stand and watch, and still they cannot find the end of his feet. The songs say the serpent Adisesha bowed his hoods as the Lord danced."
**Sound:** the rhythm continues under a warm choir hum.

### 6. The burning ground (20 sec)
**Visual:** The scene shifts: dusk over a cremation ground, smoke and embers. The Lord dances there among shadowy ghosts and goblins, who leap and clap. The Goddess watches, and smiles.
**Narration:** "He dances in the burning ground too, at dusk, among ghosts and goblins. The Linga Purana says he danced so to please the Goddess. Where all things end, he dances."
**Sound:** a wilder, darker rhythm; crackling fire.

### 7. The contest (20 sec)
**Visual:** A brief flash at Thiruvalangadu: Kali, fierce and many-armed, dances opposite him, step for step. Then he lifts one foot straight up to the sky. Kali stops, and bows.
**Narration:** "Tradition tells that at Thiruvalangadu, Kali challenged him to dance. Step for step she matched him, until he lifted his foot to the sky. Manikkavasagar says that if he did not dance, Kali's wild company would fill the world."
**Sound:** two rhythms clashing, then one.

### 8. The stillness (15 sec)
**Visual:** Back in the golden hall. The dance slows; the ring of fire glows quietly. A last drum beat. The image holds, still, as it stands in bronze in every Tamil temple.
**Narration:** "Then the drum falls silent. But the devotees say the dance never stops. Every heartbeat, every breath, is a step of that dance. And Arunagiri sings of Murugan as the son of the dancer of Thillai."
**Sound:** one last drum beat; a temple bell; silence.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

About forty-five songs praise the dancing Lord. Songs to show: 83 (Thiruchendur), 251 (Thiruthanigai), 472 and 479 (Chidambaram), 517 (Kayilaimalai), 583 (Viralimalai).

## Production notes

- **A film made of music.** Use an authentic rhythm.
- **Do not make the dwarf a villain.** He is the symbol of forgetfulness.
