# The Wandering Beggar (Bhikshatana)

`deeds-bhikshatana` | Shiva | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: strange, playful, humbling | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Carrying a skull-bowl, Shiva wanders naked through the Daruka forest asking alms, and the sages' wives are bewitched while their husbands' pride in their rites is broken.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The treatment is modest: the "naked" wanderer is shown in light, as ash and shadow, and the film makes no suggestion of a sensual scene.

## Characters

- **Shiva as Bhikshatana:** a dark, handsome, radiant wanderer with a skull-bowl, smeared in ash.
- **Vishnu as Mohini:** a graceful companion.
- **The sages of the Daruka forest:** proud and austere.
- **Their wives.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; a little mysterious at first, humbled and reverent at the end.

### 1. The forest of the proud (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Daruka forest at morning: neat huts, smoking fire altars, sages in a row pouring offerings with long ladles, chanting with chins raised. Their wives tend the huts.
**Narration:** "In the Daruka forest lived a company of sages. They kept their sacred fires and their rites with great care, and they were proud of it. They believed their rites were all they needed, and they had forgotten the Lord whom the rites were meant to serve."
**Sound:** steady chanting; the crackle of fire.

### 2. The beggar (25 sec)
**Visual:** A stranger walks in along the forest path: dark, beautiful, radiant, his body clothed in ash and soft light, a skull held as a begging bowl. Beside him walks a graceful woman, Mohini, in quiet colours.
**Narration:** "One day a stranger walked into the forest. He was dark and beautiful, covered in ash, and he carried a skull for a begging bowl. The Linga Purana says it was Shiva himself, come to test the sages. Tradition adds that Vishnu came with him, in the graceful form of Mohini."
**Sound:** a single flute, wandering.

### 3. The wives (20 sec)
**Visual:** He goes from hut to hut. The women come to their doors with rice, and stop, spellbound; a bangle slips from a wrist. They follow him a few steps, forgetting their work.
**Narration:** "He went from door to door asking for alms. The women of the forest came out with food, and stopped, and stared. They forgot their work and followed him. The Tamil hymns say he stole their hearts."
**Sound:** the flute grows warmer; a bangle chimes.

### 4. The sages' anger (20 sec)
**Visual:** The sages look up from their altars and see their wives following the beggar. Their faces harden. They gather around a great fire.
**Narration:** "When the sages saw this, they were furious. They did not know who he was."
**Dialogue:** A sage: "Who is this beggar who walks into our forest? Let our rites destroy him."
**Narration (continued):** "And they lit a great fire against him."
**Sound:** the chanting turns harsh; the fire roars.

### 5. The failure of the rites (25 sec)
**Visual:** From the fire leaps a tiger; the stranger touches it and it becomes a striped cloth at his waist. A serpent; it coils on his arm as a jewel. A deer; it rests in his hand. A dark dwarf; he sets one foot on it and begins to dance.
**Narration:** "Tamil tradition tells what came out of that fire. A tiger leapt at him, and he wore it as his garment. A serpent, and he wore it as a jewel. A deer, and he held it in his hand. A dwarf of darkness, and he set his foot on it and danced."
**Sound:** each creature a sharp beat; then a dancing drum.

### 6. The bowing (25 sec)
**Visual:** The fire sinks to embers. The sages stand frozen, then lower their ladles one by one and kneel.
**Narration:** "The sages saw that nothing they did could touch him. Their rites, their curses and their pride had all fallen at his feet. One by one they laid down their ladles, and knelt."
**Sound:** the drum stops; a soft, falling chord.

### 7. The Lord (20 sec)
**Visual:** The stranger stands in full light: Shiva, the crescent moon in his hair, the skull bowl still in his hand. The sages and their wives bow together.
**Narration:** "Then he showed them who he was. Rites alone had not found him. The beggar with the skull for a bowl was the Lord of all the worlds, and what he asks for is the heart."
**Sound:** temple bells; the flute returns, gentle.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers the Lord who went begging with Brahma's skull for a bowl. Songs to show: 509 (Chidambaram), 702 (Madambakkam), 1166 (general songs).

## Production notes

- **Treat with decorum.** The nudity in the sources is symbolic, shown here as ash and light.
- **Mohini appears only as a graceful companion,** no more.
