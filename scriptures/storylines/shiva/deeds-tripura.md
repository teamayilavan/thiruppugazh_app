# The Three Flying Cities (Tripura)

`deeds-tripura` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: grand, epic, one smile | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Three flying cities make three demons unconquerable; the gods build Shiva a chariot, with Meru as the bow, Vishnu as the arrow and Vasuki as the string, and when the cities align he destroys them with a single arrow.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **The three demon kings:** proud, noble, devoted to Shiva in their own way.
- **Maya:** the demon architect.
- **Shiva:** the warrior, with Meru for a bow.
- **Vishnu, Brahma, Vasuki, Ganesha.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; grand and epic through the building of the chariot, hushed in the waiting, and almost playful at the single smile.

### 1. The three cities (25 sec)
**Visual:** Three shining cities float through the sky on separate paths: one of gold high in heaven, one of silver in the middle air, one of iron near the earth. From their walls, dark shapes swoop down on the worlds below.
**Narration:** "Long ago, three demon lords, Tarakaksha, Kamalaksha and Vidyunmali, had three wonderful cities built by Maya, the master builder. One was of gold, one of silver, and one of iron, and all three could fly. From them the demons troubled heaven and earth."
**Sound:** a deep, circling hum for each city.

### 2. The gods' plea (20 sec)
**Visual:** The three cities wheel apart across the sky. The gods, weary from failed battles, climb to Kailasa and bow before Shiva.
**Narration:** "The cities could be destroyed only by one arrow, at the one moment when all three stood in a single line. No god could do it. So they came to Shiva and begged him for help."
**Dialogue:** The gods: "Lord, only you can end this."
**Sound:** a pleading choir.

### 3. The chariot (25 sec)
**Visual:** The whole of creation gathers into a chariot: the earth becomes its body, the sun and the moon its wheels, the gods its parts. Brahma takes the reins.
**Narration:** "So the gods built him a chariot from the whole of creation. The texts say the earth itself was its body, and the gods themselves became its parts. Brahma, the creator, took the reins and became the charioteer."
**Sound:** a building drumbeat, piece by piece.

### 4. The bow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Golden Mount Meru bends into a great bow. The serpent Vasuki stretches between its tips as the string. Vishnu becomes a shining arrow with fire at its tip, laid on the string.
**Narration:** "Mount Meru, the golden mountain, bent itself into a bow. The great serpent Vasuki became its string. And Vishnu himself became the arrow, with the god of fire at its tip."
**Sound:** the creak of a vast bow; a hiss as the string is drawn.

### 5. The axle (20 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva steps onto the chariot; the axle cracks and the chariot sinks. Small, smiling Ganesha appears beside a wheel. Shiva remembers him and blesses him; the chariot rises again.
**Narration:** "Tamil tradition adds a story here. As Shiva mounted, the chariot's axle snapped, because Ganesha, the remover of obstacles, had not first been honoured. Once he was remembered, the chariot rolled on. Arunagiri's very first song recalls it."
**Sound:** a crack; a light, playful tune for Ganesha.

### 6. The waiting (20 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva stands on the chariot, bow drawn, perfectly still, while the three cities wheel slowly through the sky toward one another.
**Narration:** "Shiva waited, with the great bow drawn, while the three cities wheeled through the sky. At last, for a single moment, all three came into one line."
**Sound:** a long, held note, rising.

### 7. The arrow (20 sec)
**Visual:** The cities align. Shiva smiles, and at that smile the three cities burst into light, empty of people, like three lamps flaring out. The arrow rests still on the string.
**Narration:** "The Tamil hymns say he did not even need the arrow. He smiled, and the three cities burst into flame. Others say a single arrow was enough. Either way, there was no second."
**Sound:** a bright, ringing chord; a rush of flame.

### 8. The ash (10 sec)
**Visual:** Light and fine ash drift down from the sky; the gods bow, and the sky clears.
**Narration:** "The cities fell as light and ash, and heaven and earth were at peace again."
**Sound:** temple bells.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the burning of the three cities in about sixty songs, beginning with his very first. Songs to show: 1 (the Vinayaka song), 11 (Thiruparankundram), 52 and 71 (Thiruchendur), 124 (Palani), 263 (Thiruthani).

## Production notes

- **No people shown burning.** The cities burn, empty, as a light.
- **The tradition about the axle** (song 1) should be said to be Tamil tradition.
