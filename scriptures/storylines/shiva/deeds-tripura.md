# The Three Flying Cities (Tripura)

`deeds-tripura` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: grand, epic, one smile

## One-line story

Three flying cities make three demons unconquerable; the gods build Shiva a chariot, with Meru as the bow, Vishnu as the arrow and Vasuki as the string, and when the cities align he destroys them with a single arrow.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **The three demon kings:** proud, noble, devoted to Shiva in their own way.
- **Maya:** the demon architect.
- **Shiva:** the warrior, with Meru for a bow.
- **Vishnu, Brahma, Vasuki, Ganesha.**

## The storyline

### 1. The three cities (25 sec)
Three vast cities of gold, silver and iron drift through the sky, unassailable.
**Narration:** "Three flying cities made three demons unconquerable."

### 2. The gods' plea (20 sec)
The gods come to Shiva: "Only you can end them."
**Narration:** "The gods begged Shiva."

### 3. The chariot (25 sec)
The earth becomes a chariot, the sun and moon its wheels, Brahma its charioteer.
**Narration:** "They built him a chariot of the world itself."

### 4. The bow (25 sec)
Meru is the bow, Vasuki the string, Vishnu the arrow.
**Narration:** "Meru was the bow, Vasuki the string, and Vishnu the arrow."

### 5. The axle (20 sec)
A small elephant-faced god, Ganesha, touches the axle, which snaps; the chariot lurches.
**Narration:** "The Tamil tradition tells that the axle broke."

### 6. The waiting (20 sec)
Shiva waits; the three cities slowly align.
**Narration:** "He waited until the three cities stood in one line."

### 7. The arrow (20 sec)
A single arrow flies, or a single smile; the cities burn in a long slow glow.
**Narration:** "One arrow, one smile, and the three cities burned."

### 8. The ash (10 sec)
Ash drifts; the gods bow.
**Narration:** "And the sky was clear."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

60 songs recall it, including 1, 11, 52 and 71.

## Production notes

- **No people shown burning.** The cities burn, empty, as a light.
- **The tradition about the axle** (song 1) should be said to be Tamil tradition.
