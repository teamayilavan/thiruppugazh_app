# The Calming of Narasimha (Sarabha)

`deeds-sarabha` | Shiva | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: mighty, anxious, resolved | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

After Narasimha kills the demon Hiranyakashipu, his rage does not cool; Shiva takes the fierce form of Sarabha, part lion and part bird, and calms him.

**Honesty note:** the story is told in later Puranas and Shaiva tradition, not in the Bhagavata; the film says so. It is also a story about two gods that some devotees hold sacred in different ways, so it is handled with restraint.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Narasimha:** the man-lion, golden, mane blazing.
- **Sarabha:** Shiva's form, part lion, part bird, with wings.
- **The gods:** frightened.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; mighty at the start, anxious as the worlds tremble, then soft and reverent. The story is about protection, never rivalry.

### 1. The rage (25 sec)
**Visual:** A broken pillar in a palace hall. Narasimha, half man and half lion, stands blazing with golden light, his mane like fire. The child Prahlada is safe at the edge of the frame. The demon king is gone; only a shadow fades on the floor.
**Narration:** "Long ago, Vishnu came as Narasimha, half man and half lion, to save the child Prahlada from his cruel father, the demon king Hiranyakashipu. The demon was destroyed. But the Shaiva Puranas tell that, even then, the lion's great fury did not cool."
**Sound:** a deep lion's roar fading into a long, burning hum.

### 2. The gods' fear (20 sec)
**Visual:** The roar rolls across the sky. Mountains tremble, seas rise. The gods gather in fear and turn toward the white mountain, Kailasa.
**Narration:** "His roar shook the worlds. Mountains trembled and the seas rose. The gods were afraid, and they went to Shiva, and asked him to bring back peace. Who else, they said, could calm such a power?"
**Sound:** rumbling; anxious voices.

### 3. The form (25 sec)
**Visual:** First a calm attendant, Virabhadra, approaches Narasimha with folded hands and gentle words; the lion does not listen. Then on Kailasa a great light gathers, and out of it rises Sarabha: part lion and part great bird, with vast wings of light that fill the sky.
**Narration:** "The Shiva Purana tells that Shiva first sent Virabhadra to speak gently to Narasimha. But the lion would not listen. Then Shiva himself took a wondrous form, Sarabha, part lion and part mighty bird, with wings that filled the sky."
**Sound:** a soft voice, then a great rush of wings.

### 4. The meeting (25 sec)
**Visual:** Sarabha descends on wings of light and folds them around Narasimha, holding him firm, the way a parent holds a child who cannot stop crying. Only light and wings are seen; there is no fight.
**Narration:** "Sarabha came down on wings of light, and folded them around Narasimha. He held the blazing lion firm, the way a parent holds a child who cannot stop crying, until the storm should pass. There was no battle, only a strong and patient embrace."
**Sound:** the roar and the wings together, then slowly softening.

### 5. The calming (25 sec)
**Visual:** Inside the wings, Narasimha's mane cools from flame to gold. His eyes soften. Outside, the mountains settle and the seas grow still.
**Narration:** "Slowly the fury went out of Narasimha. The fire in his eyes softened, and the light around him cooled. Far below, the mountains grew still, and the seas lay down again."
**Sound:** a long, settling chord; a gentle wind.

### 6. The prayer (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sarabha opens his wings. Narasimha, peaceful now, folds his hands and bows; Sarabha's form glows and becomes Shiva, calm and smiling.
**Narration:** "The Linga Purana says that Narasimha then praised Shiva with a hymn, and his peace returned. Two great powers stood together in quiet, and the worlds could breathe again."
**Sound:** a soft hymn; temple bells.

### 7. The calm (10 sec)
**Visual:** Prahlada, safe, bows; the sky clears to dawn.
**Narration:** "This story is told in the later Puranas and in Shaiva tradition, not in the Bhagavata. There, it is the child Prahlada whose prayer brings the peace."
**Sound:** a single bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls, in passing, the Lord who took the Sarabha form, in praise of the Goddess at his side. Songs to show: 424 (Tiruvannamalai), 448 (Thirukkalathi).
