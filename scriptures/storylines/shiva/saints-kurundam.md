# Under the Kurundam Tree (Manikkavasagar Meets His Guru)

`saints-kurundam` | Shiva | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: stirring, quiet, transformative | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The Pandya king's minister, sent to buy horses, meets Shiva as a guru under the kurundam tree at Tiruperunthurai, gives the money to the temple, and gives his life to Shiva.

**Honesty note:** this film is the inner story of the same events as `n-manikkavasagar` (which shows the horses and the foxes). It is the same story with a different focus, the transformation of the minister.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Manikkavasagar:** a young, learned minister, restless.
- **The guru:** Shiva, a teacher beneath the tree.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice; brisk in the court, slow and hushed under the tree. About two words a second.

### 1. The minister (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Pandya court at Madurai: pillars, banners with the fish emblem, courtiers bowing. At the king's right hand stands a young minister in fine silk, clever and respected. But when the court looks away, his face is restless, as if searching for something.
**Narration:** "In Madurai, the Pandya king had a young minister from the village of Thiruvadavur. He was learned and wise, and the king trusted him above all. He had wealth and honour. Yet in his heart there was a longing that nothing in the palace could fill."
**Sound:** court drums; a lute; under it, one searching flute note.

### 2. The mission (20 sec)
**Visual:** The king hands him chests of gold. A caravan sets out from the city gates, guards on horseback, toward the coast.
**Narration:** "One day the king gave him a great treasure of gold, and sent him to buy fine horses for the army. The minister set out with his guards toward the sea."
**Dialogue:** King: "Bring me the best horses in the land."
**Sound:** hoofbeats; a marching rhythm.

### 3. The sound of chanting (25 sec)
**Visual:** The caravan passes Thiruperunthurai, a village among green fields. The minister hears chanting and stops. Through a grove he sees a kurundam tree, and beneath it a teacher sitting calmly, with devotees around him.
**Narration:** "On the way he came to Thiruperunthurai. There he heard the sound of devotees singing. He followed it, into a grove, and under a kurundam tree he saw a teacher sitting in perfect peace."
**Sound:** the marching stops; distant chanting; birds.

### 4. The guru (25 sec)
**Visual:** The minister drops his reins, walks forward as if in a dream, and falls at the teacher's feet. Tears run down his face. The teacher looks at him with great kindness.
**Narration:** "The minister forgot the horses, the king and the gold. He fell at the teacher's feet and wept. He knew, without being told, that this was the one he had longed for all his life. The teacher was the Lord himself."
**Sound:** the flute note from scene one returns, now warm and whole.

### 5. The teaching (20 sec)
**Visual:** The guru places his hand on the minister's head. Light spreads through him. His fine silk seems to fade; he sits like a humble devotee.
**Narration:** "The guru touched his head and taught him. In that moment the minister was changed. He later sang of the Lord who sat under the kurundam tree, and of the grace he found there."
**Sound:** a long, quiet chord.

### 6. The gold given (25 sec)
**Visual:** The chests of gold are opened before a half-built temple. Masons work; lamps are lit; devotees are fed. The guards look at each other, worried.
**Narration:** "Then he did something no minister should do. He gave the king's gold to the temple and to the Lord's devotees. Every coin. He had found a treasure greater than horses."
**Sound:** chisels on stone; temple bells.

### 7. The guru is gone (20 sec)
**Visual:** He turns back to the tree. The seat beneath it is empty. He searches the grove, calling, then sits under the tree with folded hands, at peace even in his longing. A final image: the kurundam tree in the evening light.
**Narration:** "When he turned back, the teacher was gone. He would long for him all his life, and from that longing came the Tiruvasagam. The world knows him as Manikkavasagar, he whose words are gems."
**Sound:** a soft song melody; evening crickets.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the guru under the kurundam tree in song 844 (Thiruperunthurai), song 7 (Thiruparankundram) and song 1174 (general songs).

## Production notes

- **Pair with `n-manikkavasagar`.** The two films cover the same story from two sides.
