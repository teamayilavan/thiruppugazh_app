# The Teacher Who Spoke in Silence (Dakshinamurti)

`forms-dakshinamurti` | Shiva | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: still, deep, luminous | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Shiva sits facing south under a banyan tree and teaches the sages Sanaka and his brothers by silence alone.

## What the story rests on

**Honesty note:** the research gives the story in a single line; the film adds only what makes a silent teaching visible.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Dakshinamurti:** a young, radiant teacher, seated, with a hand in a gesture of knowledge.
- **The four sages (Sanaka and his brothers):** old in wisdom, young in form.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, very slow and quiet, with long pauses for the music. There is no dialogue: the teacher never speaks. About two words a second.

### 1. The banyan (25 sec)
**Visual:** A wide, quiet hill at dawn. A vast banyan tree, its roots hanging like pillars. Beneath it, on a stone seat, sits a young, radiant teacher, facing south. One hand rests in the sign of knowledge, thumb and finger touching.
**Narration:** "Under a great banyan tree, the Tamil hymns say, the Lord sits as a teacher. He is young, and he faces the south. So he is called Dakshinamurti, the Lord who faces south. Around him the world is quiet. Even the birds seem to wait."
**Sound:** wind in the leaves; a single low drone.

### 2. The four come (20 sec)
**Visual:** Four sages walk up the hill, old in wisdom, young in body. Their faces are lined with thought. They bow and sit before the teacher.
**Narration:** "Four sages came to him, Sanaka and his brothers. They had read every scripture. Yet their minds were not at rest. They had questions without end, and every answer only led to another question. They had come to the one teacher who could end them."
**Sound:** footsteps on grass; the drone continues.

### 3. The questions (20 sec)
**Visual:** Around the sages' heads, faint shapes rise like smoke: scrolls, symbols, swirling words, a storm of thoughts.
**Narration:** "What is the world? What is the self? What is the truth beneath the Vedas? Their questions rose like smoke, and filled the air around them. They waited for the teacher to answer. They were ready for a long, long lesson."
**Sound:** a soft, restless murmur in the music.

### 4. The silence (25 sec)
**Visual:** The teacher does not move his lips. He only raises his hand, in the sign of knowledge, and looks at them with great kindness. The wind stops. A single leaf hangs in the air.
**Narration:** "The teacher did not speak. He only lifted his hand and looked at them. And in that silence, the songs say, he taught them everything. No book, no long speech. Only the silence, and the look of the teacher."
**Sound:** the music stops. Only a faint breath of wind.

### 5. The doubts fall (25 sec)
**Visual:** One by one, the smoky shapes of the questions around the sages fade and drift away. Their faces soften. Their eyes close.
**Narration:** "One by one, their doubts fell away, like leaves in the wind. The Tamil hymns say that under the banyan he taught the four the meaning of the Vedas, and the whole of dharma."
**Sound:** the drone returns, softer and warmer.

### 6. The light (20 sec)
**Visual:** A soft golden light spreads from the teacher's raised hand over the four, over the roots of the banyan, over the hill. All five sit perfectly still.
**Narration:** "Nothing was said, and nothing was missing. The teacher was silent, and the students were still. Their questions had become peace. They had found what they had searched for in all the books of the world."
**Sound:** a high, sustained note; leaves stirring gently.

### 7. The teacher in the songs (15 sec)
**Visual:** The banyan seen from far off at evening. The image slowly turns into a shrine on the south wall of a Tamil temple, where the same teacher sits under the same tree.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri calls him the Lord of the silent teaching. And once he gives that very name, Dakshinamurti, to Murugan himself."
**Sound:** a temple bell, far away.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri praises the Lord of the silent teaching in song 110 (Palani), and in song 823 (Periyamadam) he calls Murugan himself Dakshinamurti.

## Production notes

- **No spoken dialogue.** Use only music and the stirring of leaves.
- **A film of stillness.**
