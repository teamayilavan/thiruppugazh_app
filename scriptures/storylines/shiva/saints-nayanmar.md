# Servants of the Lord (Songs of the Nayanmar)

`saints-nayanmar` | Shiva | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: affectionate, a gallery of devotion | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Arunagiri's songs name the saints of the Tamil Shaiva tradition: Appar, Sundarar, Manikkavasagar and Kachchiyappar. This film is a short gallery of three of them and of the poet who wrote the Kanda Puranam.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Songs name Appar, Sundarar, Manikkavasagar and Kachchiyappar | Thiruppugazh songs 555, 862, 911, 960 (and others) |
| Shiva went as Sundarar's messenger to his wife Paravai | Periya Puranam; see `n-sundarar-messenger` |
| Shiva turned jackals into horses for Manikkavasagar | Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam; see `n-manikkavasagar` |
| Kachchiyappar is the poet of the Kanda Puranam (that Murugan gave him the first line is tradition) | Research set; song 1114 |
| Appar bound to a stone and cast into the sea; the stone floats | Periya Puranam, Appar Puranam 126; Tevaram 4.11.1 |
| Sundarar claimed at his wedding; "Pitha" | Tevaram 7.1.1 and its history note |
| The Lord as messenger to Paravai | Periya Puranam, Eyarkon Kalikkama Puranam 329-344 |

**Honesty note:** songs 555, 862 and 911 use the word "Appar" for "father" (Shiva), not the saint. Second pass: the film shows Appar through the stone-and-sea episode, which the research reads in the Periya Puranam; the temple-path sweeping is left out.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Sundarar, Manikkavasagar, Kachchiyappar, Appar** (a glimpse).
- **Shiva** (in different forms).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, affectionate, like turning the pages of a family album. About two words a second.

### 1. The servants (15 sec)
**Visual:** A wide golden field at dawn. Along a path walk glowing figures, one after another, each with folded hands, toward a distant temple tower.
**Narration:** "The Tamil country remembers the servants of the Lord, the Nayanmar. Arunagiri remembers them too. Here are a few of those whose names live in his songs."
**Sound:** a gentle tambura; footsteps.

### 2. Appar and the stone (25 sec)
**Visual:** An old saint, Appar, is tied to a great stone and thrown into the sea by the king's soldiers. Underwater he folds his hands and sings. The stone rises and floats like a raft, carrying him to the shore.
**Narration:** "Appar was once tied to a stone and thrown into the sea, by a king who had turned against him. In the water he sang to Shiva, calling on the five holy letters. The Periya Puranam tells that the stone floated, and carried him safely to shore."
**Sound:** waves; muffled singing underwater; then calm surf.

### 3. Sundarar claimed (20 sec)
**Visual:** A young bridegroom, Sundarar, at his wedding. An old Brahmin with a palm-leaf deed interrupts. Sundarar shouts at him angrily; then the old man vanishes into a temple, and Sundarar understands, and folds his hands.
**Narration:** "Sundarar was stopped at his own wedding by an old man who claimed him as his servant. Sundarar called him a madman. Then he learned the old man was the Lord, and that word became the first line of his first song."
**Sound:** wedding drums stopping short; a soft bell.

### 4. The Lord as messenger (25 sec)
**Visual:** Night in the streets of Thiruvarur. The Lord himself walks softly to the door of a house and knocks. Inside, Paravai, Sundarar's wife, wakes, still angry. The Lord speaks gently to her.
**Narration:** "Later, Sundarar's wife Paravai was angry with him, and would not let him come home. So Sundarar asked the Lord to speak for him. And the Lord of the worlds walked through the streets at night, as his messenger, to her door."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Forget his faults, and put away your anger."
**Sound:** night insects; a soft knock.

### 5. Manikkavasagar and the horses (25 sec)
**Visual:** A minister kneels under a tree. Then: a pack of jackals in the forest shimmers and turns into fine horses, led into Madurai by the Lord as a horseman. By night they turn back into jackals and run off.
**Narration:** "Manikkavasagar spent his king's gold on the Lord's temple. To save him, the Lord turned the jackals of the forest into horses, and brought them to Madurai. But by night, the horses were jackals again."
**Sound:** a playful rhythm of hoofbeats; then yelps in the night.

### 6. Kachchiyappar (25 sec)
**Visual:** At Kanchipuram, a poet-priest sits by lamplight writing on palm leaves. A glow falls on the first leaf. Later he reads the work aloud to an assembly of scholars.
**Narration:** "Kachchiyappar of Kanchi wrote the Kanda Puranam, the great Tamil telling of Murugan's story. Tradition says that Murugan himself gave him the first words of the book. Arunagiri calls Murugan the guru who blessed Kachchiyappar."
**Sound:** a lamp flickering; the scratch of a stylus.

### 7. The poet bows (15 sec)
**Visual:** Arunagiri, a lean wandering poet, stands at the edge of the field and bows to the line of saints as they pass.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri bowed to them all. He sang of them in a word or a line, as one remembers family."
**Sound:** a single veena phrase.

### 8. The field (15 sec)
**Visual:** The figures walk on toward the temple tower; Arunagiri joins the end of the line. The sun rises fully.
**Narration:** "Each of them has his own story, and his own film. Here they walk together, the servants of the Lord, and the poet who sang of them."
**Sound:** temple bells; a warm closing chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the servants of the Lord in brief. Songs to show: 972 (Ilanji: the Lord as Sundarar's messenger), 960 (Madurai) and 1313 (Pazhamudhircholai: Manikkavasagar and the horses), 1114 (Kachchiyappar), 555 (Thirusirappalli: Appar).

## Production notes

- **A gallery.** Each saint has his own full film in the Saints set.
- **Do not claim Appar's story** beyond what the research gives; his sweeping the temple paths is widely told but not read in this research, so mark it as tradition.
