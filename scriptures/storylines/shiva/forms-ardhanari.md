# Half Shiva, Half Uma (Ardhanari)

`forms-ardhanari` | Shiva | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: serene, balanced, devotional | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Shiva shares half his body with Parvati; at Kanchi, Kamakshi's penance wins her the left half of Ekambareshwara.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Ardhanari:** one figure, right half Shiva (ash, matted hair, snake), left half Parvati (silk, jewels, flowers).
- **Murugan** (a child in the end).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, slow and balanced, with a sense of wonder. About two words a second.

### 1. The two on Kailasa (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa at dawn, snow lit gold. Shiva sits in stillness, ash-grey, matted hair, a serpent at his neck. Beside him sits Parvati, Uma, in green silk with flowers in her hair. Two figures, two halves of one picture.
**Narration:** "On Kailasa sat Shiva, the ascetic, grey with ash, and beside him sat Uma, the daughter of the mountain. He was stillness. She was the power that moves. The poets say they were never truly two."
**Sound:** a soft drone; a single veena note for each of them.

### 2. Creation that would not grow (20 sec)
**Visual:** Brahma, four-faced, sits on his lotus, shaping small creatures of light. They stand still and do not multiply. He frowns, then closes his eyes in prayer to Shiva.
**Narration:** "The Shiva Purana tells that once Brahma made the creatures of the world, but they did not increase. So Brahma prayed to Shiva for help."
**Sound:** a faltering, unfinished melody.

### 3. The form appears (25 sec)
**Visual:** Before Brahma a radiance takes shape: one figure, the right half Shiva, with ash, matted hair and a serpent; the left half the Goddess, with silk, jewels and a flower. The join down the middle is seamless, like dawn meeting day.
**Narration:** "And Shiva came before him in a wonderful form: half man, half woman. One body. The right side was Shiva, the left side was the Goddess. In her the power of creation was made plain, and the world began to grow. From that day there were man and woman, and every living thing could bring forth its young."
**Sound:** the two veena notes from scene one sound together as one chord.

### 4. Penance at Kanchi (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kanchi, the south. Under a single great mango tree by a river, the Goddess sits in penance, eyes closed, hands folded, before a small linga she has shaped from sand. Rain clouds gather.
**Narration:** "Temple tradition at Kanchi tells another story. The Goddess came there and sat under a mango tree, worshipping a linga she had made from sand. She wished for one thing: never to be apart from the Lord, not for a single moment, in this world or any other."
**Sound:** rain beginning; a quiet chant.

### 5. The flood (20 sec)
**Visual:** The river swells into a flood and rushes toward the sand linga. The Goddess throws her arms around it to shield it. The water swirls around her and passes; the linga stands unharmed.
**Narration:** "A flood rose and rushed toward the linga. She held it close in her arms, so that it would not be washed away. She did not think of herself at all. She thought only of him."
**Sound:** rushing water, then calm.

### 6. Half of himself (25 sec)
**Visual:** Light rises from the linga. The Lord appears and smiles. Slowly the two figures draw together and become one shining form, half Shiva, half Uma, beneath the mango tree.
**Narration:** "The Lord was moved by her love. And at Kanchi, it is said, he gave her the left half of his own body. Arunagiri sings of the Lord of Kanchi who melted for the mountain's daughter, and gave her half."
**Sound:** the joined chord, fuller now.

### 7. The child of both (20 sec)
**Visual:** The single figure sits on Kailasa again. A small radiant child, Murugan, with his spear, runs up and climbs into the lap, which belongs to both. Final image: father, mother and son in one balanced circle of light.
**Narration:** "So the Thiruppugazh calls Murugan, again and again, the son of the Lord with Uma at his left. Father and mother in one body, and the child who is the joy of both."
**Sound:** a gentle melody; a temple bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

About forty songs name Murugan as the son of the Lord with Uma at his left. Songs to show: 179 (Palani), 391 (Thiruvarunai), 463 (Chidambaram), 517 (Kayilaimalai), 719 (Madhuranthakam: the Lord of Kanchi who gave her half).
