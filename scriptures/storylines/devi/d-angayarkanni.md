# The Lady of Madurai (Angayarkanni)

`d-angayarkanni` | Devi | running time 2:45 (7 scenes) | tone: wondering, proud, then festive | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Madurai is founded in a kadamba forest; a girl born from a sacrificial fire grows up to be its warrior queen, conquers the world, meets Shiva at Kailasa and marries him, and he rules Madurai beside her.

**Honesty note:** the story is told from padalams 3 to 5 of the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam (read in a modern Tamil prose retelling); song 708 gives only the name and the pair. The fisher-girl story (`d-fisher`, padalam 57) is a later, separate game, not her first marriage.
**Added for the screen:** the whole visual treatment; the narration.

## Characters

- **Meenakshi (Angayarkanni):** the goddess, calm, with fish-shaped eyes.
- **Shiva** (Sundareswarar): beside her.
- **Pilgrims, priests, flower-sellers.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; wondering at the start, brisk and proud for the warrior queen, hushed at Kailasa, festive at the wedding.

### 1. The kadamba forest (25 sec)
**Visual:** Night in a dense kadamba forest. A merchant, Dhananjayan, lost on his way home, wanders with a small lamp. Through the trees a light glows: a shrine with a shining tower over a lingam. Radiant figures, the gods, arrive with flowers and worship through the four watches of the night. Dhananjayan shyly brings them flowers too.
**Narration:** "Long ago, where Madurai now stands, there was only a forest of kadamba trees. One night a merchant named Dhananjayan was lost there. He found a lingam glowing under a shining tower, and he saw the gods themselves come down to worship it, all night long."
**Sound:** night insects; a far bell; soft heavenly voices.

### 2. The city of sweetness (20 sec)
**Visual:** Morning. The merchant tells King Kulasekhara Pandyan. In a dream a siddha shows the king the plan of a city. Builders raise walls, towers and streets. Shiva lets drops of nectar fall from the moon on his head, through the river in his hair, over the new city, which shines.
**Narration:** "The merchant told the Pandya king, and Shiva, in a dream, showed the king how to build a temple and a city. Then Shiva sprinkled nectar from the moon over it, and the city was named Madurai, the sweet one."
**Sound:** hammers and chisels; a bright, honeyed chime for the nectar.

### 3. The child of the fire (25 sec)
**Visual:** Years later. King Malayadhvaja and Queen Kanchanamalai, childless, pour offerings into a sacred fire. The flames rise and a little girl of three steps out of them and climbs into the queen's lap. The king's face falls; he prays in the temple. A voice from the sky answers him.
**Narration:** "A later king, Malayadhvaja, and his queen Kanchanamalai had no child. They made a great fire offering, and out of the fire came a little girl, who ran into the queen's lap. But she had a strange sign: a third breast. A voice from the sky said: when she meets the one she is to marry, it will vanish."
**Sound:** crackling fire; a child's laugh; a deep, calm voice.

### 4. The warrior queen (25 sec)
**Visual:** The girl, named Tadatakai, grows up. She rides elephants and horses, fences with sword, draws the bow, and studies with scholars. Her father crowns her; she sits on the throne and gives judgment, with the fish flag of the Pandyas above her.
**Narration:** "They named her Tadatakai. She learned the sword and the bow, horse and elephant, and every kind of learning. Her father had no son, so he crowned her, and she ruled the Pandya land with justice."
**Sound:** clash of practice swords; galloping; a royal drum.

### 5. To the ends of the earth (25 sec)
**Visual:** Tadatakai rides out in her chariot at the head of her army. Kings of the north bow; Indra's forces draw back. She reaches the white mountain Kailasa. Shiva's attendants fall back before her. Then Shiva himself comes forward. She sees him, and stops. Her bow lowers; she bows her head, her long, fish-shaped eyes cast down. Her old minister Sumati steps forward.
**Narration:** "When her mother worried about her marriage, the young queen rode out to conquer the four quarters. She came even to Kailasa. Shiva's attendants fled before her. Then Shiva himself came, and the moment she saw him the sign vanished, and she lowered her eyes."
**Dialogue:** Sumati: "Mother, this is the one you are to marry."
**Sound:** war conches fading to a single held note; silence when she sees him.

### 6. The wedding (25 sec)
**Visual:** Madurai decorated with garlands and drums. Shiva arrives on his bull; Vishnu and Brahma stand on either side. Queen Kanchanamalai washes his feet with Ganga water. Petals fall from the sky. Then Shiva sits on the Pandya throne as Sundara Pandyan: his bull banner turns into the fish banner, his snakes into gold jewels.
**Narration:** "Shiva promised to come to Madurai on the eighth day. He came on his bull, with Vishnu and Brahma beside him, and the queen mother washed his feet. Gods and kings and the people of the city saw them married. Then Shiva himself took the crown and ruled Madurai, as Sundara Pandyan."
**Sound:** wedding drums, conches, women's blessings; a rain of petals.

### 7. The poet's song (20 sec)
**Visual:** The great temple of Madurai at dusk, lamps in every hall. Shiva and the Goddess stand together in the shrine. A boy poet sings before them. The fish banner moves in the evening wind.
**Narration:** "So the Goddess came to be called Meenakshi, the lady with eyes like fish, and Angayarkanni, the lady of beautiful eyes. Arunagiri sings that Murugan, as the boy poet Sambandar, praised the Lord who rules Madurai with Angayarkanni beside him."
**Sound:** temple bells and evening chant; a single song rising.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the Goddess of Madurai as Angayarkanni, the consort of the Lord who rules the city. Song to show: 708 (Kodainagar).

## Production notes

- **Follows the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalams 3 to 5** (the founding of Madurai, Tadatakai born from the sacrificial fire, her marriage to Shiva). Do not add a plot beyond these padalams.
- **The fish-shaped eyes are in the text** (padalam 5: she looks down with eyes like the kendai fish), so the eye imagery may be used.
- **`d-fisher` is a separate, later episode** (padalam 57), not the story of her first marriage; the two films can be shown as a pair but should not be presented as one story.
