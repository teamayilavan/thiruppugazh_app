# Abirami at Thiruvathigai (Where the Cities Burn)

`d-thiruvathigai` | Devi | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: grand, bright, a single arrow | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Thiruvathigai, Shiva burns the three flying cities with a single arrow, Meru as his bow, and the Goddess Abirami stands by him.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Song 292 places the Goddess Abirami at Thiruvathigai, where Shiva burnt the three cities with Meru as his bow and she stood by him | Thiruppugazh song 292 |
| Sundarar's hymn at Thiruvathigai Veerattanam (Tevaram 7.38, v. 9) praises the lord who bent the bow and burned the three cities with one arrow | Tevaram 7.38 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration. For the Tripura story in full, see `shiva/deeds-tripura`.

## Characters

- **Shiva:** a tall archer, calm, with Meru as his bow.
- **Abirami:** the Goddess, beside him, serene.
- **The three cities:** golden, silver and iron, floating.
- **The gods:** watching.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; grand and bright, gathering to a single held moment for the arrow, then quiet. The burning is stylised: no people are seen in the cities.

### 1. The three cities (25 sec)
**Visual:** In the sky float three great cities, one of gold, one of silver, one of iron, drifting like clouds over the earth. Their shadows pass over villages and fields, and where they pass, the land darkens.
**Narration:** "Long ago, three cities of the demons wandered through the sky: one of gold, one of silver, and one of iron. Wherever they went, they brought fear. The gods themselves could not stand against them."
**Sound:** a deep, grinding hum as the cities move; uneasy wind.

### 2. The gods' plea (20 sec)
**Visual:** The gods, anxious, gather before Shiva and bow, their hands joined. Shiva listens, calm. Beside him, sharing his body, is the Goddess, serene.
**Narration:** "The gods came to Shiva, bowed before him, and asked him to save them. Shiva listened, calm and unhurried. And at his side, as always, was the Goddess, who shares one half of his body."
**Sound:** murmuring prayers; a calm, steady drone.

### 3. The bow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva rises and takes hold of Mount Meru, the golden mountain at the centre of the world, and bends it like a bow. The earth trembles. The gods gasp. The Goddess's hand rests on the bow with his.
**Narration:** "Shiva took Mount Meru, the great golden mountain, and bent it into a bow. The saint Sundarar sang of this at Thiruvathigai, on the bank of the Gedilam river: the Lord who bent the bow, and burned the three cities with a single arrow."
**Sound:** the groan of the mountain bending; a deep, resonant twang.

### 4. The Goddess (20 sec)
**Visual:** Close on the Goddess Abirami: radiant, calm, beautiful, her eyes on the cities. Light flows from her into the bow.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri goes further. He sings that it was she, Uma, the daughter of the mountain-king, who bent Meru like a bow and burned the cities in a moment. She is Abirami, the beautiful one, who is one half of Shiva."
**Sound:** a pure, high note over the drone.

### 5. The arrow (25 sec)
**Visual:** A single arrow on the string, glowing. Shiva and the Goddess, one figure, draw it back. Time seems to stop: the cities hang in the sky, the gods stand still, a single leaf hangs in the air. Then the arrow flies.
**Narration:** "One arrow was set on the string. Everything in the three worlds stood still and waited. The gods held their breath, and even the wind was silent. Then the arrow flew. There was no need for a second one."
**Sound:** silence; the creak of the string; then a rushing whistle.

### 6. The fire (25 sec)
**Visual:** The three cities burst into flame: gold, silver and iron melting into light, folding in on themselves like burning paper, and falling as ash that turns to sparks and vanishes. No people are seen. The sky clears.
**Narration:** "In a single moment, all three of the cities were ablaze together. Gold, silver and iron melted into light and fell away like ash. The sky was clear again, and the fear was gone from the earth."
**Sound:** a great roar of fire, then crackling, then calm.

### 7. The quiet (20 sec)
**Visual:** The temple of Thiruvathigai at evening, on the bank of the Gedilam. In the shrine Shiva and the Goddess stand together, calm. Lamps glow.
**Narration:** "At Thiruvathigai, temple tradition says, the Goddess is worshipped beside the Lord who burned the cities. Arunagiri calls her Abirami of Thiruvathigai, the half of Shiva, and the loving mother of Murugan."
**Sound:** temple bells; a soft evening chant.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri sings of Abirami of Thiruvathigai, the half of Shiva who bent Meru as a bow and burned the three cities. Song to show: 292 (Thiruthani).
