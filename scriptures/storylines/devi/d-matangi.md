# The Sage's Daughter (Matangi)

`d-matangi` | Devi | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: quiet, mystical, brief | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

In one song the poet names the Goddess as the daughter of the sage Matanga: Matangi, one of the forms of the Shakta tradition.

## Characters

- **Matanga:** an old sage in a forest hermitage.
- **Matangi:** the Goddess, in green.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; quiet and slow, like a prayer. The film is an invocation: it tells no legend, and says so.

### 1. The hermitage (25 sec)
**Visual:** A forest hermitage at dawn: a thatched hut, a sacred fire, a stream. An old sage, Matanga, sits in meditation, white-haired, utterly still. Deer graze near him without fear.
**Narration:** "In a forest hermitage lived a sage named Matanga. We know almost nothing of him from the songs. Arunagiri gives us only his name, and one thing more: that the Goddess was born as his daughter."
**Sound:** a stream; morning birds; the crackle of a small fire.

### 2. A daughter (25 sec)
**Visual:** A soft green light gathers beside the sacred fire. The sage opens his eyes and joins his hands. The light takes the shape of a young girl, radiant, in green, who bows to him.
**Narration:** "One of Arunagiri's songs calls the Goddess Matangi, the daughter of the sage Matanga. Shakta tradition honours Matangi as one of the forms of the Goddess. The song does not tell how she came to the sage, and we will not invent it."
**Sound:** a soft, shimmering chime; the sage's whispered prayer.

### 3. Her name (20 sec)
**Visual:** The girl stands at the stream; her reflection becomes the Goddess in her many forms, one after another, then the girl again.
**Narration:** "In the same breath, the song gives her many other names. She is the one who swallowed the dark poison. She is praised by the Vedas. She is the great Durga."
**Sound:** a gentle drone; the water.

### 4. The green (20 sec)
**Visual:** Close on the forest: green leaves, green shadows, green light on the water. The Goddess's presence fills the whole forest, quiet and alive.
**Narration:** "The song goes on. She is the one who, as Kali, kicked away Death himself to save the boy Markandeya. She is the one who danced in challenge against Shiva."
**Sound:** leaves rustling; a far-off drumbeat, then quiet.

### 5. The song (25 sec)
**Visual:** Arunagiri at the seashore temple of Thiruchendur, singing, eyes closed. Behind him, faintly, the forest hermitage and the green light.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri sang all these names at Thiruchendur, by the sea. He was not telling a story. He was praising the Mother, the half of Shiva, whom devotees come to worship every day. And all her names lead to one name: the mother of Murugan."
**Sound:** the poet's voice over waves and temple bells.

### 6. The silence (15 sec)
**Visual:** The hermitage again, empty and peaceful. The fire burns low. The green light lingers, then fades.
**Narration:** "Of the sage's daughter herself, the songs say no more. We do not know what she said, or how long she stayed. Some things are left in silence, and that is right."
**Sound:** silence, then a single bird.

### 7. The bow (10 sec)
**Visual:** The old sage bows deeply toward the light. The screen fades to green, then gold.
**Narration:** "So we bow, as the sage bowed, to Matangi, the Mother."
**Sound:** a single soft bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the Goddess as Matangi, the sage Matanga's daughter, in a garland of her names. Song to show: 94 (Thiruchendur).
