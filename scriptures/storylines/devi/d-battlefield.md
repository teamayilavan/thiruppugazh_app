# The Dance on the Battlefield (Durga and Bhadrakali)

`d-battlefield` | Devi | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: wild, festive, thunderous | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

During Murugan's war with the demons, Durga and Bhadrakali and the Goddess's attendants dance on the battlefield, shouting the "kulavai" cry, as an army of fiends feasts.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| When Murugan fought the demons, the Goddess's attendants and Bhadrakali danced on the field, shouting the "kulavai" cry, as the army of fiends feasted | Thiruppugazh songs 138, 900, 903, 1079, 1127 |

**Honesty note:** the research gives this as the songs' image; it is not a narrative in the Kanda Puranam read here. The film is a spectacle, not a plot, and it follows the songs.
**Added for the screen:** all visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Bhadrakali:** a fierce, bright goddess, dancing.
- **Durga:** radiant, on a lion.
- **The Goddess's attendants.**
- **The army of fiends (bhootas):** grotesque and comic, shown feasting.
- **Murugan** (in the distance, calm).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; it rides the drums, rising with the dance and settling at dawn. The battle itself is seen only from far away, as light and banners.

### 1. The war (20 sec)
**Visual:** From a hilltop at dusk: the plain below is full of banners, chariots and flashes of light. Murugan's peacock banner is in the centre; far away, the dark towers of the demon king Suran.
**Narration:** "This is the great war of Murugan against Suran and the demons. The Kanda Puranam fills this war with Kali and her hosts, and with crowds of fiends. Arunagiri sings of what happened at its edges."
**Sound:** distant war drums and conches, like thunder far off.

### 2. The goddesses arrive (25 sec)
**Visual:** At the edge of the field a light flares. Durga, radiant, on her lion, and Bhadrakali, dark and bright, wild-haired, with a trident, step out of it. Behind them their hosts: the Goddess's attendants and a crowd of round, goggle-eyed fiends with drums.
**Narration:** "The songs say the Goddess came to that field. Durga, who serves Uma, came with her hosts, and Bhadrakali came, the fierce one of the battlefield. Behind them came a whole army of fiends, hungry, noisy and pleased with themselves."
**Sound:** a lion's roar; a sudden rush of wind; excited chatter.

### 3. The cry (20 sec)
**Visual:** Bhadrakali throws back her head; the attendants join her. The sound rolls over the plain; the fiends throw up their arms. Even the demons' banners tremble.
**Narration:** "Then they raised the kulavai, the high, rolling cry that Tamil women raise at moments of joy. Song one hundred and thirty-eight says Bhadrakali raised it as Murugan's spear won the day."
**Sound:** the kulavai cry, long and thrilling, echoing back from the hills.

### 4. The dance (30 sec)
**Visual:** Durga begins to dance: measured, classical steps, hands in exact gestures. Bhadrakali whirls beside her, faster, hair flying. The fiends form two rows on either side and beat their hand-drums, slowly at first, then quicker, keeping time with the dancers. Indra, on his white elephant, raises his arm and shouts.
**Narration:** "And they danced. One song says Durga danced like the waves of a stormy sea. Another says she danced by the rules of the classical art. The fiends lined up on either side and beat their drums to the time of the dance. Indra, on his white elephant, cried out: victory to our commander!"
**Sound:** hand-drums building in tempo; anklet bells; Indra's shout.

### 5. The feast (25 sec)
**Visual:** Comedy: the fiends, round-bellied, sit down to a vast banquet laid on the field, grabbing from enormous platters of fruit and rice, squabbling over the biggest share, licking their fingers, falling asleep with full bellies. No bodies, no blood.
**Narration:** "The songs say the army of fiends had a feast on that field such as they had never had before. They ate until they could eat no more. Even hunger, it seems, had its share in Murugan's victory."
**Sound:** a jaunty tune; burps and giggles; snoring.

### 6. The calm (20 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan, high on his peacock, calm, his spear shining, looks across the field. The dancers turn toward him and bow. Brahma and the gods in the sky join their hands.
**Narration:** "Above it all, Murugan stood calm, with his spear in his hand. The dance was in his honour. The gods in the sky joined their hands, and the whole world called out: Hara, Hara!"
**Sound:** the drums stop; a single bell; a soft chant of "Hara, Hara".

### 7. The dawn (20 sec)
**Visual:** The dark sky pales. The dust of battle sinks. The goddesses fade into the light with their hosts; the fiends trot after them, sleepy. The plain lies quiet in the sunrise, with Murugan's banner flying.
**Narration:** "Then the dust settled, and dawn came over the field. The dancers went back into the light from which they came. Arunagiri remembers that dance in his songs, as the sound of Murugan's victory."
**Sound:** birdsong; a last, faint kulavai far away; a warm closing chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri hears Murugan's victory in the cry and the dance of the Goddess's hosts. Songs to show: 138 (Palani), 900 and 903 (Vayalur), 1079 and 1127 (general songs).

## Production notes

- **Make the feast comedy,** not horror: no body parts, no blood.
- **A film of sound.** The "kulavai" cry and the drums are its core.
