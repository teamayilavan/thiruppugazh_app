# The Forest of Idumbai (Durga and the Pandavas)

`d-idumbai` | Devi | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: mysterious, protective, quiet | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

During the Pandavas' exile, the song remembers the forest of Idumbai, where the Goddess Durga dwelt.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Song 574 mentions the forest of Idumbai where the Goddess Durga dwelt, in the passage about the Pandavas' exile | Thiruppugazh song 574 |

**Honesty note:** the research has only this one reference. The Mahabharata's account of the Pandavas in the forest (Hidimba episode) is the wider background, but it is not read in this research and the film does not narrate it. This is a short atmospheric film, not a story.
**Added for the screen:** the whole visual treatment; the narration.

## Characters

- **The Pandavas:** five brothers and their mother Kunti, weary, in forest clothes (shown at a distance).
- **Draupadi:** their wife.
- **Durga:** an unseen presence, a glow in the forest.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; low and hushed, as if speaking in a forest at night. The Goddess is never seen, only felt.

### 1. The wanderers (25 sec)
**Visual:** A narrow path into a deep forest. Far off, small figures walk in single file: five brothers in plain forest clothes, with their mother. They are tired; their faces are not seen clearly. Tall trees close over them.
**Narration:** "The Pandavas, five brothers, once had to leave their home and live in the forest. Arunagiri remembers one of those forests by name. He calls it Idumbai. The eldest brother, Dharma, lived there with the others."
**Sound:** footsteps on leaves; a low wind; a slow flute.

### 2. The forest of Idumbai (25 sec)
**Visual:** Inside the forest: giant roots, hanging vines, deep green shadows, shafts of light. Animals watch from the dark. The brothers make camp in a small clearing.
**Narration:** "Idumbai is the Tamil form of the name Hidimba. It was a deep and wild forest, the kind of place where travellers lose their way, and where wild beasts and stranger things are said to live. Yet the song does not speak of danger there. It speaks of something else."
**Sound:** crickets; distant animal calls; a creaking branch.

### 3. A presence (25 sec)
**Visual:** As evening falls, a faint golden glow moves between the trees, never quite seen. Where it passes, flowers open and the animals grow calm. One of the brothers looks up, as if he has heard his name.
**Narration:** "The song says the Goddess Durga was present in that forest. We are not told how she appeared, or what she did. Arunagiri only says that she was there, where the Pandavas lived."
**Sound:** a soft, shimmering chime that comes and goes.

### 4. The night (20 sec)
**Visual:** Night. A small fire in the clearing; the brothers and their mother sleep. Around the edge of the firelight, the glow keeps watch. A shadow at the forest edge draws back.
**Narration:** "So we may think of the night there, the fire burning low, the brothers asleep, and the Goddess keeping her quiet watch over the forest and over all who live in it."
**Sound:** crackling fire; an owl; the chime, very soft.

### 5. The shrine (20 sec)
**Visual:** Morning mist. Among the roots of a great tree, a simple stone is marked with turmeric and kumkum; a few flowers lie before it. No figure, no image: only the stone and the light.
**Narration:** "People of the forest have always set up such simple stones for the Goddess, under great trees, with a little turmeric and a few flowers. The forest is her temple."
**Sound:** birdsong; a single small bell.

### 6. The poet (20 sec)
**Visual:** Arunagiri, in a temple hall, sings with closed eyes. Behind him, faint images rise: Krishna as a messenger, Krishna driving Arjuna's chariot, and the dark forest with its glow.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri tells all this in a song about Krishna. Krishna, he sings, went as the messenger of Dharma, who had lived in the forest of Idumbai where Durga was present. And Krishna drove Arjuna's chariot."
**Sound:** the poet's voice rising over a tambura.

### 7. Dawn (15 sec)
**Visual:** Sunrise over the forest canopy. The glow sinks into the trees and is gone. A peacock calls from a branch.
**Narration:** "Murugan, the song ends, is the nephew of that Krishna. The poet says no more about the forest of Idumbai, or about the Goddess who was there. And the forest keeps its secret."
**Sound:** a peacock's cry; a warm, final chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls, in a song about Krishna, the forest of Idumbai where the Pandavas lived and where the Goddess Durga was present. Song to show: 574 (Viralimalai).
