# The Mountain King's Daughter (Parvati, Daughter of Himavan)

`d-himavan` | Devi | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: serene, tender, hopeful | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The Mountain-King Himavan does penance for a child, and the Goddess is born to him as a boon: Parvati, who will one day be the mother of Murugan.

**Honesty note:** the penance and the lotus birth are told in the Kanda Puranam itself (Utpatti Kandam 375-410); nothing is taken from the Kumarasambhava.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Himavan:** a majestic, white-bearded mountain king; his form is half-person, half-mountain.
- **Menaka:** his queen, gentle.
- **Parvati:** a child, then a young woman, calm.
- **The sage Narada** (tradition): omitted.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; serene and tender throughout, with a smile for the little girl's courage.

### 1. The mountain (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Himalaya at dawn, white peaks above clouds. As the light grows, the greatest peak shows a face: Himavan, the king of the mountains, white-bearded, half person and half mountain. Beside him, on a lower slope, his gentle queen Menai.
**Narration:** "Far in the north stands the Himalaya, the snowy mountain. Its king is Himavan, the mountain-king, and his queen is Menai. This is the story of how the Goddess came to be their daughter."
**Sound:** high wind; a deep, slow drone; a single bird.

### 2. A name to lay down (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa. The Goddess Uma stands before Shiva, troubled, her hands joined. Behind her, as a faint memory, the proud figure of Daksha turning away from Shiva. Shiva listens, then smiles and points far away, toward the snowy range.
**Narration:** "Once the Goddess had been born as the daughter of Daksha, who scorned Shiva. Now she came to Shiva and asked to be free of that name and that body. Shiva said: Himavan is doing a hard penance to have you as his daughter. Go to him as a little child."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Grow up there, and do your penance. Then we will come with all the gods and marry you."
**Sound:** a soft harp; Shiva's voice low and kind.

### 3. The penance (25 sec)
**Visual:** High on the mountain, beside a still pool full of lotuses, Himavan sits in penance, thin and weary, snow on his shoulders, eyes closed. Days and nights pass over him.
**Narration:** "High on the snowy mountain there was a pool, as white and pure as nectar. Beside it Himavan sat in penance, until his body grew thin and tired. He had one wish: that the Goddess should be born as his daughter, and that he might give her to Shiva."
**Sound:** silence; drops of melting snow; slow breathing.

### 4. The lotus (25 sec)
**Visual:** One lotus in the pool opens wide. On it lies a tiny baby girl, glowing. Himavan opens his eyes. Tears run down his face; he trembles, laughs, sings and dances on the shore with joy, then bows to her.
**Narration:** "One day a lotus opened in the pool, and on it lay a baby girl: the mother of every living thing, now a little child. Himavan wept for joy. He sang and danced on the shore, and folded his hands to her."
**Dialogue:** Himavan: "I am saved. This is the grace of the Lord."
**Sound:** a bright chime as the lotus opens; a joyous, dancing tune.

### 5. Menai (20 sec)
**Visual:** Himavan carries the baby carefully on his head, down to his palace. Menai takes her in her arms. Her face fills with wonder, and then with a mother's love. A golden cradle swings.
**Narration:** "He carried her on his head to his palace, and gave her to Menai. When Menai heard what had happened, she was filled with joy, and her heart overflowed with a mother's love. They raised her as tenderly as the waxing moon."
**Sound:** a lullaby; the creak of a swinging cradle.

### 6. The child's wish (25 sec)
**Visual:** The girl, now five years old, stands before her father with a serious face. Himavan, enormous and loving, bends down to her and shakes his head. She laughs, and points upward, toward Kailasa.
**Narration:** "When she was five years old, she came to her father with a wish. She wanted to go apart and do penance, to win Shiva as her husband."
**Dialogue:** Himavan: "My child, you are only five. Your body cannot bear such a penance."
**Dialogue:** The girl: "Shiva himself will protect me. No one else can."
**Sound:** a playful flute; her small, clear laugh.

### 7. The penance begins (20 sec)
**Visual:** On a quiet slope, Himavan builds a hermitage for her, and girls of the mountain come to keep her company. The little Goddess sits down, closes her eyes and turns her mind to Shiva. The camera rises over the snows toward Kailasa.
**Narration:** "So Himavan built her a hermitage on the mountain, and she began her penance, thinking only of Shiva. Arunagiri calls her the daughter of the mountain-king, the boon of his long penance, and the mother of Murugan."
**Sound:** a single held note; a temple bell far away.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri calls the Goddess the daughter of the mountain-king, born as the boon of his penance, and the mother of Murugan. Songs to show: 32 (Thiruchendur), 107 (Palani), 239 (Thiruthani) and 354 (Thiruvanaikka).
