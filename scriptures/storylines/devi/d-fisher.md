# The Fishermen's Daughter (Parvati and the Shark)

`d-fisher` | Devi | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: gentle, comic, romantic | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The Goddess, cursed to be born a fisherman's daughter, is found under a punnai tree; Shiva comes as a young fisherman, catches the shark that has wrecked the nets, and marries her.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Parvati hears Shiva's Vedic teaching inattentively and is cursed to be born a fisherman's daughter | Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam, padalam 57 |
| She is found as a baby under a punnai tree and raised by the chief of the Pandya coast's fishermen | same |
| Nandi, also cursed, becomes a shark that wrecks the nets | same |
| The chief promises his daughter (Meenakshi) to whoever catches the shark | same |
| Shiva comes as a young fisherman, catches the shark and marries her | same |
| Song 1165 adds that Murugan came to end the curse | Thiruppugazh song 1165 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Parvati:** first a baby, then a young woman with fisherfolk jewelry, radiant.
- **The fishermen's chief:** a broad-shouldered, kind man.
- **Shiva:** a young fisherman with a net, laughing eyes.
- **Nandi:** a great shark.
- **Murugan** (a golden figure).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; gentle at the start, with a smile when the young fisherman appears, and festive at the wedding.

### 1. The teaching (20 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva and the Goddess sit alone together. Shiva explains the meaning of the Vedas, his hand in a teaching gesture. The Goddess's eyes wander to the window, to the sky. Shiva notices, and his face grows grave.
**Narration:** "Once, when Shiva and the Goddess were alone together, he began to explain the deep meaning of the Vedas. But her mind was not on it. Because she paid no heed to the Vedas, Shiva said, she would be born on earth as a fisherman's daughter."
**Sound:** a soft chant; it breaks off on a low note.

### 2. The sons and Nandi (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Goddess bows, sorrowful. Shiva, gentle again, raises his hand: a promise. At the door two boys, Murugan and Ganesha, look at each other, then seize the bundles of Veda books and fling them far out into the sea. Shiva turns to Nandi, the guardian at the door.
**Narration:** "She grieved, and Shiva promised that he would come himself and marry her. But their sons thought the punishment too heavy for so small a fault. Murugan and Ganesha threw the books of the Vedas into the sea. Shiva turned to Nandi, the guard at the door."
**Dialogue:** Nandi: "Lord, they are your own sons. How could I stop them?"
**Narration (continued):** "Nandi too was cursed: to live in the sea as a shark."
**Sound:** splash of books in the sea; a deep gong.

### 3. The baby under the punnai tree (20 sec)
**Visual:** The east coast of the Pandya land. The chief of the fishermen, broad-shouldered and kind, has prayed long for a child. Under a punnai tree on the shore, a baby girl lies shining in the shade. He lifts her in wonder and runs home to his wife.
**Narration:** "On the eastern shore of the Pandya land, the chief of the fishermen had no child, and prayed to Shiva at Madurai. One day, in the shade of a punnai tree, he found a baby girl. He carried her home to his wife."
**Sound:** waves; a baby's coo; the wife's cry of joy.

### 4. The shark (20 sec)
**Visual:** The girl grows up in the fishing village, mending nets, laughing with the other girls. Out at sea a vast shark tears through the nets, overturns a boat, and slips away. The fishermen come home empty-handed and afraid.
**Narration:** "The girl grew up among the fisherfolk, and grew beautiful. But out at sea a great shark was wrecking the nets. No fisherman could catch it, and no one could stop it."
**Sound:** ripping nets; a heavy splash; anxious voices.

### 5. The promise (15 sec)
**Visual:** On the beach, before the whole village, the chief raises his hand.
**Dialogue:** The chief: "Whoever catches that shark shall have my daughter."
**Narration:** "So the chief made a promise, before everyone."
**Sound:** murmurs of the crowd; the surf.

### 6. The young fisherman (25 sec)
**Visual:** A young stranger walks up the beach with a net over his shoulder, laughing eyes. The chief questions him. The young man rows out alone, casts his net in a wide circle, and hauls. The shark thrashes, is caught, and is drawn to the shore.
**Dialogue:** The chief: "Who are you, and what brings you here?"
**Dialogue:** The young man: "I have come to make my living by casting nets."
**Narration:** "Then a young fisherman came to the shore. He rowed out, cast his net, and caught the great shark, and he brought it, tamed, to the beach."
**Sound:** oars; the hiss of a flung net; a cheer from the shore.

### 7. Meenakshi (25 sec)
**Visual:** The chief brings his daughter forward. The young fisherman looks at her and smiles. The fishermen dance on the beach; the two are married under a canopy of nets and flowers. For a moment, the young fisherman shines as Shiva.
**Dialogue:** The young man: "She has eyes like fish."
**Dialogue:** The chief: "Yes, we call her Meenakshi. Take her, she is yours."
**Narration:** "The shark was caught in his net, and the girl was caught in his eyes. The young fisherman was Shiva. He married her, and the curse was over."
**Sound:** drums, conches and the fishermen's song.

### 8. The Vedas again (15 sec)
**Visual:** A quiet temple by the sea. Shiva explains the Vedas again; this time the Goddess listens with her whole heart. Then a golden boy with a spear appears in the light.
**Narration:** "This time she listened to every word. The Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam tells it so. And Arunagiri sings that Murugan came to end the curse."
**Sound:** the chant resumes, clear and complete; a soft bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri sings of the Goddess who came to earth as the fishermen's daughter, and of Murugan's part in ending the curse. Songs to show: 291 (Thiruthani) and 1165 (general songs).

## Production notes

- **Do not make the fishermen comic.** They are the heroes.
- **Pair with `d-angayarkanni`** (Meenakshi at Madurai).
