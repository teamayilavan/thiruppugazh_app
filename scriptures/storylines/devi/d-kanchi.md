# The Sand Lingam and the Flood (Parvati at Kanchi)

`d-kanchi` | Devi | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: devout, then urgent, then tender | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Parvati makes a lingam of sand under the mango tree beside the river and worships it; when the river floods she clasps the lingam to save it, and Shiva takes the marks of her embrace and marries her.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Parvati's worship is broken off when the river Kampa rises; she clasps the lingam; the Lord of the one mango tree takes the mark of her breasts | Kanchi Puranam (Sivagnana Munivar), invocation vv. 4-6 |
| Temple legend: she made a sand lingam under the mango tree beside the Vegavati; when the river flooded she embraced it, and her bangles left their marks; Shiva then wedded her | Temple tradition |
| The songs add the scar on his chest left by her embrace | Thiruppugazh songs 338, 347, 352, 438 |

**Honesty note:** the "Vegavati" and the bangle marks are not in the parts of the Puranam that could be read: they are temple tradition. The film says so.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Parvati:** a young woman at prayer, devoted, in simple clothes.
- **Shiva:** present as a light, then in person.
- **The river:** a character in itself.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; devout and still at first, urgent in the flood, tender at the end. Where a detail is temple tradition, the narration says so.

### 1. The journey to Kanchi (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa. The Goddess, quiet and resolved, steps away from Shiva's side. She mounts a great white bull and rides south over hills and rivers, until she comes to Kanchi and a single great mango tree beside a river.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri sings that the Goddess once parted from Shiva's side, rode the great bull, and came south to Kanchi to do penance. There, beside a river, stood a single mango tree."
**Sound:** wind on the heights; hoofbeats fading; birdsong under the mango tree.

### 2. The sand lingam (25 sec)
**Visual:** Under the mango tree, the Goddess, in simple clothes, kneels on the riverbank. With wet hands she gathers sand and shapes a small lingam. She sets flowers on it and lights a little lamp.
**Narration:** "Temple tradition says she had no stone image. So she gathered the sand of the riverbank in her hands, and shaped it into a lingam under the mango tree, and began to worship Shiva there."
**Sound:** the soft trickle of water; the scrape of wet sand; a small bell.

### 3. The penance (20 sec)
**Visual:** Time passes. Seasons change over the mango tree: blossoms, fruit, falling leaves. The Goddess sits before the sand lingam, eyes closed, unmoving, the lamp still burning.
**Narration:** "Day after day she worshipped, and her mind did not wander. The songs say Shiva watched her penance, and his heart melted with compassion."
**Sound:** seasons in sound: rain, crickets, wind, birdsong.

### 4. The flood (25 sec)
**Visual:** A roar upstream. The river rises suddenly, brown and fast, spilling over its banks, rushing toward the mango tree. Water swirls around the Goddess's knees and reaches for the sand lingam.
**Narration:** "Then, before her worship was over, the river rose. The Kanchi Puranam calls the river Kampa, and says that Shiva himself sent it, like a messenger, because he could not wait. The water rushed toward the lingam of sand."
**Sound:** a rising roar of water; the lamp hissing out.

### 5. The embrace (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Goddess does not run. She throws her arms around the sand lingam and holds it close to her, shielding it with her whole body as the flood pours around her. Her eyes are closed; her face is calm.
**Narration:** "The Goddess did not run. She put her arms around the lingam and held it close to her, so that the water could not wash it away. She held on, and held on, until the flood passed."
**Sound:** the roar of water; then, slowly, its fading.

### 6. The mark (20 sec)
**Visual:** The water goes down. The sand lingam stands unharmed. On it, faintly, are marks where she held it. A close-up of her bangles and of the faint lines on the lingam.
**Narration:** "When the water went down, the lingam was safe. The Puranam says the Lord took the marks of her embrace. Temple tradition adds that her bangles left their marks on it too, and calls the river the Vegavati."
**Sound:** dripping water; birds return; a gentle chime.

### 7. The Lord (20 sec)
**Visual:** Light rises from the sand lingam. Shiva stands before the Goddess, smiling. She bows; he raises her. Behind them the single mango tree shines, and the shrine of Ekambam, the Lord of the one mango tree, takes shape.
**Narration:** "Then Shiva appeared before her, smiling, and raised her up, and took her as his own. He is worshipped there as the Lord of the one mango tree, and the Goddess is worshipped at Kanchi as Kamakshi."
**Sound:** conches and temple drums; a joyful chord.

### 8. The scar (10 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva's chest, with a faint mark like a scar; the Goddess beside him; temple lamps.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri sings that the Lord of Kanchi still bears on his chest the mark of her embrace: the sign that her devotion and his love became one."
**Sound:** a single bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri sings of the Goddess's penance at Kanchi and of the Lord who bears the mark of her embrace. Songs to show: 338, 347 and 352 (Kanchipuram) and 438 (Thiruvannamalai).

## Production notes

- **Keep the embrace chaste.** It is a gesture of devotion.
- **Mark the Vegavati and the bangle marks** as temple tradition.
