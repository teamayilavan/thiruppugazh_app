# The Dance Contest at Thiruvalangadu (Kali and Shiva)

`d-kali-dance` | Devi | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: fierce, then playful, then reverent | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A furious Kali threatens Shiva's town; Shiva proposes a dance contest, and when she matches every pose he lifts his foot to his ear, a pose she will not match.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Kali, furious after killing demons, threatens Shiva's town at Thiruvalangadu | Temple legend (accounts differ in the details) |
| Shiva proposes a dance contest | same |
| She matches every pose; he lifts his foot to his ear (Urdhva Tandava), a pose she will not match, and she yields | same |
| Songs 94 and 117 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Honesty note:** this is temple legend and the songs; Sambandar's hymn at Thiruvalangadu (Tevaram 1.45) does not describe the dance.
**Added for the screen:** the poses and the music, the narration.

## Characters

- **Kali:** a fierce, dark goddess in red, wild-haired, with an unbroken energy.
- **Shiva:** a graceful, laughing dancer, calm.
- **The gods and attendants** watching from the sidelines.
- **Mount at the forest of Alangadu.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; tense at Kali's fury, quickening with the dance, and soft and reverent at the end. The narration says plainly that this is a temple legend.

### 1. The fury (20 sec)
**Visual:** A dark sky lit red. Kali, dark, wild-haired, in red, stands over a field where the demons she has fought have vanished as smoke. Her eyes still burn; her trident still shakes. She turns, looking for something more to fight.
**Narration:** "This is a temple legend of Thiruvalangadu, the forest of banyan trees, and it is told in more than one way. It begins with Kali. She had destroyed the demons, but her fury did not cool."
**Sound:** a low rumble; her heavy breathing; drums pounding unevenly.

### 2. The threat (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kali strides into the banyan forest of Alangadu. Trees bend; the gods and attendants who live there scatter. She stands before Shiva's dwelling and calls out a challenge.
**Narration:** "In her rage she came to the banyan forest where Shiva dwelt, and the beings of that place were afraid. She would not leave, the legend says, and she claimed the place for her own."
**Sound:** wind tearing through branches; frightened cries fading.

### 3. The challenge (20 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva steps out, calm and smiling, his hair loose, a small drum in his hand. He does not raise a weapon. He raises one foot, lightly, as if beginning a dance, and points to an open ground.
**Narration:** "Shiva did not fight her. He smiled and offered a contest of a different kind: let us dance, he said, and whoever is the greater dancer shall have the place."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Come, let us dance."
**Sound:** a single tap of Shiva's drum; silence after it.

### 4. The first steps (25 sec)
**Visual:** On the open ground, ringed by the gods and attendants watching from the edges, the two begin. Shiva makes a pose; Kali matches it exactly. He turns; she turns. He leaps; she leaps. Their shadows on the ground move as one.
**Narration:** "So they danced. Every step that Shiva took, Kali took after him. Every pose that he made, she made too. The gods watched from the edges, holding their breath. She was his equal in every movement."
**Sound:** drums and cymbals in a steady rhythm; anklet bells, two sets, in step.

### 5. The pace (20 sec)
**Visual:** Faster and faster. Fire flickers at the edge of the ground. Ghosts and fiends of the burning ground clap and stamp. The two dancers become streaks of light and colour, still matching.
**Narration:** "The dance grew faster. Long before Arunagiri, the poet Karaikkal Ammaiyar had sung of Shiva dancing at Alangadu, among the fires of the burning ground, with ghosts and fiends around him."
**Sound:** drums at full speed; the crackle of fire; clapping.

### 6. The foot (25 sec)
**Visual:** At the height of the dance, Shiva stops still on one foot, and lifts the other straight up, high above his head, to his ear: a single, perfect pose. Kali stops. She looks at him, and does not move.
**Narration:** "Then Shiva lifted one foot straight up, as high as his ear, and held it there. The tellings differ here. Some say an earring fell, and he lifted it back with his foot. But all agree on this: Kali would not take that pose."
**Sound:** all music stops; a single long note.

### 7. The yielding (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kali's fierce face softens. Her trident lowers. She joins her hands and bows to Shiva. He lowers his foot gently and bows to her in return.
**Narration:** "And so Kali yielded. Her fury was gone. She bowed to Shiva, and Shiva bowed to her. Neither was shamed. The dance had brought her back to herself."
**Sound:** a soft drone; a single bell.

### 8. The peace (15 sec)
**Visual:** The banyan forest at dawn, quiet and green. A temple stands where they danced. Light falls on a dancing figure of Shiva with his foot raised.
**Narration:** "At Thiruvalangadu, Shiva is still worshipped as the dancer. Arunagiri sings of this: of Kali who challenged Shiva in the dance, and who is also the mother of Murugan."
**Sound:** birdsong; temple drums, far away and gentle.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri sings of the Goddess who, as Kali, challenged Shiva in the dance, and who is Murugan's mother. Songs to show: 94 (Thiruchendur) and 117 (Palani).

## Production notes

- **Respect both.** Neither is humiliated: the film ends in peace.
- **Pair with `sam-thiruvalangadu`.**
- **Say that accounts differ.** The narration makes clear it is a temple legend.
