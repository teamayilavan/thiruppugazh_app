# The Spider and the Elephant (Thiruvanaikka)

`t-spider-elephant` | Tamil lore | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: gentle, tragic, then redemptive

## One-line story

At Thiruvanaikka, a white elephant and a spider both love the same lingam under the jambu tree; their quarrel ends in both dying, and the spider is reborn as a Chola king.

## The storyline

### 1. The grove (20 sec)
A grove of jambu trees at Thiruvanaikka; under one, a lingam in a quiet clearing.
**Narration:** "Under a jambu tree at Thiruvanaikka stood a lingam."

### 2. The elephant (25 sec)
Each morning a white elephant brings water in his trunk and flowers, and bows.
**Narration:** "Each day a white elephant came to worship it."

### 3. The spider (25 sec)
A tiny spider spins a canopy of web over the lingam to shade it from falling leaves.
**Narration:** "And a spider spun a roof of web above it."

### 4. The tearing (20 sec)
The elephant, seeing the web as dirt, tears it away.
**Narration:** "The elephant tore it down, thinking it unfit."

### 5. The weaving (20 sec)
The spider weaves again; the elephant tears again. Many days pass.
**Narration:** "The spider spun again. The elephant tore it again."

### 6. The bite (25 sec)
In despair the spider climbs into the trunk and bites. The elephant swings in pain and falls; both lie still.
**Narration:** "At last the spider crept into the elephant's trunk. Both died."

### 7. The Lord's grace (20 sec)
A light touches the two: the elephant is taken to Shiva's side; the spider is promised a rebirth.
**Narration:** "The Lord took both to himself, and promised the spider a new birth."

### 8. The king (10 sec)
A palace: a royal baby, born to Subadeva and Kamalavati: Kochchengat, the Chola king.
**Narration:** "The spider was born as the Chola king Kochchengat."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 358 names Thiruvanaikka.

## Production notes

- **No cruelty.** Show the bite as a ripple.
- **Show both creatures' love.** The film's point is that both loved the Lord.
- **The cursed-attendant story** is tradition and can be added as a card.
