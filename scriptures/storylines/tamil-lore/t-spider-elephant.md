# The Spider and the Elephant (Thiruvanaikka)

`t-spider-elephant` | Tamil lore | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: gentle, tragic, then redemptive | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Thiruvanaikka, a white elephant and a spider both love the same lingam under the jambu tree; their quarrel ends in both dying, and the spider is reborn as a Chola king.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; gentle and loving, hushed at the sad turn, glowing at the grace.

### 1. The grove (20 sec)
**Visual:** A cool, green forest beside the river Kaveri, near a clear pool. Under a white jambu tree, in a quiet clearing, stands a lingam. Dry leaves drift down through shafts of sunlight.
**Narration:** "Long ago, beside the river Kaveri, there was a cool forest. In it, under a white jambu tree, the Lord Shiva showed himself as a lingam. This is told in the Periya Puranam."
**Sound:** river water; birdsong; a soft drone.

### 2. The elephant (25 sec)
**Visual:** Each morning a great white elephant walks out of the forest. He fills his trunk at the pool, pours the water over the lingam, lays bunches of flowers on it, and bows his huge head.
**Narration:** "Every day a white elephant came to worship there. He carried water in his trunk and bathed the Lord, and laid fragrant flowers on him, and bowed down. Because of him, the place came to be called Thiruvanaikka, the holy grove of the elephant."
**Sound:** heavy, gentle footsteps; water pouring; a temple bell.

### 3. The spider (25 sec)
**Visual:** Close on a tiny golden spider on a branch above the lingam. It spins thread after thread until a fine canopy of web stretches over the lingam, glinting in the light. A dry leaf falls and is caught in the web.
**Narration:** "There was also a spider there, a spider full of wisdom. It saw the dry leaves falling on the Lord. So, with the thread from its own mouth, it spun a canopy over him, so that no leaf would fall on his head."
**Sound:** a delicate, shimmering motif.

### 4. The tearing (20 sec)
**Visual:** The elephant arrives, sees the web over the lingam, and frowns. To him it looks like dirt. With a sweep of his trunk he clears it away and bathes the lingam clean.
**Narration:** "But when the elephant came, he saw the web, and thought it was not fit to be there. To him it was only dirt on the Lord. So he tore it away."
**Sound:** a soft tearing sound; the shimmering motif breaks off.

### 5. The weaving again (20 sec)
**Visual:** The spider patiently spins the canopy again. Next day, the elephant clears it again. The two never meet face to face; each loves the Lord in its own way.
**Narration:** "The spider spun its canopy again. The next day, the elephant tore it down again. Each of them loved the Lord. Neither understood the other's love."
**Sound:** the two motifs, heavy and delicate, alternate and clash.

### 6. The bite (25 sec)
**Visual:** The spider, grieving and angry, climbs down and creeps into the elephant's trunk. A ripple of pain runs through the great body. The elephant strikes his trunk on the ground and sinks down. The tiny spider is still too. Nothing harsh is shown: only stillness and falling leaves.
**Narration:** "At last the spider could bear it no longer. In grief and anger it crept into the elephant's trunk, and bit him. The elephant beat his trunk upon the ground in pain, and fell. And the little spider, too, gave up its life."
**Sound:** a single deep thud; then silence; leaves falling.

### 7. The Lord's grace (20 sec)
**Visual:** A soft light rises from the lingam and touches both still creatures. The elephant's form glows and rises; a tiny golden light rises from the spider and floats away toward distant palaces.
**Narration:** "But the Lord had seen the love of both. He granted the elephant his grace. And to the spider he gave a new birth, in the royal family of the Cholas, to protect the world."
**Sound:** a warm, rising chord.

### 8. The king (10 sec)
**Visual:** A Chola palace. Queen Kamalavati and King Subadeva with a newborn son whose eyes are a little red. Later, the grown king builds a temple around the jambu tree.
**Narration:** "The spider was born as Kochchengat Chola, who built a temple for the Lord at Thiruvanaikka."
**Sound:** palace drums and temple bells together.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 358, sung at Thiruvanaikka, recalls the spider that spun a canopy of web over the Lord by the Kaveri. The full story is told in the Periya Puranam, in the life of Kochchengat Chola Nayanar.

## Production notes

- **No cruelty.** Show the bite as a ripple.
- **Show both creatures' love.** The film's point is that both loved the Lord.
- **The cursed-attendant story** is tradition and can be added as a card.
