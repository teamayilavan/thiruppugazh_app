# The Pundit Who Knew the Names (The Poet's List of Classics)

`t-classics` | Tamil lore | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: gently satirical, then humble | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The poet lists the Tamil classics (the Tirukkural, the Sangam poems, the Tevaram) that false scholars claim to know, and says that mere names are no substitute for the Lord's grace.

**Honesty note:** this is not a story but a list. The film is a small invented vignette that dramatises the poet's point; every scene is added for the screen, and it makes no claim about any real person.
**Added for the screen:** the scholar, the audience, all dialogue.

## Characters

- **The pundit:** a puffed-up man with a stack of palm-leaves and a loud voice.
- **The poet Arunagiri:** a quiet figure in the corner.
- **The audience:** villagers who admire and doubt.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; gently amused in the square, humble and warm at the end. This film is an illustration of the poet's words, not a record of a real event.

### 1. The market square (20 sec)
**Visual:** A busy temple town square in the evening: stalls, lamps, a flower seller, children running. A well-dressed man with rings on his fingers and long earrings climbs onto a platform with a tall stack of palm-leaf books.
**Narration:** "This is not a story from any book. It is a picture of something the poet Arunagiri saw in his own time. One evening, a learned man climbed onto a platform in the square, with a great pile of books."
**Sound:** market murmur; a hand drum calling people together.

### 2. The names (25 sec)
**Visual:** He lifts book after book and booms out their names. Each name appears in the air as a glowing title: the Tirukkural, the poems of the Sangam, the Tevaram, the epics. The crowd gathers, impressed.
**Narration:** "He named the great works of Tamil, one after another. The Tirukkural of Tiruvalluvar, with its thirteen hundred and thirty couplets. The poems of the Sangam age. The hymns of the Tevaram. The epics, the stories, the sixty-four arts."
**Sound:** a grand, slightly pompous fanfare with each name.

### 3. The question (20 sec)
**Visual:** A small girl at the front tugs at his robe and asks a question. He stops, opens his mouth, and nothing comes. His face goes pale. A few people smile behind their hands.
**Narration:** "Then a child asked him what one couplet of the Kural meant. The learned man opened his mouth, and nothing came out. His face turned pale. The poet says it plainly: such men know the names, but not the meaning."
**Dialogue:** Child: "Sir, what does this verse mean?"
**Sound:** the fanfare stops; one awkward note.

### 4. The poet watches (20 sec)
**Visual:** At the edge of the square, a thin man in simple clothes sits under a tree: the poet. He watches with a quiet, kind smile, not mocking, a little sad.
**Narration:** "At the edge of the square sat a quiet poet. He did not laugh. He knew how easy it is to collect names, and how hard it is to know the truth behind them."
**Sound:** a soft flute.

### 5. The poet's song (25 sec)
**Visual:** The poet begins to sing softly. People turn toward him one by one. Behind him, faintly, the image of Murugan with his spear appears in the evening light.
**Narration:** "Then he began to sing to Murugan. In his songs he says, enough of such learning. He asks the Lord to end it, and to give him a better gift. He even sings that the Tevaram, the hymns of devotion, is worth more than all clever books."
**Sound:** a gentle, devoted melody.

### 6. The grace (25 sec)
**Visual:** Lamplight gathers around the poet. The learned man lowers his stack of books and listens. His proud face softens.
**Narration:** "For Arunagiri, learning was a good thing. He loved the old books of Tamil. But learning used only for show, to flatter a rich man or to win praise, was empty. What fills it is the grace of the Lord."
**Sound:** the melody grows warmer.

### 7. The bow (15 sec)
**Visual:** The learned man bows his head. The child takes his hand. The whole square begins to sing together under the lamps.
**Narration:** "The learned man bowed his head. And the whole square sang with the poet."
**Sound:** many voices together; temple bells.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the Tamil classics, the Tirukkural, the Sangam poems and the Tevaram, to say that learning without devotion is empty. Songs to show: 60 and 76 (Thiruchendur), 104 and 171 (Palani).

## Production notes

- **A gentle satire.** The pundit should be human, not a villain.
- **Mark the film as an illustration,** not a historical event.
