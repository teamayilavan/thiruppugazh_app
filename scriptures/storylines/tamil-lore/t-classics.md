# The Pundit Who Knew the Names (The Poet's List of Classics)

`t-classics` | Tamil lore | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: gently satirical, then humble

## One-line story

The poet lists the Tamil classics (the Tirukkural, the Sangam poems, the Tevaram) that false scholars claim to know, and says that mere names are no substitute for the Lord's grace.

**Honesty note:** this is not a story but a list. The film is a small invented vignette that dramatises the poet's point; every scene is added for the screen, and it makes no claim about any real person.
**Added for the screen:** the scholar, the audience, all dialogue.

## Characters

- **The pundit:** a puffed-up man with a stack of palm-leaves and a loud voice.
- **The poet Arunagiri:** a quiet figure in the corner.
- **The audience:** villagers who admire and doubt.

## The storyline

### 1. The market square (20 sec)
A busy square. A pundit mounts a platform with a pile of manuscripts.
**Narration:** "A learned man came to the market square."

### 2. The names (25 sec)
He booms the names of the great works, one after another.
**Narration:** "He named the Kural, the Sangam poems, the Tevaram."

### 3. The crowd (20 sec)
The crowd gapes; a child asks him what the Kural says; he stammers.
**Narration:** "But when a child asked what was in them, he had nothing to say."

### 4. The poet watches (20 sec)
In the corner, a thin poet smiles quietly.
**Narration:** "The poet watched, and smiled."

### 5. The song (25 sec)
The poet sings softly: "Learning without love is a name only."
**Narration:** "He sang that it was not enough to know the names."

### 6. The grace (25 sec)
Light gathers behind the poet; the pundit lowers his scrolls.
**Narration:** "What counts is the Lord's grace."

### 7. The bow (15 sec)
The pundit bows his head; the crowd sings.
**Narration:** "And the whole square sang."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)
