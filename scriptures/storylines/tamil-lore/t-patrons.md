# The Great Givers (Pari, Kari and the Model Donors)

`t-patrons` | Tamil lore | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: warm, admiring, then wry | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The poet says he wasted his verse praising miserly patrons by comparing them to the great givers of the Sangam age, Pari, Kari, Ay, Pekan, Nalli, Kumanan and Ori; this film shows two of those legendary givers.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The poet says he wasted his verse comparing miserly patrons with Pari, Kari and the other great donors | Thiruppugazh songs 169, 175, 306, 436, 935 |
| Sangam poems in the Purananuru praise Pari (poems 105-111, by Kapilar), Kari (121-124, 126), Ay (127-130), Pekan (141-142), Nalli (148-150), Kumanan (151), Ori (204) | Purananuru |
| The songs also name Karna and "twenty-one donors", who are not Sangam patrons | research |

**Tradition note:** the well-known tales of Pari giving his chariot to a jasmine creeper and of Pekan giving a shawl to a shivering peacock belong to the tradition around these poems; the Purananuru poems for Pekan (141-142) are the source for the peacock, and the creeper is later tradition.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Pari:** a hill chieftain, tall, generous, with a gentle face.
- **Pekan:** a chieftain on a chariot, soft-hearted.
- **The poet Kapilar:** Pari's friend, in white.
- **Arunagiri:** a voice, rueful.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; wry in the hall, warm and admiring with the great givers, light at the end.

### 1. The poet's complaint (20 sec)
**Visual:** A rich man's hall, heavy with gold. A poet stands before the host, reading flowery verses of praise from a palm leaf. The host lounges on cushions, nodding, his money chest firmly shut beside him. The host is never named.
**Narration:** "In the poet Arunagiri's time, many poets went to rich men's halls and praised them in sweet verses, hoping for a gift. Arunagiri says he did the same once, and that it was a waste of good Tamil."
**Sound:** a sugary, over-sweet melody.

### 2. The measure (20 sec)
**Visual:** The poet flatters: the screen splits. On one side the comfortable rich man; on the other, glowing figures of ancient chieftains standing on their hills.
**Narration:** "He compared these misers to the great givers of old, the chieftains of the Sangam age, whose names every Tamil poet knew. Pari. Kari. Who were they?"
**Sound:** the sweet tune falters; an older, nobler theme begins.

### 3. Pari (25 sec)
**Visual:** Long ago, on the green hill of Parambu, the chieftain Pari rides in his chariot. He sees a jasmine creeper trembling in the wind with nothing to climb on. He stops, leaves his chariot beside it for a support, and walks home smiling.
**Narration:** "Pari was the lord of the hill country of Parambu. Tradition tells that once he saw a jasmine creeper with nothing to cling to. He left his own chariot beside it, so the creeper could climb, and walked home."
**Sound:** wind in leaves; a gentle, open melody.

### 4. The friend (20 sec)
**Visual:** The poet Kapilar, in white, stands on the hill with Pari. Below, three great armies surround the hill. Kapilar speaks to them calmly.
**Narration:** "His friend, the poet Kapilar, sang of him. When three kings came against Pari's hill, Kapilar told them: his land had three hundred villages, and he has given all of them away to those who asked. Only Pari and I are left, and the hill."
**Sound:** distant drums; a steady voice-like tone.

### 5. Pekan (25 sec)
**Visual:** On a cold, rainy hillside a peacock spreads its feathers and shivers. The chieftain Pekan stops his chariot, takes off his own fine shawl, and lays it gently over the bird.
**Narration:** "Then there was Pekan. The poet Paranar sang how Pekan saw a peacock in the cold and gave it his own cloak. He knew a peacock cannot wear a cloak. He gave anyway, because it was in need. He gave, the poet says, like the rain."
**Sound:** soft rain; a tender phrase on strings.

### 6. The line of givers (15 sec)
**Visual:** One by one, more chieftains appear on their hills like lamps being lit: Kari, Ay, Nalli, Kumanan, Ori.
**Narration:** "There were others: Kari, Ay, Nalli, Kumanan, Ori. The old poems praise each one by name."
**Sound:** a lamp-like chime for each name.

### 7. The miser (20 sec)
**Visual:** Back in the hall. The rich man scratches his chin, yawns, and pushes a single small coin across the floor. The money chest stays shut.
**Narration:** "But the man in the hall was no Pari. He listened to the praise, and gave almost nothing. The poet had spent his best words on a closed fist."
**Sound:** one small coin clinking on stone.

### 8. The poet leaves (20 sec)
**Visual:** The poet rolls up his palm leaf, smiles, and walks out into the sunlight, toward a hill temple of Murugan. He begins to sing as he climbs.
**Narration:** "So Arunagiri turned away from such halls. Why praise a miser as a giver, he asks, when Murugan gives freely to all who come? And he went to sing to him instead."
**Sound:** a bright, free melody; temple bells.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri says he wasted his verse comparing misers to Pari, Kari and the other great givers of the Sangam age, and turns to sing of Murugan instead. Songs to show: 169 and 175 (Palani), 306 (Kundruthoradal), 436 (Thiruvannamalai), 935 (Rajapuram).

## Production notes

- **Be fair to the "miser".** He is a humorous background figure, never named.
- **Mark the creeper and the shawl** as tradition and poem.
