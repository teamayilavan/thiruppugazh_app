# The Great Givers (Pari, Kari and the Model Donors)

`t-patrons` | Tamil lore | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: warm, admiring, then wry

## One-line story

The poet says he wasted his verse praising miserly patrons by comparing them to the great givers of the Sangam age, Pari, Kari, Ay, Pekan, Nalli, Kumanan and Ori; this film shows two of those legendary givers.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The poet says he wasted his verse comparing miserly patrons with Pari, Kari and the other great donors | Thiruppugazh songs 169, 175, 306, 436, 935 |
| Sangam poems in the Purananuru praise Pari (poems 105-111, by Kapilar), Kari (121-124, 126), Ay (127-130), Pekan (141-142), Nalli (148-150), Kumanan (151), Ori (204) | Purananuru |
| The songs also name Karna and "twenty-one donors", who are not Sangam patrons | research |

**Tradition note:** the well-known tales of Pari giving his chariot to a jasmine creeper and of Pekan giving a shawl to a shivering peacock belong to the tradition around these poems; the Purananuru poems for Pekan (141-142) are the source for the peacock, and the creeper is later tradition.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Pari:** a hill chieftain, tall, generous, with a gentle face.
- **Pekan:** a chieftain on a chariot, soft-hearted.
- **The poet Kapilar:** Pari's friend, in white.
- **Arunagiri:** a voice, rueful.

## The storyline

### 1. The poet's complaint (20 sec)
A poet sits in a rich man's hall, writing praise; the host is stingy.
**Narration:** "The poet says he wasted his verses on misers."

### 2. The measure (20 sec)
He compares the stingy man to Pari: the screen splits.
**Narration:** "He compared them to the great givers of old."

### 3. Pari (25 sec)
On a hill, Pari sees a jasmine creeper trembling; he leaves his chariot beside it as a support, and walks home.
**Narration:** "Pari, the lord of the hills, gave his chariot to a creeper."

### 4. The friend (20 sec)
Kapilar, the poet, sings of Pari's three hundred villages.
**Narration:** "The poet Kapilar sang of his giving."

### 5. Pekan (25 sec)
On a rainy hill, a peacock opens its feathers and shivers; Pekan gives it his shawl.
**Narration:** "Pekan covered a shivering peacock with his own shawl."

### 6. The line (15 sec)
Other names appear: Kari, Ay, Nalli, Kumanan, Ori.
**Narration:** "There were others: Kari, Ay, Nalli, Kumanan, Ori."

### 7. The miser (20 sec)
Back in the hall, the stingy man scratches his chin.
**Narration:** "But the man in the hall had given nothing."

### 8. The poet leaves (20 sec)
He closes his book and walks out into the sun, singing to Murugan.
**Narration:** "And the poet went away, and sang to the one who gives freely."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Songs 169, 175, 306, 436 and 935.

## Production notes

- **Be fair to the "miser".** He is a humorous background figure, never named.
- **Mark the creeper and the shawl** as tradition and poem.
