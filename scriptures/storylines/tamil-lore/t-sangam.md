# The Mute Boy Who Judged the Poets (Murugan as Rudrasarman)

`t-sangam` | Tamil lore | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: comic, then reverent, then bright | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

When the Sangam poets of Madurai quarrel over whose poems are best, Shiva sends a five-year-old mute boy to judge; the boy shows delight only at true poetry, and the songs say that boy was Murugan.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The Sangam poets of Madurai quarrel over whose poems are best | Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam |
| Shiva sends the five-year-old mute boy Rudrasarman, son of the merchant Dhanapati, to judge | TVP |
| The boy shows his delight only at true poetry and ranks Kapilar, Paranar and Nakkeerar highest | TVP padalam 55 |
| The plank (a magic seat that fits only the worthy) is in padalam 51 | TVP 51 |
| Songs 126, 568 and 987 say Murugan was that boy | Thiruppugazh songs |
| **Legend:** 49 poets disputed the meaning of Shiva's Akapporul, which the boy settled | Tradition (some songs) |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the dialogue, the narration.

## Characters

- **Rudrasarman:** a five-year-old boy, mute, bright-eyed.
- **The poets:** a dozen scholars in white.
- **Kapilar, Paranar, Nakkeerar:** three poets, dignified.
- **Dhanapati:** his father, a merchant.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; lightly comic in the quarrel, hushed and reverent as the boy listens, bright at the end.

### 1. The plank and the poets (20 sec)
**Visual:** Madurai, the temple city, at morning. In a hall near the great temple, poets in white sit along a shining wooden plank that seems to stretch to make room for each worthy poet.
**Narration:** "Long ago in Madurai, the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam says, Shiva gave the poets of the Sangam a wonderful plank to sit on. It was small, but it grew to make room for every worthy poet."
**Sound:** temple bells; a calm, stately theme.

### 2. The quarrel (20 sec)
**Visual:** The poets argue, each holding up his palm leaves, each pointing to his own verses. Voices rise; the plank wobbles. Never cruel, only human.
**Narration:** "But the poets began to quarrel. Each one said his own poems were the best. There was no judge among them, and no one could settle it."
**Sound:** overlapping voices; a comic, tangled rhythm.

### 3. The Lord's answer (25 sec)
**Visual:** The poets go together to the temple and bow before Shiva, Lord Somasundarar. A soft light answers them.
**Narration:** "So they went to the Lord of the temple and asked him to decide. Shiva told them: in this city lives a merchant named Dhanapati. His son is called Rudrasarman. He is not yet five years old, and he has never spoken a word. Take him to your hall. He will judge your poems."
**Sound:** the quarrel fades; a deep, gentle tone.

### 4. The mute boy (25 sec)
**Visual:** A small, bright-eyed boy is led into the hall by the hand and seated before the poets. The poets look at each other, puzzled. The boy sits very still.
**Narration:** "The poets wondered. How could a little child who could not speak judge their poems? But they did not refuse the Lord. They brought the boy, and seated him before them, and began."
**Sound:** a hush; a single small bell.

### 5. The listening (25 sec)
**Visual:** One poet after another recites. At dull verses the boy's face stays still. At a fine word his eyes light up. At a deep meaning, the hair on his arms stands on end. At a verse that has beauty, meaning and music together, his face blossoms and tears of joy fill his eyes.
**Narration:** "One after another the poets recited. At empty verses the boy showed nothing. At a fine word, his eyes shone. At a deep meaning, he trembled with joy. And when a poem had everything, beauty and meaning and music, tears of joy filled his eyes."
**Sound:** each recitation as a different gentle melody.

### 6. The three (25 sec)
**Visual:** The boy's shining eyes rest on three poets: Kapilar, Paranar and Nakkeerar. The other poets turn and look at them, then nod in agreement.
**Narration:** "So, without a word, the boy made his judgment. Three poets stood above all the rest: Kapilar, Paranar and Nakkeerar. And every other poet, too, was honoured as he deserved."
**Sound:** a bright, rising chord.

### 7. Peace in the hall (15 sec)
**Visual:** The poets bow to one another and to the boy. The plank grows steady and long again; everyone has a place.
**Narration:** "The quarrel was over. The poets bowed to the silent child, and then to each other, and once again the plank had room for all."
**Sound:** the stately theme returns, calm.

### 8. Who was the boy? (10 sec)
**Visual:** A golden glow rises behind the boy; for a moment the outline of a peacock and a spear shows in it.
**Narration:** "The Puranam calls him the merchant's son. But the songs of Arunagiri say who that silent child really was: Murugan himself."
**Sound:** a single radiant bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri says that the mute boy who judged the poets of the Madurai Sangam was Murugan. Songs to show: 126 (Palani), 451 (Chidambaram), 568 (Viralimalai), 987 (Odukkathu Cheruvai), 992 (general songs).

## Production notes

- **No mockery of the poets.** The boy is wise, not a prankster.
- **The 49 poets** are tradition: say so.
