# Tied to the Mortar (Damodara)

`krishna-mortar-bound` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: comic, then tender, then moving | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Caught stealing, Krishna sits on an upturned mortar giving butter to monkeys and runs when Yashoda comes with a stick; every rope she brings is two fingers too short until he lets her bind him, and so he is called Damodara.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Krishna:** a small boy.
- **Yashoda:** his mother, tired and loving.
- **The monkeys.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; comic in the chase, slow and tender at the end. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The mischief (20 sec)
**Visual:** Early morning in Nanda's house. Yashoda, in a saffron sari, churns curd, pulling the rope, bangles ringing, singing softly about her son. Little Krishna toddles in, grabs the churning rod and stops it, wanting milk. She laughs, takes him on her lap and feeds him.
**Narration:** "One morning Yashoda sat churning curd, singing to herself about her little son. Krishna came and caught hold of the churning rod. He wanted his milk, and he wanted it now. So she took him on her lap."
**Sound:** the rhythmic swish of churning, a soft song, bangles.

### 2. The mother (20 sec)
**Visual:** On the stove, the milk pot begins to boil over. Yashoda sets Krishna down and runs to it. Krishna's lip trembles, then sets hard. He picks up a stone, cracks the curd pot, and slips into a back room, where butter waits.
**Narration:** "Then the milk on the stove began to boil over, and she put him down and ran. Krishna was furious. He broke the curd pot with a stone, and crept away to a quiet room, where the fresh butter was kept."
**Sound:** hiss of boiling milk, a crack, a trickle, tiptoeing.

### 3. The chase (25 sec)
**Visual:** Yashoda returns to find curd everywhere and no Krishna. She follows small buttery footprints. He sits on an upturned wooden mortar, handing out butter to a row of delighted monkeys, glancing around guiltily. She picks up a stick. He sees her, jumps down and runs. She runs after him, flowers falling from her hair.
**Narration:** "Yashoda came back and saw the broken pot, and knew at once who had done it. She found him sitting on an upturned mortar, feeding butter to the monkeys, and looking about him nervously. She picked up a stick. He jumped down and ran, and she ran after him."
**Sound:** chattering monkeys, a comic chase on drums and flute.

### 4. The catch (20 sec)
**Visual:** Round the courtyard, past the cows, she catches him. He cries; tears and black eye-paint smear across his face as he rubs his eyes. Her face softens. She throws the stick away.
**Narration:** "The sages cannot catch him in their meditation, but his mother caught him. He cried and rubbed his eyes, and the black kohl smeared all over his face. She threw the stick away. But he would not get off so easily."
**Sound:** the chase stops; a small sob; the stick clatters.

### 5. The rope (25 sec)
**Visual:** Yashoda fetches a rope and wraps it around his waist, ready to tie him to the heavy mortar. It is two fingers short. She frowns, ties on another rope. Still two fingers short.
**Narration:** "She decided to tie him to the mortar, so he would stay out of trouble. She wrapped a rope round his little waist. It was just two fingers too short. She tied on another rope. It was still two fingers too short."
**Dialogue:** Yashoda: "How can such a small boy need such a long rope?"
**Sound:** a puzzled plucked note, repeated, rising each time.

### 6. The ropes (25 sec)
**Visual:** A comic montage: every rope in the house, then the neighbours' ropes, knotted into a long coil heaped on the floor. The neighbour women watch and laugh; Yashoda laughs too, but she is sweating, her hair coming loose. Krishna watches her face.
**Narration:** "She joined every rope in the house, and still it was two fingers short. The neighbours laughed, and Yashoda laughed with them. But she was tired now, her face wet with sweat, the flowers falling from her hair. And her little son was watching her."
**Sound:** laughter, a heap of rope thudding, the music slowing.

### 7. The surrender (20 sec)
**Visual:** Close on Krishna's eyes. He sees how tired she is. Quietly, he lets the rope close. It fits exactly. She ties the knot to the mortar and goes back to her work. He sits, bound, smiling to himself.
**Narration:** "When he saw how tired his mother was, he took pity on her, and let himself be tied. The Lord whom the whole world obeys was bound, because his mother loved him."
**Sound:** everything hushes; one warm, sustained chord.

### 8. The name (10 sec)
**Visual:** The rope round his small belly glows softly; the name forms in light above him. In the garden ahead stand two great arjuna trees.
**Narration:** "So he is called Damodara, the one with a rope round his waist."
**Sound:** the churning song, soft; a hint of wind in the two trees.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri returns often to the child bound to the mortar, calling Murugan the nephew of Damodara. Songs to show: 629 (Kundrakudi), which names him Damodaran; 1098 (a general song), where Yashoda ties him and he cries; and 156 and 178 (Palani).

## Production notes

- **The point is love.** He is bound by his mother's love.
- **Comic first, moving last.**
