# The Crow and the Blade of Grass

`ramayana-kaka` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: gentle, tense, merciful | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A crow (Indra's son) pecks at Sita; Rama takes a blade of grass, empowers it as a missile and blinds the crow's eye, sparing its life.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The blinding is shown only as a flash.

## Characters

- **Rama and Sita.**
- **The crow:** a bold black crow.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The rest (25 sec)
**Visual:** Framing device: in the Ashoka grove in Lanka, Sita, thin and grieving, sits under a tree speaking to Hanuman, who kneels before her. As she speaks, the picture dissolves into a warm memory: the hill of Chitrakuta, green and peaceful, a stream nearby. Rama and Sita rest in the shade; Rama sleeps with his head in her lap.
**Narration:** "Far away in Lanka, Sita told Hanuman a story that only she and Rama knew, so that Rama would know the messenger had truly found her. Long ago, she said, we lived on the hill of Chitrakuta. One afternoon Rama fell asleep with his head in my lap."
**Sound:** a gentle stream, birds, a soft flute.

### 2. The crow (20 sec)
**Visual:** A sleek black crow lands on a branch above them, bold and restless. It hops closer, eyes glinting. It is no ordinary crow; a faint shimmer of light around it hints that it is the son of Indra in disguise.
**Narration:** "Then a crow came. It was bold and it would not go away. Valmiki tells us that this was no ordinary bird. It was the son of Indra, king of the gods, in the form of a crow."
**Sound:** a harsh caw; the flute falters.

### 3. The wound (20 sec)
**Visual:** The crow swoops at Sita. She raises her hand to shield herself, careful not to wake Rama. The crow's claws scratch her; a drop of red falls, shown simply as a single small red petal falling onto Rama's cheek.
**Narration:** "The crow flew at me, Sita said, and scratched me with its sharp claws. I did not cry out, because I did not want to wake him. But a drop of my blood fell on his face."
**Sound:** wings beating; a sharp, short note.

### 4. The waking (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rama opens his eyes, touches the drop, and sees Sita's wound and her frightened face. He sits up. His gaze turns to the crow, now perched nearby, unafraid. His face becomes stern.
**Narration:** "Rama woke. He saw that I was hurt, and he saw the crow. His face grew stern. He asked who had dared to harm me."
**Dialogue:** Rama: "Who has done this to you?"
**Sound:** silence, then a low drum.

### 5. The grass (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama reaches down and plucks a single blade of grass from the ground. He holds it, closes his eyes, and speaks a mantra. The blade begins to glow, brighter and brighter, until it shines like a spear of light.
**Narration:** "He had no weapon in his hand. So he plucked a single blade of grass, and spoke over it the mantra of the weapon of Brahma. That little blade of grass began to blaze like fire, and he sent it after the crow."
**Sound:** a deep hum rising, a bright chime.

### 6. The flash (25 sec)
**Visual:** The crow flees across the sky, the shining blade following. It flies to the heavens, to the gods, to its father Indra; each one turns away. At last, exhausted, it comes back and drops at Rama's feet, wings spread. Rama's face softens. The blade strikes with a single flash of light over one eye, and the light fades.
**Narration:** "The crow fled through all the worlds. It went to the gods, and to its own father, but no one could protect it. At last it came back and fell at Rama's feet, asking for shelter. Rama spared its life. But his weapon could not go in vain, so it took one eye, and let the crow live."
**Sound:** rushing wind; then a single soft flash of sound; stillness.

### 7. The mercy (15 sec)
**Visual:** The memory dissolves back to the Ashoka grove. Sita, with tears in her eyes, finishes. Hanuman listens with folded hands.
**Narration:** "For a crow that hurt me, Sita said, he used the weapon of Brahma. Tell him I am still waiting. And Arunagiri sings of the Lord who, in his grace, left the crow one eye."
**Sound:** the flute returns, quiet.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 228 (Swamimalai) recalls how the Lord, in his grace, spared the crow and left it with one eye, and names Murugan as the nephew of that Hari.

## Production notes

- **A film of mercy.** The crow is Indra's son and survives.
- **Quiet.** No cruelty.
