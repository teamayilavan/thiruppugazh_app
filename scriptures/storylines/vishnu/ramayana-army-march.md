# The Army of Monkeys Sets Out

`ramayana-army-march` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: eager, rhythmic, a parade | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Sugriva's vanara army sets out south for Lanka, eager to free Sita.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Sugriva's vanara army sets out south for Lanka | Valmiki Yuddha Kanda 4.62-70 |
| They are eager to free Sita | same |
| Songs 941 and 1231 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Sugriva, Hanuman, Angada, Jambavan.**
- **Rama and Lakshmana.**
- **The monkeys and bears.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The gathering (25 sec)
**Visual:** The hills of Kishkindha at dawn. From every direction, monkeys and bears pour in: leaping down cliffs, swinging through trees, running across plains. Captains arrive with their hosts; one of them, Kesari, leads a host so large it moves like the sea. They crowd around a hill where Rama, Lakshmana and Sugriva stand.
**Narration:** "From every mountain and forest, the monkeys and the bears came to Kishkindha. Captain after captain arrived with his host. Kamban says that one of them, Kesari, the father of Hanuman, came with so many that his army moved like the sea."
**Sound:** a cheerful rising rhythm; the chatter and calls of thousands.

### 2. The order (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sugriva, the monkey king, raises his arm on the hilltop. Rama looks south, toward the far horizon. Hanuman, Angada and the old bear Jambavan stand ready. The host falls silent, then roars.
**Narration:** "Hanuman had found Sita in Lanka. Now there was no more waiting. Sugriva, king of the monkeys, gave the word, and Rama turned his face toward the south."
**Dialogue:** Sugriva: "To the south! To Lanka!"
**Sound:** a single conch, then a great shout.

### 3. The march (25 sec)
**Visual:** The army moves south: a river of fur and banners flowing through forests and over hills. Hanuman carries Rama on his shoulders; Angada carries Lakshmana. Monkeys leap from tree to tree; bears lumber along, grinning. Dust rises in clouds.
**Narration:** "And so the great army set out. Valmiki says they went south in high spirits, eager for Sita's freedom. Hanuman carried Rama on his shoulders, and Angada carried Lakshmana. The dust they raised rose up to the sky."
**Sound:** marching drums made of footsteps and branches; a bright, rhythmic tune.

### 4. The songs (20 sec)
**Visual:** Playful moments along the way. Young monkeys race ahead, pick fruit and pass it back; some leap over each other in joy; a bear hums as he walks. Captains wave them back into line, smiling.
**Narration:** "They shouted and sang as they went. They leapt and played and ate the fruit of the forests. Their captains kept them in order, but nobody could keep them quiet. They were going to bring Sita home."
**Sound:** laughter, calls, a playful melody on flute.

### 5. The mountains (20 sec)
**Visual:** The host crosses great ranges, flowing over ridges like water. Forests, rivers and valleys pass. The army never thins; it fills every path.
**Narration:** "They crossed mountains and rivers, forests and valleys. Day after day they marched, and still the army stretched as far as the eye could see."
**Sound:** the rhythm deepens; wind over high ridges.

### 6. The sea (25 sec)
**Visual:** The army crests the last ridge, and below lies the endless southern ocean, glittering. The host spreads along the shore and fills it. Rama walks to the water's edge and looks out. The sun sets; on the water, lotuses close their petals for the night.
**Narration:** "At last they came to the edge of the land. Before them lay the ocean, vast and deep. The army covered the whole shore. Rama stood at the water's edge and gazed out across the waves. Kamban says he watched the lotuses close for the night, and thought of Sita."
**Sound:** the march stops; great waves; a soft, longing melody.

### 7. The pause (15 sec)
**Visual:** Night on the shore. Countless campfires along the beach. Rama stands alone, looking toward the far, unseen island of Lanka. Lakshmana comes and stands beside him.
**Narration:** "The march was over. Now only the sea stood between them and Lanka. Arunagiri sings of the Lord who sent this army on its way."
**Sound:** waves; one quiet conch.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Two songs recall the great march, naming Murugan the nephew of the Lord who sent Sugriva's army to Lanka. Show song 941 (Pattaliyur) and song 1231 (a general song, Pothu Padalgal).

## Production notes

- **A cheerful march.** Keep the monkeys playful.
