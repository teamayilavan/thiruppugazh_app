# A Handful of Beaten Rice (Sudama)

`krishna-sudama` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: poor, tender, overflowing | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The poor Brahmin friend Sudama (Kuchela) visits Krishna at Dwaraka.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Sudama:** a thin, humble Brahmin in rags.
- **His wife:** gentle, practical.
- **Krishna:** a king, dazzling, warm.
- **Rukmini.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; quiet and plain at first, overflowing with warmth in Dwaraka. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The poor home (25 sec)
**Visual:** A small hut with a patched roof. Inside, a thin brahmin in a worn cloth sits in peaceful prayer. His wife, thin too, looks at the empty cooking pot, then at him, and gathers her courage.
**Narration:** "There was once a brahmin, learned and gentle, whose heart was at peace. He lived on whatever came to him by chance, and so he and his wife were very poor, and often hungry. Tradition calls him Sudama, or Kuchela, the man in the worn cloth."
**Sound:** a single veena, slow; birds outside.

### 2. The gift (20 sec)
**Visual:** The wife speaks softly, trembling. He listens, then nods slowly. She goes from house to house among the neighbours, returning with four small handfuls of beaten rice, which she ties in a torn piece of cloth.
**Dialogue:** His wife: "Isn't the Lord of Dwaraka your own friend? Go to him. He is kind to all who come."
**Narration:** "He thought, to see Krishna again would be the greatest gift of all. But he could not go with empty hands. So his wife begged four handfuls of beaten rice from the neighbours and tied them in an old cloth."
**Sound:** footsteps, a door, the rustle of rice.

### 3. The journey (20 sec)
**Visual:** The brahmin walks a long dusty road, the little bundle held close. He crosses rivers and fields. At last Dwaraka rises by the sea, all gold and white towers. He stops, small and doubtful.
**Narration:** "He walked the long road to Dwaraka, holding the little bundle against his chest. And all the way he wondered: how will someone like me ever see the king?"
**Sound:** wind, footsteps, the distant sound of the sea.

### 4. The gate (20 sec)
**Visual:** He passes three guard posts and three gates with other brahmins, and walks among great mansions. Guards glance at his rags. He enters the palace of Rukmini. Across a shining hall, Krishna sits on his queen's couch.
**Narration:** "He passed through three guarded gates and walked among the great houses of the Yadavas. At last he came into the palace where Krishna sat with his queen Rukmini."
**Sound:** echoing footsteps on marble, soft palace music.

### 5. The welcome (25 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna sees him from far off. He leaps up, runs across the hall and throws his arms around him; tears stand in Krishna's eyes. He seats his friend on the couch, washes his feet and sprinkles the water on his own head. Rukmini fans him. The palace people stare at each other in wonder.
**Narration:** "Krishna saw him from far away. He jumped up, ran to his friend and held him close, and tears of love came to his eyes. He seated him on his own couch and washed his feet with his own hands, and Rukmini herself fanned him. The whole palace stared. Who was this poor man, served by the Lord of the world?"
**Sound:** the music swells; a soft gasp from the courtiers.

### 6. The rice (25 sec)
**Visual:** Hand in hand, they laugh, remembering their school days: a painted flashback of two boys lost in a stormy forest gathering firewood for their teacher, holding hands in the rain. Back in the hall, the brahmin hides his bundle. Krishna, smiling, takes it from him, opens it, and eats a handful of the rice with delight.
**Narration:** "Hand in hand they talked of the days when they had studied together with their teacher, and of the night they were lost in a storm gathering firewood. The brahmin was too shy to offer his little gift. But Krishna found the bundle, opened it, and ate the beaten rice with joy."
**Dialogue:** Krishna: "My friend, what have you brought me from home?"
**Sound:** laughter, rain in the flashback, then quiet.

### 7. The silence (15 sec)
**Visual:** Next morning. The brahmin walks home along the road, empty-handed, smiling to himself.
**Narration:** "He stayed the night and left in the morning. He had asked for nothing. He walked home full of joy, only to have seen his friend."
**Sound:** birdsong, a light veena.

### 8. The home (15 sec)
**Visual:** Where his hut stood, a shining house rises among gardens. His wife, in fine clothes, comes out to meet him. He looks at the house, then back toward Dwaraka, and folds his hands.
**Narration:** "But where his hut had been, he found a shining home, and his wife coming out to greet him. His friend had given everything, without a word."
**Sound:** the welcome theme returns, full and warm.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers this friendship in one line, calling Murugan the nephew of the flute-playing Gopala whose love for his friend never changed. Song to show: 355 (Thiruvanaikka).

## Production notes

- **A friendship film.** Krishna is a friend first.
- **Do not make Sudama's poverty shameful.**
