# The Ogress and the Baby (Putana)

`krishna-putana` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: ominous, comic-eerie, then triumphant | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The ogress Putana, sent by Kamsa, tries to nurse the infant Krishna with poisoned milk, and he drinks out her life.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The ogress Putana, sent by Kamsa, tries to nurse the infant Krishna with poisoned milk | Bhagavata Purana 10.6.2-13 |
| He drinks her life out | same |
| Songs 626 and 1001 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The death is shown through shadow and light.

## Characters

- **Putana:** a demoness who looks like a lovely woman, then a vast shadow.
- **Baby Krishna:** a baby, dark and smiling.
- **Yashoda:** his mother, warm.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; hushed and uneasy at first, bright at the end. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The village (20 sec)
**Visual:** Gokula at dusk: cows coming home, lamps being lit, smoke curling from roofs. Mothers carry babies; children chase calves. In Nanda's house, a dark baby sleeps in a cradle, one hand curled by his cheek.
**Narration:** "In the cowherd village of Gokula, the baby Krishna slept in Nanda's house. Far away in Mathura, King Kamsa feared a child who would end his reign, and he had sent a demoness, Putana, to kill the babies of the land."
**Sound:** cowbells, evening birds, a soft lullaby hum.

### 2. The visitor (25 sec)
**Visual:** A shape glides through the sky, then touches down at the village edge as a beautiful woman in fine clothes, jasmine in her hair, earrings flashing, smiling at everyone. The villagers stare at her beauty. Only her shadow on the wall is wrong: too long, too thin.
**Narration:** "She came through the sky, and at the edge of the village she took the form of a lovely woman, dressed in fine clothes, with jasmine in her hair. She smiled at everyone she passed, and no one thought to stop her. The Bhagavata calls her a sharp sword in a soft sheath."
**Sound:** a sweet melody, with one note slightly out of tune.

### 3. The cradle (20 sec)
**Visual:** She enters Nanda's house. Yashoda and Rohini, dazzled, step aside, thinking her a kind visitor. The baby opens his eyes for an instant, sees her, and closes them again, as if asleep. She lifts him onto her lap.
**Narration:** "She walked straight into Nanda's house. Yashoda and Rohini, charmed by her, let her be, for she held the child as tenderly as a mother. The baby knew why she had come. He only closed his eyes."
**Sound:** the lullaby, quieter; a creak of the cradle.

### 4. The nursing (25 sec)
**Visual:** Seen from behind her shoulder, never closely: she draws the baby to her breast to feed him. A faint green glow of poison at the edge of the frame. The baby's tiny hands grip hard. Her smile freezes.
**Narration:** "She had smeared her breast with poison, and she gave it to the baby to drink. But the child she held was the Lord. With both his little hands he held on, and drank, and with the milk he drew out her very life."
**Sound:** the lullaby stops; a low, slow pulse.

### 5. The scream (25 sec)
**Visual:** Her beautiful form flickers. She staggers out into the yard, crying out, arms flailing, hair loosened, trying to pull the baby away. The lamps shake; birds burst from the trees; people fall to the ground covering their ears. The baby holds on, calm.
**Dialogue:** Putana: "Let me go! Let me go! Drink no more!"
**Narration:** "Her cry shook the earth and the sky. The villagers fell down in fear, thinking thunder had struck. But the baby would not let go."
**Sound:** a single long cry, made musical, then wind.

### 6. The fall (20 sec)
**Visual:** In silhouette against the setting sun, a vast shadow crashes across the pasture, flattening trees for miles. Dust rises and settles. No face, no detail: only a huge dark shape, still.
**Narration:** "She fell in her true form, so vast that she flattened the trees for miles around. And so ended the ogress who had come to kill children."
**Sound:** a deep crash, then silence; a single wooden flute note.

### 7. The baby (15 sec)
**Visual:** The gopis run across the dust. On top of the fallen shadow sits the baby, playing happily, unafraid. Yashoda snatches him up and holds him tight; the women wave a cow's tail over him for protection. Later, smoke rises from the burning body, and people far away turn, wondering at its sweet fragrance.
**Narration:** "The women found him playing, as if nothing had happened, and Yashoda held him close. And when they burned the body, the smoke smelled sweet, for even she had fed the Lord."
**Sound:** joyful bells, the lullaby returning, warm and full.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers Putana in two songs, praising Murugan as the nephew of the Lord who, as a baby, drank the ogress's life with her milk. Songs to show: 626 (Kundrakudi) and 1001 (a general song).

## Production notes

- **Keep the monster stylised.** Avoid anything frightening to young viewers.
- **The baby is never in danger on screen.**
