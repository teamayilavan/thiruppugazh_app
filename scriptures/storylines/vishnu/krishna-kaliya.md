# Dancing on the Serpent (Kaliya)

`krishna-kaliya` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: dread, a dance, release | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Krishna jumps into the poisoned Yamuna, dances on the hoods of the five-headed serpent Kaliya, and sends him away.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Krishna:** a boy, fearless.
- **Kaliya:** a vast black serpent with many hoods (the Bhagavata speaks of many heads; art often shows five), proud, then humbled.
- **Kaliya's wives:** who plead.
- **The cowherd boys and cattle.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The poisoned river (25 sec)
**Visual:** A deep pool in the Yamuna near Vrindavan. The water is dark and steaming; a greenish mist hangs over it. A bird flying across falls into the water. On the banks the grass is grey and dead. The cowherd boys keep their cows well away and look at the pool with fear.
**Narration:** "In one deep pool of the Yamuna lived the great serpent Kaliya. His poison made the water boil and steam. Birds flying over it fell into the water, and the wind from it withered the grass and trees on the bank. Nobody in Vrindavan dared go near."
**Sound:** a low bubbling hiss; a hot, still wind.

### 2. The leap (20 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna looks at the poisoned water, tightens his sash, and climbs a tall kadamba tree on the bank. From the top branch he leaps, arms wide, high into the air, and plunges into the pool. The water rises in a great wave and spills over the banks.
**Narration:** "Krishna saw the poisoned river and decided to make it pure again. He tied his sash tight, climbed a tall kadamba tree on the bank, and jumped from the top, straight into the poisoned water."
**Sound:** a moment of silence, then a mighty splash.

### 3. The coils (25 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna splashes and plays in the pool like a young elephant. The water darkens. Kaliya rises: a vast black serpent with many hoods, eyes burning, poison on his breath. He strikes at Krishna's chest and wraps him in his coils. Krishna lies still in the coils.
**Narration:** "He played in the deep water like a young elephant, splashing and laughing. Kaliya heard the noise and rose up in anger, a black serpent with many hoods. He struck at Krishna's chest, and wrapped him tight in his coils. And Krishna lay still."
**Sound:** heavy water, a rising hiss, then a low drone.

### 4. The villagers' fear (20 sec)
**Visual:** On the bank the cowherd boys fall to the ground in grief. The cows cry and stand frozen. The whole village comes running: Yashoda and Nanda rush toward the water, and Balarama, calm, holds them back, for he knows who his brother is.
**Narration:** "On the bank his friends fell to the ground in grief. The cows stood frozen, crying. The whole village came running. His mother Yashoda wanted to rush into the water. Only Balarama stayed calm, and held them back. He knew who his brother was."
**Sound:** cries, lowing, running feet; then a hush.

### 5. The rise (20 sec)
**Visual:** In the pool, Krishna's body begins to grow. The coils strain and loosen. He slips free and dances lightly away. Kaliya rears up, hoods spread, and circles him, hissing. Krishna circles too, round and round, until the serpent tires.
**Narration:** "Then, in the coils, Krishna began to grow, until the serpent could hold him no longer. He slipped free. Kaliya reared up, hoods spread, ready to strike, and the two circled each other round and round, until the great serpent grew tired."
**Sound:** the drone lifts; a quick rhythm begins on a small drum.

### 6. The dance (30 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna leaps onto Kaliya's hoods and begins to dance, his feet striking the hoods in time. Whenever a hood rises in anger, he dances it down. The heavens open: gods and singers play drums and flutes and shower flowers. Rhythm builds; the water churns in circles. At last the hoods sink low and still. The serpent is beaten but alive.
**Narration:** "Then Krishna leapt up onto the serpent's hoods and began to dance. Tha-thimi, tha-thimi, his small feet kept time on the great hoods. Whenever one head rose in anger, he danced it down again. The gods in the sky played drums and threw flowers. And Kaliya's strength drained away, until he knew at last who was dancing on his heads."
**Sound:** a full dance rhythm, drums and cymbals, rising to a peak, then easing.

### 7. The prayer (15 sec)
**Visual:** Kaliya's wives, serpent queens with folded hands, rise from the water and bow before Krishna. They plead for their husband. Kaliya, humbled, lowers his heads and folds himself before the boy.
**Narration:** "Kaliya's wives came up from the water with folded hands and begged for their husband's life. Your punishment is a mercy, they said. And Kaliya bowed, and prayed."
**Dialogue:** Kaliya: "Lord, forgive me. I take shelter in you."
**Sound:** the dance music falls to a gentle melody.

### 8. The sending (10 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna steps down onto the bank. Kaliya and his family offer garlands and jewels, circle him, bow, and swim away down the river toward the sea. The water turns clear and blue. The village cheers.
**Narration:** "Krishna spared him and sent him away to the sea. And the Yamuna ran sweet and clear again."
**Sound:** clear water, birds, the village's joy.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

For Arunagiri the scene is above all a dance, and he gives its rhythm in syllables. Songs 163 (Palani), 620 (Poomparai), 881 (Thirukkurangaduthurai) and 1099 are the ones to show: the Lord dancing on the serpent's hoods. Murugan is his nephew.

## Production notes

- **A great dance sequence.** Make the rhythm the heart.
- **Mercy.** The serpent leaves alive.
