# The Child on the Banyan Leaf (Pralaya)

`avatar-pralaya` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: hushed, vast, tender | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The sage Markandeya asks to see the Lord's power of illusion; he is shown the flood that ends the worlds, and in it a child on a banyan leaf who holds the whole universe within him.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Markandeya, having pleased the Lord, asks to see his power of illusion; "So be it" | Bhagavata Purana 12.9.1-7 |
| Wind, rain and the rising oceans cover everything; the sage wanders alone in the flood for ages | 12.9.10-19 |
| A small island, a young banyan tree, an infant lying in a leaf whose light swallows the dark | 12.9.20-21 |
| The emerald-dark child, smiling, with his toe in his mouth | 12.9.22-25 |
| The child breathes in; the sage finds the whole universe inside him, then is breathed out | 12.9.27-30 |
| The loving glance; the sage runs to embrace him; all vanishes and he is back in his hermitage | 12.9.31-34 |
| The Lord who swallowed the worlds and lay on a banyan leaf | Mudhal Thiruvandhadhi (Poigai Alvar) 19 |
| Songs 276, 402, 727, 970 and 1103 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Note:** in the Bhagavata this is a vision the Lord grants Markandeya, and the narration keeps it so. The songs keep only the child on the leaf and the swallowed worlds; they do not name the sage.
**Added for the screen:** the look of the scenes, the timing, and the wording of the narration; "So be it" follows 12.9.7.

## Characters

- **Markandeya:** an old sage, white matted hair, thin and gentle; tired in the flood, radiant at the end.
- **The child:** a baby, dark blue like an emerald, lying in a banyan leaf, sucking his toe.
- **The banyan tree:** young, in flower and fruit, on a tiny island.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The sage's wish (25 sec)
**Visual:** A hermitage in the Himalayan foothills beside a clear river, the Pushpabhadra. An old sage, Markandeya, white-haired and thin, kneels in worship. A soft radiance appears before him; he bows. The radiance smiles and fades.
**Narration:** "The sage Markandeya had worshipped the Lord all his life. When the Lord appeared to him and offered him a gift, he asked for only one thing: to see the Lord's power of illusion, the power that makes the world seem as it does."
**Dialogue:** The Lord: "So be it."
**Sound:** a quiet river, a temple bell, a soft held chord.

### 2. The flood (25 sec)
**Visual:** Evening at the same river. A sudden wind bends the trees; black clouds roll in with lightning; rain falls in sheets. The sea rises on every side, swallowing islands and mountains, until only water remains. The sage, his matted hair loose, struggles alone in the waves.
**Narration:** "One evening, as he prayed by the river, a great wind arose. Dark clouds poured down rain, and the oceans rose on every side, until they covered the islands, the mountains and the sky. Of all the beings in the world, only the sage remained, wandering alone in the endless water."
**Sound:** wind and thunder building, then the roar of the sea.

### 3. The leaf (20 sec)
**Visual:** Time passes in the grey waters. Then a small island appears, and on it a young banyan tree in flower and fruit. On one branch lies a single leaf, and from it comes a gentle light.
**Narration:** "Ages passed. Then, far out in the flood, he saw a small island, and on it a young banyan tree, in flower and in fruit. On one of its branches lay a single leaf, and from it came a gentle light."
**Sound:** the storm falls away; a soft lullaby melody begins.

### 4. The child (25 sec)
**Visual:** On the leaf lies an infant, dark blue like an emerald, smiling, holding one foot in his hands with a toe in his mouth. His light pushes back the dark. The sage's tired face softens; he wades closer.
**Narration:** "On the leaf lay a baby, dark as an emerald, with a smile that lit the darkness. He had taken hold of his own foot and was sucking his toe. At the sight of him all the sage's weariness vanished, and he came closer, wondering who this child could be."
**Sound:** the lullaby, with a gentle cradle rhythm.

### 5. The world within (25 sec)
**Visual:** The child breathes in, and the sage is drawn in like a tiny insect. Inside: the whole universe, sky and stars, mountains and rivers, villages and pastures, and the sage's own hermitage by the river. The child breathes out, and the sage tumbles back into the flood.
**Narration:** "Then the child breathed in, and the sage was drawn inside him like a mosquito. Within that small body he found the whole universe, as it had been before the flood: the stars, the mountains, the rivers, even his own hermitage. Then the child breathed out, and he was back in the water."
**Sound:** a rush of air; inside, a shimmering, wondering music; out again with a splash.

### 6. The smile (15 sec)
**Visual:** The child glances at him from the corner of his eye, smiling with love. The sage runs through the water, arms open, to hold him; as he reaches the leaf, the child is gone.
**Narration:** "The child looked at him sideways, smiling with love. The sage ran to hold him, and in that moment the child was gone."
**Sound:** the lullaby breaks off on one unfinished note.

### 7. The hermitage (15 sec)
**Visual:** The flood, the island and the tree all vanish. Markandeya sits in his own hermitage by the river at evening, a lamp burning, exactly as before. He folds his hands and smiles.
**Narration:** "The flood was gone too. The sage sat in his own hermitage, as before. He had seen it: the whole world, held safe inside the Lord."
**Sound:** the river, the bell from the first scene; the lullaby completes its last note.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri keeps only the picture: the Lord who swallowed the worlds and slept on a banyan leaf. Songs to show: 970 (Sri Purushamangai), the Mayan who once slept on a banyan leaf; 276 (Thiruthani), the tall one who holds the earth in his belly; 402 (Thiruvannamalai), who once swallowed all the worlds; and 727 (Siruvai), who rested on the banyan and kept the worlds steady.
