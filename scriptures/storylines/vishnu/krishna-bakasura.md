# The Crane Demon (Bakasura)

`krishna-bakasura` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: eerie, then quick, then relieved | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The demon Baka, in the form of a huge crane, swallows Krishna, who burns in its throat until it spits him out; Krishna then splits its beak.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Krishna and his friends** (cowherd boys).
- **Baka:** a giant white crane, still as stone.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The riverbank (20 sec)
**Visual:** Morning in the pastures of Vraja. A crowd of small cowherd boys drives the calves down to a still pond. Krishna, dark and bright-eyed, with a peacock feather in his hair, runs ahead with his elder brother Balarama. The calves drink; the boys kneel and drink too, splashing one another.
**Narration:** "One morning Krishna and his brother Balarama took the calves down to a pond with all their friends. The calves drank, and then the boys drank too, laughing and splashing. It was an ordinary day in Vraja, or so it seemed."
**Sound:** cowbells, children's laughter, water.

### 2. The hill that moves (25 sec)
**Visual:** At the edge of the water stands something white and enormous, like a mountain peak broken off by lightning. The boys stare. Slowly the white hill turns: it has an eye, a long neck, a long sharp beak. It is a crane as large as a hill.
**Narration:** "At the edge of the water the boys saw something huge and white, like the peak of a mountain broken off by a thunderbolt. Then the peak moved. It was a crane, but a crane as tall as a hill. It was the demon Baka, who had taken the shape of a great bird with a very sharp beak."
**Sound:** the laughter stops; a long, thin, eerie note.

### 3. The swallow (20 sec)
**Visual:** The neck strikes down like a falling tree. The beak opens over Krishna and closes. Where Krishna stood there is only a ripple on the water. The crane straightens, its throat bulging.
**Narration:** "Before anyone could move, the great neck came down. The beak opened, and closed, and Krishna was gone. The demon had swallowed him whole."
**Sound:** a rush of air, a snap, then silence.

### 4. The fear (20 sec)
**Visual:** The boys freeze. Balarama stands with his mouth open. The children's faces go pale and still, as if all life has left them. The calves huddle together.
**Narration:** "Balarama and the boys stood frozen. They could not cry out; they could not even think. They felt as if their own lives had gone out of their bodies. Without Krishna, nothing in the world made sense to them."
**Sound:** a heartbeat, slow; a calf lowing far away.

### 5. The burning (25 sec)
**Visual:** Inside the dark throat, a small golden glow. It grows brighter and hotter until the long white neck shines from within like a lamp. The crane shakes, stretches, flaps its wings in distress.
**Narration:** "But inside the dark throat, a light began to glow. Krishna burned there like a fire, hotter and hotter, until the root of the demon's throat was burning. The great bird shook and twisted. It could not hold him."
**Sound:** a rising hum, like a flame drawn up a chimney; the crane's harsh cry.

### 6. The spit (25 sec)
**Visual:** The crane throws its head forward and spits Krishna out onto the grass. He lands on his feet, unhurt, and brushes his hair from his eyes. The crane, furious, lifts its beak to strike again.
**Narration:** "With a cry, the crane threw him out onto the grass. Krishna stood up, unhurt, as if nothing had happened. But the demon was not finished. In its rage it raised its sharp beak and came at him again."
**Dialogue:** Balarama: "Krishna!"
**Sound:** a whoosh, a soft landing; then the beak whistling down.

### 7. The beak (15 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna catches the upper and lower halves of the beak in his two small hands. A flash of light: the beak parts like a blade of grass, and the great white shape fades like mist. Flowers fall from the sky; drums and conches sound from the clouds. The boys run and hug him, gather the calves and run home to tell everyone.
**Narration:** "Krishna caught the two halves of the beak in his hands and split it apart, as easily as a child splits a blade of grass. Flowers fell from the sky. His friends ran to hold him, and then ran home to tell everyone in Vraja."
**Sound:** one bright chord; drums and conches far above; laughter returns.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the crane once, in song 626 (Kunrakkudi), among the messengers Kamsa sent against the child Krishna: the ogress Putana and the wide-mouthed crane. Murugan is the nephew of the Lord who overcame them.

## Production notes

- **Not frightening.** Treat as a quick adventure.
- **No gore.** The beak splits in a flash of light.
