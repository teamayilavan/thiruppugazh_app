# The Fire Ordeal (Sita and Agni)

`ramayana-sita-agni` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: grave, painful, then luminous | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Rama asks Sita to prove her purity; she enters the fire, and Agni returns her unharmed.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it. Treat this with great care: the film follows the traditional texts and does not explain or defend them. Sita's dignity is the centre; Rama is grave and grieving, never cruel. The owner may still choose to omit or reframe this film.

### 1. The request (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama's camp, the morning Sita has come. A great crowd of monkeys, bears and demons stands in a wide circle. Rama faces Sita across the open space, his face heavy, not angry. He speaks, and the crowd goes very still. Lakshmana looks down at the ground.
**Narration:** "Sita stood before Rama at last. But Rama did not come forward to take her hand. With a heavy face, before the whole army, he spoke hard words. She had lived a long time in another's house, he said. The world would ask questions. Valmiki tells it plainly, and it is painful to hear."
**Sound:** the crowd's murmur falls away; one low, aching note.

### 2. Sita's silence (20 sec)
**Visual:** Close on Sita. Tears run down her face, but she stands straight. She wipes her eyes and lifts her head. When she speaks, her voice is quiet and clear.
**Narration:** "Sita wept, but she did not bow her head in shame, for she had nothing to be ashamed of. She answered him quietly. Her body had been taken by force, she said. But her heart had never left him, not for a moment."
**Dialogue:** Sita: "My heart was always yours. That was in my power, and I kept it."
**Sound:** silence under her voice.

### 3. The pyre (25 sec)
**Visual:** Sita turns to Lakshmana. He looks at Rama; Rama says nothing. Weeping, Lakshmana gathers wood and builds a fire, carefully, in the proper way. The flames rise tall and bright in the middle of the circle.
**Narration:** "Then she turned to Lakshmana and asked him to build a fire. Lakshmana looked at his brother, and his brother was silent. Kamban says Lakshmana built it weeping, with his breath caught in his throat, as if his own life had been taken from him."
**Dialogue:** Sita: "Lakshmana, build me a fire. Let the fire be my witness."
**Sound:** crackling wood, the rising roar of the flames.

### 4. The walk (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sita folds her hands toward Rama and bows. Then she turns to the fire and speaks to it, calm and unafraid. She walks forward into the flames, her figure becoming a silhouette of light. The crowd cries out.
**Narration:** "Sita bowed to Rama. Then she spoke to the fire itself. If I have ever been untrue, she said, in my mind or in my words, then burn me, O fire. And she walked into the flames."
**Dialogue:** Sita: "If I have erred in thought or word, then burn me, O Fire."
**Sound:** the roar of fire, a cry from the crowd, then the roar alone.

### 5. The wait (20 sec)
**Visual:** The fire blazes. Faces in the crowd: Hanuman with his hands over his eyes, Sugriva frozen, Lakshmana fallen to his knees. Rama stands motionless, his eyes on the flames, tears on his face.
**Narration:** "The whole army wept aloud. Hanuman could not look. Lakshmana fell to his knees. And Rama stood like a stone, his eyes on the fire, and the tears ran down his face."
**Sound:** the flames, then even they seem to fall quiet; a long held note.

### 6. Agni (25 sec)
**Visual:** The flames part. From the heart of the fire rises Agni, the god of fire, a radiant figure of gold and red, his own body blazing. In his arms he carries Sita, unharmed, her silk unburnt, the flowers fresh in her hair. He steps out of the fire and sets her before Rama.
**Narration:** "Then the flames parted, and out of the fire rose Agni, the god of fire himself. Kamban says he came carrying Sita in his hands, his hands joined in reverence, his own body burning as if the fire could not bear her purity. Not a thread of her clothes was burnt; not a flower in her hair had faded."
**Sound:** a great soft whoosh, then a pure, clear chord.

### 7. The truth (25 sec)
**Visual:** Agni speaks to Rama, one hand raised. Rama steps forward at last and takes Sita's hands. The crowd bows. The gods appear in the sky and let fall flowers. Sita and Rama stand together, quiet, in soft gold light.
**Narration:** "Agni gave Sita back to Rama, and told him before all the world that she was pure, in thought, in word and in deed. Then Rama took her hand. Valmiki says he had never doubted her himself, but the whole world had needed to see. And now the whole world had seen."
**Dialogue:** Agni: "Here is your Sita. She is without stain. Take her back."
**Sound:** bells, a gentle rising melody, flowers falling.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

The link to the Thiruppugazh is slight. Song 161 (Palani) speaks of entering the fire, but read closely it is Ravana with his army and kin who go into the fire while Vibhishana is crowned; Murugan is the nephew of Raghupati Ramachandra. Arunagiri does not otherwise tell Sita's ordeal.
