# The Tortoise Under the Mountain (Kurma)

`avatar-kurma` | Vishnu | running time 2:35 (7 scenes) | tone: heavy, patient, steady | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

As the gods and demons churn the Ocean of Milk, the mountain begins to sink; Vishnu becomes a tortoise and holds it on his back.

## Characters

- **Kurma:** a vast golden tortoise, patient, wise.
- **The gods and demons.**
- **Mount Mandara.**
- **Vasuki.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The churning begins (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Ocean of Milk at dawn. Mount Mandara stands in the sea, the serpent Vasuki wound around it. On one side the gods hold the tail, on the other the demons hold the head. They pull, and the mountain turns.
**Narration:** "The gods and the demons had made peace for one purpose: to churn the Ocean of Milk and draw out the nectar of immortality. The mountain Mandara was their churning rod, and the great serpent Vasuki was their rope. With a shout, they began to pull."
**Sound:** a heavy, rhythmic chant; the creak of the turning mountain.

### 2. The sinking (25 sec)
**Visual:** Under the water, the base of the mountain has nothing to rest on. With each turn it grinds deeper. The peak tilts; the surface swirls; the pullers stagger as the rope drags downward.
**Narration:** "But the sea has no floor that a mountain can stand on. With every turn, Mandara sank a little deeper. However strong their arms, the gods and demons could not hold it up, and slowly the great mountain slid down into the milk."
**Sound:** the chant falters; a deep groaning from below.

### 3. The fear (20 sec)
**Visual:** Faces on both sides fall. A god lets go of the rope; a demon king stares at the swirling water where the peak is vanishing. Silence on the shore.
**Narration:** "Their faces fell. All their work, all their hope of nectar, was going down into the deep. The Bhagavata says their faces seemed to wither, like flowers in the heat."
**Sound:** the music drops away to a single low note.

### 4. The Lord's resolve (20 sec)
**Visual:** Vishnu stands watching on the shore, calm. He looks at the sinking mountain, then steps toward the water. Light gathers around him as he enters the sea.
**Narration:** "But the Lord was watching. He saw what had happened, and his purpose never fails. Without a word, he stepped into the sea and went down beneath the sinking mountain."
**Sound:** a soft, steady drumbeat begins, like a heartbeat.

### 5. The tortoise (30 sec)
**Visual:** Deep under the milk, a vast shape rises: a tortoise, its shell wide as an island, golden-brown and patterned like old stone. It slides beneath the mountain's base, and the whole mountain rises on its back, up and up, until the peak stands tall again above the waves.
**Narration:** "Deep in the ocean, the Lord took the form of a tortoise, a tortoise so vast that his shell stretched like a great island. He slid beneath the mountain and lifted it on his back. Up came Mandara, higher and higher, until it stood firm once more above the waves."
**Sound:** the heartbeat grows into a broad, rising theme; water pouring off the stone.

### 6. The steadiness (25 sec)
**Visual:** The churning resumes, fast and strong. The mountain spins on the tortoise's back; the tortoise's eyes half close in contentment. On the shore, gods and demons cheer and pull with new strength.
**Narration:** "Seeing the mountain rise, the gods and demons took heart and began again. The mountain spun round and round on the tortoise's back. The Bhagavata smiles at it: to the Lord, the grinding of the mountain felt like a pleasant scratching of his shell."
**Sound:** the chant returns, joyful; a gentle, almost humorous low phrase for the tortoise.

### 7. The sea (10 sec)
**Visual:** The camera rises above the turning mountain and the white, foaming sea. Beneath the surface, the faint outline of the shell, unmoving.
**Narration:** "Above, all was effort. Below, the Lord held everything, unmoving and unseen."
**Sound:** the churning rhythm continues, fading; one low, steady note remains.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the tortoise only in passing. Songs to show: 1272 (general songs), which lists the Lord's forms as tortoise, fish, dwarf and lion; and 723 (Thiruvakkarai), the Lord who set the mountain in the sea, wound the serpent round it and raised the nectar.
