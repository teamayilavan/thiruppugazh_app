# The Uncle Who Feared a Prophecy (Kamsa's Malice)

`krishna-kamsa-malice` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: ominous, tragic, then hopeful | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Kamsa, Krishna's maternal uncle, persecutes his sister Devaki's newborn children.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Kamsa persecutes his sister Devaki's newborn children | Bhagavata Purana 10.4.1-5 |
| Songs 181 and 1274 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |
| The prophecy that the eighth child will end him, and Krishna's birth in the prison, are the widely told frame | Bhagavata Purana, canto 10 (traditional source; not read verse by verse here) |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The persecution is shown by shadow and sound; no child is seen harmed.

## Characters

- **Kamsa:** a proud king, afraid.
- **Devaki:** his sister, gentle, steadfast.
- **Vasudeva:** her husband, quiet strength.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The wedding (25 sec)
**Visual:** Mathura, a bright wedding day. Princess Devaki, gentle and smiling, sits beside her new husband Vasudeva in a decorated chariot. Her brother, King Kamsa, proud and strong, takes the reins himself to drive them home. Drums, conches, flowers thrown from the rooftops.
**Narration:** "In the city of Mathura, Princess Devaki was married to Vasudeva. Her brother, King Kamsa, loved her, and to show it he took the reins of the wedding chariot himself, and drove the couple through the streets, with music all around them."
**Sound:** wedding drums and conches, a cheerful crowd.

### 2. The voice (20 sec)
**Visual:** The sky darkens over the chariot. A voice comes from nowhere. The music stops. Kamsa turns slowly; his face changes from joy to fear, then rage. He seizes Devaki by the hair and raises his sword. Vasudeva catches his arm, pleading.
**Narration:** "Suddenly a voice spoke from the sky. Kamsa's face turned white with fear, then dark with rage. He seized his sister and drew his sword. But Vasudeva held his arm and made him a promise."
**Dialogue:** The voice: "Fool, the eighth child of the girl you are driving will be your death."
**Dialogue:** Vasudeva: "Spare her. Every child she bears, I will bring to you myself."
**Sound:** the music cuts off; wind; a sword drawn.

### 3. The prison (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kamsa lowers the sword. Later: a stone cell, iron doors, chains. Devaki and Vasudeva sit together in the dim light, holding hands. Outside, guards everywhere. Kamsa looks in through the bars, his face lit by a torch, haunted.
**Narration:** "Kamsa let his sister live. But fear never left him. He put Devaki and Vasudeva in prison, behind iron doors and chains, with guards on every side. There they lived, and waited, and every year the fear inside the king grew darker."
**Sound:** iron doors clanging shut; dripping water; a guard's slow steps.

### 4. The first child (25 sec)
**Visual:** A baby's cry in the dark cell. Vasudeva, keeping his word, carries the child out to Kamsa. Kamsa, moved for a moment, gives the baby back. Then shadows gather around him: counsellors whispering in his ear. His face hardens. Only shadows and sound from here on; no child is ever shown harmed.
**Narration:** "When the first child was born, Vasudeva kept his promise and brought the baby to Kamsa. For a moment the king was moved, and gave the child back. But then fear whispered to him again. Any child might be his enemy. And Kamsa turned cruel."
**Sound:** a baby's cry; a long silence; a whisper; a door closing.

### 5. The grief (20 sec)
**Visual:** Seasons pass outside the high prison window. Devaki sits alone in the shadow, her head bowed, a lamp beside her. Six small lamps burn on a ledge, one after another, and go out.
**Narration:** "One child after another was taken from Devaki's arms. Six times her heart was broken. Yet she did not stop praying, and Vasudeva did not stop hoping."
**Sound:** a soft, aching melody on a single string instrument.

### 6. The waiting (25 sec)
**Visual:** A stormy night. In the cell, a light. The eighth child is born: Krishna, dark and shining. The chains fall open; the guards sleep. Vasudeva carries the baby across the flooding river to the village of Gokula and comes back with a baby girl. At dawn Kamsa bursts in. Devaki begs him. He snatches the girl, and she slips from his hands and rises into the sky as a shining goddess.
**Narration:** "Then the eighth child was born, on a night of storm, and Vasudeva carried him away in secret across the river, and brought back a baby girl instead. Kamsa came running. Devaki begged him: she is only a girl, my brother; give me this one. But he snatched the child, and she slipped from his hands, and rose into the sky as a goddess."
**Dialogue:** The goddess: "Fool, the one who will end you has already been born, somewhere else."
**Sound:** thunder, rain, a river roaring; then a ringing chord as the goddess rises.

### 7. The hope (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kamsa stands frozen. Later, in his council, his ministers lean in, whispering; he nods, and soldiers ride out into the countryside. Far away, in Gokula, a cowherd mother rocks a dark baby in a cradle, and the baby smiles.
**Narration:** "Kamsa was afraid as never before. His ministers told him to search out every newborn child in the land. But far away, in a village of cowherds, a baby lay safe in his cradle and smiled. Kamsa's own nephew had come."
**Sound:** hoofbeats fading; a lullaby; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers the uncle's cruelty. Song 1274 speaks of the wicked uncle who would not treat his nephew as a nephew and plotted to kill him; song 181 (Palani) of the Lord who saw through all the deceits Kamsa sent against him. Murugan is the nephew of that Krishna.

## Production notes

- **No harm to a child on screen.** Use sound and shadow only.
- **Kamsa's fear** is what drives him; give him weight, not caricature.
