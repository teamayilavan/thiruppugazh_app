# "Come Here, My King" (The Child Rama)

`ramayana-birth` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: tender, domestic, glowing | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Kausalya calls the infant Rama to her ("come here, king"); the Thiruppugazh image is of the child Vishnu as a mother's baby.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Kausalya calls the infant Rama to her ("come here, king") | Thiruppugazh song 68; Kamba, Thiru Avatara Padalam (traditional source; not read verse by verse here) |

**Honesty note:** the research gives the image only. The film follows it and keeps the birth of Rama (the sacrifice, the divine pudding) out, to avoid adding what was not read.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Baby Rama:** dark, bright-eyed.
- **Kausalya:** a loving mother.
- **Dasaratha** (a glimpse).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. Ayodhya (25 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn over Ayodhya: white palaces, river, gardens, temple flags. Drums and bells in the streets; people hanging garlands from every doorway. In the palace, King Dasaratha stands at a window, his face full of joy.
**Narration:** "In the city of Ayodhya, King Dasaratha had waited many years for a son. Then, in one season, four sons were born to his queens. Queen Kausalya bore the eldest, and they named him Rama. The whole city rejoiced."
**Sound:** festive drums, bells, a bright nagaswaram tune.

### 2. The nursery (20 sec)
**Visual:** A quiet palace room, soft lamplight. Kausalya sits beside a cradle. Inside lies baby Rama, dark as a rain cloud, his eyes bright. She rocks the cradle gently. For a moment, a faint glow surrounds the child.
**Narration:** "Kamban says that Kausalya bore the one who holds all the worlds within him, the one the holy books cannot fully know. And yet here he was, a small child, dark as a rain cloud, asleep in her cradle."
**Sound:** a soft lullaby hum, the creak of the cradle.

### 3. The first steps (25 sec)
**Visual:** Some months later. A sunlit courtyard. Little Rama, now a toddler, tries to walk; he wobbles, falls on his bottom, laughs and gets up again. His brothers crawl nearby. Maids clap. A peacock struts past and he chases it.
**Narration:** "The baby grew. Soon he was a little boy, trying his first steps in the palace courtyard. He wobbled, he fell, he laughed and stood up again. He chased the peacocks. And his mother never tired of watching him."
**Sound:** children's laughter, anklet bells, birds.

### 4. The call (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kausalya kneels at the far end of the courtyard with open arms, holding a small garland of flowers. Little Rama looks up from his play.
**Narration:** "Then his mother called to him, the way every mother calls her child. Arunagiri gives us her very words in one of his songs."
**Dialogue:** Kausalya: "Come here, my king, come. Come and drink your milk. Come, let me put flowers in your hair."
**Sound:** the music softens to a single flute.

### 5. The embrace (20 sec)
**Visual:** Little Rama runs to her on unsteady feet and tumbles into her arms. She lifts him, kisses his forehead, and tucks the flowers into his hair. He laughs and holds her face in his small hands.
**Narration:** "And the little one came running, and fell into her arms. She held him close and put the flowers in his hair. To her, he was simply her son, her little king."
**Sound:** warm strings, a soft laugh.

### 6. The glimpse (25 sec)
**Visual:** As she holds him, for a breath the courtyard fades: behind the child appears a vast dark-blue presence, the Lord resting on the serpent in the cosmic ocean, holding the worlds. Then it fades, and there is only a mother holding a laughing child in the sun. Dasaratha watches from a doorway, smiling.
**Narration:** "The one who rests on the serpent in the ocean, who holds all the worlds, had come as a child to be held. Kamban says the name Rama was given to him who answered the elephant's cry. And here, he came running when his mother called."
**Sound:** a deep, quiet chord, then the lullaby returns.

### 7. The song (10 sec)
**Visual:** Evening. Kausalya rocks the cradle; the child sleeps. The lamp glows.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri sings that this child, Kausalya's little king, is Murugan's uncle."
**Sound:** the lullaby, fading; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 68 (Thiruchendur) gives Kausalya's own call to her child: "Come here, my king, come; come and drink your milk; come, let me deck you with flowers." Arunagiri praises Murugan as the nephew in whom that Lord delights.

## Production notes

- **Keep it small and warm.** The film is a lullaby.
