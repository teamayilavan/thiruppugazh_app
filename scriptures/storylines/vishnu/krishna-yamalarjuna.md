# The Twin Trees (Yamalarjuna)

`krishna-yamalarjuna` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: quiet, then a crash, then release | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Dragging the mortar, Krishna passes between two arjuna trees; they break and release Kubera's sons Nalakuvara and Manigriva from Narada's curse.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Krishna:** a small boy dragging a mortar.
- **The two trees:** huge, still.
- **Nalakuvara and Manigriva:** two radiant youths, ashamed.
- **Narada** (in the curse flashback).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; quiet, then a crash, then gentle release. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The mortar (20 sec)
**Visual:** Nanda's yard in Gokula. Little Krishna sits tied by a rope round his waist to a heavy wooden mortar. Yashoda has gone back to her housework. He looks about, then begins to crawl, dragging the mortar bumping behind him.
**Narration:** "Yashoda had tied her little son to a heavy wooden mortar, and gone back to her work. But Krishna did not stay where he was put. On his hands and knees he crawled away, dragging the mortar behind him."
**Sound:** the churning song in the distance; the thump and scrape of the mortar.

### 2. The trees (20 sec)
**Visual:** At the edge of the yard stand two enormous arjuna trees, side by side, very close together, very still. Their bark seems almost to have faces. Krishna stops and looks up at them for a long moment.
**Narration:** "At the edge of the yard stood two great arjuna trees, side by side. They had stood there for a very long time. Krishna looked up at them, as if he knew them."
**Sound:** wind in high leaves; a soft, old melody.

### 3. The squeeze (25 sec)
**Visual:** A gentle flashback in a different, painted style: a river among lotus gardens near Kailasa. Two rich young gods, Nalakuvara and Manigriva, drunk and laughing, splash about without clothes. The sage Narada walks by. The others cover themselves; the two brothers do not care. Narada raises his hand, not angrily but sadly.
**Narration:** "Long ago they had been two young gods, the sons of Kubera, the lord of wealth. Proud and careless and drunk, they did not even cover themselves when the sage Narada passed by. So Narada said that since they lived like trees, they should become trees, until the Lord himself came to set them free."
**Sound:** water and laughter, then one clear tone for Narada.

### 4. The pull (25 sec)
**Visual:** Back in the yard. Krishna crawls straight between the two trees; he fits, but the mortar turns sideways and jams against both trunks. He looks back at it, then pulls. The rope tightens; the trees groan; their roots begin to lift.
**Narration:** "Now the little boy crawled between the two trees. He passed through easily, but the mortar behind him turned sideways and stuck fast. So he pulled. The trunks shuddered and groaned, and the great roots began to rise from the earth."
**Sound:** a creak building into a deep groan; the music climbs.

### 5. The crash (20 sec)
**Visual:** Both trees topple and fall to the ground with a thunderous crash, leaves and branches flying. Dust fills the yard. Krishna sits calmly in the middle of it, still tied to his mortar.
**Narration:** "With a sound like thunder both trees came crashing down, trunks, branches, leaves and all. And there sat the little boy, still tied to his mortar, without a scratch."
**Sound:** a huge cracking crash, then falling leaves and silence.

### 6. The youths (30 sec)
**Visual:** From the fallen trees rise two radiant figures, glowing like fire, lighting the whole yard. They are the two young gods, humble now. They bow their heads to the little boy and fold their hands.
**Narration:** "Out of the fallen trees came two shining beings, glowing like fire. They were the sons of Kubera, free at last. They bowed to the child tied to the mortar, and praised him as the Lord of all."
**Dialogue:** Nalakuvara: "From now on, may our words tell of you, our ears hear of you, our hands work for you, and our minds rest at your feet."
**Narration (continued):** "The child told them that Narada's curse had been a kindness, for it had brought them to him."
**Sound:** a warm chord, rising; bells.

### 7. The thanks (20 sec)
**Visual:** The two walk around him, bow, and rise into the sky, northward. The cowherds come running at the noise and stare at the fallen trees. The children point at Krishna. Nanda smiles, kneels and unties the rope.
**Narration:** "They walked around him, bowed, and returned to their home. The cowherds came running and could not believe their eyes. But Nanda only smiled, and untied his son."
**Sound:** footsteps, voices, then the old melody, light and free.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the twin maruda trees in at least nine songs, usually beside the other wonders of Krishna's childhood. Songs to show: 908 (Vayalur), where the trees crack as the child crawls with the mortar; 576 (Viralimalai); and 116 and 181 (Palani).
