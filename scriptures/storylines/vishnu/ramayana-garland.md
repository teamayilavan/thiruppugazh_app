# The Creeper Garland (Telling the Brothers Apart)

`ramayana-garland` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: practical, tense, a small clever act | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Before the second fight between Vali and Sugriva, Rama has a gaja creeper hung round Sugriva's neck so that he can tell the brothers apart.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Rama, Lakshmana.**
- **Sugriva and Vali:** two monkey kings, so alike they look the same.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The first fight (25 sec)
**Visual:** Outside the gates of Kishkindha, two great monkey kings clash in a cloud of dust: Vali and his younger brother Sugriva. They look almost exactly alike: the same build, the same golden fur, the same crowns. Hidden among the trees nearby, Rama stands with his bow drawn, watching.
**Narration:** "Sugriva had promised to help Rama find Sita, and Rama had promised to help Sugriva win back his home from his brother Vali. So Sugriva went to Kishkindha and shouted his challenge, and Vali came out to fight. Rama waited among the trees with his bow."
**Sound:** roars, thuds, dust; tense drums.

### 2. The hold (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rama's arrow is ready, but his eyes move from one brother to the other. The two whirl and tumble; in the dust he cannot tell them apart. He lowers the bow.
**Narration:** "But the two brothers were so alike, in their size, their strength and their shining ornaments, that Rama could not tell which was which. He could not shoot, for fear of striking his friend."
**Sound:** the drums continue; a held, uncertain note.

### 3. Sugriva's return (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sugriva, bruised and bleeding a little, breaks free and runs back to the forest, Vali roaring behind him. He reaches Rama, sinks down, and looks up, hurt and reproachful.
**Narration:** "Vali beat Sugriva badly, and Sugriva fled back to the forest. He came to Rama hurt and bewildered."
**Dialogue:** Sugriva: "Why did you not shoot? You promised to help me."
**Sound:** heavy breathing; the battle noise fades away.

### 4. The remedy (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama kneels and gently lays a hand on Sugriva's shoulder. He explains, gesturing at the likeness. Then he looks around the forest, where flowering creepers hang from the trees, and turns to Lakshmana.
**Narration:** "Rama spoke to him gently. He explained that in the fight he could not tell the brothers apart, and he would not risk his friend's life. Then he looked around the forest and thought of a simple answer."
**Dialogue:** Rama: "Lakshmana, bring that flowering creeper, and hang it around Sugriva's neck."
**Sound:** soft strings; birds return.

### 5. The garland (20 sec)
**Visual:** Lakshmana pulls down a long creeper covered in bright blossoms and loops it around Sugriva's neck. The flowers glow against his fur. Sugriva touches them, puzzled, then begins to smile.
**Narration:** "Lakshmana pulled down a creeper full of flowers and hung it around Sugriva's neck like a garland. Valmiki says that with it, Sugriva shone brightly in the evening light."
**Sound:** a light, bright melody.

### 6. The sign (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rama steps back and looks: now, among the trees, the garlanded Sugriva is easy to see. Rama nods, and lifts his bow. Sugriva stands up straight, courage returning.
**Narration:** "Now Rama could see his friend clearly, wherever he moved. A small sign, made of forest flowers, would tell him which brother was which. It was not a weapon or a trick. It was simply care for a friend."
**Sound:** the melody gathers strength.

### 7. The second fight (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sugriva strides back to the gates of Kishkindha, the flowers swinging on his chest, and roars his challenge again. Inside, Vali hears and rises. Rama follows quietly through the trees, his bow ready.
**Narration:** "Wearing the flowering creeper, Sugriva went back and challenged his brother once more. And this time, Rama would know whom to protect."
**Sound:** a great roar, then drums rising, cut to silence.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 266 (Thiruthani) recalls how the Lord hung a garland of kanthal flowers on Sugriva and sent him to fight, and names Murugan the nephew of that Lord, Achyuta, the one of perfect virtue.
