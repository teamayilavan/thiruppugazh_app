# The Iron Pestle (The End of the Yadavas)

`krishna-mausala` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: foreboding, tragic, resigned | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A curse leads to an iron pestle; its powder grows into reeds with which the Yadavas destroy each other, and the pestle's last piece is thrown into the sea.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The Yadavas' fight is shown in silhouette.

## Characters

- **The Yadava princes:** young, boastful.
- **The sages:** who pronounce the curse.
- **Krishna:** grave, accepting.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; light at first, then grave and still. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The prank (25 sec)
**Visual:** Pindaraka, a holy place by the sea near Dwaraka. Great sages sit in quiet circle. A gang of young Yadava princes, laughing, push forward Samba, Krishna's son, dressed as a woman with a cushion under his sari to look pregnant. They bow with mock humility.
**Narration:** "Late in Krishna's life, great sages came to the holy place of Pindaraka, near Dwaraka. Some young princes of Krishna's clan, proud and bored, thought it would be funny to test them. They dressed Samba, Krishna's own son, as a woman expecting a child."
**Dialogue:** A prince: "Wise ones, this lady is shy to ask. Will she bear a son?"
**Sound:** waves, laughter, then a sudden hush.

### 2. The curse (20 sec)
**Visual:** The sages' faces darken. One raises his hand. The laughter stops. The princes step back.
**Narration:** "The sages saw through the trick, and they were angry at being mocked."
**Dialogue:** A sage: "Fools! She will give birth to an iron pestle, and it will destroy your whole clan."
**Sound:** a deep, single gong.

### 3. The pestle (25 sec)
**Visual:** The frightened princes tear away the cushion. Out drops a heavy black iron pestle, thudding into the sand. They stare at it, pale, then carry it back to the royal court and lay it before old King Ugrasena. The court falls silent with fear.
**Narration:** "Terrified, the boys pulled away the cushion, and there inside was an iron pestle. Their faces drained of colour. They carried it back to the court of Dwaraka, and told the old king Ugrasena everything. The whole city was afraid."
**Sound:** a dull iron thud; murmurs of fear.

### 4. The sea (20 sec)
**Visual:** On the king's order, smiths grind the pestle down on stone until only a small lump remains. Ugrasena himself casts the iron powder and the last lump far out into the sea. The waves close over them.
**Narration:** "The king had the pestle ground into dust, and threw the dust and the last small lump of iron into the sea. Surely, they thought, the curse was now gone."
**Sound:** grinding, a splash, then the endless sound of waves.

### 5. The reeds (20 sec)
**Visual:** Time passes over the shore. The waves carry the iron dust back to the sand. Tall, sharp, steel-grey reeds spring up along the beach and sway in the wind. Far out, a fish swallows the shining lump, and later a fisherman's net brings it in, and a hunter fixes it to the head of an arrow.
**Narration:** "But the waves carried the dust back to the shore, and there it grew into tall, sharp reeds. A fish swallowed the last lump. A fisherman caught the fish, and a hunter named Jara made an arrowhead of the iron."
**Sound:** wind in reeds, a soft, ominous drone.

### 6. The fight (25 sec)
**Visual:** Years later, at Prabhasa by the sea. A feast; cups raised. A quarrel flares. Only silhouettes against a blood-red sunset: figures snatch up the reeds, which flash into iron clubs in their hands. The silhouettes blur and fall; the frame fades to grey. No blow is shown.
**Narration:** "Years later, the Yadavas gathered by the sea at Prabhasa. They drank, and quarrelled, and the quarrel became a fight. They pulled up the reeds from the shore, and in their hands the reeds became clubs of iron. And driven by Time, the great clan destroyed itself."
**Sound:** a rising roar cut to silence; only the sea.

### 7. The end (20 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna stands alone on the empty shore at dusk, calm and grave, his yellow cloth moving in the wind. He looks out at the sea. There is no anger in his face.
**Narration:** "Krishna could have undone the curse. The Bhagavata says he did not wish to. He knew that the time had come, and as Time itself, he let it be. His work on earth was finished."
**Sound:** a long, low flute note.

### 8. The last piece (10 sec)
**Visual:** In the forest, a hunter strings his bow; the iron arrowhead glints. The camera rises over the quiet sea.
**Narration:** "And somewhere in the forest, a hunter's arrow waited, tipped with the last piece of iron."
**Sound:** the flute fades into the waves.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers the iron pestle in one line, naming Murugan the nephew of the Lord who had it filed down and cast into the middle of the sea. Song to show: 248 (Thiruthanigai).

## Production notes

- **A tragedy, not a spectacle.** Do not show the killing.
- **The Lord's calm** is the film's tone.
