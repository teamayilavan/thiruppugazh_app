# The Sleeper of Lanka (Kumbhakarna)

`ramayana-kumbhakarna` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: huge, comic, then noble | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Rama kills Ravana's brother Kumbhakarna, cutting off his head; the body falls on the wall of Lanka.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Rama kills Ravana's brother Kumbhakarna, cutting off his head | Valmiki Yuddha Kanda 67.165-172; Kamba, Kumbakarnan Vadhai Padalam 337-359 |
| The body falls on the wall of Lanka | same |
| Song 961 recalls it | Thiruppugazh song 961 |
| His long sleep and waking are the widely told frame | Valmiki and Kamba (traditional; not read verse by verse here) |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Kumbhakarna:** a giant, sleepy, honest.
- **Ravana.**
- **Rama and the monkeys.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The sleeper (25 sec)
**Visual:** Deep inside Lanka, a cavernous hall. On a vast bed lies Kumbhakarna, a giant as big as a hill, fast asleep. His breath blows like a wind through the hall; servants lean against it to stay standing. Mountains of food wait beside him.
**Narration:** "In Lanka lived Ravana's brother Kumbhakarna, a giant as big as a mountain. He slept for months at a time, and when he breathed, the curtains of his hall blew like flags in a storm. Nothing in the world could easily wake him."
**Sound:** a huge, slow snore like distant thunder; a gentle comic tune.

### 2. The waking (25 sec)
**Visual:** Ravana's servants try everything: drums beaten beside his ears, conches blown, trumpets, elephants walking over his chest, buckets of water. Comic, never cruel. At last one eye opens, then the other. He sits up, yawning, and eats an enormous meal.
**Narration:** "But now the war was going badly, and Ravana needed him. His servants beat drums by his ears and blew conches. They even made elephants walk over him. At last the giant opened his eyes, yawned, and ate a meal fit for an army."
**Sound:** a comic din of drums and horns, then one great yawn.

### 3. The counsel (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kumbhakarna, still huge, kneels before Ravana's throne. He listens, frowning, then shakes his great head and speaks plainly, without fear.
**Narration:** "Ravana told him everything: how he had carried off Sita, and how Rama's army had come. Kumbhakarna listened, and then he spoke honestly to his brother."
**Dialogue:** Kumbhakarna: "Brother, you have done wrong. You should have listened to good counsel."
**Sound:** the comic tune stops; a grave, low note.

### 4. The loyalty (20 sec)
**Visual:** Ravana's face darkens with anger and hurt. Kumbhakarna sighs, rises, and picks up a great spear. He lays a hand on his brother's shoulder, then turns toward the gate.
**Narration:** "Ravana was angry. But Kumbhakarna would not leave his brother alone in his trouble. Right or wrong, he said, I will fight for you. And he took up his great spear and went out to the battle."
**Sound:** a heavy, slow march; one horn.

### 5. The rampage (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kumbhakarna strides across the battlefield, taller than the walls. The monkeys flee before his shadow, like leaves in a wind. He seizes Sugriva and carries him off under his arm; Sugriva struggles free and leaps away. No one is shown hurt; only scattering shapes and dust.
**Narration:** "On the field the monkeys fled before him like leaves before the wind. He caught their king, Sugriva, and carried him off, but Sugriva fought his way free and escaped. Who could stop such a giant?"
**Sound:** earth-shaking footsteps, cries of the monkeys.

### 6. Rama (20 sec)
**Visual:** The dust parts. Rama stands alone in Kumbhakarna's path, calm, with his bow drawn. The giant stops and looks down at him. Their eyes meet; there is no hatred between them.
**Narration:** "Then Rama came forward and stood in the giant's path. Kumbhakarna looked down at him. In Kamban's telling, he speaks to Rama with respect, and makes one last request."
**Dialogue:** Kumbhakarna: "Let the sages and gods not mock my wounded face. When you strike, send my head into the dark sea."
**Sound:** silence; a single deep chord.

### 7. The end (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama draws a shining arrow, like a ray of the sun. It flies. A flash of light fills the screen. When it fades, we see only the giant's huge shadow, falling slowly across the land toward the sea. Waves rise.
**Narration:** "Rama loosed a shining arrow, bright as the sun. In a flash of light, the battle ended. The great giant fell. Kamban says Rama granted his wish, and the dark sea received him, far from mocking eyes."
**Sound:** a rushing sound, then a great wave breaking; then stillness.

### 8. The wall (10 sec)
**Visual:** A vast shadow falls across the walls of Lanka; the walls tremble and dust drifts down. The monkeys stand silent, heads bowed.
**Narration:** "His fall shook the walls of Lanka. Even his enemies bowed their heads."
**Sound:** a deep rumble, fading; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 961 (Madurai) names Kumbhakarna, steadfast in his strength, among those destroyed by the bow of Mukunda, and calls Murugan the nephew who delights that Lord's heart.

## Production notes

- **Treat him as noble.** His honesty is the film's tone.
- **No head shown.** The fall is a shadow on a wall.
