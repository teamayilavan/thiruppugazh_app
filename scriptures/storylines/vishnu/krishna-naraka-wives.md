# Naraka and the Sixteen Thousand

`krishna-naraka-wives` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: grim, then liberating | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Krishna kills Narakasura and marries the princesses he had imprisoned.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Krishna kills Narakasura and marries the princesses he had imprisoned | Bhagavata Purana 10.59.2-42 (they choose him in their hearts, 10.59.34-35) |
| Song 442 recalls it | Thiruppugazh song 442 |

**Honesty note:** the research gives the story in one line. The film treats the "marriage" as the tradition does: Krishna accepts the women who, once held by Naraka, would have been outcast, and gives them honour and a home.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Narakasura:** a violent demon king in a fortified city.
- **Krishna and Satyabhama.**
- **The princesses:** frightened, then free.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; grave in the fortress, gentle and warm at the end. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The fortress (25 sec)
**Visual:** Pragjyotisha, Naraka's city, ringed by walls of rock, by fields of weapons, by moats of water, fire and wind, and by a mesh of sharp cables. On a tower stands Naraka, the son of the earth, with stolen treasures: a pair of shining earrings, a great white umbrella.
**Narration:** "Naraka, the son of the earth goddess, ruled a city ringed with walls of rock and moats of fire, water and wind. He had grown so proud that he stole the earrings of Aditi, the mother of the gods, and the royal umbrella of Varuna. Indra came to Dwaraka and told Krishna."
**Sound:** a low wind over the walls; a dull, heavy drum.

### 2. The captives (20 sec)
**Visual:** Deep inside the palace, behind barred lattices, a great hall full of young women in faded clothes, the daughters of many kings, sitting in silence. One of them looks up at a high window where a thin line of light falls.
**Narration:** "And inside his palace were sixteen thousand young women, the daughters of kings, whom Naraka had carried off by force. Year after year they waited, with no one to come for them."
**Sound:** silence, a single sad string.

### 3. The siege (25 sec)
**Visual:** Garuda sweeps across the sky. On his back, Krishna, and beside him Satyabhama, like lightning beside a dark cloud. With his club Krishna breaks the rock walls; his arrows scatter the weapons; his discus parts the fire and the water; his conch sounds, and the gates fly open.
**Narration:** "Krishna rode out on Garuda, and his queen Satyabhama rode with him. With his club he broke the walls of rock, with his discus he cut through fire and water and wind, and the sound of his conch shattered the gates."
**Sound:** wings, the conch, a cascade of breaking stone.

### 4. The fight (25 sec)
**Visual:** Light and shadow only. The five-headed guardian Mura rises from the moat and falls back into it. Naraka's army rushes out and scatters before Garuda's wings. Naraka, on an elephant, raises a trident; a flash of the discus; the trident drops. Flowers fall from the sky.
**Narration:** "The guardian Mura rose from the moat and fell. Naraka's army came out and was scattered. At last Naraka himself lifted his trident against the Lord. But before he could throw it, the discus flashed, and Naraka was gone."
**Sound:** a roar, a whirling hum, one bright stroke, then silence and soft drums.

### 5. The freedom (25 sec)
**Visual:** The earth goddess, Bhumi, comes forward with folded hands, bringing back the earrings and the umbrella. Beside her, Naraka's frightened young son. Krishna lays his hand on the boy's head. Then he walks into the palace, and the barred doors of the great hall swing open.
**Narration:** "The earth goddess, Naraka's mother, came with the stolen earrings and bowed to the Lord. She brought Naraka's young son, frightened, and Krishna laid his hand on the boy's head and gave him peace. Then Krishna walked into the palace, and opened the doors of the hall."
**Sound:** a gentle melody; the creak of doors; light pouring in.

### 6. The shame (20 sec)
**Visual:** The women rise slowly, shading their eyes. Some hold each other. Their faces show fear: where can they go now? Who will receive them? Then, one by one, they look at Krishna, and their faces change.
**Narration:** "They were free. But tradition tells that they were afraid, for who would now receive women who had been held by Naraka? The Bhagavata says that when they saw Krishna, each of them, in her heart, chose him."
**Sound:** a hush; a single flute note rising.

### 7. The honour (25 sec)
**Visual:** The women in fresh, clean garments step into a long line of palanquins, with chariots, horses and treasures. The procession winds toward Dwaraka by the sea. At Dwaraka, palaces glow in the evening light, one for each of them; lamps are lit in every window.
**Narration:** "Krishna had them dressed in clean new clothes and sent them in palanquins to Dwaraka, with chariots, horses and treasure. There he married them, each in her own palace, and gave every one of them a home and an honoured name. No one could call them outcasts again."
**Sound:** procession music, bells, the sea at Dwaraka.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri's one line on this story praises Murugan as the nephew of the emerald-hued Narayana, beloved of the sixteen thousand women. Song to show: 442 (Thiruvannamalai).
