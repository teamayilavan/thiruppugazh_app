# One Arrow, Seven Trees

`ramayana-seven-trees` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: proving, spectacular, quiet confidence | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

To prove his strength to Sugriva, Rama's single arrow pierces seven sala trees and returns to his quiver.

## Characters

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it. This is a film of wonder; the return of the arrow is its high point.

### 1. The doubt (25 sec)
**Visual:** The hills of Kishkindha. Rama and Lakshmana sit with Sugriva, the exiled monkey prince, and Hanuman. Sugriva is nervous, glancing over his shoulder. He points across the valley to a row of seven enormous sala trees, their trunks thicker than temple pillars.
**Narration:** "Rama had promised to help Sugriva against his brother Vali. But Sugriva was afraid. Vali was the strongest of all the monkeys. Sugriva told Rama that Vali could shake the mighty sala trees bare, and he wondered, though he did not dare to say it, whether anyone could stand against him."
**Dialogue:** Sugriva: "My brother's strength is beyond measure. How can I be sure?"
**Sound:** a soft, uneasy melody; birds in the hills.

### 2. The test (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rama looks at Sugriva kindly, without offence. He stands, takes up his bow, and looks across at the seven trees. Lakshmana smiles a little, knowing.
**Narration:** "Rama understood. He was not angry. A friend in fear needs to see, not only to hear. So Rama rose and took up his bow, and looked at the seven great trees."
**Dialogue:** Rama: "Do not be afraid, Sugriva. Watch."
**Sound:** the melody pauses.

### 3. The row (20 sec)
**Visual:** The camera travels along the seven sala trees, one behind another, huge and ancient, each trunk wide as a hut. Wind moves in their high crowns. Monkeys perch on the rocks to watch, quiet now.
**Narration:** "There they stood, seven ancient trees, one behind the other, each one so thick that many arms could not reach around it. The monkeys gathered on the rocks to watch, and the hills went quiet."
**Sound:** wind in tall leaves; a held note.

### 4. The bow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama sets his feet and draws the bowstring. He lets it snap once, unloaded: the twang rolls through the hills like thunder. Birds fly up; the monkeys flinch. Then he fits a single golden arrow and draws it back to his ear.
**Narration:** "First he plucked the empty bowstring, and Kamban says the sound alone struck fear into all who heard it. Then he fitted a single arrow, bright with gold, and drew the string back to his ear. Sugriva held his breath."
**Sound:** a thunderous twang, echoing; then silence and the creak of the drawn bow.

### 5. The arrow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama looses the arrow. Slow motion: it passes through the first trunk, the second, the third, through all seven in a line of light, and on into the hillside behind them and down into the earth. Small holes of light glow through the seven trees.
**Narration:** "He let it go. The arrow passed through the first tree, and the second, and the third, through all seven in one breath. And it did not stop. Valmiki says it went on through the hill behind them and deep into the earth below."
**Sound:** a rushing hiss, seven sharp knocks in quick rhythm, a deep rumble under the ground.

### 6. The return (25 sec)
**Visual:** A pause. Everyone stares at the trees. Then a glint far off: the arrow rises out of the earth, flies back across the valley, and slides quietly into Rama's quiver. Rama lowers his bow. Sugriva, his mouth open, falls at Rama's feet; Hanuman bows deeply.
**Narration:** "There was a long silence. Then, in a moment, the arrow came flying back from the depths of the earth, and slid into Rama's quiver, as if it had never left. Sugriva's fear fell from him. He bowed at Rama's feet."
**Dialogue:** Sugriva: "Now I know. With you beside me, I have nothing to fear."
**Sound:** a bright rising whoosh, the soft click of the arrow in the quiver, then a warm chord.

### 7. The faith (10 sec)
**Visual:** The seven trees in evening light, each pierced by a small point of sunlight. Rama and Sugriva walk away together down the hill.
**Narration:** "One arrow, seven trees. From that day Sugriva did not doubt again."
**Sound:** the opening melody, now calm and sure.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the seven trees in one song. In song 731 (Thiruvamathur) Murugan is the nephew of the dark-hued Lord whose single arrow brought down the seven trees together with Vali, and Ravana's ten heads and twenty arms.
