# Shiva's Bow Is Broken (Mithila)

`ramayana-bow` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: expectant, mighty, then comic-humble | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Mithila King Janaka offers Sita to whoever strings Shiva's bow; Rama lifts and breaks it, the weddings follow, and Parashurama then challenges Rama and is humbled.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Rama and Lakshmana.**
- **Janaka:** a wise king.
- **Sita:** a young princess, grave and calm.
- **Parashurama:** an axe-wielding sage.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The bow (25 sec)
**Visual:** The royal hall of Mithila, bright with lamps and banners. In the centre, on a great iron chest on eight wheels, lies an enormous bow, dark and ancient, so heavy that many strong men strain to pull its cart into the hall. Kings and princes sit in rows, staring at it.
**Narration:** "In the city of Mithila, King Janaka kept a great treasure: the bow of Shiva himself, handed down in his family for generations. It was so heavy that many strong men were needed just to bring it into the hall. Kings came from far and wide to look at it."
**Sound:** a deep drum as the cart rolls in; murmurs of the crowd.

### 2. The condition (20 sec)
**Visual:** King Janaka, wise and gentle, rises from his throne. Behind a screen, Princess Sita stands with her companions, calm and grave.
**Narration:** "Janaka's daughter Sita had been found as a baby in a furrow of the earth. The king had made a promise. Whoever could lift Shiva's bow and string it would marry her."
**Dialogue:** Janaka: "Let the one who can string this bow come forward."
**Sound:** a single conch; then silence.

### 3. The kings fail (20 sec)
**Visual:** One by one, proud kings step up. One pushes; one pulls; one cannot even move it. They return to their seats, red-faced, avoiding each other's eyes. The bow lies unmoved.
**Narration:** "Many kings had tried before. Some could not lift it. Some could not even move it. One after another they went back to their seats, and the bow lay there as before."
**Sound:** strained grunts, a few murmurs; a light, wry tune.

### 4. Rama steps up (25 sec)
**Visual:** The sage Vishwamitra turns to the young prince Rama and nods. Rama rises, bows to his teacher and to the king, and walks calmly to the bow. Lakshmana watches proudly. Rama lifts the bow easily, as though it were a garland of flowers.
**Narration:** "Then the sage Vishwamitra turned to young Rama and said, look at the bow. Rama rose, bowed to his teacher, and walked to it without hurry. He lifted it as easily as you might lift a garland. Kamban says he raised it as if it were the garland he would place on Sita."
**Sound:** the hall goes silent; a slow rising note.

### 5. The crack (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rama bends the bow to string it and draws the string back to his ear. With a flash of light and a thunderous crack, the bow breaks in the middle. The lamps flicker; people cover their ears; the sky flashes. Then Rama stands holding the two halves, calm.
**Narration:** "He bent it to fix the string, and drew it back, and with a sound like thunder the great bow broke in two. The earth shook. Kamban says the gods in heaven feared the whole universe had split."
**Sound:** a huge crack echoing, then ringing silence.

### 6. The weddings (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sita steps forward with a garland and places it on Rama's shoulders; their eyes meet with quiet joy. Then a bright montage: King Dasaratha arrives from Ayodhya, wedding fires, and four couples walking around the sacred fire, with flowers falling.
**Narration:** "Sita placed her garland on Rama. King Dasaratha was called from Ayodhya, and there were four weddings that day: Rama with Sita, and his three brothers with three princesses of Mithila."
**Sound:** joyous wedding music, nagaswaram and drums.

### 7. Parashurama (20 sec)
**Visual:** On the road home to Ayodhya, the wedding procession halts. Wind and dust rise. Out of the storm strides Parashurama, a tall sage with matted hair, an axe on his shoulder and another bow in his hand. Dasaratha is afraid. Parashurama faces Rama.
**Narration:** "On the way home, the sky darkened. The fierce sage Parashurama came striding toward them. He had heard of the broken bow, and he held out another, the bow of Vishnu."
**Dialogue:** Parashurama: "You broke Shiva's bow. Now string this one, if you can."
**Sound:** wind, a low ominous drum.

### 8. The humbling (15 sec)
**Visual:** Rama takes the bow and strings it at once, fitting an arrow. Parashurama's fierceness melts. He bows his head, recognising who stands before him, and walks away with dignity into the hills. The sky clears.
**Narration:** "Rama took the bow and strung it at once. Parashurama understood who stood before him. He bowed with respect and went his way in peace."
**Sound:** the storm fades; the wedding melody returns softly.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs recall the breaking of Shiva's bow, naming Murugan the nephew of the Lord who broke it and won Sita. Show song 649 (Kathirgamam), song 799 (Thiruvidaikkazhi) and song 968 (Sri Purushamangai).

## Production notes

- **Parashurama is dignified.** He is humbled, not humiliated.
- **Sita's choice is clear.**
