# The Flute of Vrindavan

`krishna-flute` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: sweet, musical, hypnotic | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Krishna plays the flute in Vrindavan, drawing cows, animals and gopis.

## Characters

- **Krishna:** a boy playing a bamboo flute.
- **Cows, deer, peacocks.**
- **The gopis.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it. This film has no dialogue: the flute is the only other voice.

### 1. The forest (20 sec)
**Visual:** Autumn in Vrindavan. Clear water, lotus ponds, bees over the flowers, birds in the trees. Krishna walks into the forest with Balarama and the cowherd boys, driving the cows ahead. He wears a peacock feather, a garland of forest flowers, and golden-yellow cloth. A bamboo flute is tucked in his sash.
**Narration:** "It was autumn in Vrindavan. The water was clear, the lakes were full of lotuses, and the forest hummed with bees and birds. Into that forest walked Krishna, with his brother Balarama and the cowherd boys, driving the cows to graze."
**Sound:** bees, birds, cowbells, the boys singing.

### 2. The first note (20 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna stops under a tree, lifts the flute to his lips, and his small fingers move over the holes. One long, clear note rises. The camera follows it like a thread of light out over the treetops.
**Narration:** "Then he lifted his flute to his lips. His little fingers moved over the holes, and one clear note rose into the air. The old poets say that the birds came down from the sky to hear it."
**Sound:** a single flute note, held; the birdsong falls silent around it.

### 3. The cows (25 sec)
**Visual:** All over the meadow the cows lift their heads. They stop grazing, turn their ears toward the sound, and walk slowly toward him. Calves stand still with milk on their mouths, eyes half closed. A scattered herd gathers into one.
**Narration:** "The cows stopped grazing. They lifted their heads and turned their ears to the music, as if they were drinking it. The calves stood still, with milk still in their mouths, and tears in their eyes. From every corner of the pasture the herd came walking home to him."
**Sound:** the flute melody begins, simple and slow; soft lowing.

### 4. The animals (25 sec)
**Visual:** Peacocks spread their tails and dance on the rocks. Deer, does and bucks together, step out of the trees and gaze at him. Birds sit on the branches with their eyes closed, perfectly still. Honey drips from the trees; flowers fall without a breeze.
**Narration:** "The peacocks began to dance. The deer came out of the trees and gazed at him with love. The birds sat on the branches with their eyes closed, quiet as sages in meditation. The trees themselves seemed to tremble, and drop honey and flowers on the ground."
**Sound:** the melody grows, with a soft drum like a heartbeat.

### 5. The gopis (25 sec)
**Visual:** In the village, the cowherd women stop in their work: one with a churn, one at the well, one braiding her hair. They hear the flute from far away, look at each other, and begin to speak of him softly, then fall silent, lost in remembering.
**Narration:** "In the village, the cowherd women heard the flute from far away. They stopped their work and began to speak of him to each other, of his peacock feather and his yellow cloth. But as they remembered him, their words ran out, and they could only listen."
**Sound:** the flute, more distant; the churn falls silent.

### 6. The river (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Yamuna slows. Its currents curl into whirlpools, and its waves rise to touch Krishna's feet on the bank, offering lotuses. A cloud drifts over him and drops a fine cool rain, like a canopy.
**Narration:** "Even the river heard. The Yamuna slowed and turned, and its waves reached up to touch his feet, offering him lotuses. And a cloud drifted over him to shade him from the sun."
**Sound:** water swirling; a light patter of rain.

### 7. The stillness (15 sec)
**Visual:** Wide shot of the forest at evening: cows, deer, peacocks, birds, the river, all still, all turned toward one small figure with a flute. The last note fades. Krishna lowers the flute and smiles.
**Narration:** "For as long as he played, the whole forest stood still and listened. And when the music ended, nobody wanted to move."
**Sound:** the last note, held, then silence; one bird begins to sing again.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri turns to the flute in about eight songs, usually in a single line. Songs 43 (Thiruchendur), 158 (Palani), 1009 and 1170 are the ones to show: fingers moving over the holes of the bamboo, birds coming down from the sky, and the herds gathered by its music. Murugan is the flute-player's nephew.
