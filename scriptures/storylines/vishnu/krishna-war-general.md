# The Eighteen Days (The Bharata War)

`krishna-war-general` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: grave, vast, sombre | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The songs refer in general to the Bharata war, the Kauravas' fall and its aftermath; this film gives a short overview of the eighteen days at Kurukshetra.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |

**Honesty note:** the research marks this entry "General references". The film is an overview; its episodes are told in full in the neighbouring films (`krishna-gita-chariot`, `krishna-bhishma-shikhandi`, `krishna-jayadratha`, `krishna-duryodhana-fall`).
**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Krishna, Arjuna, the Pandavas, the Kauravas, the armies.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; grave and spacious, a lament rather than a glory. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The armies (25 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn over the plain of Kurukshetra. Two vast armies face each other, banners without number, a sea of chariots and elephants, dust rising in the low sun. Between them, a single white chariot with four white horses. Krishna holds the reins; Arjuna stands behind him with his bow.
**Narration:** "Every attempt at peace had failed. On the plain of Kurukshetra the Pandavas and their cousins the Kauravas faced each other, with all the kings of the land behind them. In one chariot, holding the reins, stood Krishna. He had promised not to fight. He would only drive."
**Sound:** a deep wind, distant conches, the restless stamping of horses.

### 2. The Gita (20 sec)
**Visual:** The chariot halts between the armies. Arjuna sees his grandfather and teachers across the field; his bow slips from his hand and he sinks down. Krishna turns to him. Time seems to stop; the light around them turns gold.
**Narration:** "Arjuna saw his own grandfather and teachers in the enemy ranks, and he could not lift his bow. There, between the armies, Krishna spoke to him the song of the Lord, the Bhagavad Gita."
**Dialogue:** Krishna: "Do your duty without clinging to its fruit, and offer every act to me."
**Sound:** everything falls silent but a single sustained tone.

### 3. The eighteen days (25 sec)
**Visual:** No combat. A time-lapse over the field: eighteen suns rise and set, each one redder through the dust. Banners fall one by one. The white chariot moves through the haze, always at the centre, its horses' hooves never touching the dark.
**Narration:** "For eighteen days the war went on. Day after day the banners fell, and the great names of the age fell with them. And through it all the white chariot moved across the field, with the Lord holding the reins."
**Sound:** a slow drum, one beat for each day, growing fainter.

### 4. The fall of Bhishma (20 sec)
**Visual:** In silhouette, the aged Bhishma lies on a bed of arrows, as if resting, his face calm, looking at the sky. The warriors of both sides stand around him with bowed heads.
**Narration:** "Bhishma, the grandfather, who could not be defeated, at last lay down on a bed of arrows. Warriors from both sides came and stood around him, and bowed."
**Sound:** a soft lament on the flute.

### 5. The sun hidden (20 sec)
**Visual:** Late afternoon. Krishna lifts his discus and the sky darkens for a moment, as if the sun has set. Arjuna's bow rises. Then the light returns.
**Narration:** "On another day, tradition tells, Krishna's discus veiled the sun, so that Arjuna could keep his vow before nightfall. The Lord guarded his friend in every way."
**Sound:** a sudden hush, then a single bright string.

### 6. The mace (20 sec)
**Visual:** By a still lake at evening, two shadows with maces circle each other: Bhima and Duryodhana. A shadow falls. Krishna watches from a distance, his face unreadable.
**Narration:** "On the last day, Duryodhana, the eldest Kaurava, fell to Bhima's mace by the lake. The hundred brothers were gone, and the war was over."
**Sound:** one heavy, muffled blow, then silence.

### 7. The silence (30 sec)
**Visual:** The empty plain at night. Smoke drifts across the moon. Women in white walk slowly among the funeral fires. Krishna stands alone in the white chariot, the reins slack in his hands, looking over the field. Slowly the stars come out.
**Narration:** "The Bhagavata says that through this war the Lord lifted a heavy burden from the earth. But the poets who tell it do not sing of glory. They sing of the cost, and of the friend who stood beside the righteous all the way. When the noise was over, there was only silence, and the One who had held the reins."
**Sound:** the lament returns, very quiet; the wind; a far-off conch at the end.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the eighteen days of the Bharata war in many songs, nearly always through Krishna, the charioteer who carried Arjuna through it and lifted the earth's burden. Songs to show: 339 (Kanchipuram), 541 (Thirukkazhukundram), 930 (Neruvur) and 1060 (a general song).

## Production notes

- **An overview and a lament,** not a glory.
- **No scenes of combat.** Use light and silence.
