# The Golden Deer (Maricha)

`ramayana-maricha` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: beautiful, ominous, tragic | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Ravana asks Maricha to lure Rama away; Maricha becomes a golden deer, Sita asks for it, and Rama follows and kills it; Maricha's cry draws Lakshmana off, and Ravana carries Sita away.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Maricha:** a demon who becomes a golden deer.
- **Ravana.**
- **Sita, Rama, Lakshmana.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The plan (25 sec)
**Visual:** A lonely hermitage by the sea. Ravana arrives in his flying chariot and enters. Inside sits Maricha, an old rakshasa who has given up his wild ways and lives as a hermit. He listens to Ravana, and his face fills with fear. In a brief flash he remembers an arrow of Rama's that once flung him far across the sea.
**Narration:** "Ravana had decided to carry off Sita. He went to the rakshasa Maricha, a master of magic, and asked for his help. Maricha was afraid. Long ago he had felt Rama's arrow, and he begged Ravana to give up the plan. But Ravana threatened him, and Maricha had no choice."
**Sound:** a dark, low drone; waves on a lonely shore.

### 2. The deer (25 sec)
**Visual:** The forest of Panchavati, morning. Near Rama's hut, a deer steps out of the trees: its coat of gold, scattered with silver spots, its horns tipped with jewels, its eyes soft. It grazes, leaps, glows in the sunlight. Flowers seem to turn toward it. It is truly beautiful.
**Narration:** "The next morning, near Rama's hut, there appeared a deer such as no one had ever seen. Its coat was of gold, scattered with silver spots, and its horns shone like jewels. It moved through the flowers as if it were made of light. It was Maricha, in disguise."
**Sound:** a lilting, enchanting melody, with a faint sour note beneath it.

### 3. The wish (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sita, gathering flowers, sees the deer and gasps with delight. She calls Rama and points. Lakshmana frowns and shakes his head, speaking quietly. Rama looks at Sita's happy face and smiles.
**Narration:** "Sita saw it and her eyes shone. She asked Rama to catch it for her. Lakshmana was uneasy. No real deer looks like that, he said. It may be Maricha. Kamban says Sita, kind and trusting, did not see the trick."
**Dialogue:** Sita: "My lord, please bring me that deer."
**Sound:** the melody continues, sweet.

### 4. The chase (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama takes up his bow, tells Lakshmana to guard Sita, and follows the deer into the forest. The deer leaps away, vanishes, reappears, always just out of reach, leading him deeper and deeper into the trees until the hut is far behind.
**Narration:** "Rama took his bow and told Lakshmana to guard Sita. Then he followed the deer. It ran, it vanished, it appeared again, always just ahead, leading him farther and farther away from the hut, deeper into the forest."
**Sound:** quick footsteps, rustling leaves; the melody becomes teasing, then uneasy.

### 5. The arrow (20 sec)
**Visual:** Deep in the forest, Rama draws and looses a single arrow. A flash of light. The golden deer falls, and as it falls its form melts away and becomes the huge shape of the rakshasa Maricha, crashing down like a hill. With his last breath, he cries out in Rama's own voice.
**Narration:** "At last Rama loosed one arrow. The deer fell, and as it fell it became Maricha again, huge as a hill. And with his last breath, the rakshasa cried out in Rama's own voice."
**Dialogue:** Maricha: "Ah, Sita! Ah, Lakshmana!"
**Sound:** a twang, a flash, then the cry echoing through the forest.

### 6. The fear (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama stands still, understanding at once, his face pale. Kamban's moment: he thinks of Sita, her eyes full of tears. Cut to the hut: Sita hears the cry and turns to Lakshmana in panic, urging him to go. Lakshmana hesitates, then, stung by her fear, takes his bow and runs into the forest.
**Narration:** "Rama understood at once that it was a trick, and his heart filled with dread for Sita. At the hut, Sita heard the cry and believed Rama was in danger. She begged Lakshmana to go, and in her fear she spoke harshly to him. At last, he went."
**Sound:** a racing heartbeat drum; the cry echoing again, fainter.

### 7. The empty hut (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama and Lakshmana meet on the forest path; their faces fall. They run back. The hut stands empty in the evening light; flowers lie scattered on the ground. Far above, a dark chariot vanishes into the clouds toward the south.
**Narration:** "The brothers met on the forest path, and hurried back together. But the hut was empty. While they were gone, Ravana had come and carried Sita away. And Arunagiri sings of the Lord who overcame the magic deer and the rakshasas."
**Sound:** silence; wind through the empty doorway; one low note.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 113 (Palani) recalls the Lord who overcame the magic deer and the rakshasas, and whose arrow pierced Vali's chest, and names Murugan as the nephew of that Achyuta.

## Production notes

- **The deer must be beautiful,** so the audience understands.
- **Pair with `ramayana-sita-abduction` and `ramayana-sita-jewels`.**
