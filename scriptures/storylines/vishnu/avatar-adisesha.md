# The Lord on the Serpent Bed (Vishnu and Adisesha)

`avatar-adisesha` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: serene, vast, a lullaby of an image | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Vishnu lies asleep on the great serpent Adisesha (Ananta) in the Ocean of Milk, the image the poet returns to again and again.

**Honesty note:** this is an image, not an episode. The film is a meditation and adds nothing beyond what is needed to make the image visible.
**Added for the screen:** every visual (the ripples, the temple courtyard); the wording of the narration.

## Characters

- **Vishnu:** blue, reclining, calm, eyes half-closed.
- **Adisesha:** a vast, many-hooded serpent, silver-white, coiled as a bed and canopy.
- **Lakshmi:** at his feet (traditional in the image).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The milk ocean (25 sec)
**Visual:** Darkness softens into a pale glow. A calm white sea with no horizon; mist drifts slowly over it. No land, no birds, only a gentle swell rising and falling.
**Narration:** "The old books tell of a time when the three worlds lay under the waters. There was no shore, no sun and no sound. Only a white sea, softly breathing, as far as anyone could see. Even time seemed to be resting."
**Sound:** a low drone, soft as breath; the hush of water.

### 2. The coils (25 sec)
**Visual:** Far out, a silver line rises from the water, then another, curving into great rings. The coils of Adisesha settle, layer upon layer, into a broad, soft bed floating on the sea.
**Narration:** "Out of that stillness rose a great serpent, white as moonlight. His name is Ananta, the endless one, and the Tamil poets call him Adisesha. He gathered his long body into coil upon coil, and made of himself a bed upon the water."
**Sound:** a slow, gliding string phrase; water parting.

### 3. The hoods (20 sec)
**Visual:** At the head of the bed, a thousand hoods lift and open one after another, like petals, into a canopy. A jewel shines on each hood; the light spreads through the mist and the darkness draws back.
**Narration:** "Then he raised his hoods, a thousand of them, and spread them wide like an umbrella. The jewels on his hoods lit up the dark, the Bhagavata says, and the darkness all around was gone."
**Sound:** soft chimes, one for each opening hood, growing into a chord.

### 4. The Lord (25 sec)
**Visual:** The camera glides down beneath the canopy. On the coils lies Vishnu, dark blue like a rain cloud, in yellow silk, a garland on his chest, one arm beneath his head, eyes half closed. At his feet sits Lakshmi, gently pressing his feet; under her hands they glow a soft red.
**Narration:** "On that bed lies the Lord, alone on the waters. He seems to sleep, yet his eyes are not fully closed; he rests within himself and watches over all. Kamban, the Tamil poet, adds a tender touch: at his feet sits Lakshmi, and his feet turn red under her gentle hands."
**Sound:** a flute, very slow; the drone continues.

### 5. The breath (20 sec)
**Visual:** A slow close view of his chest rising and falling. With each breath a soft ripple runs out across the milk sea, ring after ring, to the edge of sight. Inside the ripples, faint shapes of worlds glimmer and fade.
**Narration:** "He lies there for a thousand cycles of the four ages. All living beings rest within him, the Bhagavata says, like fire sleeping inside wood, waiting for the next dawn of the worlds."
**Sound:** each breath a swell in the music; a faint shimmer as the worlds glimmer.

### 6. The poet (20 sec)
**Visual:** The picture softens into a temple courtyard at dusk. A singer with a palm-leaf book sings before a small lamp; behind him, painted on the wall, the same Lord on the same serpent. Then the wall painting dissolves back into the sea.
**Narration:** "Saints have sung this picture for more than a thousand years. One Alvar even envied the dark ocean, because it could touch the Lord. And Arunagiri, singing to Murugan, calls him again and again the nephew of the one who sleeps on the serpent."
**Sound:** a single voice humming a temple melody, a small bell.

### 7. The stillness (15 sec)
**Visual:** The camera rises slowly. The Lord, the hoods and the coils grow small on the white sea, until only a soft glow remains in the mist.
**Narration:** "The waters are still. The serpent keeps watch. And the Lord rests, holding all of us within him."
**Sound:** the drone fades; one last soft bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls this image in 26 songs, usually in a single line that calls Murugan the nephew of the Lord who sleeps on the serpent. Songs to show: 11 (Thiruparankundram), the one who sleeps on the serpent; 134 (Palani), the great Lord of Srirangam resting on the serpent's hood; and 895 (Athippattu), the pure green cloud asleep on the spotted serpent.
