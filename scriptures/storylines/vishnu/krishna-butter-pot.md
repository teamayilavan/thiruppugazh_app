# The Butter Thief (Krishna and the Hanging Pots)

`krishna-butter-pot` | Vishnu | running time 2:35 (7 scenes) | tone: mischievous, playful, affectionate | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Krishna steals curd and butter from the swinging pots in the gopis' houses, piling planks to reach them.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Krishna:** a small boy, bright-eyed, butter on his chin.
- **His friends (gopa boys).**
- **The gopis:** amused, exasperated.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The pots (20 sec)
**Visual:** A cowherd woman's house in Vraja, morning. Churning sounds; fresh butter goes into a clay pot. She hangs the pot high in a rope sling from the roof beam, well out of reach, and goes out with a satisfied nod. In the doorway, a small dark face with a peacock feather peeps in.
**Narration:** "In Vraja, the cowherd women churned their butter every morning. And every morning they hung it up high, in pots on ropes from the roof, far out of reach. Far out of reach, that is, of every child but one."
**Sound:** the churn's rhythm; a woman humming.

### 2. The plan (20 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna and his small friends crouch outside, whispering. Krishna points up at the pot; the boys look doubtful. He grins and taps his head. A monkey on the roof leans down to listen.
**Narration:** "Krishna gathered his friends outside the door. The pot was very high, and they were very small. But Krishna had a plan. He always had a plan."
**Dialogue:** Krishna: "Bring the planks. And the big mortar. Quietly!"
**Sound:** whispers, giggles, a monkey's chatter.

### 3. The tower (25 sec)
**Visual:** The boys roll in a heavy wooden mortar and turn it upside down. Planks go on top, then a stool. The tower wobbles. Krishna climbs, arms out for balance, while the boys hold their breath and steady the base. A friend climbs on another's shoulders to pass up a stick.
**Narration:** "They rolled in a heavy mortar and turned it upside down. They piled planks on top of it. The tower wobbled. The boys held their breath. And up climbed Krishna, steady as a little cat, until his hand could almost touch the pot."
**Sound:** wooden knocks, creaks, a held note of suspense.

### 4. The theft (20 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna pokes a hole in the bottom of the pot with the stick. Curd and butter drip down; the boys below open their mouths and catch it. Krishna scoops a handful and eats it, butter on his chin. In the dim room his body seems to glow, lighting the corner like a lamp.
**Narration:** "With a stick he made a hole in the pot, and down came the curd. The boys caught it in their mouths. The old book says that even in a dark room he could find the butter, for his own body shone like a lamp."
**Sound:** drip, drip, slurp; delighted giggles.

### 5. The sharing (25 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna sits on the upturned mortar, handing out butter to his friends, then to the monkeys who have crept in through the window. One monkey turns its nose up; Krishna shrugs and tips the pot over. The boys collapse laughing.
**Narration:** "Krishna never ate alone. He shared with his friends, and then with the monkeys, who came in through the window. And if the monkeys would not eat any more, he broke the pot. Nobody could say he was greedy. He was simply generous with other people's butter."
**Sound:** a playful tune, a clay pot cracking, laughter.

### 6. The catch (20 sec)
**Visual:** Footsteps. The woman of the house appears in the doorway, hands on hips. The boys scatter in every direction. Krishna is last off the tower, butter on his face, a pot still in his hands.
**Narration:** "Then came footsteps at the door. The boys vanished. Only Krishna was left, with butter on his chin and the pot still in his hands."
**Dialogue:** The cowherd woman: "Thief! I knew it!"
**Dialogue:** Krishna: "I am not a thief. You are the thief!"
**Sound:** footsteps, a gasp, scattering feet.

### 7. The smile (25 sec)
**Visual:** The women march Krishna to his mother Yashoda and list his crimes. Krishna sits beside his mother with folded hands, frightened eyes, the picture of a good boy. The women look at his face and forget what they were saying. Yashoda tries to frown and cannot. Everyone laughs.
**Narration:** "The women marched him home to his mother Yashoda and told her everything. But there he sat beside her like the best boy in the world, with big, frightened eyes. They looked at his face and forgot to scold him. And Yashoda only smiled."
**Sound:** the playful tune, slowed and tender; a last giggle.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the butter thief in about ten songs. Songs 121 and 178 (Palani), 683 (Vada Thirumullaivayil), 987 (Odukkathu Cheruvai) and 1098 are the ones to show: the curd thief scolded by the cowherd women, the hand stretched into the hanging pot, and the boy tied to the mortar.
