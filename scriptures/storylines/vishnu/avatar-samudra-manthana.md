# Churning the Ocean of Milk (Samudra Manthana)

`avatar-samudra-manthana` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: epic, strenuous, then dazzling

## One-line story

At Vishnu's word the gods and demons churn the Ocean of Milk with Mount Mandara as the rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope; wonders rise from the sea, and the jug of nectar is snatched by the demons.

## Characters

- **The gods (devas):** shining, tired.
- **The demons (asuras):** powerful, determined.
- **Vasuki:** the serpent rope, in pain.
- **Mandara:** the mountain.
- **Vishnu:** guiding, calm.
- **Lakshmi, Dhanvantari.**

## The storyline

### 1. The need (20 sec)
The gods, weak, ask Vishnu for strength; he tells them to churn the ocean.
**Narration:** "The gods asked Vishnu, and he told them to churn the Ocean of Milk."

### 2. The rod and the rope (25 sec)
Mandara is pulled out and set in the sea; Vasuki coils around it.
**Narration:** "Mount Mandara was the rod, and the serpent Vasuki the rope."

### 3. The pulling (25 sec)
Gods and demons pull from either end; the mountain turns.
**Narration:** "Gods pulled one end, demons the other."

### 4. The wonders (25 sec)
A white horse rises, then a great white elephant, a jewel that shines.
**Narration:** "Treasures rose from the sea: a horse, an elephant, a jewel."

### 5. Lakshmi (20 sec)
Lakshmi rises on a lotus, takes a garland and walks to Vishnu.
**Narration:** "And Lakshmi rose, and chose Vishnu."

### 6. The physician (20 sec)
Dhanvantari rises with a jug of nectar.
**Narration:** "Then Dhanvantari came, with the nectar."

### 7. The snatching (20 sec)
The demons seize the jug and flee.
**Narration:** "The demons snatched it away."

### 8. The turn (10 sec)
The gods turn to Vishnu, hopeful.
**Narration:** "And the gods turned to Vishnu again."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Songs 11, 27 and 169 among nine.

## Production notes

- **Set the scale.** The churning is the grandest image of the Vishnu set.
- **Show the poison briefly or not at all;** it has its own film (`avatar-halahala`).
