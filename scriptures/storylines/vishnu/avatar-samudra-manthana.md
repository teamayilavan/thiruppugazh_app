# Churning the Ocean of Milk (Samudra Manthana)

`avatar-samudra-manthana` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: epic, strenuous, then dazzling | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Vishnu's word the gods and demons churn the Ocean of Milk with Mount Mandara as the rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope; wonders rise from the sea, and the jug of nectar is snatched by the demons.

## Characters

- **The gods (devas):** shining, tired.
- **The demons (asuras):** powerful, determined.
- **Vasuki:** the serpent rope, in pain.
- **Mandara:** the mountain.
- **Vishnu:** guiding, calm.
- **Lakshmi, Dhanvantari.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The need (20 sec)
**Visual:** Heaven grown dim: the gods pale and tired, their garlands wilted. Across a valley the demons' camp glows with strength. The gods go to Vishnu, who stands calm in a soft light, and bow.
**Narration:** "The gods had grown weak, and the demons were strong. The Tamil saints remember why: a sage's curse had sunk the gods' good fortune into the sea. So the gods went to Vishnu for help."
**Dialogue:** Vishnu: "Make peace with the demons. Together, churn the Ocean of Milk, with the mountain Mandara for your rod and the serpent Vasuki for your rope."
**Sound:** a hollow, tired melody; Vishnu's voice with a warm chord beneath.

### 2. The rod and the rope (25 sec)
**Visual:** Gods and demons together tear Mount Mandara from the earth and stagger with it toward the sea; it slips and crashes down, and they fall. A gold light: Vishnu arrives on Garuda, raises the fallen with a glance, lifts the mountain with one hand onto Garuda's back, and sets it beside the sea. The great serpent Vasuki is coiled around it.
**Narration:** "Gods and demons pulled up the golden mountain and carried it toward the sea, but it was too heavy, and it fell on them. Then Vishnu came on Garuda. With one glance he raised the fallen, and with one hand he lifted the mountain and set it by the sea. They wound Vasuki around it as a rope."
**Sound:** heaving chants, a crash, silence; then wings and a bright fanfare.

### 3. The pulling (25 sec)
**Visual:** Vishnu takes the serpent's head; the demons fold their arms and refuse the tail. Vishnu smiles and moves to the tail with the gods. The pulling begins; the mountain spins. It starts to sink, and a vast tortoise rises beneath it and holds it up. Fire and smoke pour from Vasuki's mouths over the demons.
**Narration:** "The demons would not hold the tail; only the head was good enough for them. The Lord smiled and gave it to them, and took the tail with the gods. As they churned, the mountain began to sink, until the Lord himself, as a tortoise, held it up. And Vasuki's fiery breath fell on the demons at the head."
**Sound:** a churning rhythm on drums, rising; a deep thud as the tortoise takes the weight.

### 4. The wonders (25 sec)
**Visual:** A dark smoke rises first and is quickly gone (its story is told elsewhere). Then the sea foams, and out of it come wonders one by one: a gentle cow; a horse as white as the moon; a white elephant with four tusks; a red jewel that floats to Vishnu's chest; a flowering tree of heaven.
**Narration:** "The sea gave up a terrible poison first, but Shiva took it away. Then came wonders: the cow of plenty; the white horse Uccaihshravas; the elephant Airavata with four tusks; and the jewel Kaustubha, which Vishnu took to wear upon his chest."
**Sound:** a new instrument for each wonder, building into a joyful theme.

### 5. Lakshmi (20 sec)
**Visual:** Lightning-bright, Lakshmi rises from the sea, a garland of lotuses in her hands, bees circling it. She walks among gods, demons and sages, looking at each in turn, then goes to Vishnu and places the garland on his shoulders. Flowers fall from the sky.
**Narration:** "Then rose Lakshmi, shining like lightning. She walked among them all, looking for one who had every good quality and no flaw. She found him in Vishnu alone, and placed her lotus garland on his shoulders."
**Sound:** soft bells and the hum of bees; then conches and drums of celebration.

### 6. The physician (20 sec)
**Visual:** The churning goes on. From the foam steps a young man, dark-skinned and strong, in yellow silk with pearl earrings, holding a golden jug that glows from within.
**Narration:** "At last, from the foam, came a young man, dark and strong, dressed in yellow. He was Dhanvantari, master of healing, and in his hands he held the jug of nectar, the drink that frees from death."
**Sound:** a hush; a single clear, sweet tone.

### 7. The snatching (20 sec)
**Visual:** A rush of demons; the jug is torn from Dhanvantari's hands and carried away. The gods stand empty-handed and dismayed, then turn to Vishnu.
**Narration:** "The moment the demons saw the jug, they snatched it away. The gods stood empty-handed after all their labour, and turned to the Lord."
**Dialogue:** Vishnu: "Do not grieve. I will see that you have the nectar."
**Sound:** a sudden discord, running feet; then quiet.

### 8. The turn (10 sec)
**Visual:** Close on Vishnu's face: a small smile. A shimmer of light begins to change his form. The sea, calm now, holds the reflection.
**Narration:** "The Lord smiled. He already knew what he would do."
**Sound:** a playful flute phrase that leads into the next film.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the churning in nine songs, praising Murugan as the nephew of the Lord who churned the sea. Songs to show: 27 (Thiruchendur), the tall cloud who churned the ocean; 723 (Thiruvakkarai), who set the mountain in the sea, wound the serpent round it and raised the nectar; 952 (Keeranur), who had the milk ocean churned and gave out the nectar; and 11 (Thiruparankundram).

## Production notes

- **Set the scale.** The churning is the grandest image of the Vishnu set.
- **Show the poison briefly or not at all;** it has its own film (`avatar-halahala`).
