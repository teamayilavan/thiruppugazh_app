# The Poison from the Sea (Halahala)

`avatar-halahala` | Vishnu | running time 2:35 (7 scenes) | tone: alarm, then a lone act of courage | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

When the churned ocean throws up the kalakuta poison, all beings go to Shiva, who drinks it from the hollow of his palm.

**Note:** the same event is told from Shiva's side in `deeds-poison`. This film is the Vishnu-set view and follows the Bhagavata; it ends with the churning going on.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the spoken lines (plain paraphrases of 8.7.21 and 8.7.40) and the wording of the narration.

## Characters

- **The gods and demons, all beings.**
- **Shiva:** calm.
- **Vishnu:** nearby, watchful.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The churning (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Ocean of Milk foaming around Mount Mandara as it spins, the serpent Vasuki wound around it, gods at the tail and demons at the head, all straining. The sea heaves; fish and great sea creatures surface in alarm.
**Narration:** "The gods and the demons were churning the Ocean of Milk, hoping for the nectar of immortality. They pulled with all their strength, and the whole ocean heaved and boiled. But the first thing the sea gave up was not nectar."
**Sound:** a pounding churning rhythm, waves crashing.

### 2. The poison (25 sec)
**Visual:** From the depths a dark cloud rises, first a thread, then a spreading mass of black and violet smoke with no face or form. It rolls across the water; the milk turns grey beneath it; trees on the far shore wither as it passes.
**Narration:** "Out of the depths rose a dark, burning poison, which the books call kalakuta and halahala. It spread up and down and in every direction, faster than anyone could run. Wherever it passed, the air grew hot and heavy, and nothing could stand against it."
**Sound:** the rhythm stops dead; a low, rising, hissing roar.

### 3. The flight (25 sec)
**Visual:** Gods and demons drop the rope and flee. Sages, animals and people run before the creeping cloud. Even the gods' leaders look back in fear. Vishnu walks among them, calm, leading them toward the north.
**Narration:** "The churning stopped. Gods and demons let go of the rope and ran, and every living thing ran with them. There was nowhere to hide from the poison. So all of them, the Lord among them, went together to the one who could help: to Shiva on Mount Kailasa."
**Sound:** running feet, cries; the hiss of the cloud behind.

### 4. The shelter (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa, white and still. Shiva sits in meditation with Parvati beside him. The crowd arrives breathless and bows at his feet.
**Narration:** "On Kailasa sat Shiva, with Parvati at his side. The frightened beings fell at his feet."
**Dialogue:** The gods: "Great God, save us from this burning poison, which is spreading through all the three worlds. We come to you for shelter."
**Sound:** sudden quiet; the wind of the high mountain.

### 5. The palm (25 sec)
**Visual:** Shiva opens his eyes. He looks at the trembling crowd with great compassion, then turns to Parvati. She looks back at him steadily and gives a small nod. He stands and holds out his right hand, the palm cupped.
**Narration:** "Shiva looked at them with compassion. He turned to Parvati and said that it is the duty of the great to protect those who suffer. Parvati knew his power, and she gave her consent. Then he held out his hand and gathered the whole poison into the hollow of his palm."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Let me drink this poison, so that all these beings may live."
**Sound:** a deep, single drum stroke; the hiss gathers into one point and falls silent.

### 6. The drinking (25 sec)
**Visual:** The dark mass swirls down into his cupped palm. He raises it and drinks. A blue mark spreads at his throat and stays there, like a jewel. A few drops fall from his hand; scorpions and snakes slip in to take them.
**Narration:** "Shiva drank it. The poison did not harm him; it only left a blue mark on his throat, and that mark became his ornament. A few drops fell from his hand, and the scorpions and snakes took them, which is why, the Bhagavata says, they carry poison to this day."
**Sound:** a long, low chord, then a soft brightening.

### 7. The churning goes on (20 sec)
**Visual:** Everyone bows, Vishnu and Brahma among them, praising Shiva. Then back at the sea: the air is clear, the milk white again. Gods and demons take up the rope and the mountain turns once more.
**Narration:** "Everyone praised what Shiva had done, Brahma and Vishnu too. The air was clear again. And gods and demons went back to the sea, took up the rope, and the churning began once more."
**Sound:** the churning rhythm returns, lighter and hopeful.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri puts both gods into a single line: Shiva who drank the poison and Vishnu who churned the sea. Songs to show: 27 (Thiruchendur), the son of the one who drank the poison and nephew of the cloud who churned the sea; 46 (Thiruchendur), where the halahala goes to Shiva and the nectar to the gods; and 169 (Palani).
