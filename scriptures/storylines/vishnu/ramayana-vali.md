# The Arrow from Behind the Tree (Vali)

`ramayana-vali` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: tense, tragic, a hard question | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Rama draws his bow and looses an arrow that strikes Vali in the chest and fells him.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Rama draws his bow and looses an arrow that strikes Vali in the chest and fells him | Valmiki Ramayana, Kishkindha Kanda 16.32-36; Kamba, Vali Vadhai Padalam 65-67 |
| Songs 113, 266 and 437 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The film treats the moral question (Vali's reproach and Rama's answer) lightly, since the research gives the arrow only.

## Characters

- **Vali:** a mighty monkey king, proud.
- **Sugriva, Rama, Lakshmana.**
- **Tara:** Vali's wife, who warns him.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it. This is a hard story. The film gives no verdict and leaves the question open; no gore.

### 1. The challenge (25 sec)
**Visual:** The gates of Kishkindha, a city of caves and cliffs. Sugriva, wearing a garland of flowering creeper round his neck, stands before the gates and roars a challenge. Behind him, hidden among the trees, wait Rama and Lakshmana. Inside the city, Vali, huge and golden, rises from his seat in fury.
**Narration:** "Once before, Sugriva had fought his brother Vali, and Rama had not shot, because the two brothers looked so alike he could not tell them apart. Now Sugriva came again, with a garland of flowers round his neck so that Rama would know him, and shouted his challenge at the gates."
**Sound:** Sugriva's roar echoing off the cliffs; drums.

### 2. The warning (20 sec)
**Visual:** Inside the palace, Vali's wife Tara catches his arm. She speaks urgently, her face pale. Vali gently frees himself, proud and certain, and strides toward the gate.
**Narration:** "Vali's wife, Tara, begged him not to go. She had heard that Sugriva had a new friend, a prince named Rama. But Vali was proud. He would not hide from his younger brother."
**Dialogue:** Tara: "Do not go out today. He would not come back so soon without help."
**Dialogue:** Vali: "I will not hide from my own brother. I will only humble him."
**Sound:** a tense string line.

### 3. The duel (25 sec)
**Visual:** The two brothers meet in a clearing and fight: a whirl of golden and brown, fists like thunder, trees uprooted and hurled, rocks shattered. The duel is shown as dance and silhouette. Slowly Sugriva tires; he staggers and looks toward the trees.
**Narration:** "The two brothers fought like two mountains at war. They struck with fists and trees and stones, and the hills rang. But Vali was the stronger. Little by little Sugriva weakened, and he looked toward the trees where Rama stood."
**Sound:** heavy blows, crashing trees, the drums building.

### 4. The hidden bow (20 sec)
**Visual:** Among the trees, Rama watches, very still. He sees the garland on Sugriva's neck. His face is grave, not eager. He lifts his bow and fits an arrow. Lakshmana looks at him and says nothing.
**Narration:** "Rama saw the garland, and knew which brother was Sugriva. He had given his word to help his friend. He lifted his bow, and fitted an arrow, and his face was grave."
**Sound:** the fight sounds fade into the distance; Rama's breath; the creak of the string.

### 5. The arrow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Close on Rama's hands drawing the string back to his shoulder. He looses. The arrow streaks out of the trees as a line of light. A flash at Vali's chest; the duel freezes.
**Narration:** "Rama drew the string to his shoulder and let the arrow go. It flew like a flash of lightning and struck Vali in the chest. Kamban says it pierced the breast of one who held the strength of the whole earth."
**Sound:** the twang, a rushing hiss, then sudden silence.

### 6. The fall (25 sec)
**Visual:** Vali stands a moment in silhouette, then falls slowly backward, like a great mountain toppling. Dust rises around him. He lies on the ground, alive for now, and turns his head toward the trees. Rama steps out into the open and walks toward him.
**Narration:** "Vali fell, Kamban says, like Mount Meru torn up by its roots. Lying on the earth, he looked up and saw Rama coming out from the trees. And he asked Rama why he had struck him from hiding. Rama answered him. People have thought about that question, and that answer, ever since."
**Dialogue:** Vali: "Why, Rama? I was not fighting you."
**Sound:** a deep, slow drum; the wind.

### 7. The silence (20 sec)
**Visual:** Evening. Tara and the young prince Angada kneel beside Vali. Sugriva stands apart, head bowed, grieving now for his brother. Rama stands at a little distance, quiet. The light fades on the hills.
**Narration:** "Then Vali grew quiet. He asked his brother to care for his son Angada. Sugriva wept for him. The kingdom was won, but no one in Kishkindha rejoiced that night."
**Sound:** a single flute, the evening wind.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls Vali's fall in seven songs, always in a single line. Songs to show: 113 (Palani), the strong-armed Achyuta who loosed the arrow; 437 (Thiruvannamalai), the one who gave Tara to Sugriva; 731 (Thiruvamathur), the single arrow that felled the seven trees with Vali; and 840 (Vedaranyam), the one who pierced the trees and Vali's chest.
