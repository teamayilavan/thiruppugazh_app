# The Stone That Woke (Ahalya)

`ramayana-ahalya` | Vishnu | running time 2:35 (7 scenes) | tone: hushed, sorrowful, redemptive | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

On the way to Mithila the dust of Rama's feet frees Ahalya, Gautama's wife, who had stood as stone, or unseen, for years after Indra's deceit.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| On the way to Mithila the dust of Rama's feet frees Ahalya | Research set `ramayana-ahalya`; Kamba, Akalikai Padalam (traditional source; not read verse by verse here) |
| She had stood as stone/unseen for years after Indra's deceit | same |
| Murugan's hymn picks "the foot that touched the stone" | Thiruppugazh songs 483 and 669 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The film follows Kamba's tradition of a stone; Valmiki has her unseen, in penance.

## Characters

- **Ahalya:** a graceful woman, still as stone.
- **Gautama:** a sage.
- **Rama, Lakshmana, Vishwamitra.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The hermitage (25 sec)
**Visual:** Morning on the road to Mithila. The young princes Rama and Lakshmana walk behind the sage Vishwamitra. Beside the road lies an old hermitage, overgrown: a broken fence, a cold fire altar, creepers over the doorway. No birds sing there.
**Narration:** "On the road to Mithila, the sage Vishwamitra led the two young princes, Rama and Lakshmana. Beside the road stood an old hermitage. It had once been full of prayer. Now it was empty, and even the birds kept away."
**Sound:** soft footsteps on a dusty road; the birdsong fades as they reach the hermitage.

### 2. The stone (25 sec)
**Visual:** In a quiet clearing inside the hermitage, a smooth grey stone, its outline faintly like a woman kneeling with her head bowed. Dust and dry leaves lie on it. The light around it is cool and still.
**Narration:** "In the middle of the clearing lay a stone. If you looked closely, it had the shape of a woman, kneeling, her head bowed. In the telling of the Tamil poet Kamban, this stone was Ahalya, the wife of the sage Gautama."
**Sound:** a single sustained note; a light wind.

### 3. The reason (20 sec)
**Visual:** A soft, hand-drawn flashback in muted colours. The hermitage alive and bright. Indra, king of the gods, arrives disguised in the form of Gautama. Then the real Gautama returns from the river; his face falls; he raises his hand. Indra flees as a shadow. The flashback fades to grey.
**Narration:** "Long ago, Indra, the king of the gods, had come to this place in the form of her husband, to deceive her. When Gautama learned of it, he spoke a curse. And for many long years, Ahalya waited in silence."
**Sound:** a quiet old tune; the curse is a low drum, not a shout.

### 4. The princes (20 sec)
**Visual:** Back in the present. Vishwamitra stops at the edge of the clearing and gestures. Rama looks at the stone with gentle eyes. Lakshmana waits a step behind. Rama walks forward into the clearing.
**Narration:** "Vishwamitra stopped and showed the place to Rama. The prince did not hurry. He looked at the stone with kindness, as if he already knew someone was waiting there. Then he stepped into the clearing."
**Sound:** the note softens; a faint temple bell far away.

### 5. The dust (25 sec)
**Visual:** Close on Rama's bare feet on the path. A little dust lifts from his step and drifts, glowing gold in a beam of sunlight, onto the stone. Where it settles, the grey begins to warm, and colour spreads slowly across it like dawn.
**Narration:** "As he passed, a little dust rose from his feet and settled on the stone. Kamban says that was enough. Just the dust of his feet. The long sleep began to break, and the stone grew warm."
**Sound:** a soft shimmer, like wind chimes; the music begins to rise.

### 6. The waking (25 sec)
**Visual:** The stone softens into the form of Ahalya, in a plain hermitage robe, radiant and calm. She opens her eyes. Flowers open in the clearing; birds return to the trees. She folds her hands to Rama. Rama and Lakshmana bow to her with respect, and Vishwamitra smiles. Far down the path, Gautama appears, coming home.
**Narration:** "Ahalya stood up in her own form again, as she had been in the old days. She folded her hands. In Valmiki's telling, the two princes bowed and touched her feet in respect, and Gautama came home to his wife."
**Dialogue:** Vishwamitra: "Rama, her waiting is over."
**Sound:** birdsong returns; a warm chord.

### 7. The song (15 sec)
**Visual:** Ahalya and Gautama stand together at the hermitage gate as the princes walk on toward Mithila. The camera rests on the empty place where the stone lay, now covered with small flowers.
**Narration:** "Centuries later, Arunagiri sang of this. Murugan, he says, is the nephew of the Lord whose golden feet touched a stone, and a woman rose to her own form."
**Sound:** the opening melody returns, gentle; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls Ahalya in two songs, calling Murugan the nephew of the Lord whose foot touched the stone and made it a woman again. Show song 483 (Chidambaram) and song 669 (Virinchipuram).

## Production notes

- **Treat Ahalya with respect.** Her story is of waking, not blame.
- **Say that the stone is Kamba's telling.**
