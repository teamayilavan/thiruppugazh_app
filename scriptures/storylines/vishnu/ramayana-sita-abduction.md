# Across the Sky (The Abduction of Sita)

`ramayana-sita-abduction` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: anguished, swift, ominous | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

After Lakshmana cuts off Surpanakha's nose and ears, Ravana lures Rama away with the golden deer and carries Sita through the sky.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Lakshmana cuts off Surpanakha's nose and ears | Research set `ramayana-sita-abduction`; Valmiki Aranya Kanda; Kamba, Surpanakhai Padalam (traditional source; not read verse by verse here) |
| Ravana lures Rama away with the golden deer | same (see `ramayana-maricha`) |
| He carries Sita through the sky | same |
| Song 272 recalls it | Thiruppugazh song 272 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The mutilation is mentioned in narration only and not shown.

## Characters

- **Surpanakha:** a demoness, jealous.
- **Ravana:** ten-headed, disguised as a holy man at first.
- **Sita:** brave, in tears.
- **Jatayu** (a flash).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it. Sita is shown with dignity and courage throughout. Surpanakha's wounding is told in narration only and never shown; Ravana never lays hands on Sita on screen (the film follows Kamban's telling for the seizure).

### 1. The demoness (25 sec)
**Visual:** The hermitage at Panchavati, by the Godavari: a neat leaf hut, flowers, a deer grazing. Rama sits outside; Sita gathers flowers. A tall woman in shimmering clothes appears at the edge of the clearing, Surpanakha, Ravana's sister, and speaks to Rama. He answers courteously and shakes his head. Her face darkens; she turns toward Sita in anger. Lakshmana steps between them. Cut away to the trees, swaying in a sudden wind.
**Narration:** "In the forest of Panchavati, Rama, Sita and Lakshmana lived in a little hut by the river. One day Surpanakha, the sister of Ravana, came and asked Rama to marry her. Rama refused, for he had his wife. In her anger she rushed at Sita, and Lakshmana stopped her, and wounded her, cutting off her nose and ears."
**Sound:** gentle river sounds, then a sharp discord; wind in the trees.

### 2. The brother's revenge (20 sec)
**Visual:** Lanka: a golden hall of pillars and lamps. Surpanakha, her face hidden in her veil, falls before the throne. Ravana, ten-headed, rises slowly. She speaks of Rama's strength, and of Sita's beauty.
**Narration:** "She fled, first to her brother Khara, whose army fell before Rama's arrows, and then to her brother Ravana, king of Lanka. She told him of the princes in the forest, and of Sita, the most beautiful woman in the world."
**Dialogue:** Surpanakha: "Take her, brother, and you will have your revenge."
**Sound:** a deep drum, the echo of the great hall.

### 3. The plan (20 sec)
**Visual:** Ravana's faces harden. He goes to the old demon Maricha by the sea. Maricha pleads with him, then bows his head. A golden deer with silver spots appears at the edge of the hermitage. Sita sees it and gasps with delight. Rama follows it into the forest; later a cry rings out in Rama's voice, and Lakshmana, at Sita's urging, runs after it.
**Narration:** "Ravana made a plan. The demon Maricha took the shape of a golden deer, and Sita longed for it. Rama went after it into the forest. Then a cry came back in Rama's voice, and Sita, afraid for him, sent Lakshmana to help. Now she was alone."
**Sound:** a playful tune for the deer that turns sour; the false cry, echoing.

### 4. The beggar (25 sec)
**Visual:** An old holy man in saffron, with a staff and a water pot, comes slowly to the hut. Sita, true to her duty, welcomes him, offers him water and a seat, and tells him who she is. He asks questions; his eyes are too bright.
**Narration:** "Then an old holy man came to the hut, begging. Sita did what every good host does: she greeted him, offered him water and a seat, and told him who she was. She did not know that this was Ravana himself."
**Dialogue:** Sita: "Be welcome, holy one. My husband will return soon with food from the forest."
**Sound:** a quiet, uneasy melody; the forest strangely silent.

### 5. The seizure (25 sec)
**Visual:** The holy man rises; his shadow grows huge, ten heads and twenty arms against the sky. Sita steps back but stands straight, unafraid, and answers him with scorn. Ravana's chariot appears. Following Kamban, he does not touch her: he tears up the earth around the hut, a whole block of ground with Sita upon it, and lifts it into the sky.
**Narration:** "Then he showed his true form. He told her to come to Lanka and be his queen. Sita answered him without fear: she would never leave Rama. Valmiki says Ravana seized her. Kamban says he did not touch her, but tore up the very earth beneath her and carried it away."
**Dialogue:** Sita: "I belong to Rama, as light belongs to the sun. You will never have me."
**Sound:** a roar of wind, the ground cracking, then the rush of flight.

### 6. The sky (25 sec)
**Visual:** The chariot flies south over forests and rivers. Sita looks down, calling out. An old vulture, Jatayu, rises to fight the chariot; a flurry of wings and shadow; he falls back toward the earth. Sita keeps calling to the trees, the river, the animals below.
**Narration:** "He carried her across the sky. The old vulture Jatayu, a friend of Rama's father, flew up to stop him and fought bravely, but he was struck down. Sita called to the trees and the rivers and the beasts of the forest: tell Rama where I have gone."
**Sound:** the wind, the cry of a great bird, then Sita's voice, carried away.

### 7. The cry (20 sec)
**Visual:** Back at the hut: empty. A scattered pile of flowers, a fallen water pot. Rama and Lakshmana run into the clearing and stop. Rama looks around, calling her name. The camera rises above the silent forest.
**Narration:** "When Rama came back, the hut was empty. He called her name, and only the forest answered. So began the long search, that would lead him across the sea to Lanka."
**Dialogue:** Rama: "Sita! Sita!"
**Sound:** Rama's call echoing, then a long held note.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri tells this story in song 272 (Thiruthani): the brute who carried her through the clouds on his chariot and kept her in his towered city, and the Lord of bowmen who then ended him, root and all. Murugan is that Lord's nephew.

## Production notes

- **Treat Sita with dignity.** Show her courage.
- **No violence to Surpanakha on screen.**
