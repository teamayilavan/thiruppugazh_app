# The Enchantress (Mohini and the Nectar)

`avatar-mohini` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: graceful, witty, then decisive | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The demons snatch the jug of nectar, and Vishnu takes the form of the beautiful woman Mohini; they are charmed, and she smiles and speaks to them.

**Honesty note:** Rahu's theft and beheading (8.9.24-26) is left out of the film to keep it gentle; the book narrative mentions it. Mohini's reply is softened from the text (8.9.9-10) to a plain, dignified line. The songs do not name Mohini.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the timing, the spoken lines (plain paraphrases of 8.9.7 and 8.9.9) and the wording of the narration.

## Characters

- **Mohini:** a graceful, radiant woman, calm in the eyes.
- **The demons:** quarrelling over the jug.
- **The gods.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The quarrel (25 sec)
**Visual:** On the shore of the Ocean of Milk the demons crowd around the golden jug of nectar. It passes from hand to hand; each grabs it, each is pushed away. Old friends glare at one another. Far off, the gods stand empty-handed.
**Narration:** "The demons had snatched the jug of nectar from the sea, but now they could not agree who should drink first. Each one cried: me first, not you! They pulled the jug from one another's hands, and their old friendship turned to anger."
**Sound:** a tangle of raised voices, clashing drums.

### 2. The vision (25 sec)
**Visual:** Through the evening light a young woman walks slowly toward them along the shore, dark and radiant like a fresh rain cloud, in a simple silk sari, a lotus in her hair, ankle bells softly ringing. One by one the demons fall silent and turn.
**Narration:** "Then, the Bhagavata says, the Lord himself took the form of a woman, more beautiful than anyone had ever seen. The demons stopped their quarrel and stared. How wonderful she is, they said to one another. Who is she, and where has she come from?"
**Sound:** the quarrel fades; ankle bells and a graceful veena melody.

### 3. The charm (20 sec)
**Visual:** The demons gather around her, respectful and eager. Their leader steps forward and holds out the jug with both hands.
**Dialogue:** A demon chief: "We are brothers, children of one father, yet we fight over this jug. Be our judge. Divide the nectar fairly among us."
**Narration:** "They called her Mohini, the enchantress, and begged her to settle their quarrel."
**Sound:** the veena continues, lightly.

### 4. The smile (20 sec)
**Visual:** Mohini smiles, a little amused. She does not take the jug at once; she tilts her head, teasing. The demons laugh, reassured, and press the jug into her hands.
**Dialogue:** Mohini: "Why should you trust a stranger like me? Wise men do not."
**Narration:** "She spoke as if in jest, and her joking made them trust her all the more. Laughing, they put the jug into her hands, and promised to accept whatever she did, fair or not."
**Sound:** soft laughter, a playful flute phrase.

### 5. The jug (25 sec)
**Visual:** An arena prepared with lamps, garlands and incense. Gods and demons, having bathed and fasted, sit facing east in two separate rows. Mohini enters slowly with the jug, her bells ringing, and every eye follows her.
**Narration:** "They bathed, they fasted, and they made offerings in the fire. Then gods and demons sat down in two long rows, in a hall bright with lamps and flowers. Mohini entered with the jug in her hands, and seated each side in its own place."
**Sound:** a stately processional; bells.

### 6. The serving (25 sec)
**Visual:** Mohini walks first along the demons' row, speaking sweetly to each one; they smile and wait, content. Then she turns to the gods' row and pours the nectar into their cupped hands, one after another. The demons watch in silence, keeping their promise.
**Narration:** "She went first to the demons and pleased them with sweet words. Then she went to the gods and poured the nectar for them. The demons said nothing. They had promised to accept her choice, and they did not want to quarrel with her."
**Sound:** a gentle pouring sound, a rising, happy theme.

### 7. The form (20 sec)
**Visual:** As the last god drinks, Mohini's form shimmers and opens into light. Vishnu stands there, dark blue, in yellow silk, with conch and discus. The demons gasp; the gods fold their hands.
**Narration:** "When the gods had drunk, the Lord showed his own form to everyone. The enchantress had been Vishnu all along. The same work had been done by gods and demons alike, the Bhagavata says, but only those who sheltered at the Lord's feet received its fruit."
**Sound:** a conch, then a full, bright chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri never names Mohini; he praises the Lord who gave the nectar to the gods. Songs to show: 46 (Thiruchendur), who gave the nectar to the frightened gods so that they might live; 169 (Palani), who shared out the nectar; 367 (Thiruvannamalai), the cloud of grace who ended the gods' hunger; and 952 (Keeranur).

## Production notes

- **Tasteful.** Mohini is graceful, not sensual.
- **No humiliation of the demons.** They are charmed, not mocked.
