# The Brahma Weapon (The End of Ravana)

`ramayana-ravana-death` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: solemn, final, still | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Rama fits the Brahma weapon to his bow; it tears Ravana's heart and he falls from his chariot.

## Characters

- **Rama, Ravana, Matali.**
- **The gods.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it. The tone is solemn: this is an ending, not a celebration.

### 1. The last attempt (25 sec)
**Visual:** Late afternoon over the battlefield of Lanka. Dust hangs in red light. Two chariots face each other across a field gone quiet: Rama's, driven by Matali, Indra's charioteer, and Ravana's, black and gold. Both warriors are worn; Rama's face is calm, Ravana's tight with fury. Arrows lie broken on the ground between them.
**Narration:** "The battle had gone on for a long time. Arrow had met arrow, weapon had met weapon, and still Ravana stood. Rama had struck him again and again, and again and again the king of Lanka rose up to fight. The whole sky seemed to be holding its breath."
**Sound:** a low drone, distant wind, no drums.

### 2. The weapon (25 sec)
**Visual:** Matali turns on the chariot seat and speaks. Rama nods and reaches into his quiver. He draws out one arrow unlike the others: its head blazes like the sun, its shaft smokes with a pale fire. The light of it falls on Rama's face.
**Narration:** "Then Matali, the charioteer, reminded him of the weapon he had been given. It was the Brahma weapon, made by Brahma, the Creator, long ago. Rama drew it from his quiver. It shone like a fire at the end of time."
**Dialogue:** Matali: "Lord, why wait? Use the weapon of Brahma. The hour of his end has come."
**Sound:** a single deep bell; a hum that grows as the arrow is drawn.

### 3. The blessing (20 sec)
**Visual:** Rama holds the arrow before him and closes his eyes; his lips move. Above the clouds the gods gather, hands folded, and look down in silence. The arrow's glow steadies and deepens.
**Narration:** "Rama spoke over it the sacred words, as the old teachings direct. Above the clouds the gods gathered to watch, and the whole world grew still. The earth itself seemed to tremble."
**Sound:** soft chanting under the music, then stillness.

### 4. The loosing (25 sec)
**Visual:** Rama fits the arrow to his bow and draws the string back to his ear. A long held moment. He looses it. The arrow leaves the bow as a line of white fire across the darkening sky; the hills and the sea light up beneath it.
**Narration:** "He fitted the arrow to his bow and drew the string to his ear. Then he let it go. Kamban says it carried the power of wind and fire together, and that beside its light, even the sun of the last day would look like a firefly."
**Sound:** the creak of the bow, one breath, then a rushing roar.

### 5. The strike (25 sec)
**Visual:** Seen only as light: the line of fire reaches Ravana's chariot. A flash fills the frame, bright and silent. For a moment the whole battlefield, the hills and the sky turn white, then sway, as if the world itself has reeled.
**Narration:** "The arrow struck Ravana in the chest, and tore through his heart. Kamban says that at that moment the earth, all the directions and the sky itself reeled. Then the arrow, its work done, came back to Rama."
**Sound:** the roar cuts to silence; a long ringing tone.

### 6. The fall (20 sec)
**Visual:** In silhouette against the fading light, Ravana's great bow slips from his hand. His figure leans and falls slowly from the chariot. We never see him on the ground; the camera stays on the empty chariot, its banner drooping.
**Narration:** "The great bow fell from Ravana's hands. And the king of Lanka, who had made the three worlds afraid, fell from his chariot to the earth, and did not rise again."
**Sound:** one low drum, then nothing.

### 7. The stillness (20 sec)
**Visual:** Silence over the field. The armies lower their weapons. Flowers drift down from the sky, slowly, settling on the dust. Rama lowers his bow and bows his head, not in triumph but in quiet. Lakshmana and Vibhishana stand beside him.
**Narration:** "Then the gods let fall a rain of flowers. The war was over, and Sita would soon be free. Rama did not cheer. He lowered his bow, and stood a long while in silence."
**Sound:** a single sustained note, a soft flute, the wind.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers Ravana's end in nine songs, each time calling Murugan the nephew of the Lord whose arrow brought it about. Songs to show: 19 (Thiruparankundram), Hari who loosed one arrow so that Ravana fell with all his clan; 597 (Thiruchengode), the one who destroyed Ravana; 707 (Kodainagar), Rama who fitted the arrow; and 959 (Madurai), the Lord who cut down the demon who wronged Janaki and came back in victory to Ayodhya.

## Production notes
