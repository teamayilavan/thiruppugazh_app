# The End of Kamsa

`krishna-kamsa` | Vishnu | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: tense, swift, a release | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Mathura Krishna leaps onto the royal dais, seizes Kamsa by the hair and throws him down.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| At Mathura Krishna leaps onto the royal dais, seizes Kamsa by the hair and throws him down | Bhagavata Purana 10.44.34-37 |
| Songs 615, 851 and 936 and others recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |
| The wrestling arena and the fear of Kamsa are the traditional frame | Bhagavata Purana, canto 10 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The end is shown in shadow.

## Characters

- **Krishna and Balarama:** two boys, bright.
- **Kamsa:** a king on a dais, hard.
- **The crowd of Mathura.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; tense in the arena, quiet at the end. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The arena (25 sec)
**Visual:** Mathura at morning. Kettledrums on the walls. A great wrestling arena, packed with townspeople, cowherds and sages. On a high royal dais sits Kamsa, crowned, rigid, his eyes never still. Below him the huge wrestlers Chanura and Mushtika oil their arms. In the crowd, Vasudeva and Devaki, thin and anxious.
**Narration:** "Kamsa, king of Mathura, had been told that the eighth child of his sister Devaki would end his life. All his life he had feared that child. Now he held a great wrestling festival, and he invited two cowherd boys from Gokula, hoping his wrestlers would finish what his demons could not."
**Sound:** kettledrums, the hum of a crowd, a low tense drone.

### 2. The brothers (20 sec)
**Visual:** Through the gate walk Krishna, dark as a rain cloud, and Balarama, fair, each carrying an elephant's tusk on his shoulder. The crowd turns. Women lean forward; old men smile; the wrestlers scowl. On the dais Kamsa grips the arm of his throne.
**Narration:** "Krishna and his brother Balarama walked in, carrying the tusks of the king's own elephant. Each person saw something different. The women saw beauty, the cowherds saw their own boys, and the king saw only his death."
**Sound:** the drone breaks; a bright flute phrase.

### 3. The wrestlers (25 sec)
**Visual:** Silhouettes against the bright sand: Krishna faces the giant Chanura, Balarama faces Mushtika. The women in the crowd protest at the unfair match. A whirl of dust; the great shapes fall one after another. The other wrestlers run. The cowherd boys rush in and dance with the brothers, ankle bells ringing.
**Narration:** "The women cried out that it was wrong to match such tender boys with such giants. But the giants fell, one after another, and the rest of the wrestlers fled. The brothers called their friends, and they danced in the arena."
**Sound:** a drum roll, two heavy thuds, then ankle bells and laughter.

### 4. The king's fear (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kamsa rises. He waves the musicians silent. His face is grey; he points and shouts at his guards. The crowd falls still. Vasudeva and Devaki shrink back.
**Narration:** "Only the king did not rejoice. He stopped the music and began to shout orders."
**Dialogue:** Kamsa: "Drive those two boys out of the city! Seize the cowherds' cattle! Arrest Nanda! Kill Vasudeva, and kill my own father too!"
**Sound:** the music cuts off; a single harsh gong.

### 5. The leap (25 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna's eyes go still. In one light movement he springs from the sand to the high dais. Kamsa snatches up a sword and shield and darts from side to side like a hawk. Krishna's hand closes on him as an eagle takes a snake.
**Narration:** "While the king was still raving, Krishna leapt, as easily as a bird, up onto the royal dais. Kamsa seized a sword and a shield and darted this way and that, like a hawk in the sky. But no one can escape the Lord."
**Sound:** a rising rush of wind, a sword drawn, silence.

### 6. The throw (25 sec)
**Visual:** Seen only as shadows on the canopy: Krishna grasps Kamsa by the hair; the crown falls and rolls across the dais in close-up, spinning to rest. A shadow falls from the dais to the arena floor. A thud. The crowd gasps as one.
**Narration:** "He caught Kamsa by the hair. The crown fell. He threw the king down from the dais onto the wrestling ground, and the tyrant who had feared him all his life was gone."
**Narration (continued):** "The Bhagavata says that Kamsa, eating, sleeping and breathing, had thought of Krishna every moment, in fear. And even that constant thought brought him to the Lord."
**Sound:** one deep drum, then a long held note.

### 7. The peace (20 sec)
**Visual:** Flowers drift from the sky. Krishna and Balarama kneel before Vasudeva and Devaki, untying their bonds, and touch their feet with their heads. The parents stand with joined palms, not daring to embrace them. Mathura's people breathe again; a child laughs.
**Narration:** "Then the two brothers went to their father and mother, who had waited so long in chains. They set them free and bowed at their feet. And Mathura breathed again."
**Sound:** a soft drum of joy, the flute melody returning.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls Kamsa's end in a few short lines, praising Murugan as the nephew of the Lord who overcame the cruel uncle. Songs to show: 615 (Thenserigiri), 851 (Thiruppandhanainallur), 936 (Vijayamangalam) and 1274 (a general song), which names Kamsa as the uncle who plotted against his own nephew.

## Production notes

- **A swift film.** Do not linger on the fall.
- **No violence shown.** A shadow and a thud.
