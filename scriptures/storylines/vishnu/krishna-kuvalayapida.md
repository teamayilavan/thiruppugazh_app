# The Elephant at the Gate (Kuvalayapida)

`krishna-kuvalayapida` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: thrilling, quick, triumphant | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Mathura's gate Krishna meets Kamsa's elephant Kuvalayapida, breaks its tusks and kills it.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| At Mathura's gate Krishna meets Kamsa's elephant Kuvalayapida, breaks its tusks and kills it | Bhagavata Purana 10.43.2-18 (one tusk pulled out; the Tamil verses and song 158 say both tusks) |
| Songs 158 and 1157 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration. The elephant's death is stylised.

## Characters

- **Krishna and Balarama.**
- **Kuvalayapida:** a huge, maddened elephant.
- **The mahout:** a hard-faced rider.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; quick and bright during the chase. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The gate (20 sec)
**Visual:** Morning in Mathura. Kettledrums sound from the wrestling arena. Krishna and Balarama, fresh from bathing, walk up the broad street toward the arena gate. A crowd follows them, curious and smiling.
**Narration:** "Kamsa had invited the two cowherd boys to his festival at Mathura, meaning them never to leave it. One morning, hearing the drums of the wrestling arena, Krishna and Balarama went to see what was happening."
**Sound:** distant kettledrums, footsteps, the murmur of a crowd.

### 2. The elephant (25 sec)
**Visual:** The gate is blocked by a vast elephant, Kuvalayapida, painted and armoured, swaying. On its neck sits a hard-faced keeper with a goad. Krishna tucks his cloth tight and ties back his curls. He looks up at the keeper, calm.
**Narration:** "At the gate stood the king's elephant, Kuvalayapida, huge as a hill, placed there by his keeper to block the way. Krishna bound up his clothes, tied back his hair, and spoke in a voice like the rumble of a cloud."
**Dialogue:** Krishna: "Keeper, move aside and let us pass. Do not stand in our way."
**Sound:** the elephant's heavy breathing; the drums stop.

### 3. The charge (25 sec)
**Visual:** The keeper scowls and drives the goad down. The elephant trumpets and charges; its trunk wraps around Krishna. Krishna slips free, strikes it, and vanishes between its legs. The elephant turns in circles, sniffing for him.
**Narration:** "The keeper only grew angry, and goaded the elephant forward. Its trunk caught Krishna, but he slipped away and disappeared between its great legs. The elephant turned round and round, searching for him by his scent."
**Sound:** a trumpet blast, the crowd's cry, then comic plucked strings as the elephant circles.

### 4. The dodge (20 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna holds the elephant's tail and is swung left and right, like a boy pulling a calf by the tail. He runs in front of it, falls, rolls aside; the tusks strike only earth. The crowd laughs and gasps.
**Narration:** "Then he caught the elephant by the tail and let himself be swung from side to side, the way a boy plays with a calf. He ran ahead of it, fell down on purpose, and rolled away. The great tusks struck only the ground."
**Sound:** quick drums, the crowd's laughter, a dull thump as tusks hit earth.

### 5. The tusk (25 sec)
**Visual:** The keepers goad the furious elephant once more. Krishna meets it head on and catches its trunk. In silhouette against the bright gateway, the great shape tips and goes down. Krishna stands on it for a moment like a lion on a hill, and draws out a single white tusk.
**Narration:** "The keepers drove the frantic animal at him once more. This time Krishna met it face to face, seized its trunk, and threw it to the ground. Then, light as a lion, he climbed onto it and drew out one of its tusks."
**Sound:** a great crash, dust, then a single bright chord.

### 6. The end (20 sec)
**Visual:** Dust settles in the gateway. Only shadows: the fallen elephant, the fallen keepers. No wounds are shown. Then the light moves to Krishna, the tusk resting on his shoulder, beads of sweat on his face, shining.
**Narration:** "With that tusk he ended the elephant and its cruel keepers. The poor beast had only done what it was driven to do."
**Narration (continued):** "The Tamil poets remember this moment too: the angry elephant, and its white tusk broken."
**Sound:** silence, then a low gentle note for the animal.

### 7. The brothers (15 sec)
**Visual:** Krishna and Balarama, each with a tusk on his shoulder, walk through the gate into the arena. The wrestlers stiffen. On the high dais Kamsa sees them, and goes pale.
**Narration:** "Each brother carrying a tusk, they walked into the arena. And when Kamsa saw his elephant was dead, fear took hold of him."
**Sound:** the arena's drums resume, louder; a single dark gong for Kamsa.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers the elephant at the gate in two songs, calling Murugan the nephew of the Lord who broke the tusks of the angry elephant. Songs to show: 158 (Palani) and 1157 (a general song).

## Production notes

- **Excitement without cruelty.** The elephant is a trained animal under a bad master; handle with care.
