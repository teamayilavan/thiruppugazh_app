# Kumbha Falls to Sugriva

`ramayana-kumbha` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: fierce, brisk, decisive | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Kumbha, son of Kumbhakarna, is felled by a blow from Sugriva.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Kumbha, son of Kumbhakarna, is felled by a blow from Sugriva | Valmiki Yuddha Kanda 76.92-93; Kamba, Atikayan Vadhai Padalam 236-246 |
| Song 835 recalls it | Thiruppugazh song 835 |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Kumbha:** a young demon warrior, large.
- **Sugriva:** the monkey king.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The young warrior (25 sec)
**Visual:** The gates of Lanka open at dusk. Out rides Kumbha, a young rakshasa warrior, huge and proud, in a chariot hung with bells, a great bow in his hand. He is the son of Kumbhakarna, and his face is set with grief and fury. Rakshasa banners stream behind him.
**Narration:** "The giant Kumbhakarna had fallen in battle. Now his son Kumbha rode out of the gates of Lanka to take his place. He was young and enormous, a master of the bow, and he burned to avenge his father."
**Sound:** war drums, chariot bells, a harsh horn.

### 2. The monkeys (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kumbha's arrows fly like sparks across the field. Monkey warriors scatter; young Angada stands his ground but is driven back, dazed. The monkey ranks waver.
**Narration:** "His arrows flew thick and fast. The monkeys fell back before him. Even brave Angada, the son of Vali, was struck and driven back. It seemed that no one could stand against him."
**Sound:** the whistle of arrows, cries of the monkeys.

### 3. The king (20 sec)
**Visual:** Through the dust strides Sugriva, king of the monkeys, broad and golden, his eyes fixed on Kumbha. The monkeys part to let him through.
**Narration:** "Then Sugriva, the king of the monkeys, came forward himself. He had seen his warriors fall back, and he would not let it go on."
**Dialogue:** Sugriva: "Turn your arrows on me, son of Kumbhakarna."
**Sound:** the drums pause; one deep beat.

### 4. The exchange (25 sec)
**Visual:** Kumbha shoots; Sugriva leaps through the arrows, seizes the great bow and snaps it in two. Kumbha leaps down from his chariot. The two grapple like two mountains colliding, circling again and again, raising clouds of dust. Monkeys and rakshasas stop fighting to watch.
**Narration:** "Kumbha shot at him, but Sugriva sprang through the arrows, seized the great bow and broke it. Then the two of them closed with bare hands. Kamban says they wheeled around each other a thousand times, like mountains clashing, and both armies stopped to watch."
**Sound:** thuds, the snap of the bow, a rhythmic, rising drum.

### 5. The blow (25 sec)
**Visual:** Stylised and brief. Sugriva draws back his fist; it glows like iron in a smith's forge. One blow lands on Kumbha's armoured chest with a flash of light. Kumbha's great silhouette sways and falls like a mountain, raising dust.
**Narration:** "At last Sugriva gathered all his strength into one blow. Kamban says his hand came down like a smith's hammer on iron in the forge. Valmiki says that single blow, struck on Kumbha's chest, brought him down, and he did not rise again."
**Sound:** one great drum stroke and a flash; then silence.

### 6. The shout (20 sec)
**Visual:** The monkey army leaps up and roars, waving branches. Rakshasa banners fall back toward the gates. Sugriva stands, breathing hard, unsmiling.
**Narration:** "A great shout went up from the monkeys. Their king had won. The rakshasas fell back to Lanka, and the field was theirs. But Sugriva did not rejoice. He knew Kumbha had fought for his father."
**Sound:** the roar of the monkeys, fading.

### 7. The calm (15 sec)
**Visual:** Night falls on the field. Far off, Rama watches, bow lowered. Sugriva walks back toward him through the dust.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri sings of the Lord whose war shook Kumbha and all the might of Lanka, the cloud-dark Rama who won with his arrows."
**Sound:** quiet drums; one conch far away.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 835 (Enkan) recalls the Lord, dark as a rain cloud, who won with his arrows so that Kumban and all the might of Lanka were shaken, and names Murugan as his nephew.

## Production notes

- **A brisk action film.** No bodies.
