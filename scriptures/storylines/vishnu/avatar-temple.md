# The Reclining Lord of Srirangam (Ranganatha)

`avatar-temple` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: devotional, still, a pilgrimage | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Ranganatha reclines on Adisesha at Srirangam, the great temple of Vishnu that the poet also links with Kanchi and Tirupati.

**Honesty note:** there is no scripture story here, only the temples, the image and the Alvars' songs about them. The film is a pilgrimage in pictures, not a plot. The Thiruppugazh lines in this set name Srirangam and Kanchi; Tirupati comes from the Alvars.
**Added for the screen:** every visual and the wording of the narration.

## Characters

- **Ranganatha:** a vast reclining Lord, blue, with Adisesha under him.
- **Pilgrims.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The river (25 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn mist over the Kaveri. From above, the river divides in two and wraps around a long island; coconut palms, bathers on the steps, a coracle drifting. Through the palms rises the first temple tower.
**Narration:** "In the Tamil country the river Kaveri divides in two and wraps its arms around an island. On that island stands Srirangam, and there, the devotees say, the Lord of the milk ocean has come to rest on earth."
**Sound:** birdsong, water lapping, a distant nagaswaram tuning.

### 2. The towers (20 sec)
**Visual:** Pilgrims walk through gateway after gateway, under towering gopurams covered with carved figures. Each gateway frames the next. Flower sellers, a family with a child, an old man with a staff.
**Narration:** "Pilgrims walk through gateway after gateway, under towers that seem to touch the clouds. They come from every village, carrying flowers, carrying their troubles, and carrying their songs. Each gateway takes them one step closer."
**Sound:** footsteps, temple drums growing nearer.

### 3. The sanctum (25 sec)
**Visual:** Inside, lamplight in the dark. A long, dark figure reclines: Ranganatha. The camera moves slowly from his feet to his calm face, lit by flickering lamps.
**Narration:** "In the inner shrine, in the light of the lamps, the Lord lies at rest. The people call him Ranganatha, or simply the Great Lord. One Alvar, Kulasekhara, sang that his one longing was to stand before him and see him lying there."
**Sound:** the drums stop; only the hiss of lamps and a soft bell.

### 4. The serpent (20 sec)
**Visual:** Close on the serpent bed beneath him and the hoods spread over his head. The stone dissolves into the white Ocean of Milk with the same Lord on the same serpent, then dissolves back into stone.
**Narration:** "He lies on the serpent Adisesha, whose hoods rise over his head, just as he lies in the Ocean of Milk. The image in stone and the image on the sea are one and the same picture."
**Sound:** a long drone, the sound of the sea blending into the temple's quiet.

### 5. The waking (20 sec)
**Visual:** Before dawn, a poet stands at the closed shrine doors and sings. The doors open; pilgrims surge gently forward; an old woman folds her hands; a child on her father's shoulders stares.
**Narration:** "Before dawn, the poet Thondaradippodi Alvar would stand at the shrine and sing to wake the Lord, asking only to be a servant of those who serve him. Pilgrims still come at that hour, and wait for the doors to open."
**Sound:** a single voice singing, then the creak of the doors and a conch.

### 6. Kanchi and Tirupati (20 sec)
**Visual:** A montage: the great tank and tower at Kanchi; then the Tirumala hills, pilgrims climbing stone steps, a sacred pool with fish moving in the water.
**Narration:** "The same Lord stands in other homes. In Kanchi he is the Lord of Ooragam. On the hill of Venkatam, at Tirupati, he stands so that all who come may take refuge at his feet. Kulasekhara sang that he would rather be a fish in the pool there than a king in heaven."
**Sound:** bells of different temples, one after another.

### 7. The lamp (20 sec)
**Visual:** Night at Srirangam. A single lamp burns before the reclining Lord. A palm-leaf page with a line of Arunagiri's song lies by the lamp. The camera pulls back through the gateways to the dark river.
**Narration:** "Arunagiri sang of these temples too. In his songs Murugan is the nephew of the Lord of Srirangam, who lies upon the serpent. The lamp burns on, and the Lord rests."
**Sound:** the nagaswaram from the opening, now soft and slow; the river.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names Vishnu's temples in his one-line praises of Murugan's uncle. Songs to show: 652 (Kasi), Narayana asleep on the serpent bed at Srirangam; 134 (Palani), the great Lord of Srirangam on the serpent's hood; 971 (Ilanji), the Lord of Thiruvarangam; and 727 (Siruvai), the Lord of Thiru Ooragam at Kanchi.
