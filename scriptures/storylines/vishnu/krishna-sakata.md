# The Cart That Broke (Sakata)

`krishna-sakata` | Vishnu | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: tender, then comic, then bright | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Baby Krishna kicks a heavy cart haunted by a demon, and it shatters.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Baby Krishna kicks a heavy handcart and it collapses | Bhagavata Purana 10.7.4-12 (no demon is named there) |
| A demon hid in the cart (Sakata) | Tamil tradition: Periyazhwar Thirumozhi 3.9.9, Tiruppavai 24; the songs |
| Songs 116, 186, 193 and 4 others recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals and the narration.

## Characters

- **Baby Krishna.**
- **Yashoda:** busy, loving.
- **The cart (Sakata):** a heavy cart with a demon inside.
- **The villagers.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; busy and festive, then comic, then tender. Where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The morning (20 sec)
**Visual:** Nanda's courtyard in Gokula, decorated with mango leaves and kolam. It is a festival for the baby Krishna, three months old, who has just begun to turn over by himself. Yashoda bathes him; priests chant; guests arrive with gifts.
**Narration:** "In Gokula, Yashoda held a festival for her baby. He was three months old, and he had just begun to turn over by himself. The priests chanted blessings, and all the village came to the house."
**Sound:** festive drums, chanting, chatter, a nadaswaram in the distance.

### 2. The hungry baby (20 sec)
**Visual:** The baby grows sleepy. Yashoda lays him down in the shade under a big handcart in a corner of the courtyard, then hurries back to greet guests and hand out cows and cloth. Soon the baby wakes and cries for milk. Nobody hears him.
**Narration:** "When he grew sleepy, Yashoda laid him down in the shade beneath a heavy handcart in a corner of the yard. Then she hurried off to look after her guests. Soon the baby woke up, hungry, and cried for his mother. But in all the noise, no one heard."
**Sound:** the festive noise, and under it a small thin cry.

### 3. The cart (25 sec)
**Visual:** The cart looms over him, piled high with brass pots and vessels of milk and curd. The camera tilts from the tiny baby to the huge wheels. A faint dark shimmer passes over the cart's wood, like a shadow with no owner.
**Narration:** "Above him stood the cart, piled high with pots and vessels of brass. The Tamil poets, and the songs, say a demon had hidden in that cart. The Bhagavata says only that it was a cart. But either way, it was about to meet the baby's foot."
**Sound:** a low creak of wood; a faint whisper of wind.

### 4. The kick (25 sec)
**Visual:** The baby, cross and hungry, kicks his little feet in the air, soft as new leaves. One foot touches the wheel. A pause. Then the whole cart flips over with a tremendous crash: wheels fly off the axle, spokes scatter, the pole snaps, pots ring and roll everywhere. The dark shimmer bursts like smoke and is gone.
**Narration:** "Cross and hungry, he kicked his little feet, soft as new leaves. One tiny foot struck the wheel, and the great cart turned right over. The wheels flew off, the pole broke, and the pots went rolling across the yard."
**Sound:** a tiny thump, a beat of silence, then a gigantic comic crash and the ringing of rolling pots.

### 5. The scatter (20 sec)
**Visual:** Everyone comes running. The grown-ups stare at the wreck and look about for the cause. A group of small children point at the baby, jumping up and down. The adults shake their heads and laugh at them.
**Narration:** "Everyone came running. How could a cart fall over by itself? Was it a demon, or a bad star? The little children who had been playing nearby said that the baby had kicked it. But the grown-ups only laughed at such childish talk."
**Dialogue:** A child: "We saw it! He kicked it with his foot!"
**Sound:** murmuring, a child's insistent voice, adult laughter.

### 6. The baby (20 sec)
**Visual:** Close on the baby, lying unhurt among the scattered pots, waving his feet, looking up with a calm, knowing smile. A single brass pot rocks to a stop beside him.
**Narration:** "And in the middle of it all lay the baby, not a scratch on him, waving his feet in the air, as if he had done nothing at all."
**Sound:** a gentle flute phrase, the last pot rocking to a stop.

### 7. The embrace (20 sec)
**Visual:** Yashoda pushes through the crowd, sweeps him up and holds him close; she nurses him at last. Priests light a sacred fire for his protection; the men set the cart upright again. Evening falls over the courtyard; the baby sleeps in her arms.
**Narration:** "Yashoda gathered him up, afraid that some evil had come near her child, and she fed him at last. The priests lit a fire and chanted for his protection. And the baby slept, safe in his mother's arms."
**Sound:** chanting, the crackle of the sacred fire, the lullaby.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri remembers the baby who kicked the cart in several songs, always beside the other wonders of Krishna's childhood. Songs to show: 116 and 186 (Palani), which set the cart beside the twin trees and the lifted hill, and 1157 (a general song), which speaks of the strong cart kicked by his foot.

## Production notes

- **Comedy of the huge and the tiny.**
- **No fear.** The demon is a shadow that dissolves.
