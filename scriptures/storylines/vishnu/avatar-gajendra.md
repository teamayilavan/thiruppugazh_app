# The Elephant Who Called Out (Gajendra Moksha)

`avatar-gajendra` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: wonder, fear, stillness, joy | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A mighty elephant king, held in a lake by a crocodile for a thousand years, finally gives up on his own strength and calls out to the Lord of all, and the Lord comes at once.

**Tradition, not text:** the exact words of the elephant's cry in the Tamil tradition; Gajendra's spoken prayer here is a plain paraphrase of the Bhagavata prayer (8.3.2-29), not a quotation. The old book note that Huhu pulled the sage's leg is not in 8.4.3, so it is left out.
**Added for the screen:** the seasons time-lapse, Hari stepping across the lake, the look of each character, and the wording of the narration.

## Characters

- **Gajendra:** the largest elephant on the mountain, warm grey, gentle eyes, one tusk slightly chipped. Proud at first; thin and slow after the thousand years; radiant at the end.
- **His herd:** mates and calves, a few bulls. They stay on the bank.
- **The crocodile:** a dark shape, then eyes and a long back; never shown whole until the end. At the end he becomes **Huhu**, a gandharva in bright silks.
- **Hari (Vishnu):** dark cloud-blue, yellow silk, four hands with discus, conch, mace and lotus. Unhurried in the face, fast in motion.
- **Garuda:** a great eagle, gold and crimson.
- **Gods and sages:** far off on clouds, watching.
- **Indradyumna and Agastya** (flashback): a thin king with matted hair in a hermitage; a short radiant sage with a water pot and disciples.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The mountain and the king (20 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn over the Ocean of Milk. The camera climbs Trikuta: milk-white surf at its foot, three peaks of iron, silver and gold lit by the sun, waterfalls, heavenly singers in the caves. Out of the forest strides Gajendra; the ground shakes. Lions and tigers slip away; a fox, deer and hares look up at him without fear.
**Narration:** "In the Ocean of Milk stands Trikuta, a mountain of iron, silver and gold. On its slopes lived Gajendra, the king of the elephants. The great beasts fled from his scent, but the small ones, the deer and the hares, never feared him."
**Sound:** soft temple-bell tones, a low rhythmic step as he walks.

### 2. The lake (20 sec)
**Visual:** They crest a ridge: a lake of golden lotuses in a garden where it is always spring. The herd runs in. Gajendra drinks deep and sprays his mates and calves. In the background, one lily pad drifts against the current.
**Narration:** "He led his herd down to a lake of golden lotuses, in a garden where the flowers never fade. He drank, he bathed, he sprayed his family with water, and for that one bright morning he wanted nothing more."
**Sound:** water, birdsong; the music brightens, then a single low note under the water.

### 3. The seizure (15 sec)
**Visual:** Gajendra stands deep. A shape rises under him; jaws close on his leg. He bellows; birds burst from the trees. His mates cry out; the bulls wade in and pull him from behind. The crocodile does not yield.
**Narration:** "But in that lake lived a crocodile, and its jaws closed on the elephant's leg. His family cried out and pulled him from behind. It was not enough."
**Sound:** silence, then the bellow; the herd's cry.

### 4. A thousand years (20 sec)
**Visual:** A slow time-lapse over the lake: day, night, the moon, monsoon, autumn. The silhouettes of elephant and crocodile strain toward the shore, then the deep. The herd grows smaller and drifts away. The gods watch from the clouds. Gajendra grows thinner and slower; the crocodile does not tire.
**Narration:** "So began a struggle of a thousand years. The elephant pulled toward the land; the crocodile pulled toward the deep. In the water the crocodile grew stronger, and the elephant grew weaker. Even the gods watched, astonished."
**Sound:** the same low note, rising slowly; wind; the sound of seasons.

### 5. The prayer (25 sec)
**Visual:** Close on Gajendra's face, alone in grey dawn. He stops pulling. He looks at the empty bank, then at the distant gods. He remembers a prayer from an earlier life, and the picture warms. He lifts one lotus in his trunk. The cry spreads across the sky as a ripple of light.
**Narration:** "At last he understood. His wives could not save him, nor his herd, and now his own strength was gone. Then he remembered a prayer from an earlier life. He did not call on Brahma, nor on Shiva, nor on Indra. He called to the One behind them all."
**Dialogue:** Gajendra: "O First Cause, from whom everything comes, I have no strength left. I take shelter in you."
**Sound:** everything falls quiet; his voice alone, then a single clear tone.

### 6. He comes (30 sec)
**Visual:** A point of gold in the far sky. Garuda crosses the clouds, wings wide; on his back sits Hari with the discus. Gajendra lifts the lotus higher. Hari steps down from Garuda onto the lake, lifts the great elephant and the crocodile out of the water onto the bank, and with one turn of the discus frees the leg. Drums, flowers from the sky.
**Narration:** "No god came, for he had named none. But the Lord who lives in every heart had heard him. He came across the sky on Garuda, with his discus in his hand. Gajendra, in his pain, raised a lotus in his trunk."
**Dialogue:** Gajendra: "Narayana, Lord of the universe, I bow to you."
**Narration (continued):** "The Lord did not wait. He came down, and drew the elephant and the crocodile out of the water together, and with his discus he set him free."
**Sound:** a rising wind, a conch, then drums and a surge of music.

### 7. Who they had been (25 sec)
**Visual:** Warm hand-drawn flashback. A Pandya king, Indradyumna, in silent prayer in the Malaya hills, does not see the sage Agastya arrive; the sage speaks the curse; the king bows his head and accepts it. Then a singer, Huhu, cursed by the sage Devala, sinks into a river and rises a crocodile. Back on the bank: Hari's hand rests on Gajendra's forehead; light flows through him; he stands radiant in the Lord's own form. The crocodile's body becomes mist, and Huhu stands where it lay, folding his hands.
**Narration:** "Neither was what he seemed. The elephant had once been a king, Indradyumna, cursed by a sage whom he had not greeted, for he was lost in prayer. The crocodile had been a singer, Huhu, cursed by another sage. The Lord's touch set both of them free."
**Dialogue:** Agastya: "May he take the dull body of an elephant."
**Sound:** a gentle, old tune; the curse is spoken softly; the release is a warm chord.

### 8. Home (10 sec)
**Visual:** Hari mounts Garuda with Gajendra, now radiant, beside him. They rise over Trikuta and the milk sea. Final image: the empty lake, one golden lotus on the still water.
**Narration:** "He had called out, and he was heard. And the Lord took him home."
**Sound:** the first melody returns, quiet; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri refers to this story in more than twenty songs, usually in a single line, calling Murugan the nephew of the Lord who answered the elephant's cry and cut the crocodile with his discus. Songs 25, 70 and 82 (Thiruchendur) and 173 and 175 (Palani) are the ones to show; each tells how Murugan's uncle, Vishnu, saved the elephant.

## Production notes

- In the elephant's prayer, **never name a god.** In the text no deity answers because none was named; the prayer is to the Supreme.
- **No gore.** The crocodile's end is a flash and a dissolve.
- **Gajendra is gentle,** not cruel: his pride is strength, and he protects the small animals.
- **The two curses are told lightly,** as the doorway to freedom, not as punishment.
