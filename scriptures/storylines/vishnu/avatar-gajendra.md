# The Elephant Who Called Out (Gajendra Moksha)

`avatar-gajendra` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: wonder, fear, stillness, joy

## One-line story

A mighty elephant king, held in a lake by a crocodile for a thousand years, finally gives up on his own strength and calls out to the Lord of all, and the Lord comes at once.

**Tradition, not text:** the exact Tamil wording of the elephant's cry, from the Alvar tradition. The old book note that Huhu pulled the sage's leg is not in 8.4.3 (it only says Devala cursed him), so it is left out.
**Added for the screen:** the seasons time-lapse, Hari stepping across the lake, the details of how each character looks, and the wording of the narration.

## Characters

- **Gajendra:** the largest elephant on the mountain, warm grey, gentle eyes, one tusk slightly chipped. Proud and strutting at first; thin and slow after the thousand years; radiant at the end.
- **His herd:** mates and calves, a few bulls. They stay on the bank.
- **The crocodile:** a dark shape, then eyes and a long back; never shown whole until the end. At the end he becomes **Huhu**, a gandharva in bright silks.
- **Hari (Vishnu):** dark cloud-blue, yellow silk, four hands with discus, conch, mace and lotus. Unhurried in the face, fast in motion.
- **Garuda:** a great eagle, gold and crimson.
- **Gods and sages:** seen far off on clouds, watching.
- **Indradyumna and Agastya** (flashback): a thin king with matted hair in a hermitage; a short radiant sage with a water pot and disciples.

## The storyline

### 1. The mountain and the king (20 sec)
Dawn over the Ocean of Milk. The camera climbs Trikuta: milk-white surf at its foot, three peaks of iron, silver and gold lit by the sun, waterfalls, heavenly singers in the caves. Out of the forest strides Gajendra; the ground shakes, lions and tigers slip away, but the small animals (fox, deer, hares) look up without fear. His herd follows.
**Narration:** "In the middle of the Ocean of Milk stands Trikuta, a mountain of three golden peaks. And on its slopes lived the king of the elephants."

### 2. The lake (20 sec)
They crest a ridge: a lake of golden lotuses in a garden where it is always spring. The herd runs in. Gajendra drinks and sprays his family; calves splash. In the background, one lily pad drifts against the current.
**Narration:** "He drank, he bathed, he played with his family, and for that one bright hour he wanted nothing."

### 3. The seizure (15 sec)
Gajendra stands deep. A shape rises under him; jaws close on his leg. He bellows; birds burst from the trees. His mates cry out; the bulls wade in and pull him from behind, and the crocodile does not yield.
**Narration:** "But in the water lived a crocodile. His family pulled, and cried, and could not free him."

### 4. A thousand years (20 sec)
A slow time-lapse over the lake: day, night, moon, monsoon, autumn. The silhouettes of elephant and crocodile strain toward the shore, then the deep. The herd grows smaller and drifts away. The gods watch astonished from the clouds. Gajendra grows thinner and slower; the crocodile does not tire.
**Narration:** "A thousand years passed. In the water the crocodile grew stronger. The elephant grew weaker. And the gods looked on, and did not come."

### 5. The prayer (25 sec)
Close on Gajendra's face. He stops pulling. He looks at the empty bank, then the distant gods. He remembers a prayer from an earlier life, and the picture warms. He lifts one lotus in his trunk and speaks: not to Brahma, nor Shiva, nor Indra, but to the One behind them all, "the shelter of those who have nothing left." The cry spreads across the sky as a ripple of light.
**Gajendra:** "O First Cause, from whom everything comes, I have no strength left. I take shelter in you."
**Narration:** "Then he understood: no friend, no family, and not even his own strength could save him. And he called out, to no god by name, but to the One who is behind them all."

### 6. He comes (30 sec)
A point of gold in the far sky. Garuda crosses the clouds, wings wide; on his back sits Hari with the discus. Gajendra lifts the lotus higher.
**Gajendra:** "Narayana, Lord of the universe, I bow to you."
Hari steps down from Garuda onto the lake, lifts the great elephant and the crocodile out of the water onto the bank, and with one turn of the discus frees the leg. Drums, flowers from the sky.
**Narration:** "He did not send anyone else. He came himself, and he lifted the elephant out of the water."

### 7. Who they had been (25 sec)
Warm hand-drawn flashback. A Pandya king, Indradyumna, in silent prayer in the Malaya hills, does not see the sage Agastya arrive; the sage, offended, speaks the curse, and the king bows his head and accepts it. Then a singer, Huhu, cursed by the sage Devala, sinks into a river and rises a crocodile. Back on the bank: Hari's hand rests on Gajendra's forehead; light flows through him; he stands radiant in the Lord's own form. The crocodile's body becomes mist, and Huhu stands where it lay, folding his hands.
**Narration:** "Neither was what he seemed. The elephant had been a king, cursed in his silence. The crocodile had been a singer, cursed in his play. The Lord's touch set both of them free."

### 8. Home (10 sec)
Hari mounts Garuda with Gajendra, now radiant, beside him. They rise over Trikuta and the milk sea. Final image: the empty lake, one golden lotus on the still water.
**Narration:** "He had called out, and he was heard."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri refers to this story in more than twenty songs, usually in a single line, calling Murugan the nephew of the Lord who answered the elephant's cry and cut the crocodile with his discus. Songs 25, 70 and 82 (Thiruchendur) and 173 and 175 (Palani) are the ones to show; each tells how Murugan's uncle, Vishnu, saved the elephant.

## Production notes

- In the elephant's prayer, **never name a god.** In the text no deity answers because none was named; the prayer is to the Supreme.
- **No gore.** The crocodile's end is a flash and a dissolve.
- **Gajendra is gentle,** not cruel: his pride is strength, and he protects the small animals.
- **The two curses are told lightly,** as the doorway to freedom, not as punishment.
