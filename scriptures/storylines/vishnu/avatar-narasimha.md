# The Lion from the Pillar (Narasimha and Prahlada)

`avatar-narasimha` | Vishnu | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: dread, then the roar, then peace | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The demon king Hiranyakashipu has a boon that he cannot be killed by day or night, indoors or outdoors, by man or beast; Vishnu appears from a pillar as a man-lion at dusk and tears him open, saving his devoted son Prahlada.

**Tradition, not read here:** the boon (no man or beast, day or night, inside or outside) and the king's attempts on Prahlada's life are told earlier in Bhagavata book 7; the narration marks them as tradition. Dusk comes from Kamban.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the spoken lines (plain paraphrases of 7.8.6-7 and 7.8.12) and the wording of the narration. The killing is shown only as shadow and a flash.

## Characters

- **Hiranyakashipu:** a proud king with a golden mace.
- **Prahlada:** a small, serene boy.
- **Narasimha:** man-lion, golden, mane blazing.
- **The gods:** watching from the sky.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; where a scene has no voice, the music carries it.

### 1. The king's pride (20 sec)
**Visual:** A vast golden palace. Hiranyakashipu, huge and proud, sits on his throne; ministers bow low; far above, the gods hide behind clouds. At his frown the lamps shiver.
**Narration:** "Hiranyakashipu, king of the demons, ruled the three worlds. Tradition tells that a boon protected him: no man or beast could kill him, by day or by night, inside or outside. When he was angry, he said, all the worlds trembled."
**Sound:** heavy brass and drums; the throne room's echo.

### 2. The boy (20 sec)
**Visual:** A school in the palace grounds. Among the demon boys sits Prahlada, small and calm, speaking quietly; the other boys lean in to listen. The two teachers watch, alarmed, and hurry off toward the throne room.
**Narration:** "But his own son, the boy Prahlada, loved only the Lord. He spoke of him to the other children, and they listened. His teachers grew afraid, and ran to tell the king."
**Sound:** a gentle flute for Prahlada, cut by hurried footsteps.

### 3. The anger (20 sec)
**Visual:** The king rises, trembling with rage, and the boy is brought before him. Brief, shadowy glimpses of the trials tradition tells of: a shadow of a raised weapon, a dark shape of an elephant, flames, each fading away harmlessly from the boy.
**Narration:** "The king was furious. Tradition tells how he tried again and again to destroy his son, and each time the boy came to no harm. Now he called Prahlada before him once more."
**Sound:** the brass returns, harsh; each danger dissolves with a soft chime.

### 4. The question (20 sec)
**Visual:** The throne room. The king, sword in hand, towers over the small boy, who stands unafraid. The king strikes his fist against a great carved pillar.
**Dialogue:** Hiranyakashipu: "By whose strength do you defy me? You say your Lord is everywhere. Then is he in this pillar?"
**Dialogue:** Prahlada: "Father, he is the strength of all, of you and of me. He is everywhere."
**Sound:** silence around the voices; the thud of a fist on stone.

### 5. The pillar splits (25 sec)
**Visual:** A sound like the sky cracking. Dust falls from the pillar; the courtiers cower; the king spins around, looking for the source. A seam of golden light runs up the stone, and the pillar splits open.
**Narration:** "From inside the pillar came a sound so terrible that it seemed to crack the shell of the universe. The demons looked everywhere and could not tell where it came from. Then the pillar burst open, to prove the boy's words true: the Lord was there too."
**Sound:** a vast, rolling roar; stone splitting.

### 6. The man-lion (25 sec)
**Visual:** From the broken pillar steps a form never seen before: the body of a man, the head of a lion, golden, with blazing eyes and a mane that sweeps the clouds aside. Kamban's touch: the lion laughs. Prahlada, seeing him, claps his hands and dances.
**Narration:** "Out stepped a form that was neither man nor lion, but both. His eyes were like molten gold, and his mane filled the hall. The Tamil poet Kamban says the lion laughed, and the boy, seeing him, danced, wept and sang for joy."
**Sound:** a deep, joyous roar folded into music; Prahlada's flute bursts out.

### 7. The dusk (25 sec)
**Visual:** The king attacks with his mace, then sword and shield. Light and shadow only: the man-lion catches him as an eagle catches a snake, carries him to the threshold of the hall, as the sky outside turns the red of sunset. A single flash fills the doorway. Then stillness.
**Narration:** "The king rushed at him with his mace, then with sword and shield. The Lord caught him as an eagle catches a snake. At the doorway of the hall, neither inside nor out, as day turned into night, he laid the king across his lap, and ended his cruelty."
**Sound:** clashes, then one great chord, then silence.

### 8. The calm (10 sec)
**Visual:** The fierce Lord sits on the throne; no one dares approach. Little Prahlada walks forward and bows at his feet. The Lord lifts him and lays his hand gently on the boy's head.
**Narration:** "No one dared go near him. But the boy went, and bowed, and the Lord put his hand upon his head."
**Sound:** the flute melody, soft and warm.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the man-lion in ten songs. Songs to show: 545 (Perainagar), the angry lion Narasingan who rose from inside the golden pillar; 150 (Palani), the lion who took Hiranya's life; and 526 (Thiruvengadam), the man-lion. Murugan is praised as his nephew.

## Production notes

- **Do not frighten small children.** The killing is a shadow.
- **Prahlada's calm is the film's centre.**
- **The Sarabha film `deeds-sarabha` follows;** keep the lion's design consistent.
