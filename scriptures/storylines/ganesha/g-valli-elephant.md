# The Elephant in the Millet Field (Vinayaka Helps His Brother)

`g-valli-elephant` | Ganesha | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: comic, quick, affectionate | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

When Valli will not listen to Murugan in disguise as an old ascetic, he thinks of his elder brother Vinayaka, who comes roaring as a wild elephant; Valli runs back to the old man, and Murugan thanks his brother.

**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Vinayaka:** shown as a great wild elephant, comic and gentle at heart.
- **Murugan** as the old ascetic.
- **Valli:** quick and brave.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; light and quick, comic in the chase, warm at the end.

### 1. The old man's trouble (20 sec)
**Visual:** The millet field on Valli's hill. An old ascetic with white ash on his brow stands by the platform. Valli, arms folded, has turned her back on him in anger.
**Narration:** "Murugan had come to Valli's hill in disguise, as an old holy man. She had given him food and water kindly. But when he spoke of love, she grew angry, and told him to be gone."
**Sound:** wind in the millet; a sulky little drum beat.

### 2. A thought (20 sec)
**Visual:** The old man closes his eyes and thinks of his elder brother. A soft glow passes over the hills.
**Narration:** "So the old man closed his eyes, and thought of his elder brother, the elephant-faced Vinayaka. In the Kanda Puranam, that is all he needed to do."
**Sound:** a single, mischievous note on a flute.

### 3. The roar (25 sec)
**Visual:** From the hills comes a deep trumpet. A huge wild elephant bursts out of the trees and charges through the millet, ears flapping, stalks flying. It is fearsome to Valli but plainly playful to us.
**Narration:** "At once a great wild elephant came roaring out of the forest. It crashed through the millet, trumpeting, and came straight toward Valli. One song says it had a single tusk, as Vinayaka has."
**Sound:** trumpeting; crashing stalks; a galloping, comic rhythm.

### 4. The flight (25 sec)
**Visual:** Valli shrieks, forgets all her anger, runs, and clings tightly to the old man's arm, hiding behind him.
**Narration:** "Valli forgot her anger in a moment. She did not stop to think. She ran back to the old man and held on to him tightly, and begged him to save her."
**Dialogue:** Valli: "Save me! Save me from the elephant!"
**Dialogue:** Old man: "Do not be afraid. Do not cry. Come to me."
**Sound:** the rhythm peaks, then stops.

### 5. The thanks (25 sec)
**Visual:** The elephant halts. The old man gives it a small, grateful bow. The elephant lowers its head, turns, and ambles away into the trees, with a last glance back.
**Narration:** "Then Murugan thanked his brother, and the elephant turned and went quietly back into the forest. Its work was done. Valli was still holding on to the old man, and she was no longer telling him to go away."
**Sound:** soft, fond music.

### 6. The true form (25 sec)
**Visual:** Light gathers. The old man is gone: Murugan stands in his true form, with six faces and twelve arms, the spear in hand and the peacock beside him. Valli looks up in wonder.
**Narration:** "And then the old man was gone. Murugan stood before her as he truly is, with six faces and twelve arms, the spear and the peacock. He told her that she had longed for him in an earlier birth."
**Sound:** a radiant chord.

### 7. A brother's gift (10 sec)
**Visual:** Far off at the forest's edge, the elephant looks back, content.
**Narration:** "So the elder brother helped the younger to his bride. The songs call it an act of love."
**Sound:** the fond theme, ending warmly.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Four songs remember the elephant in the millet field: 24 (Thiruchendur), 146 (Palani), 446 (Thirukkalathi) and 715 (Uthiramerur). The full story of Murugan and Valli is told in Murugan Woos Valli.

## Production notes

- **Comic, not scary.** The elephant should be recognisably Ganesha at heart.
- **Pair with `m-valli-disguise`,** which has the full story.
