# The Race Around the World (and the Fruit)

`g-race` | Ganesha | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: playful, then a quiet ache | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Murugan races round the world on his peacock to win a prize fruit, while Ganesha goes round his parents; the fruit goes to Ganesha, and, popular tradition says, Murugan leaves for Palani.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Song 30 says Murugan flew round the world on his peacock in a contest with Ganesha | Thiruppugazh song 30 |
| Song 532 says the prized fruit was nonetheless given to Ganesha | Thiruppugazh song 532 |
| Popular Palani tradition adds that Ganesha went round his parents and that Murugan left for Palani in anger | Tradition |

**Honesty note:** a keyword search of the Kanda Puranam (Utpatti, Daksha, Deva) found no such contest: it is later tradition and the songs. The film says so in its narration.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the dialogue, the narration.

## Characters

- **Murugan:** a young god on a peacock, fast and proud.
- **Ganesha:** a gentle, round elephant-faced god, quick-witted.
- **Shiva and Parvati:** the parents, warm.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; playful in the race, gentle and a little wistful at the end.

### 1. The fruit (20 sec)
**Visual:** Kailasa, white and bright. Shiva and Parvati sit together; in Shiva's hand rests a single glowing fruit. Before them stand their two sons: young Murugan with his peacock, and round, gentle Ganesha with his little mouse.
**Narration:** "This is a story the Tamil people love, and the poet Arunagiri sings of it in two songs. At Kailasa, Shiva held up a wonderful fruit. Whichever son went round the whole world first, he said, would have it."
**Sound:** soft temple bells; a bright, playful melody.

### 2. The start (20 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan leaps onto his peacock, which spreads its red-gold tail and shoots into the sky. Ganesha looks down at his mouse; the mouse looks back up at him.
**Narration:** "Murugan did not wait a moment. He leapt onto his peacock and was gone into the sky. Ganesha looked at his little mouse, and the mouse looked at him."
**Sound:** a rush of wings; a small, comic squeak.

### 3. The flight (25 sec)
**Visual:** The peacock soars over snowy mountains, wide seas, forests, rivers and cities; the whole round world turns below. Murugan's face is eager and joyful.
**Narration:** "The songs say Murugan flew round the whole world on his peacock of red gold, in a contest with his elder brother. Over mountains and oceans he flew, over forests and kingdoms, faster than the wind."
**Sound:** a soaring, quick theme; wind and waves passing below.

### 4. The thought (20 sec)
**Visual:** Ganesha stands quietly before his parents. He closes his eyes, thinks, and slowly smiles.
**Narration:** "Ganesha did not run. He stood still before his mother and father, and he thought. Then he smiled. Tradition tells what he did next."
**Sound:** the music slows to a single soft flute.

### 5. The circling (25 sec)
**Visual:** Hands joined, Ganesha walks slowly round Shiva and Parvati, once, completely, and bows at their feet. Parvati smiles; Shiva's eyes are warm.
**Narration:** "He walked slowly round his parents, with his hands joined, and bowed at their feet."
**Dialogue:** Ganesha: "You are my whole world. To go round you is to go round the world."
**Narration (continued):** "And the fruit was placed in his hand."
**Sound:** a gentle bell at the bow.

### 6. The return (20 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan lands, breathless and shining from the journey. He sees the fruit already in Ganesha's hand. His face falls.
**Narration:** "When Murugan came back from his great journey, the fruit was already Ganesha's. The songs say it plainly: he flew round the world, yet the fruit was given to his brother."
**Sound:** the playful theme stops on one unfinished note.

### 7. The hill (20 sec)
**Visual:** Murugan turns away, sets aside his fine ornaments, and walks down from Kailasa. Far in the south he climbs a quiet hill and sits alone at the top as dawn breaks.
**Narration:** "Tradition says that Murugan went away, far to the south, to the hill of Palani, and stayed there alone. No book of the Puranas tells this race. It lives in the songs, and in the memory of the people."
**Sound:** a quiet, aching melody on strings.

### 8. Wisdom (15 sec)
**Visual:** On the hill, the young god in simple saffron, calm and radiant. Pilgrims climb the steps toward him.
**Narration:** "But the poet does not leave him sad. In the same song he praises Murugan, who taught the meaning of the sacred sound Om. Today, people climb that hill to see him."
**Sound:** temple bells; the melody resolves warmly.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Two songs name the race round the world: 30 (Thiruchendur), where Murugan flies round the world on his peacock in a contest with Ganapati, and 532 (Vallimalai), where the fruit goes to Ganapati and Murugan is praised for teaching the meaning of Om. The rest of the tale is the tradition of Palani.

## Production notes

- **Do not make either brother the villain.** The film's heart is wisdom and love.
- **The tradition note stays in the narration.** It is not in the Kanda Puranam.
