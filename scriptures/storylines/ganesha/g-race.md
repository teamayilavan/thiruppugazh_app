# The Race Around the World (and the Fruit)

`g-race` | Ganesha | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: playful, then a quiet ache

## One-line story

Murugan races round the world on his peacock to win a prize fruit, while Ganesha goes round his parents; the fruit goes to Ganesha, and, popular tradition says, Murugan leaves for Pazhani.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Song 30 says Murugan flew round the world on his peacock in a contest with Ganesha | Thiruppugazh song 30 |
| Song 532 says the prized fruit was nonetheless given to Ganesha | Thiruppugazh song 532 |
| Popular Pazhani tradition adds that Ganesha went round his parents and that Murugan left for Pazhani in anger | Tradition |

**Honesty note:** a keyword search of the Kanda Puranam (Utpatti, Daksha, Deva) found no such contest: it is later tradition and the songs. The film says so in its narration.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the dialogue, the narration.

## Characters

- **Murugan:** a young god on a peacock, fast and proud.
- **Ganesha:** a gentle, round elephant-faced god, quick-witted.
- **Shiva and Parvati:** the parents, warm.

## The storyline

### 1. The fruit (20 sec)
At Kailasa, a single bright fruit sits in Shiva's hand: whoever goes round the world first shall have it.
**Narration:** "A fruit was offered to whichever son could go round the world first."

### 2. The start (20 sec)
Murugan leaps onto his peacock and flies off; Ganesha looks at his small mouse.
**Narration:** "Murugan was off at once, on his peacock."

### 3. The flight (25 sec)
The peacock soars over mountains, seas, cities, a green world below.
**Narration:** "He flew round the world."

### 4. The thought (20 sec)
Ganesha stands before his parents, thinking, and smiles.
**Narration:** "Ganesha stood still, and thought."

### 5. The circling (25 sec)
He walks round Shiva and Parvati, hands joined: "You are my world."
**Narration:** "He walked round his parents. They were his world."

### 6. The return (20 sec)
Murugan lands, panting. The fruit is in Ganesha's hand.
**Narration:** "When Murugan came back, the fruit was already Ganesha's."

### 7. The ache (20 sec)
Murugan, hurt, turns away, climbs a hill, and sits alone.
**Narration:** "The songs say the fruit went to Ganesha. Tradition says Murugan went away to Pazhani."

### 8. The hill (15 sec)
A lonely hill in the dawn, a young god in saffron on top.
**Narration:** "And there, on a hill, he stayed."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Songs 30 and 532 name the race and the fruit.

## Production notes

- **Do not make either brother the villain.** The film's heart is wisdom and love.
- **The tradition note stays in the narration.** It is not in the Kanda Puranam.
