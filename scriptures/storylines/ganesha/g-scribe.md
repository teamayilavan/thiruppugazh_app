# The Scribe with One Tusk (Ganesha Writes the Mahabharata)

`g-scribe` | Ganesha | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: grand, humorous, devoted | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

On Mount Meru, Ganesha breaks off his own tusk to use as a pen and writes down the whole Mahabharata as the sage Vyasa speaks it.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The songs say Ganesha wrote the Mahabharata on Mount Meru with his broken tusk while Vyasa narrated it | Thiruppugazh songs 88, 1016, 1315 |
| The Critical Edition of the Adi Parva has no such passage, only an invocation to Ganapati | research |

**Honesty note:** this is later tradition and the songs. The popular tradition's two conditions (Vyasa must not pause; Ganesha must understand each verse before writing it) are not in the texts read here; the film includes them as tradition and says so.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the dialogue, the narration.

## Characters

- **Vyasa:** an old sage, dark, with matted hair and a calm smile.
- **Ganesha:** an elephant-faced god, round and serene, with a mouse.
- **Mount Meru:** golden, with clouds.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; grand at the start, gently humorous in the contest, devoted at the end.

### 1. The epic in the mind (20 sec)
**Visual:** A cave high on golden Mount Meru, above the clouds. The sage Vyasa, dark-skinned, with matted hair and a calm smile, sits in meditation. Around his head, glowing pictures drift: kings, armies, a great war, a chariot with a charioteer.
**Narration:** "The sage Vyasa carried a great story in his mind: the story of the Bharata war, of kings and brothers, of right and wrong. It was so long that no ordinary hand could write it down."
**Sound:** a deep, slow drone; distant war drums, very faint.

### 2. The scribe (20 sec)
**Visual:** Vyasa opens his eyes and calls. In a soft golden light, Ganesha arrives, round and serene, his little mouse at his feet. He sits down with a pile of palm leaves.
**Narration:** "So, tradition says, Vyasa called on Ganesha, the remover of obstacles and the lord of learning, to be his scribe. Ganesha came, smiling, and sat down to write."
**Sound:** a bright bell; a gentle, curious melody.

### 3. The conditions (25 sec)
**Visual:** The two face each other, each raising a finger in turn, like friends making a bargain. Both smile.
**Narration:** "Tradition tells of a friendly bargain between them, made before a single word was written. These conditions are not in the songs."
**Dialogue:** Ganesha: "I will write, but you must not stop. If you pause, I put down my pen."
**Dialogue:** Vyasa: "Then you must understand every verse before you write it."
**Sound:** a playful plucked rhythm.

### 4. The pen breaks (25 sec)
**Visual:** Ganesha begins writing swiftly. With a small crack, his pen snaps. Without a pause he takes hold of one of his own tusks, breaks it off, and goes on writing with it. No pain, only resolve.
**Narration:** "Ganesha began to write, swift as the wind. Then his pen broke. The songs say what he did: rather than stop, he broke off his own tusk and wrote the story with it. To this day he is shown with one tusk."
**Sound:** a small crack; then the scratch of writing goes on without a break.

### 5. The telling (25 sec)
**Visual:** Vyasa speaks; his words become streams of light that flow onto the leaves. Ganesha writes and writes; the pile of leaves grows.
**Narration:** "Vyasa spoke, and Ganesha wrote. Verse after verse, the great story flowed onto the leaves: the brothers, the game of dice, the long exile, the war on the field of Kurukshetra."
**Sound:** a flowing, steady theme.

### 6. The hard verses (20 sec)
**Visual:** Vyasa, needing a moment to think, speaks a knotty, difficult verse. Ganesha stops, tilts his head, thinks, smiles, and writes. Vyasa uses the moment to compose the next part.
**Narration:** "Now and then, tradition says, Vyasa spoke a very hard verse. While Ganesha paused to understand it, the sage had time to think of what came next."
**Sound:** a pause; a small comic note; the theme resumes.

### 7. The great book (20 sec)
**Visual:** At last the work is done. Mountains of palm leaves, tied in bundles, glow in the cave. Vyasa bows to Ganesha; Ganesha bows back.
**Narration:** "At last the whole epic was written. The sage bowed to the scribe, and the scribe bowed to the sage. The great story was safe for all the ages to come."
**Sound:** a full, warm chord.

### 8. The song (10 sec)
**Visual:** The scene fades to a temple of Murugan, where a devotee sings.
**Narration:** "Centuries later, Arunagiri sang of the scribe on Meru who wrote with his own tusk. And in every such song, he praises Murugan as that scribe's younger brother."
**Sound:** a single sung phrase.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs name Ganesha as the scribe of the Mahabharata on Mount Meru: 88 (Thiruchendur), 1016 (general songs) and 1315 (Pazhamudircholai), each praising Murugan as the younger brother of the scribe.

## Production notes

- **Do not mock the broken tusk.** It is a gift.
- **Say it is tradition** in the narration of scene 3.
