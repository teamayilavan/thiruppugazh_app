# The Scribe with One Tusk (Ganesha Writes the Mahabharata)

`g-scribe` | Ganesha | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: grand, humorous, devoted

## One-line story

On Mount Meru, Ganesha breaks off his own tusk to use as a pen and writes down the whole Mahabharata as the sage Vyasa speaks it.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The songs say Ganesha wrote the Mahabharata on Mount Meru with his broken tusk while Vyasa narrated it | Thiruppugazh songs 88, 1016, 1315 |
| The Critical Edition of the Adi Parva has no such passage, only an invocation to Ganapati | research |

**Honesty note:** this is later tradition and the songs. The popular tradition's two conditions (Vyasa must not pause; Ganesha must understand each verse before writing it) are not in the texts read here; the film includes them as tradition and says so.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the dialogue, the narration.

## Characters

- **Vyasa:** an old sage, dark, with matted hair and a calm smile.
- **Ganesha:** an elephant-faced god, round and serene, with a mouse.
- **Mount Meru:** golden, with clouds.

## The storyline

### 1. The epic in the mind (20 sec)
Vyasa sits in a cave on Meru, with a vast, glowing story in his mind: kings, a great war.
**Narration:** "The sage Vyasa had a great story in his mind, and no one to write it down."

### 2. The scribe (20 sec)
He calls Ganesha. Ganesha comes, with a smile.
**Narration:** "He asked Ganesha to be his scribe."

### 3. The conditions (25 sec)
Ganesha agrees on one condition: Vyasa must not stop. Vyasa agrees, with his own: Ganesha must understand every verse before he writes it.
**Narration:** "Tradition says each set a condition."

### 4. The pen breaks (25 sec)
Ganesha takes a palm-leaf; his reed pen snaps. He breaks off his own tusk, dips it in ink.
**Narration:** "When his pen broke, he broke his own tusk and wrote with that."

### 5. The telling (25 sec)
Vyasa speaks; the verses become light; Ganesha writes without pause.
**Narration:** "The sage spoke, and the Lord wrote."

### 6. The puzzles (20 sec)
Vyasa weaves in a hard verse to make Ganesha pause; Ganesha thinks, smiles, and writes.
**Narration:** "Vyasa slipped in a hard verse, to gain a moment. Ganesha understood it, and wrote."

### 7. The scroll (20 sec)
A great pile of palm-leaf: the whole story.
**Narration:** "So the great epic was written."

### 8. The song (10 sec)
A Thiruppugazh line floats over the scene.
**Narration:** "And the poet remembered it, in a line."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Songs 88, 1016 and 1315 name the story.

## Production notes

- **Do not mock the broken tusk.** It is a gift.
- **Say it is tradition** in the narration of scene 3.
