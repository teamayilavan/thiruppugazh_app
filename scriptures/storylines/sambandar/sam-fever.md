# The King's Fever and the Holy Ash

`sam-fever` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tense, then relieving | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Madurai, a Pandya king burns with fever after a plot against Sambandar's lodging turns back on him; the child saint sings a hymn, rubs holy ash on the king, and the fever leaves.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Queen Mangaiyarkkarasi and the minister Kulachchirai call Sambandar to Madurai | Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam, vv. 603-613 |
| He reaches Madurai | 645-668 |
| Under Jain influence the king (called Maran in the Puranam) has the saint's lodging set on fire; the fire turns on the king as a burning fever | 678-705 |
| The Jains try to cure the king's left side; it only gets worse | 763-766 |
| Sambandar sings "Mantiramavathu Neeru" (Tevaram 2.66.1), rubs the ash on the king, and the fever leaves | 764-765, 772 (719 and 772 checked) |
| He also sings "Maninernvizhi" to the queen | Tevaram 3.39.1 |
| The songs often join the straightening of the king's hunchback to this cure; in the Puranam it comes later, at the river test | PP 847 (see `sam-ordeals`) |

**Tradition note:** later tradition names the king Koon Pandyan ("the hunchback") and Nindrasir Nedumaran; the Puranam calls him Maran.
**Added for the screen:** the colour of the fever, the expressions, the narration.

## Characters

- **Sambandar:** a young saint, calm.
- **The Pandya king:** a proud ruler, burning, in pain.
- **Queen Mangaiyarkkarasi:** devout, anxious, brave.
- **The minister Kulachchirai:** loyal and quiet.
- **The Jain ascetics:** shown as men with peacock-feather brushes, never as villains of caricature.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; tense in the night, then cool and relieved.

### 1. A call from Madurai (20 sec)
**Visual:** The palace at Madurai. Queen Mangaiyarkkarasi, devout, a thread of holy ash on her brow, speaks quietly with the old minister Kulachchirai. Behind them, the king's court is full of Jain ascetics with peacock-feather brushes. A messenger rides north.
**Narration:** "In Madurai, the Pandya king Maran had turned away from Shiva and followed the Jain teachers. But his queen, Mangaiyarkkarasi, and his minister, Kulachchirai, still loved Shiva in secret. They sent a message to the child saint of Sirkazhi: please come."
**Sound:** a quiet court drone; hoofbeats fading.

### 2. The child arrives (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar arrives in his pearl palanquin with a crowd of devotees singing. They are given a lodging, a monastery hall near the temple of Alavai. The minister bows to the boy.
**Narration:** "Sambandar came, with his devotees singing around him. The minister bowed to the boy and lodged them in a hall near the temple of Shiva. In the palace, the king heard the news, and he was not pleased."
**Sound:** conches and singing, then a low, uneasy note.

### 3. Fire in the night (25 sec)
**Visual:** Night. Shadowy figures carry torches to the edge of the hall and set the thatch alight. Devotees wake and beat out the flames. No one is hurt. The child saint stands calm in the doorway, looking at the smoke.
**Narration:** "That night, the king's Jain advisers set fire to the hall where the devotees slept. The devotees woke and put it out. The child was grieved. Fire, set against the servants of Shiva, with the king's consent. That was the king's wrong to answer for."
**Sound:** crackling, shouts, then water hissing and silence.

### 4. "Go slowly" (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar closes his eyes and sings softly toward the temple. The last embers rise as sparks, drift together into a glowing thread, and float away over the dark city toward the palace.
**Narration:** "He sang a hymn to the Lord of Alavai, and asked that the fire set by the Jains should go to the Pandya king. But he asked that it go slowly, gently, for the sake of the good queen."
**Sound:** a single young voice, wordless; a soft whoosh.

### 5. The burning king (20 sec)
**Visual:** The palace bedchamber. The king tosses on his bed, his skin glowing a feverish red. The queen sits beside him, frightened. Wet cloths steam on his forehead.
**Narration:** "In the palace, the king woke burning. A fever, hot as fire, filled his body. Nothing cooled it. The queen sat by his side, and wept."
**Sound:** a low, pulsing heat-tone.

### 6. Two cures (25 sec)
**Visual:** The court gathers around the king's bed. Sambandar is brought in. The Jain teachers stand on the king's left side, stroking it with their peacock brushes. Sambandar stands on his right, holding holy ash.
**Narration:** "The queen and the minister sent for the child. The Jain teachers said: we will cure his left side, let the boy cure the right. So they stood on either side of the king. The Jains chanted, and stroked his left side with their peacock feathers."
**Sound:** two different chants, one on each side.

### 7. The holy ash (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar sings and rubs the ash on the king's right side. The red glow drains from it and it turns cool and pale, like a lotus pond. On the left, the glow flares hotter. The king looks from one side to the other.
**Narration:** "Sambandar sang the hymn of the holy ash, 'Mantiramavathu Neeru', and touched the right side with it. That side grew cool, like a lotus pond. But the left side burned hotter than before."
**Sound:** a cool, clear chord on one side; a hot crackle on the other.

### 8. Cool morning (15 sec)
**Visual:** The king turns his head to the child. Sambandar touches the ash to the left side as well; the last red fades. Dawn light enters. The king folds his hands; the queen and minister bow.
**Dialogue:** The king: "Holy one, take the rest of this fever from me too."
**Narration:** "The child touched him once more, and the fever was gone. The king folded his hands and said: I am saved."
**Sound:** morning birds; a gentle, relieved melody.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Twelve songs recall the Pandya's fever cured by the holy ash, and many join to it the straightening of his back, which the Puranam places at the river test. Songs to show: 171 (Palani), 573 (Viralimalai), 768 (Sirkazhi), 962 (Madurai).

## Production notes

- **Do not show the Jains as cruel caricatures.** The film's tone is relief, not triumph.
- **The fire:** keep it stylised; show no one burning.
