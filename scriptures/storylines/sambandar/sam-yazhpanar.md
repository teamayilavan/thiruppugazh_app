# The Minstrel and the Lyre (Thiruneelakanta Yazhpanar)

`sam-yazhpanar` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: devotion, a crisis, a lesson

## One-line story

A minstrel who plays Sambandar's hymns on his lyre finds that some hymns will not fit the instrument; ready to destroy it, he is stopped by the child saint, who tells him the grace of a hymn cannot be measured by an instrument.

## The storyline

### 1. The travellers (20 sec)
A minstrel and his wife walk into Sirkazhi, a lyre on his back, at dusk.
**Narration:** "A minstrel came to Sirkazhi, with his wife, and his lyre."

### 2. The meeting (20 sec)
Sambandar welcomes them; the minstrel bows low.
**Narration:** "He had heard of the child saint's hymns, and he came to play them."

### 3. The first notes (25 sec)
The lyre's notes rise, matching the words. The audience is spellbound.
**Narration:** "He played the hymns, note for note."

### 4. The gift (15 sec)
A golden cymbal is placed in Yazhpanar's hands.
**Narration:** "He was given a cymbal of gold."

### 5. The hymn that would not fit (25 sec)
At a temple, he plays a hymn; the strings will not hold its shape. He tries again; the sound breaks. He lowers the lyre, grief on his face.
**Narration:** "But one hymn would not fit the instrument."

### 6. The breaking (20 sec)
He raises the lyre to smash it. A hand takes his wrist: Sambandar.
**Narration:** "He would have broken it. But the child stopped him."

### 7. The lesson (25 sec)
Sambandar smiles: "The grace of a hymn cannot be measured by an instrument. Play as the music comes."
**Narration:** "The grace of a hymn cannot be measured by an instrument."

### 8. The music comes (15 sec)
The lyre sounds again, without strain.
**Narration:** "And the lyre sang."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 354 names the minstrel at Thiruvanaikka.

## Production notes

- **Respect the minstrel.** His wish to destroy the instrument is out of love for the hymns, not anger.
- **Silence is a character here:** let music cut out in scene 5.
