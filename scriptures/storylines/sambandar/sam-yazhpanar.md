# The Minstrel and the Lyre (Thiruneelakanta Yazhpanar)

`sam-yazhpanar` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: devotion, a crisis, a lesson | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A minstrel who plays Sambandar's hymns on his lyre finds that some hymns will not fit the instrument; ready to destroy it, he is stopped by the child saint, who tells him the grace of a hymn cannot be measured by an instrument.

## Characters

- **Yazhpanar:** a minstrel with a lyre, patient, humble.
- **Matanga Chulamani:** his wife, who sings.
- **Sambandar:** a young saint with a smile.
- **Temple crowd.**

## Places

Sirkazhi; temple halls; the roads of the Chola country.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; full of music, then a sudden silence, then gentle wisdom.

### 1. The travellers (20 sec)
**Visual:** A road into Sirkazhi. A minstrel walks with his wife: Thiruneelakanta Yazhpanar, holy ash on his brow, carrying a yazh, a curved harp-like lyre, wrapped in cloth; beside him Matanga Chulamani, a singer, calm and bright.
**Narration:** "A minstrel came walking to Sirkazhi with his wife. His name was Thiruneelakanta Yazhpanar, and he played the yazh, the old Tamil harp. His wife, Matanga Chulamani, was a singer. They had come to bow at the feet of the child saint."
**Sound:** footsteps; a few soft, plucked notes.

### 2. The welcome (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar comes out to meet them and welcomes them with a bright smile. He leads them into the temple courtyard, before the Thoni shrine.
**Narration:** "Sambandar came out to meet them, smiling, and was glad. He led them into the temple, before the Lord of the Boat."
**Dialogue:** Sambandar: "Play your yazh here, for our Lord."
**Sound:** a welcoming chord.

### 3. The first notes (25 sec)
**Visual:** The minstrel tunes his strings carefully, finds the mode, and plays a hymn of Sambandar's. His wife sings with him. The music rises; birds settle on the temple walls to listen; in the sky, faint figures of heavenly musicians join in.
**Narration:** "The minstrel tuned his strings, found the right mode, and played the child's hymns, while his wife sang. The sound of the strings and the two voices became one. The Puranam says even the heavenly musicians in the sky joined in."
**Sound:** the yazh, warm and full; a woman's voice, wordless.

### 4. Always beside him (20 sec)
**Visual:** Montage: the minstrel walks behind the child's pearl palanquin from temple to temple; at each shrine, as the child sings, he plays. The child keeps time with small golden cymbals.
**Narration:** "The minstrel begged to stay with the child always, and to play every hymn he sang. Sambandar agreed. From then on, wherever the child sang, the yazh played beside him."
**Sound:** a travelling rhythm; cymbals and strings together.

### 5. A hard word (20 sec)
**Visual:** Dharmapuram, the minstrel's mother's town. His relatives crowd around, proud of him. One of them says something; the minstrel's face falls, and he trembles.
**Narration:** "Then they came to Dharmapuram, his mother's town. His relatives praised him, and said: the hymns are famous because you play them on your yazh. The minstrel was afraid. That was not true, and it must not be said."
**Sound:** chatter, then a sudden low note.

### 6. The hymn that would not fit (20 sec)
**Visual:** The minstrel falls at the child's feet. Sambandar listens, then sings a new hymn in the temple. The minstrel takes up his yazh, but his fingers stop; the strings cannot hold the music. Silence.
**Narration:** "He asked the child to sing a hymn whose music no instrument could hold, so that everyone would know the truth. Sambandar sang. The minstrel took up his yazh, and could not play it. The music would not fit the strings."
**Sound:** the child's voice; then the yazh tries, falters, and stops. Total silence.

### 7. The child stops him (25 sec)
**Visual:** The minstrel, ashamed of his instrument, lifts the yazh high to break it. A small hand catches his arm. Sambandar gently takes the yazh from him.
**Narration:** "The minstrel lifted his yazh to break it. But the child stopped him, and took the yazh into his own hands."
**Dialogue:** Sambandar: "Why break it? The greatness of the Lord's grace cannot be measured by any instrument. Play what this yazh can hold. Play the hymns as they come to you."
**Sound:** a gentle, low string held under the voice.

### 8. The lyre sings (15 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar hands the yazh back. The minstrel bows, places the yazh on his head in reverence, then plays again, simply, with joy. His wife sings; the child smiles.
**Narration:** "The minstrel bowed, and lifted the yazh onto his head, as a gift from the Lord. Then he played again, with a lighter heart."
**Sound:** the yazh returns, soft and glad.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 354, sung at Thiruvanaikka, names the minstrel Yazhpanar as the companion of Sambandar. Song to show: 354 (Thiruvanaikka).

## Production notes

- **Respect the minstrel.** His wish to destroy the instrument is out of love for the hymns, not anger.
- **Silence is a character here:** let music cut out in scene 5.
- **Corrections from reading the verses:** the broken-lyre episode happens at Dharmapuram, the minstrel's mother's town (vv. 443-452): his kinsmen credit the yazh for the hymns' fame, so he asks Sambandar for a hymn the yazh cannot hold ("Mathar Madappidi", v. 447). The golden cymbals (talam) were Sambandar's, not a gift to the minstrel (v. 278); the script drops the cymbal-gift scene.
