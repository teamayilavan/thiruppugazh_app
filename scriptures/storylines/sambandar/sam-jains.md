# After the Contests (the Jains and the King's Turning)

`sam-jains` | Sambandar | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: grave, sombre, a turning | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

After the Jains lose the debates and the tests, the king takes the holy ash and turns to Shiva; the songs also repeat a tradition that the Jains were impaled, which this film treats with restraint.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |

| After the contests the Jains are defeated | Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam, vv. 853-856 (856 checked) |
| The king takes the holy ash | vv. 857-858 |

**Honesty note:** the research keeps the impalement as the tradition the songs repeat. The Periya Puranam verses read here (853-858) carry the defeat and the king taking the ash.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; grave and quiet, a turning rather than a triumph.

### 1. The leaf comes home (20 sec)
**Visual:** The royal hall at Madurai. The minister Kulachchirai walks in, still wet from the river, holding Sambandar's palm leaf in both hands. He lays it before the king. The hall is silent.
**Narration:** "The minister brought the leaf back from the river, still wet, and laid it before the king. The tests were over. The leaf had stayed green in the fire, and it had gone against the flood."
**Sound:** footsteps on stone; then silence.

### 2. The defeated teachers (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Jain teachers stand together, their peacock-feather brushes lowered, their faces grave. They are shown with dignity, not mocked. The court does not jeer.
**Narration:** "On the other side stood the Jain teachers. They had lost both tests, before the king and the whole city. They had nothing more to say. No one in the court laughed at them.
**Sound:** a low, sombre drone.

### 3. The king stands up (20 sec)
**Visual:** The king rises from his throne, standing tall now, his back straight. He looks at the child saint, then down at his own hands, as if seeing them for the first time.
**Narration:** "The king stood up. His body was cool, and his back was straight. He looked at the child, and then at his own hands. He had been far from Shiva for a long time."
**Sound:** a single held note.

### 4. The holy ash (25 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar holds out holy ash in his small palm. The king bows low and receives it. He smears it on his forehead, his arms, his chest, all over his body.
**Narration:** "Then Sambandar gave him the holy ash. The king bowed low and took it, and put it on, all over his body. The king who had turned away from Shiva wore the white ash again."
**Sound:** a soft temple bell; a warm chord.

### 5. The city follows (25 sec)
**Visual:** The news runs out of the palace. In street after street of Madurai, people take ash from pots and bowls and mark their foreheads. Shopkeepers, children, soldiers, old women. The white marks spread across the city like dawn.
**Narration:** "The people heard: the king has put on the holy ash. And all of Madurai followed him. In every street, men and women and children marked their foreheads white. The Puranam says the darkness lifted from the land."
**Sound:** voices rising; drums; conches.

### 6. The queen and the minister (20 sec)
**Visual:** Queen Mangaiyarkkarasi and Kulachchirai kneel before the child saint, overcome. He blesses them. The queen's eyes are full of tears.
**Narration:** "The queen and the minister fell at the child's feet. For years they had kept their love for Shiva alive in a court that did not share it. Now their long prayer had been answered."
**Sound:** a tender flute melody.

### 7. The temple of Alavai (20 sec)
**Visual:** The king, the queen and the minister walk with Sambandar to the temple of Shiva at Madurai. Lamps are lit; the temple fills with worshippers. The camera rises over the city, white with ash, under a clear sky.
**Narration:** "Together they went to the temple of the Lord of Alavai, and worshipped. The child had come with a song and a handful of ash. And the Pandya country turned again to Shiva."
**Sound:** temple bells, voices singing, fading into a calm chord.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

In some forty songs Arunagiri recalls the end of the contest at Madurai, when the Jains were defeated and the king and his city took the holy ash. Songs to show: 17, 132, 263, 306, 405, 1162.

## Production notes

- **Never show violence.** Show the court's emptiness, not any harm to any person.
- **Keep respect for Jain viewers.**
