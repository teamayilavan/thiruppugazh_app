# The Girl Who Rose from Her Bones (Poompavai)

`sam-poompavai` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: sorrow, then wonder, then joy

## One-line story

A merchant's daughter, promised to the saint Sambandar, dies of a snakebite; years later, when Sambandar comes to Mylapore and sings a hymn, she rises alive from the pot that holds her bones.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| At Mylapore the merchant Sivanesar vows his daughter Poompavai to Sambandar | Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam, vv. 1033-1053 |
| A snake bites her in a garden and she dies; her father keeps her bones in a pot | 1055-1058 |
| Sambandar comes to Mylapore | around 1084 |
| He sings the hymn "Mattitta Punnai" (Tevaram 2.47), named in the Puranam, and she rises alive from the pot | 1084-1100 (v. 1088 names the hymn) |
| The songs say he "brought the skeleton back as a girl" | Thiruppugazh songs (3 songs) |

**Added for the screen:** the staging of the garden and the pot (the verses give events, not the look), the narration.

## Characters

- **Poompavai:** a girl in her teens, kind and bright; then a still figure; then radiant.
- **Sivanesar:** the father, a devout merchant, steadfast through grief.
- **Sambandar:** a young saint.
- **Townspeople.**

## Places

Mylapore; a garden; the home; the Kapaliccharam temple.

## The storyline

### 1. A vow (20 sec)
A merchant in his home at Mylapore looks at his young daughter and vows her to the saint he admires: Sambandar.
**Narration:** "A merchant of Mylapore promised his daughter to the saint of Sirkazhi."

### 2. The garden (20 sec)
Poompavai picks flowers; a snake in the leaves; a startled cry.
**Narration:** "One day, in the garden, a snake bit her."

### 3. The loss (25 sec)
Her father holds her; she is gone. He weeps. He has her bones placed in a pot, which he keeps.
**Narration:** "She died. Her father kept her bones, and waited."

### 4. Waiting (20 sec)
Years pass: seasons over the pot in a quiet room, the father lighting a lamp each night.
**Narration:** "He did not give up his vow."

### 5. The saint arrives (20 sec)
Sambandar enters Mylapore with his devotees; the father runs to him.
**Narration:** "Then Sambandar came to Mylapore."

### 6. The pot (20 sec)
The father brings the pot to the temple and places it before the saint.
**Narration:** "The father brought the pot."

### 7. The hymn (25 sec)
Sambandar sings. Light gathers over the pot; slowly, a girl rises from it, alive.
**Narration:** "He sang a hymn, and she rose."

### 8. Reunion (15 sec)
Poompavai steps out, and her father falls at her feet. The town cheers.
**Narration:** "A girl came back from her bones."

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs say he "brought the skeleton back as a girl". The hymn is Tevaram 2.47, "Mattitta Punnai".

## Production notes

- **A gentle approach to death.** Show the bones only as a covered pot; no skeleton.
- **Do not suggest she married him in the film;** the research records only the vow and the miracle. Check the Puranam before adding more.
