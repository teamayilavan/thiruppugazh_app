# The Girl Who Rose from Her Bones (Poompavai)

`sam-poompavai` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: sorrow, then wonder, then joy | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A merchant's daughter, promised to the saint Sambandar, dies of a snakebite; years later, when Sambandar comes to Mylapore and sings a hymn, she rises alive from the pot that holds her bones.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| At Mylapore the merchant Sivanesar vows his daughter Poompavai to Sambandar | Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam, vv. 1033-1053 |
| A snake bites her in a garden and she dies; her father keeps her bones in a pot | 1055-1058 |
| Sambandar comes to Mylapore | around 1084 |
| He sings the hymn "Mattitta Punnai" (Tevaram 2.47), named in the Puranam, and she rises alive from the pot | 1084-1100 (v. 1088 names the hymn) |
| The songs say he "brought the skeleton back as a girl" | Thiruppugazh songs (3 songs) |

**Added for the screen:** the staging of the garden and the pot (the verses give events, not the look), the narration.

## Characters

- **Poompavai:** a girl in her teens, kind and bright; then a still figure; then radiant.
- **Sivanesar:** the father, a devout merchant, steadfast through grief.
- **Sambandar:** a young saint.
- **Townspeople.**

## Places

Mylapore; a garden; the home; the Kapaliccharam temple.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; sorrow, then wonder, then joy.

### 1. A merchant's vow (20 sec)
**Visual:** Mylapore by the sea: a busy harbour town, the tall tower of the Kapaleeswarar temple. In a rich house, the merchant Sivanesar, a devotee with holy ash on his brow, holds his little daughter Poompavai on his knee and listens to travellers telling of the child saint.
**Narration:** "In Mylapore, by the sea, lived a rich merchant named Sivanesar, who loved Shiva. He heard of the child saint Sambandar, and his heart was full. He made a vow: my daughter Poompavai, and all that I own, belong to Sambandar."
**Sound:** sea waves; harbour bells.

### 2. The garden (20 sec)
**Visual:** Years later. Poompavai, a young girl, gathers flowers with her friends in a garden full of jasmine. A dark shape moves in the grass. She cries out and falls. Her friends run to her. (The snake is only a shadow; no bite is shown.)
**Narration:** "Poompavai grew up, gentle and bright. One day she went into the garden to pick flowers. A snake hidden among the leaves bit her, and she fell."
**Sound:** birdsong, then a sharp cry; silence.

### 3. Grief (20 sec)
**Visual:** Healers and charm-singers gather around her bed; they shake their heads. Sivanesar sits by her, broken. A lamp goes out.
**Narration:** "Healers came, and charm-singers, but nothing could save her. Poompavai died. Her father's grief was beyond words."
**Sound:** a single low string.

### 4. The covered pot (20 sec)
**Visual:** After the funeral, the father places a clay pot, covered with a cloth, in a quiet room of the house, garlanded and lit by a lamp. He bows before it.
**Narration:** "He did not give up hope. He kept her bones and ashes in a covered pot, and set it in a room of his house. She was promised to the saint. He would wait for the saint, however long it took.
**Sound:** a soft, patient drone.

### 5. The saint comes (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar arrives in Mylapore in his pearl palanquin, with crowds of devotees. Sivanesar comes and falls at his feet, weeping, telling his story.
**Narration:** "At last Sambandar came to Mylapore. The merchant fell at his feet and told him everything. Sambandar asked him to bring the pot to the gate of the temple."
**Sound:** conches, then a quiet voice.

### 6. Before the temple (25 sec)
**Visual:** The covered pot is set down before the temple tower. A great crowd gathers: townspeople, devotees, and Jains and Buddhists who have come to watch. Sambandar looks at the pot with compassion, bows to the Lord within, and begins to sing.
**Narration:** "A great crowd gathered to watch. Sambandar bowed to the Lord of Mylapore, and began a hymn, 'Mattitta Punnai'. In every verse he named a festival of the temple, and asked her, by name:"
**Dialogue:** Sambandar: "Poompavai, will you go, without seeing this festival?"
**Sound:** a young voice singing, temple drums softly beneath.

### 7. She rises (25 sec)
**Visual:** With each verse, a soft light grows inside the pot. At the last verse, the cloth lifts, and the pot opens like a lotus bud. Poompavai rises from it, alive, now a girl of twelve, glowing. The crowd cries out.
**Narration:** "As he sang, verse after verse, she took form again inside the pot. And when the hymn was done, the pot opened like a lotus flower, and Poompavai stood up, alive, as lovely as a lotus in bloom."
**Sound:** a rising chord; the crowd shouting "Hara! Hara!"

### 8. Home (15 sec)
**Visual:** Poompavai bows before the temple and before the saint. Her father falls at the saint's feet. Sambandar gently raises him and sends father and daughter home together.
**Narration:** "Her father fell at the saint's feet. Sambandar said: by the Lord's grace I have given her life. She is like my own child. Take your daughter home."
**Sound:** a gentle, joyful melody.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs recall the girl brought back from her bones at Mylapore. Songs to show: 150 (Palani), 693 (Thirumayilai), 944 (Avinasi).

## Production notes

- **A gentle approach to death.** Show the bones only as a covered pot; no skeleton.
- **Do not suggest she married him in the film;** the research records only the vow and the miracle. Check the Puranam before adding more.
- **Marriage checked (vv. 1112-1116):** the father asks Sambandar to marry her; he declines, since he gave her life by Shiva's grace. Scene 8 shows only that he sends her home as his own child.
