# The Pearl Palanquin (Thiruvarathurai)

`sam-palanquin` | Sambandar | running time 2:30 (7 scenes) | tone: gentle, glowing, a gift | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Thiruvarathurai, tired from walking, the child saint is given by Shiva a pearl palanquin, a pearl umbrella and the insignia of a king, and the local Brahmins carry them to him.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| At Thiruvarathurai Shiva gives the child saint a pearl palanquin, umbrella and royal insignia | Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam, vv. 195-212 (195 checked) |
| The local Brahmins carry the gifts to him | vv. 195-212 |
| Song 758 names the gift | Thiruppugazh song 758 |

**Added for the screen:** the setting of the road and the dusk, the reaction of the child, the narration. The verses give the gift and the carrying; they are not read here for the Brahmins' dialogue.

## Characters

- **Sambandar:** a small child, tired from walking.
- **His father,** carrying him on his shoulders.
- **Shiva** (a voice and a light).
- **The temple Brahmins:** respectful, carrying the gifts.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; gentle and glowing.

### 1. The long road (20 sec)
**Visual:** A dusty road through green Chola fields under a hot sun. The child saint, still a small boy, walks barefoot among a band of devotees; his father beside him. The sun is fierce.
**Narration:** "The child saint was travelling from temple to temple, singing at each one. He was small, and he walked on his own feet, and the summer road was long."
**Sound:** footsteps; cicadas; a slow walking rhythm.

### 2. The tired child (20 sec)
**Visual:** At the village of Maranpadi, the devotees wipe their brows. The child's feet are dusty and red. He quietly repeats the five sacred syllables. The sun sinks into the western sea.
**Narration:** "At Maranpadi everyone was tired from the heat. The child said the five holy syllables and walked on. The Puranam says even the sun seemed to hide its rays, as if afraid of tiring him."
**Sound:** the rhythm slows; evening birds.

### 3. A dream in Arathurai (25 sec)
**Visual:** Night in the temple village of Thiruvarathurai on the river. In house after house, Brahmin priests sleep; in each dream the same radiant form of Shiva appears, pointing to the temple.
**Narration:** "That night, at Thiruvarathurai, the Lord came in a dream to the priests of the town, to every house."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Gnanasambandan is coming to us. Take from my temple a palanquin of pearls, a pearl umbrella, and golden trumpets, and give them to him."
**Sound:** a soft, deep voice over a drone.

### 4. The same dream (20 sec)
**Visual:** The child, asleep under the stars with his devotees, smiles in his sleep; the same light touches his face.
**Narration:** "And the Lord came to the child too, and told him: these are my gifts, gladly given. Take them, and ride."
**Sound:** a quiet chord.

### 5. Found in the temple (25 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn at Thiruvarathurai. The priests meet in the street, each telling his dream, amazed. They open the temple: in the hall stand a palanquin shining with pearls, a white pearl umbrella like the moon, and gleaming trumpets.
**Narration:** "In the morning the priests met, and found they had all had the same dream. They opened the temple, and there they were: a palanquin covered with pearls, an umbrella of pearls, and trumpets of gold."
**Sound:** gasps; a bright, astonished flourish.

### 6. The gift on the road (25 sec)
**Visual:** A joyful procession with conches and drums carries the gifts out along the road. At sunrise they meet the child and his devotees. The priests bow and set the palanquin down before him.
**Narration:** "With conches and drums they carried the gifts out along the road, singing as they went, and met the child and his devotees at sunrise."
**Dialogue:** A priest: "The Lord of Arathurai sends you these. Please receive his grace."
**Sound:** conches, drums, a crowd crying "Hara!"

### 7. The ride (15 sec)
**Visual:** The child bows to the gifts as to Shiva himself, circles them, then climbs into the palanquin. The pearl umbrella opens above him like the full moon, and the trumpets sound as they go.
**Narration:** "The child bowed to the gifts as to the Lord himself. Then he rode on, with the white umbrella above him like the full moon. From that day the pearl palanquin carried him to temple after temple, a gift from the Lord to his little singer."
**Sound:** golden trumpets, the walking rhythm now light and happy.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 758, sung at Thiruvarathurai, names the palanquin that Shiva gave to Murugan come as Sambandar. Song to show: 758 (Thiruvarathurai).

## Production notes

- **A small film.** Keep it quiet and warm.
- **Scene 3 checked against the Puranam (vv. 194-212):** Shiva appears in a dream to the Brahmins of every house at Nelvayil (Arathurai) and names the gifts (vv. 196-197); they find the gifts in the temple (v. 201); Shiva also tells the child in a dream to accept them (vv. 205-206).
