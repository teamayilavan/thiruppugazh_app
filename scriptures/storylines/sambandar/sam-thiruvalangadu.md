# The Dance at Thiruvalangadu

`sam-thiruvalangadu` | Sambandar | running time 2:35 (7 scenes) | tone: mystic, grand, a vision | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The songs say Murugan came as Sambandar to Thiruvalangadu, where Shiva danced for the gods; Sambandar sang a hymn there, and the temple's tradition holds the dance.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Song 674 says Murugan came as Sambandar to Thiruvalangadu, where Shiva danced for the celestials | Thiruppugazh song 674 |
| Sambandar's own hymn at Thiruvalangadu exists: Tevaram 1.45, twelve verses; verse 1 names Alangadu and the story of Neeli at Pazhaiyanur | Tevaram 1.45 |
| The hymn does **not** describe the dance | Tevaram 1.45 |
| The dance (Shiva and Kali) is the temple's tradition | `devi/d-kali-dance` |

**Tradition note:** the dance is shown as the temple's tradition and the song's image, not as something the hymn describes. The details of the contest between Shiva and Kali are not in any text read here.
**Added for the screen:** every visual; the narration.

## Characters

- **Sambandar:** a young saint, singing.
- **Shiva:** dancing, with fire.
- **The gods:** watching from the sky.
- **Murugan** (a golden figure in the first and last shots).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; quiet and reverent at night, grand and mystic at the vision.

### 1. The forest of banyans (20 sec)
**Visual:** Evening. Sambandar and his devotees walk through northern Tamil country toward a vast grove of banyan trees, their hanging roots like pillars. Beyond the trees, the temple tower of Thiruvalangadu, and the old village of Pazhaiyanur.
**Narration:** "On his long journey north, Sambandar came near Thiruvalangadu, the forest of banyan trees, beside the old village of Pazhaiyanur. The Thiruppugazh says it was Murugan himself who came there, as Sambandar."
**Sound:** evening birds; wind in the leaves.

### 2. He will not enter (25 sec)
**Visual:** The child stops at the edge of the town. He looks at the road with awe and bows to the ground itself. In a soft, glowing memory-image, a thin, aged woman-saint, Karaikkal Ammaiyar, moves toward the holy place with her head bowed to the very earth.
**Narration:** "But he would not walk into the town. This was the holy place where the great woman-saint Karaikkal Ammaiyar, the Mother, had come, walking on her head out of reverence. How could he tread where she had walked? So he stayed the night in a village nearby."
**Sound:** a hushed, reverent drone.

### 3. The dream (25 sec)
**Visual:** Night. The child sleeps on a mat in a small village house. A soft light fills the room; the Lord of the banyan forest appears to him in a dream, smiling.
**Narration:** "In the middle of the night, the Lord of the banyan forest came to him in a dream, and spoke to him."
**Dialogue:** Shiva: "Have you forgotten to sing of us?"
**Narration (continued):** "The child woke at once, folded his hands, and was filled with love."
**Sound:** a deep, kind voice; a single bell.

### 4. The midnight hymn (25 sec)
**Visual:** Still at midnight, the child sits up and sings toward the banyan forest, by the light of one lamp. His devotees wake and gather around him, listening. In the dark, the hanging roots of the banyans seem to sway to the song.
**Narration:** "There and then, at midnight, he sang a new hymn for the Lord of Alangadu. In it he praised the people of Pazhaiyanur, who, the old story says, kept their promise to a stranger even at the cost of their lives, against the wiles of Neeli."
**Sound:** a young voice singing, wordless; crickets.

### 5. The dancing place (25 sec)
**Visual:** Morning. The child tells his devotees what the Lord said. Then the camera moves into the temple of Thiruvalangadu, where the dance hall stands. A vision rises: Shiva in his fierce, whirling dance, one leg lifted high to the sky, before the gods.
**Narration:** "In the morning he told the devotees of the Lord's kindness, and sang again. This temple is remembered as one of the great halls of Shiva's dance. The people of this place say that here the Lord danced for the gods, his foot raised to the sky."
**Sound:** drums building to a powerful rhythm.

### 6. What the hymn says, and what the temple says (20 sec)
**Visual:** The dance fades into a quiet image of the old temple at dawn, worshippers walking in, a lamp before the shrine.
**Narration:** "Sambandar's hymn does not describe that dance. It praises the Lord, and the people who kept their word. The dance is the temple's own memory, kept for centuries by those who worship there."
**Sound:** the drums fade; a calm melody.

### 7. The poet remembers (15 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar's small figure walks on through the banyan grove into the morning light. Faintly, a peacock feather drifts across the frame.
**Narration:** "Centuries later, Arunagiri sang at this temple too. He remembered that Murugan had come here as the child saint, to the place where his father danced."
**Sound:** a soft peacock call; the melody resolves.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 674, sung at Thiruvalangadu, says Murugan came there as Sambandar, to the place where Shiva danced for the gods. Song to show: 674 (Thiruvalangadu).

## Production notes

- **Do not claim the hymn describes the dance.** The narration says the temple does.
- **Pair with the Devi film** that tells the dance contest in full.
- **The visit is in the Puranam (vv. 1007-1011):** Sambandar will not enter the town where Karaikkal Ammaiyar walked on her head, sleeps nearby, and Shiva in a dream asks "Have you forgotten to sing of us?"; he sings Tevaram 1.45 at midnight. Scenes 2-4 follow these verses; the dance stays the temple's tradition.
