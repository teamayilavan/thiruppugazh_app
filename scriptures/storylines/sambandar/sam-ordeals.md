# The Leaf in the Fire and the Leaf Against the River

`sam-ordeals` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: suspense, then wonder | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

To settle which faith is true, two palm-leaf scriptures are put in a fire and then in a flooded river: Sambandar's leaf stays green in the fire and swims upstream against the flood.

**Added for the screen:** the staging of the debate and the crowd, the shape of the flood, the narration.

## Characters

- **Sambandar:** young and calm.
- **The king and queen of Madurai.**
- **The Jain ascetics.**
- **The minister Kulachchirai.**

## Places

Madurai; the court; the river Vaigai in flood; Tiruvedagam on the bank.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; suspense at each test, wonder at each answer.

### 1. The challenge (20 sec)
**Visual:** The royal hall at Madurai. The king, now well, sits on his throne; the queen and minister near him. On one side, Sambandar and his devotees; on the other, the Jain teachers with their peacock brushes and bundles of palm-leaf books.
**Narration:** "The king was cured, but the question was not settled. Whose teaching was true? The Jain teachers proposed a test, before the king and all his court. Let each side give its book to the fire."
**Sound:** a murmuring crowd; a single drum.

### 2. A leaf from the hymns (20 sec)
**Visual:** A fire is lit in the middle of the hall. Sambandar opens his own bundle of palm leaves, the hymns he has sung, and draws out one leaf. He holds it up, bows, and drops it into the flames.
**Narration:** "A great fire was lit in the hall. Sambandar untied his bundle of hymns, drew out one leaf, praised the Lord, and laid it in the flames."
**Sound:** the roar of fire; a held breath.

### 3. Green in the fire (25 sec)
**Visual:** The flames lick around the leaf. It does not curl or blacken; it stays fresh and green, glowing. The Jain teachers put in their leaf; it catches and turns to ash. They step back, their brushes lowered.
**Narration:** "The fire blazed around it. But the leaf did not burn. It stayed green and fresh, as if it had just been cut. The Jains put their own leaf in the fire, and it burned to ash, like cotton."
**Sound:** fire crackling, then a soft, clear tone for the green leaf.

### 4. Out of the fire (15 sec)
**Visual:** When the time is over, Sambandar lifts out his leaf. It shines, greener than before. The court gasps.
**Narration:** "When the time was up, Sambandar took his leaf out of the fire. It was greener and fresher than before. Everyone in the hall was amazed, even the king.
**Sound:** a wave of astonished voices.

### 5. The river in flood (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Vaigai river, wide and brown with flood, rushing past the city. Crowds line the banks. The Jain teachers, still unbowed, point at the water.
**Narration:** "But the Jains asked for one more test. Let each side write its truth on a leaf and put it into the Vaigai river. The leaf that goes against the current wins."
**Sound:** the roar of floodwater.

### 6. Against the current (25 sec)
**Visual:** Sambandar writes a hymn on a leaf, "Vazhga Andhanar", "Let the Brahmins thrive", and sets it on the water. The Jains' leaf is swept away downstream and vanishes. Sambandar's leaf turns, and moves upstream, cutting through the flood.
**Narration:** "Sambandar wrote a new hymn on a leaf, a blessing on the whole world, and set it on the water. The Jains' leaf was swept away. But his leaf turned, and swam upstream, against the flood."
**Sound:** rushing water, then a bright rising melody against it.

### 7. The king stands straight (20 sec)
**Visual:** On the bank, the king watches. His bent back slowly straightens; he rises to his full height, amazed. The queen covers her mouth with joy.
**Narration:** "The hymn on that leaf blessed the king too. And as the leaf went against the river, the Pandya king, whose back had been bent, stood up straight."
**Dialogue:** The queen: "Look! The king stands straight!"
**Sound:** a deep, warm chord.

### 8. The leaf comes to rest (20 sec)
**Visual:** The minister Kulachchirai gallops along the bank on a swift horse, following the leaf. Far upstream, by a small temple at Thiruvedagam, the leaf stops in the water. Sambandar, far behind, is singing. The minister wades in and lifts the leaf, bowing.
**Narration:** "The minister rode after it on a fast horse. Sambandar sang again, and the leaf came to rest at the temple of Thiruvedagam. There the minister lifted it from the water, and held it high."
**Sound:** galloping hooves, then quiet water and a temple bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

In about ten songs Arunagiri recalls the leaf that stayed green in the fire and went up the Vaigai against the flood. Songs to show: 104 and 149 (Palani), 358 (Thiruvanaikka), 542 (Thirukkazhukundram), 920 (Poovalur).

## Production notes

- **A test, not a battle.** Keep the Jains' side respectful: they lose a test, they are not mocked.
- **Link with `sam-jains`,** which is about what followed.
