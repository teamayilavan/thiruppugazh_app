# Three Saints and the Doors of Vedaranyam

`sam-three-saints` | Sambandar | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: friendly, a little playful, wondrous | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

At Vedaranyam, the temple doors have been shut for ages; Appar sings them open, and then the child Sambandar sings them shut again.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Sambandar meets Appar at Sirkazhi and Tiruvarur and travels with him | Periya Puranam, Sambandar Puranam, vv. 274, 576-592 |
| At Vedaranyam (Thirumaraikkadu), Appar's hymn opens the temple doors and Sambandar's closes them | vv. 582, 587 checked; 597-609 (Maraikkadu) |
| The songs only name the places or the three saints together; they do not narrate the doors | Thiruppugazh songs (3 songs) |

**Added for the screen:** the visuals of the doors, the narration. Sundarar, the third of the three great saints, lived later than Appar and Sambandar, so he does not appear in the film; he is named only on the end card, as the songs name the three together.

## Characters

- **Appar:** an old saint, thin, with a staff, deeply gentle.
- **Sambandar:** a young saint, quick and merry.
- **The temple priests.**
- **Villagers.**

## Places

Sirkazhi; Tiruvarur; Vedaranyam, with its great shut doors.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; friendly and a little playful, wondering at the doors.

### 1. An old saint comes (20 sec)
**Visual:** A road into Sirkazhi. An old ascetic walks toward the town: thin, bare-chested, holy ash on his body, a small hoe for weeding temple courtyards in his hand, tears of devotion in his eyes. This is Appar, called Thirunavukkarasar, the King of Speech.
**Narration:** "One day an old saint came walking into Sirkazhi. His name was Thirunavukkarasar, the King of Speech. He carried a little hoe, for he spent his life clearing the weeds from temple courtyards. He had come to see the child saint."
**Sound:** footsteps; a slow, kind melody.

### 2. Father and son (20 sec)
**Visual:** The young Sambandar hurries out to meet him. The old man bows to the child; the child bows to the old man. They laugh, take each other's hands, and walk into the temple together.
**Narration:** "Sambandar ran out to meet him. The old man bowed to the child, and the child bowed to the old man. The boy called him Appar, which means father. And from that day they were friends."
**Sound:** a warm, happy chord.

### 3. Two pilgrims (20 sec)
**Visual:** A montage: the old man walking and the boy in his pearl palanquin, side by side along the roads of the Chola country, singing at temple after temple. The child steps down from his palanquin to walk beside his friend.
**Narration:** "They travelled together, from temple to temple. The old saint walked, and the boy rode in his pearl palanquin. But whenever they met, the child stepped down to greet his friend."
**Sound:** two voices, one old, one young, humming in harmony.

### 4. The shut doors (20 sec)
**Visual:** Vedaranyam, Thirumaraikkadu, by the sea. A great temple with huge golden doors, firmly shut, dusty with age. Worshippers file in through a small side door instead.
**Narration:** "At Vedaranyam, the Forest of the Vedas, they found the great doors of the temple shut. The Vedas themselves had worshipped there long ago and closed them. No one since had been able to open them. So the people went in by a little side door."
**Sound:** sea wind; a deep, closed silence.

### 5. Appar sings (25 sec)
**Visual:** The child turns to the old man and asks. Appar stands before the doors and sings. Verse after verse; the doors do not move. His face falls. He sings the last verse with all his heart, pleading, and slowly the great doors swing open.
**Dialogue:** Sambandar: "Appar, sing, so that we can go in by the front doors and worship."
**Narration:** "So Appar sang. He sang verse after verse, but the doors did not move. The old saint grew sad. Then he sang his last verse, begging the Lord, and the great doors opened wide."
**Sound:** a single old voice; a long creak; a burst of joyful music.

### 6. Inside (20 sec)
**Visual:** The crowd cheers. The two saints walk in through the open doors, side by side. Inside, they fall down before the Lord, weeping with joy, and sing.
**Narration:** "Everyone cried out with joy. The two saints went in together, by the front doors, and fell down before the Lord of Vedaranyam. They wept, and sang, and could not stop singing."
**Sound:** drums, conches, voices.

### 7. Sambandar sings (25 sec)
**Visual:** Outside again. Appar turns to the child with a smile and gestures to the doors. Sambandar sings; the great doors swing gently and close. Flowers fall from the sky.
**Dialogue:** Appar: "Now you sing, so that the doors may close again, and open and close for everyone from now on."
**Narration:** "Appar asked the child to sing, so that the doors might close, and from then on open and close as doors should. Sambandar sang, and the doors closed, softly, and flowers fell from the sky."
**Sound:** a young voice; a soft, satisfied thud; bells.

### 8. Two kinds of grace (15 sec)
**Visual:** The two saints bow to each other in front of the closed doors, then walk on together along the seashore at sunset, the old man and the boy.
**Narration:** "One hymn opened the doors, and one hymn closed them. Two friends, two kinds of grace. And from that day the doors of Vedaranyam opened for everyone."
**Sound:** waves; the friendly melody returns.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri names the three great singers of the Tevaram, Sambandar, Appar and Sundarar, together, and lists Maraikkadu (Vedaranyam) among the shrines Sambandar sang. Songs to show: 121 (Palani), 343 (Kanchipuram), 1166 (general songs).

## Production notes

- **No rivalry.** The story's spirit is two friends, and two kinds of grace.
- **Reason for the closing checked (v. 586):** Appar asks Sambandar to sing so that the doors may both open and close from then on. Scene 7 follows the verse.
