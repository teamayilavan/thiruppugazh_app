# The Lord Who Knocked at Night (Shiva as Sundarar's Messenger)

`n-sundarar-messenger` | Saints | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: tender, humorous, a quiet miracle | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Sundarar's first wife Paravai is hurt and angry when he marries again, and she will not see him; at his prayer, Shiva himself goes to her door at night as his messenger.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| After Sundarar married Sangili at Tiruvorriyur, breaking a vow, his first wife Paravai at Tiruvarur is sunk in sulking anger | Periya Puranam, vv. 314-323 |
| At Sundarar's prayer Shiva himself goes to her door at night as his messenger | Eyarkon Kalikkama Puranam, vv. 331-340 |
| The Puranam says he went again later | same |
| Songs 134, 940, 1298 and 1317 recall it | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** Paravai's expressions, the dialogue at the door (the research gives the event, not the words), the narration.

## Characters

- **Sundarar:** a handsome saint, ashamed and loving.
- **Paravai:** a proud woman of Tiruvarur, wounded and fierce.
- **Shiva:** in the guise of a gentle messenger, tall, calm.
- **Sangili:** at Tiruvorriyur (a brief shot).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; tender, a little humorous, and quietly full of wonder whenever the Lord walks the street.

### 1. A second marriage (20 sec)
**Visual:** Tiruvorriyur by the sea. Sundarar, the young saint in white, stands beside a gentle young woman, Sangili; wedding garlands. Then the camera travels far south to Tiruvarur, where a messenger whispers at the door of a great house.
**Narration:** "The saint Sundarar was married to Paravai of Tiruvarur. But at Tiruvorriyur, far to the north, he married a second wife, Sangili. And the news travelled to Tiruvarur before him."
**Sound:** wedding music, then a sudden stop; waves.

### 2. Paravai's anger (25 sec)
**Visual:** Paravai, proud and beautiful, hears the news. She stiffens, turns away, and goes inside. Later, some of Sundarar's friends come to her door to plead for him; she sends them away, and the great door closes.
**Narration:** "Paravai heard it and was deeply hurt. When Sundarar came home to Tiruvarur and sent friends to speak for him, she would not hear them. She shut her door, and her anger would not leave her."
**Dialogue:** Paravai: "Let him stay with the one who has bound him at Tiruvorriyur."
**Sound:** a heavy door; then silence.

### 3. The prayer (20 sec)
**Visual:** Night. Sundarar lies on the floor of a room, sleepless and miserable. Then he sits up and folds his hands. In the dark, a light gathers.
**Narration:** "Sundarar could not sleep. In the middle of the night he turned to the only one who could help him, the Lord who had once made him his own servant and friend."
**Dialogue:** Sundarar: "My Lord, you are my mother and my friend. Go to her tonight, and end her anger."
**Sound:** night crickets; a soft, pleading melody.

### 4. The Lord goes (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Lord stands before Sundarar, smiling. Then he walks out into the moonlit streets of Tiruvarur. Behind him, hidden from mortal eyes, follow his hosts, sages and gods; flowers fall on the empty street.
**Narration:** "And the Lord himself agreed to be his messenger. He who is beyond the sight of Brahma and Vishnu walked through the streets of Tiruvarur at midnight, to a woman's door, for the sake of his friend."
**Sound:** soft anklet bells on the Lord's feet; a whisper of music.

### 5. The knock (25 sec)
**Visual:** At Paravai's door the Lord takes the form of a temple priest, an old brahmin with the sacred thread. He knocks and calls. Inside, Paravai, awake, rises in alarm and opens the door.
**Narration:** "At her door he took the form of the brahmin priest who served the Lord in the temple, and called her softly to open. Paravai was awake. She wondered why the priest had come at midnight, and opened the door."
**Dialogue:** The Lord: "Lady, open the door."
**Sound:** three knocks; the creak of the door.

### 6. The message (25 sec)
**Visual:** The priest asks her to let Sundarar come back. She listens, with folded hands but a firm face, then shakes her head politely. The Lord smiles to himself and goes away. Back home, Sundarar hears the news and falls to the floor in despair.
**Narration:** "He asked her to forgive Sundarar and let him come home. Paravai said politely that it was not proper for a holy priest to carry such a message, and refused. The Lord only smiled, and went back. When Sundarar heard, he wept."
**Sound:** a single sad note; then the pleading melody again.

### 7. The second visit (20 sec)
**Visual:** Paravai, alone, realises who the priest must have been. Then the Lord comes again, in his own form, with the moon in his matted hair and all his hosts around him, and the house shines like Kailasa. She falls at his feet.
**Narration:** "Then Paravai understood that the priest had been the Lord, and she was afraid. And the Lord came again, this time in his own form, with all his hosts. She fell at his feet, and he asked her once more."
**Sound:** a great swell of music; bells; a conch.

### 8. Reconciliation (10 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn. Sundarar arrives at the open door; Paravai stands there, gentle now. Above them both, the Lord's light fades away with a smile.
**Narration:** "This time she said yes. The Lord had carried a message, twice, for his friend."
**Sound:** the first, gentle melody; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Four songs recall the night Shiva went as Sundarar's messenger to Paravai's door. Show songs 134 (Palani), 940 (Pattaliyur), 1298 and 1317 (Pazhamudircholai).

## Production notes

- **Treat Paravai with dignity.** Her anger is just; the film is not against her.
- **The Lord's humility** (serving as a messenger) is the heart.
- **Check scenes 6 and 7** against verses 331-340 and the later passage before animation.
