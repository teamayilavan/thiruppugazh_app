# Gold for Paravai (Sundarar's Gold)

`n-sundarar-gold` | Saints | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: wry, tired, then relieved | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

Sundarar walks from shrine to shrine to earn gold for his wife Paravai and is worn out; in another telling, hunters who are Shiva's own hosts take away the gold he has been given.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Song 947's commentary: Sundarar went to many Shaivite shrines to earn gold for Paravai, and was exhausted by the walking | Thiruppugazh song 947 |
| The Puranam tells a different gold episode: Shiva's hosts, as hunters, take Sundarar's gold at Tirumurugan Poondi | Periya Puranam, vv. 164-172 |
| The song's version was not found in the text | research |

**Honesty note:** scenes 1 to 4 follow the song's commentary (not found in the Puranam). Scenes 5 to 8 follow the Puranam. The film says so in its narration.
**Added for the screen:** the walking montage and the comic touches, the narration.

## Characters

- **Sundarar:** a handsome saint, weary and wry.
- **Paravai:** his wife at Tiruvarur, waiting.
- **Shiva's hosts:** a band of hunters, jolly and fierce.
- **The Lord** (a voice).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; wry and tired in the first half, bright and amused in the second. The film says plainly which telling is the song's and which is the Puranam's.

### 1. A debt of love (20 sec)
**Visual:** Tiruvarur at dusk. In a fine house a lamp is lit. Paravai, Sundarar's wife, stands at the door, gracious and expectant. Sundarar, the young saint in white, looks at the empty chest in the corner and scratches his head.
**Narration:** "There are two stories of Sundarar and gold. The first comes from a Thiruppugazh song. Sundarar loved his wife Paravai in Tiruvarur, and he wanted to shower her with a thousand pieces of gold."
**Sound:** a gentle veena; a soft comic pluck as he looks at the chest.

### 2. The road (25 sec)
**Visual:** A montage of temple towers, one after another. At each, Sundarar sings with his hands keeping the beat; at each he receives a little gold and walks on. The road gets longer, the sun hotter, his bag only a little heavier.
**Narration:** "So, the song says, he went from shrine to shrine of the Lord, singing his hymns at each one, keeping the beat with his hands, with a determined heart. At each place the Lord gave him a little gold, and he walked on."
**Sound:** a hymn, a few bars at each temple; footsteps; cicadas.

### 3. The weariness (20 sec)
**Visual:** Midday. Sundarar sits under a tree by the road, fanning himself with a leaf, rubbing his sore feet. The small bag of gold sits beside him. He looks at it and laughs at himself.
**Narration:** "The commentary on the song says he grew exhausted from all the walking in the burning heat. Even a saint has feet, and even a saint's feet get sore."
**Sound:** heat haze hum; a tired sigh; a little laugh.

### 4. The song says (15 sec)
**Visual:** The scene turns to a palm leaf. A line of the Thiruppugazh glows on it. Arunagiri, in a small inset, smiles as he writes.
**Narration:** "That is how Arunagiri remembers it in his song. The Periya Puranam does not tell this version. It tells another story of Sundarar's gold."
**Sound:** a single bell; a turning page.

### 5. The Puranam's tale (20 sec)
**Visual:** A new journey. Sundarar travels home from the Chera country with porters carrying chests of gifts, through the dry, rocky forests of the Kongu country. Shadows move among the rocks.
**Narration:** "In the Puranam, Sundarar was going home from the Chera country, carrying rich gifts. His road passed through a wild forest near Tirumurugan Poondi."
**Sound:** porters' footsteps; the creak of carrying poles; a twig snaps.

### 6. The hunters (25 sec)
**Visual:** A band of hunters with bows springs out from the rocks, laughing and whooping. The porters drop everything and flee. The hunters seize every chest and bundle and vanish into the trees, still laughing. Sundarar is left standing on an empty road.
**Narration:** "Suddenly a band of hunters leapt out of the rocks. They took everything, every chest and bundle, and disappeared. But these were no ordinary hunters. They were the Lord's own attendants, and the Lord himself had sent them."
**Sound:** whoops; running feet; a comic drumroll; then silence.

### 7. The cry (25 sec)
**Visual:** Sundarar walks into the temple at Tirumurugan Poondi, falls before the Lord, and sings, half in tears and half in reproach, waving a hand toward the forest.
**Narration:** "Sundarar went into the temple of Tirumurugan Poondi, and sang to the Lord. He asked: why do you live here, in this wild place, where hunters rob the travellers on the road?"
**Dialogue:** Sundarar: "Lord, why do you stay here, in a place where hunters rob your devotees?"
**Sound:** his voice singing; a gentle drum.

### 8. The Lord's play (15 sec)
**Visual:** Outside, at the temple gate, all the stolen chests and bundles lie piled high, almost to the sky. Sundarar comes out, stops, and bursts into laughter. He bows to the temple tower.
**Narration:** "When he came out, all his treasure lay heaped at the temple gate. The Lord had only been playing with his friend, for the sake of a song."
**Sound:** a bright, playful melody; temple bells.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 947 (Thirupukkoliyur) recalls Sundarar walking from shrine to shrine to earn gold for Paravai, until he was worn out by the heat. Show song 947.

## Production notes

- **Dignity with humour.** The Lord's hosts are comedy, never harm.
- **Say which version is which** in the voice-over.
