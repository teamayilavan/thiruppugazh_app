# The Poet in the Cave (Nakkeerar and the Tirumurugatruppadai)

`n-nakkeerar` | Saints | running time 2:40 (8 scenes) | tone: dread, then a hymn that opens a cave | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The poet Nakkeerar loses his concentration at Thiruparankundram and is seized by a demon and shut in a cave with many other prisoners; he sings the Tirumurugatruppadai, and Murugan opens the cave.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Nakkeerar loses his concentration at Thiruparankundram, is seized by a demon and shut in a cave with many other prisoners | Tradition (the songs; 9 songs) |
| He sings the Tirumurugatruppadai | Tradition |
| Murugan opens the cave and frees the prisoners | Tradition |
| The quarrel between Nakkeerar and Shiva over the Pandya's poem is told in the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam padalams 52-54 | TVP 52-54 |

**Honesty note:** the cave story is **not** in the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam padalams checked. It is tradition and the songs. The film says so in its narration.
**Added for the screen:** every visual; the narration.

## Characters

- **Nakkeerar:** a Sangam poet, proud and sharp, in white.
- **The demon:** a shadowy figure, not a monster, an echo in the cave.
- **The other prisoners:** weary captives.
- **Murugan:** a radiant young god with a spear.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; still at the pool, shadowed in the cave, rising with the hymn, bright at the end. The film says plainly that this story comes from the songs and tradition.

### 1. The poet (20 sec)
**Visual:** The rock of Thiruparankundram near Madurai, at dawn. Below it a still pool and a tree. An old poet in white, Nakkeerar, sits by the water with his eyes closed, at his prayers.
**Narration:** "This story is told by tradition and by the songs of Arunagiri. Nakkeerar was a great poet of Madurai, so bold that he once argued with the Lord himself. One morning he sat by a pool at Thiruparankundram, at his prayers."
**Sound:** water; a few birds; a temple bell far away.

### 2. The lapse (20 sec)
**Visual:** A leaf falls from the tree. Half of it touches the water and half lies on the bank. The half in the water becomes a fish, the half on land a bird; the two pull against each other. The poet's eyes open. He watches, his prayer forgotten.
**Narration:** "Tradition tells that a leaf fell from the tree, half into the water and half on the bank. One half became a fish, and the other half a bird. The poet opened his eyes to watch, and his prayer was broken."
**Sound:** a strange, light flutter; the bell stops.

### 3. The seizure (20 sec)
**Visual:** A shadow falls over the pool: a tall dark shape with the long head of a horse, seen only as a silhouette. It closes over the poet, and both are gone. The pool is still again.
**Narration:** "At that moment a spirit seized him. The songs describe her as a treacherous spirit with the face of a horse. She had been waiting for anyone whose mind wandered from prayer."
**Sound:** a gust of wind; silence.

### 4. The cave (25 sec)
**Visual:** Inside a round cave in the hill, lit by a crack of grey light. Many men sit along the walls, thin and silent, poets and travellers. A great stone blocks the mouth. Nakkeerar is set down among them. He looks at their faces and understands.
**Narration:** "He woke in a round cave inside the hill. Many others were already there, all of them prisoners who had broken their prayers. The cave was shut with a stone, and none of them could see a way out."
**Sound:** dripping water; a low, hollow drone.

### 5. A vow (20 sec)
**Visual:** The other prisoners weep. Nakkeerar sits up straight, washes his face from a trickle in the rock, and quietly does his daily rites as though he were at home. Then he folds his hands and turns his face to the hill above them.
**Narration:** "The poet did not despair. The songs say that even in the cave he kept his daily worship. Then he turned to the Lord of that hill, Murugan, and began to sing."
**Dialogue:** Nakkeerar: "Lord of the hill, hear a song from the dark."
**Sound:** a single voice beginning to chant.

### 6. The hymn (25 sec)
**Visual:** As he sings, lines of verse rise from his lips as golden light and travel along the walls. In the light appear the places of Murugan in turn: Thiruparankundram, the shore of Tiruchendur, the hills where he dances, the grove of Pazhamudircholai. The prisoners lift their heads.
**Narration:** "He sang the Tirumurugatruppadai, the Guide to Murugan. In it he tells the seeker where Murugan may be found: on the hill of Thiruparankundram, on the shore at Tiruchendur, on every hill where he dances, and in the grove of Pazhamudircholai."
**Sound:** the chant swells; drums join it softly.

### 7. The opening (20 sec)
**Visual:** The crack of light widens. Outside, a young god with a shining spear stands at the cave mouth. He lays his hand on the stone; it rolls aside. Light pours into the cave. The horse-faced shadow vanishes.
**Narration:** "Murugan heard him. The songs say he had been listening, hidden, to the poet's rich Tamil. Now he came to the cave, and with his own hands he opened the door, and the spirit fled."
**Sound:** a great bell; the stone rolling; a rush of wind.

### 8. The prisoners go free (10 sec)
**Visual:** The prisoners walk out blinking into the dawn, the poet last. Nakkeerar turns and bows to the young god with the spear. Final image: the hill at sunrise.
**Narration:** "Every prisoner walked out free. And the poet's song is still sung today."
**Sound:** the hymn's melody returns, quiet.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Nine songs recall the poet in the cave and the hymn that opened it. Show songs 92 (Tiruchendur), 104 (Palani), 726 (Siruvai) and 1192, and name 431 (Thiruvannamalai), 650 (Thirukkonamalai), 681 (Thiruverkadu), 1008 and 1279.

## Production notes

- **Say it is tradition.** The film's voice-over opens with "The songs tell that...".
- **No harm to the poet on screen.** The demon is a shadow, not a monster.
