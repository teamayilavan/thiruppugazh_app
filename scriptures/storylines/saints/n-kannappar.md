# Kannappar, the Hunter Who Gave His Eyes

`n-kannappar` | Saints | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: rough, loving, then intense and tender | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A hunter who knows no ritual worships the Shiva lingam in the forest his own way, with water carried in his mouth and meat he has tasted himself; when the lingam's eye bleeds, he gives his own eyes.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The hunter bathes the Kalahasti lingam with water carried in his mouth, clears the old flowers with his sandalled foot, and offers meat he first tasted himself to be sure it was good | Periya Puranam, Kannappar Puranam, vv. 118-123 |
| When the lingam's eye bleeds, he gives his own eyes | vv. 173-176 |
| Songs 510, 664 and 1211 recall him | Thiruppugazh songs |

**Added for the screen:** the forest setting, the priest who comes to worship by the book (the research does not give this scene's details), the stopping of the hunter's hand at the end, and the narration. The verses read give the hunter's acts and the giving of the eyes; the Lord's words are not quoted here.

## Characters

- **Kannappar:** a young hunter, strong and simple, with bow and a bag of meat.
- **The priest** (tradition): a Brahmin who worships with ritual.
- **Shiva** (a voice and a light).

## Places

The forest hill of Kalahasti; a lingam under a tree.

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; rough and loving with the hunter, hushed and intense at the eyes, tender at the end.

### 1. The hill (20 sec)
**Visual:** Dawn over the forest hill of Kalahasti. A young hunter, Thinnan, climbs the slope with his bow, his companions behind him. On the summit, under the trees, he sees a lingam. He stops. His bow slips down. His face fills with a joy he cannot explain.
**Narration:** "Thinnan was a hunter, the son of a hunter chief. One day, on the hill of Kalahasti, he came upon the Lord in the form of a lingam. And from that moment, he could not leave him."
**Sound:** forest birds; then a single held note as he sees the lingam.

### 2. The love (25 sec)
**Visual:** He runs down to the river and back, his mouth full of water, flowers tucked in his hair, his bow and meat in his hands. On the summit he brushes the old flowers from the lingam with his sandalled foot, pours the water from his mouth over it, and lays his flowers there.
**Narration:** "He knew no rites and no prayers. He carried water from the river in his own mouth to bathe the Lord. He brushed away the old flowers with his sandalled foot. The Lord's head was decked with the flowers from his own hair."
**Sound:** water; a rough, happy hum.

### 3. The offering (25 sec)
**Visual:** By a fire in the forest he roasts meat on a stick, tastes each piece, and keeps only the best on a leaf plate, mixed with honey. His companions watch, shaking their heads; at last they leave him. He sets the leaf plate before the lingam.
**Narration:** "He hunted and cooked the best meat, and tasted each piece first, to be sure it was good enough. His friends thought he had lost his mind, and went home. He did not notice. He sat before the Lord and offered the meat with love."
**Dialogue:** Thinnan: "This is better than yesterday. I tasted it myself. Eat, my Lord."
**Sound:** crackling fire; birds settling for the night.

### 4. The priest (20 sec)
**Visual:** Morning. A temple priest, Sivagochariyar, climbs the hill, sees meat and scattered flowers by the lingam, and is grieved. He cleans it with care and worships by the book. That night the Lord comes to him in a dream. Next day the priest hides behind a tree.
**Narration:** "Each morning the priest found the shrine in disorder, and grieved. Then the Lord came to him in a dream: do not think of him as a rough hunter. Hide, and see what his love is."
**Sound:** chanting; then a hush of night.

### 5. The test (20 sec)
**Visual:** The sixth day. Thinnan comes running up the hill with his offerings. He stops: from one eye of the lingam a red glow flows. The water falls from his mouth; the meat drops from his hand. He falls, rises, and wipes the eye, but the glow will not stop.
**Narration:** "On the sixth day the Lord showed the priest the hunter's love. As Thinnan came near, he saw blood flowing from the Lord's right eye. He dropped everything and ran to him."
**Sound:** the music breaks off; a low drone.

### 6. The first eye (25 sec)
**Visual:** He searches the forest for herbs, squeezes their juice on the eye. The glow does not stop. He stands still, then remembers a hunter's saying. He kneels with an arrow. The screen goes to soft white light; when it returns, the red glow has stopped, and Thinnan leaps and dances for joy.
**Narration:** "He brought healing herbs. Nothing helped. Then he remembered what hunters say: flesh heals flesh. So he took out his own eye with an arrow, and set it in the place of the Lord's eye. The bleeding stopped, and he danced for joy."
**Sound:** silence, a single drumbeat, then a burst of joyful drums.

### 7. The second eye (20 sec)
**Visual:** The left eye of the lingam begins to glow red. Thinnan does not hesitate. He sets his foot on the lingam's eye to mark the place, since he will soon be blind, and lifts the arrow to his other eye. A hand of light rises from the lingam and holds his hand.
**Narration:** "Then the other eye began to bleed. He was not afraid. He set his foot there to mark the place, for he would soon see nothing, and lifted the arrow again. But a hand rose out of the lingam and held his."
**Dialogue:** The Lord: "Stop, Kannappa. Stop, Kannappa. Stop, Kannappa."
**Sound:** the voice, deep and gentle, three times.

### 8. The Lord (10 sec)
**Visual:** Light fills the hill; flowers fall from the sky. The priest kneels behind his tree. The Lord holds the hunter's hand.
**Narration:** "The Lord held his hand and said: stay always at my right side. He is Kannappar now, the one who gave his eye."
**Sound:** a soft chorus; flowers falling.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Arunagiri recalls the hunter who gave his eye in three songs: the meat he tasted first, the water from his mouth, and the learned priest he taught. Show songs 510 (Chidambaram), 664 (Vellikaram) and 1211.

## Production notes

- **No gore.** The "eye" is shown as a light, the bleeding as red glow.
- **The hunter's love is the point.** Keep the priest respectful: he is the other kind of devotion.
- **Verify the last scene** (the hand that stops him) against the Puranam, which is beyond the verses checked.
