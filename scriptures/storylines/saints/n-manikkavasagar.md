# The Minister, the Guru and the Horses (Manikkavasagar at Perunthurai)

`n-manikkavasagar` | Saints | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: purposeful, then surrendered, then comic | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

A Pandya minister sent to buy horses meets Shiva as a guru under a kurundam tree and gives all the money to build a temple; the horses that arrive later are foxes turned into horses.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| The Pandya king sends the minister Vathavurar to buy horses | Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam (Manikkavasagar story); Shiva set, `saints-kurundam` |
| At Tiruperunthurai, under the kurundam tree, he meets Shiva as a guru and gives the money to build the temple | same |
| The horses come later as foxes turned horses | same |
| Song 512 names the story | Thiruppugazh song 512 |

**Tradition note:** this story has a fuller telling in the Shiva set's `saints-kurundam`. Beats here follow that file and the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam; the details of the foxes turning back to foxes at night are the tradition, not read verse by verse here.
**Added for the screen:** every visual; the narration.

## Characters

- **Vathavurar (Manikkavasagar):** a young minister, intelligent, in rich robes, then humble.
- **The guru:** Shiva in the guise of a teacher, calm and radiant, under the tree.
- **The Pandya king:** stern, impatient.
- **Foxes and horses.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; purposeful at first, hushed under the tree, tense at the court, smiling at the end.

### 1. The commission (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Pandya court at Madurai. The army's generals tell the king, Arimarthana Pandyan, that the cavalry is weak. The king turns to his chief minister, Vathavurar, and hands him the key of the treasury.
**Narration:** "In Madurai, the Pandya king needed horses for his army. He trusted one man above all, his chief minister, Vathavurar of Thiruvathavur. He gave him the key of the treasury, and sent him to buy horses."
**Dialogue:** The king: "Take what gold you need. Bring me the finest horses from the coast."
**Sound:** court drums; a heavy key turning.

### 2. The road (15 sec)
**Visual:** A caravan with chests of gold sets out across the dry plains toward the sea. The minister rides in front, rich robes, a thoughtful face; his eyes keep turning to the temple towers on the way.
**Narration:** "He set out for Perunthurai, a port on the coast where foreign horses landed. But his heart had always longed for the Lord."
**Sound:** hoofbeats; caravan bells.

### 3. The tree (20 sec)
**Visual:** At Perunthurai, under a kurundam tree, a teacher sits in silence, his disciples around him. The minister stops. He climbs down from his horse without knowing why, and walks to the tree.
**Narration:** "At Perunthurai, under a kurundam tree, sat a silent teacher with his disciples. The minister saw him, and forgot the horses."
**Sound:** the caravan sounds fade; leaves in the wind.

### 4. The teaching (20 sec)
**Visual:** The minister kneels. The teacher places his foot on the minister's head. Light passes through him; his rich robes seem to fall away into simple white. He weeps. When he looks up, the teacher is gone. He searches around the tree like a man who has lost his heart.
**Narration:** "He bowed, and the teacher placed his feet on his head and taught him the truth. The darkness left his mind. The teacher was the Lord himself. Then he vanished, and the minister searched for him everywhere."
**Sound:** a single deep bell; a long held note.

### 5. The gold (25 sec)
**Visual:** The chests are opened. The gold becomes stone and sculpture: walls, pillars and a shrine rise around the kurundam tree. Craftsmen sing as they work. A servant, anxious, reminds the minister of the king's order.
**Narration:** "He spent the king's gold on the Lord's temple at Perunthurai, and kept no account. When the time came to return, he prayed for help, and a voice told him: the horses will come. Go back to Madurai."
**Dialogue:** The Lord's voice: "The horses will come. Do not worry. Go to Madurai."
**Sound:** chisels on stone, singing; then a quiet voice.

### 6. The king's anger (20 sec)
**Visual:** Madurai. The minister promises horses; the days pass; the stables stay empty. The king's face darkens. The minister is led away; we see only his shadow against a wall, hands folded, calm, his lips moving in prayer.
**Narration:** "He promised the king the horses. The days went by, and none came. The angry king had him punished. But the minister only held to the Lord's feet."
**Sound:** a heavy drum; then quiet prayer.

### 7. The horses arrive (25 sec)
**Visual:** Evening. Out of the forest come splendid horses, led by riders, and at their head a horseman of strange majesty. The king rises from his throne without meaning to. The horseman hands him a horse's rope as a sign of the sale. The king gives him a fine silk cloth. The minister is set free.
**Narration:** "Then, one evening, the horses came: hundreds of them, led by a horseman of strange beauty. The Lord had turned the foxes of the forest into horses, and his own attendants into riders, and he rode at their head. The king rose to greet him, and gave him a silk cloth."
**Sound:** thundering hooves; trumpets; cheering.

### 8. Foxes (15 sec)
**Visual:** Night in the royal stables. The horses shimmer, shrink, and become foxes. They slip their ropes and run off into the dark, a stream of tails under the moon. A groom drops his lamp in astonishment.
**Narration:** "But that night the horses turned back into foxes, and ran away into the forest. The Lord's play was not yet over."
**Sound:** a fox's yip; a little comic flute; the music settles.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 512 (Chidambaram) recalls how the Lord met Manikkavasagar at Perunthurai, when he came to buy horses for the king, and set his feet on his head as his teacher. Show song 512.

## Production notes

- **Comedy, but with dignity.** The minister is not a fool: he was given a gift.
- **Pair with the Shiva set's `saints-kurundam`,** which tells the same saint's life.
