# Vandi's Pittu (The Blow Felt by All)

`n-vandi-pittu` | Saints | running time 2:45 (8 scenes) | tone: homely, then thrilling, then moving | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

When the Vaigai floods, every household owes a share of the embankment; old Vandi has no one, so Shiva comes as a labourer, eats her pittu as wages, sleeps on the job, and is struck with a cane by the king, and the blow is felt by every creature.

**Honesty note:** the research file summarises the story; it is not read verse by verse here. The ending (the heavenly voice and Vandi's rise) is the tradition's, to be checked against the padalam.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals, the narration.

## Characters

- **Vandi:** an old woman who sells pittu, a sweet dish of rice flour, alone and poor.
- **Shiva:** a young labourer, with a basket, humble and smiling.
- **The Pandya king:** an imperious ruler with a cane.
- **The villagers.**

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; homely and tender with Vandi, playful with the labourer, hushed and awed at the blow.

### 1. The flood (20 sec)
**Visual:** Madurai under a grey sky. The river Vaigai, swollen and brown, roars past its banks. Drums in the streets: royal officers mark out the riverbank and give each household its stretch to build up with earth. People run with baskets and spades.
**Narration:** "Once the river Vaigai rose in a great flood and broke its banks at Madurai. The Pandya king gave an order: every household must send someone to raise its share of the riverbank."
**Sound:** rushing water; a royal drum; shouting voices.

### 2. Vandi (20 sec)
**Visual:** A tiny house by the road. An old woman, Vandi, steams pittu, sweet rice flour, in a clay pot and sells it to passers-by for a few coins. An officer points to her stretch of the bank. She has no one to send. She folds her hands toward the temple of the Lord, Somasundarar.
**Narration:** "Old Vandi lived alone. She had no husband and no son. She earned her living by selling pittu. Who would build her share of the bank? She prayed to the Lord of Madurai, for he was all she had."
**Dialogue:** Vandi: "Lord, everyone has someone. I have only you. Help me."
**Sound:** a soft flute; steam hissing from the pot.

### 3. The labourer (25 sec)
**Visual:** Down the road comes a handsome young labourer, a spade on his shoulder and a pad on his head for carrying baskets, calling out for work. He stops at Vandi's stall and smiles.
**Narration:** "Then a handsome young labourer came down the road, calling: does anyone need a worker? Vandi called him over. She had no money, she said, only pittu. He said the crumbled pittu would be wage enough."
**Dialogue:** The labourer: "Give me the broken pieces of your pittu. That is all I ask."
**Sound:** a cheerful whistle; birds.

### 4. The pittu (20 sec)
**Visual:** He sits down and eats with delight, licking his fingers. He asks for more. Vandi happily gives him all she has made. He picks up his basket and spade and strolls off toward the river, humming.
**Narration:** "But first, he said, he was tired: let him eat. He ate her pittu with delight, and said it was sweeter than the nectar of the gods. Vandi gave him all she had, and was glad."
**Sound:** a happy little tune; the clink of a spade.

### 5. The play (20 sec)
**Visual:** On the riverbank everyone works hard. The labourer carries a basket or two, then sings, dances, plays, and lies down in the shade to rest. Every other stretch of the bank is finished; Vandi's stretch alone still has a gap, and the water pours through it.
**Narration:** "At the river he did very little work. He sang and danced and played, and ate pittu, and rested in the shade. Soon every share of the bank was closed except Vandi's, and the water poured through the gap."
**Sound:** the work-song of the crowd; his lazy humming; rushing water.

### 6. The king (20 sec)
**Visual:** The Pandya king rides along the bank with a golden cane in his hand, pleased, until he sees the gap. His officers point to the young man. The king calls him over and questions him, frowning.
**Narration:** "The king came to inspect the bank, a golden cane in his hand. He saw the gap and asked whose share it was. Vandi's, they said, and her labourer has not done his work."
**Sound:** hoofbeats; a stern drum.

### 7. The blow (25 sec)
**Visual:** The king raises the cane and strikes the labourer once on the back. A flash of white light. The labourer tips his basket of earth into the gap; the bank closes; and he is gone. The light ripples out: the king flinches as if struck himself; his ministers, the workers, the birds, the fish in the river and even the paintings on the palace walls feel it.
**Narration:** "The king struck him once with the cane. At that moment the young man emptied his basket of earth into the gap, the bank was closed, and he vanished. And the blow fell on every living thing in the world. The king himself felt it on his own back."
**Sound:** a crack, then a great ringing silence.

### 8. The voice (15 sec)
**Visual:** A voice from the sky; the king bows, understanding at last. A shining heavenly chariot comes down to Vandi's little house. She steps into it with folded hands and rises, smiling.
**Narration:** "The labourer had been the Lord himself. A voice from heaven showed the king his wrong. And a heavenly chariot came down for old Vandi, and carried her to the Lord."
**Sound:** a conch; soft celestial voices; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Three songs recall the Lord who carried earth for Vandi's pittu and was struck by the king's cane. Show songs 960 (Madurai), 1317 (Pazhamudircholai) and 756 (Thirukkoodalaiyatrur).

## Production notes

- **A tender film.** Vandi's loneliness is the centre.
- **The cane scene** should be stylised: no pain to the labourer or anyone; a flash of light.
- **Verify the ending** against the Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam before animation.
