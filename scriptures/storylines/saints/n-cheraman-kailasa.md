# Two Friends Go to Kailasa (Sundarar and Cheraman Perumal)

`n-cheraman-kailasa` | Saints | running time 2:40 (7 scenes) | tone: friendship, wonder, ascent | **script level (second pass)**

## One-line story

The Tamil saint Sundarar and the Chera king Cheraman Perumal are close friends; when Sundarar is called to Mount Kailasa on a great elephant, the king rides his horse after him.

## What the story rests on

| Beat | Source |
|---|---|
| Cheraman Perumal (Kazharitrarivar) is a close friend of Sundarar | Periya Puranam, Kazharitrarivar Puranam, vv. 112-118, 173-175 |
| Sundarar goes up to Tirukkailai (Kailasa) on a great elephant; the Puranam then turns to Cheraman's act of riding his horse | PP v. 174 |
| Cheraman's own poem, the Tirukkailaya Gnana Ula (Tirumurai 11.8), is the poem the song calls the "Adi Ula" | Tirumurai 11.8 |
| Song 170's commentary says the king rode a horse to Kailasa in Sundarar's company | Thiruppugazh song 170 |

**Tradition note:** the Ula itself does not name Sundarar. The film follows the Puranam for the ascent and the song's commentary for the king's company.
**Added for the screen:** the visuals of the ascent, the music, the narration.

## Characters

- **Sundarar:** a handsome young saint, gentle, in a white garment, radiant.
- **Cheraman Perumal:** a king, strong, loyal, with a crown.
- **The white elephant:** a heavenly mount.
- **The king's horse.**
- **Shiva's attendants** (a bright escort).

## The script

Narration is one calm, warm voice, about two words a second; warm and easy with the friends, full of wonder in the sky, quiet at the end. Where the songs or tradition carry a detail, the narration says so.

### 1. Two friends (25 sec)
**Visual:** The Chera country by the western sea: green hills, coconut palms, a palace at Mahodai. In the court the king, Cheraman Perumal, rises from his throne and embraces a young saint in white, Sundarar. Later the two walk in a palace garden, talking and laughing, the king's hand on the saint's shoulder.
**Narration:** "Long ago the Chera king, Cheraman Perumal, ruled the hills by the western sea. He was a devotee of Shiva, and the dearest friend of the saint and singer Sundarar. The Periya Puranam says the two were bound like one life in two bodies."
**Sound:** a gentle veena; birds in the garden; soft laughter.

### 2. The call (25 sec)
**Visual:** Evening in the garden. A light opens in the sky above the palace. Shiva's attendants come down on the clouds, bright and smiling, leading a great white elephant hung with bells. They bow to Sundarar. The king steps back, astonished.
**Narration:** "Then the day came for Sundarar to go home to the Lord. The Puranam tells that he went up to Kailasa, the Lord's mountain, from the Chera country itself, riding on a great elephant."
**Sound:** the music stops; a high, clear tone; elephant bells.

### 3. The saint rises (20 sec)
**Visual:** Sundarar folds his hands to his friend, mounts the white elephant, and it rises slowly above the palace roofs, the palms, the sea. The king stands alone in the courtyard, looking up, his crown in his hand.
**Narration:** "Sundarar climbed onto the elephant, and it rose into the sky. The king stood below and watched his friend go. He had not been called."
**Sound:** a soft rush of wind; the bells grow faint.

### 4. The king decides (25 sec)
**Visual:** The king turns and runs to the stable. He leaps onto his horse, bends low, and speaks into its ear. The horse's eyes shine. It rears, and its hooves leave the ground.
**Narration:** "But Cheraman would not stay behind. He mounted his horse. Tradition says he whispered the five holy syllables of Shiva's name into its ear, and the horse leapt into the air. The Puranam itself only promises to tell how he rode his horse."
**Dialogue:** Cheraman: "Where my friend goes, I go."
**Sound:** hoofbeats on stone, then silence as they lift; a drum begins.

### 5. The ride (25 sec)
**Visual:** Above the clouds. The white elephant moves calmly ahead; the king's horse gallops over the clouds as if they were a field, mane flying, and draws level. The two friends look across at each other and smile. Below, the whole of the south lies like a map.
**Narration:** "The horse ran across the clouds as if they were a meadow, and it caught up with the elephant. Song 170 of the Thiruppugazh remembers this: the king who rode a dancing horse all the way to Kailasa, in the company of his friend."
**Sound:** galloping drum; a joyful melody on the nadaswaram.

### 6. Kailasa (25 sec)
**Visual:** Snow peaks rise out of the clouds, white and gold. A shining gateway. The friends dismount and walk in side by side. Within, a great light: the Lord, with the Goddess at his side. Sundarar bows; the king bows beside him.
**Narration:** "They came to the white mountain of Kailasa. The songs tell that the two friends came before the Lord together. The saint had been called, and the king had come out of love, and both were welcomed."
**Sound:** temple bells; a deep conch; the music opens out.

### 7. The poem (15 sec)
**Visual:** The king kneels and sings. Golden lines of verse float up like lamps around the throne. Final image: the two friends standing together in the light.
**Narration:** "There, the songs say, the king sang a poem of what he saw. It is called the Ula of Kailasa, and it is still sung today."
**Sound:** a single voice singing; one bell.

## The Thiruppugazh connection (end card)

Song 170 (Palani) tells how the Chera king rode a dancing horse to Kailasa with his friend Sundarar and sang the Adi Ula there. Show song 170.

## Production notes

- **Dignity.** Two friends, not a chase. Let the sky do the work.
- **Check scene 4:** the Puranam names the horse ride; the exact route in the song's commentary should be read before animation.
